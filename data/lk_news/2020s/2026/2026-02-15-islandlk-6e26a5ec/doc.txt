ZKTOR: A sovereign alternative enters Sri Lanka’s digital space

Sri Lanka has entered the beta testing phase of ZKTOR, a privacy-centric social media platform developed by Softa Technologies Limited, as concerns mount over external control of the region’s digital infrastructure.

The platform distinguishes itself through “privacy by design” architecture, incorporating multi-layer encryption, geographically bounded data handling, and structural restrictions on media extraction. Unlike mainstream platforms governed by foreign entities, ZKTOR is built on zero-knowledge principles that prevent behavioural profiling and data commodification.

Chief architect Sunil Kumar Singh, drawing on two decades of experience in Finland, has embedded Nordic governance principles of restraint and transparency into a platform designed for South Asia’s demographic diversity and digital landscape.

Significantly, ZKTOR operates debt-free, developed without venture capital or government grants—an industry rarity that insulates it from external financial pressures shaping data practices elsewhere.

Following mass testing in India and Nepal, with Bangladesh scheduled for future phases, ZKTOR’s Sri Lanka beta represents what analysts describe as a consequential shift: digital spaces that are designed, governed, and owned with regional intention rather than simply inherited from foreign providers.
