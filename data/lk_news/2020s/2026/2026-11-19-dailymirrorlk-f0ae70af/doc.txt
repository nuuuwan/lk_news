Negligence, possible poisoning concerns raised in Kawanthissa death inquiry

Legal submissions made before the Galgamuwa Magistrate’s Court in connection with the death of tusker Kawanthissa highlighted public concerns over alleged negligence by wildlife officers, possible poisoning and the veterinary treatment provided to the animal, particularly in light of an interim post-mortem report which had ruled out gunshot injuries as the cause of death and instead identified intestinal torsion (large intestinal volvulus), accompanied by severe toxaemia, as the cause.

The interim post-mortem report dated November 6, 2026 ruled out the gunshot injuries as the cause of death and identified intestinal torsion, or large intestinal volvulus, accompanied by severe toxaemia, as the cause.

President’s Counsel Upul Jayasuriya, appearing on behalf of wildlife photographer Pathum Bandara, told court that public concerns had raised questions about the conduct of wildlife officers and whether they had neglected their duties after initially treating the tusker for an injury to its right front leg.

He submitted to court that the interim post-mortem report had identified three gunshot injuries in the lower abdominal region of Kawanthissa.

He further submitted that the report had recommended obtaining an analysis from the Government Analyst to determine the nature of the toxins detected in the animal’s body, raising further questions about the need for a comprehensive investigation into its death.

“The interim post-mortem report has revealed that these three injuries were not responsible for the death of the animal. Therefore, a comprehensive investigation should be conducted into the treatment provided to Kawanthissa.

“Your Honour, this is why we are seeking an order for an independent foreign team to conduct a post-mortem examination into the death of the tusker. My client is willing to cooperate and facilitate the assistance of such a team. We understand that there are experts in Thailand capable of conducting such an examination. We are prepared to coordinate the process with the assistance of the Thai Embassy if court issues an appropriate order,” Jayasuriya submitted.

He also alleged that although Rs.1000 million had been allocated through the budget for wild elephant conservation, only around 30% of the allocated funds had been spent, while more than 70% had remained unutilised, raising concerns about the effective use of public funds for the welfare of wild elephants.

Meanwhile, Magistrate Ravindra Ranjan Bandara rejected the request by the parties to appoint an independent foreign team to conduct a post-mortem examination, observing that such a request should be made by the prosecution rather than a third party.

However, the court permitted several parties, including wildlife photographer Pathum Bandara and environmental organisation Voice for Bharhiya, to intervene in the magisterial inquiry.

The Wildlife Department informed court that the Criminal Investigation Department (CID), together with the Department of Wildlife Conservation, was conducting an investigation into the incident. It also informed court that a Presidential Commission had been appointed to investigate Kawanthissa’s death. The CID was noticed to appear before court on December 7.

President’s Counsel Upul Jayasuriya, with Attorneys-at-Law Lakmal Sooriyagoda, Gihan Siriwardena and Kanchana Samaradiwakara, appeared on behalf of Pathum Bandara.

President’s Counsel Anura Meddegoda, with Attorneys-at-Law B. Priyanga and Nipuni Dissanayaka, appeared for Voice for Bharhiya.
