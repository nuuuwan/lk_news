Budget 2027 brings relief closer to home

Colombo, Nov. 13 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka’s 2027 Budget offers salaried taxpayers higher take-home pay from April, while lower-income households stand to benefit from continued welfare payments and lower import levies on selected foods. For communities hit by Cyclone Ditwah, the Government is seeking Rs. 260 billion to carry reconstruction into next year.

President and Finance Minister Anura Kumara Dissanayake presented the Budget, third under his government, to Parliament yesterday against a backdrop of renewed price pressure. Annual inflation rose to 8.3% in October from 2.1% last December, due to higher fuel prices linked to the Middle East conflict among the factors. The economy grew 5% in 2025, but growth slowed to 4.2% in the second quarter of this year from 5.1% in the first.

“It is our duty to build economic stability, better jobs, fair incomes, a quality public service, a safe social environment and a sustainable future for future generations,” Dissanayake told Parliament.

The earliest changes are due at the border on Friday. The import levy on sprats is set to fall to Rs. 10 from Rs. 100 a kilogram, and that on Maldive fish to Rs. 10 from Rs. 302. The levy on dried chillies, including powdered chillies, is due to fall by Rs. 50 a kilogram. How much shoppers save will depend on what importers and retailers pass on. The Budget separately proposes raising the levy on imported sugar by Rs. 10 a kilogram to support local producers.

From April, the Government proposes raising the monthly income tax threshold to Rs. 162,500 from Rs. 150,000. A worker earning Rs. 160,000 a month in salary and allowances would fall below the proposed threshold. Wider bands would also reduce tax bills further up the income scale, with the top rate of 36% applying from about Rs. 433,000 a month, up from Rs. 358,000. Dissanayake put the additional monthly relief for taxpayers at between Rs. 750 and Rs. 15,750, depending on income.

The Budget allocates Rs. 230 billion for social protection allowances, including continued Aswesuma payments to eligible poor and extremely poor households. It also proposes raising the monthly allowances for people with disabilities and kidney patients to Rs. 12,500 from Rs. 10,000.

Public servants are due to receive the final phase of the basic salary revision announced in 2025, with Rs. 110 billion allocated for 2027. Across all three phases, the revision raises the minimum monthly basic salary by Rs. 15,750. The Government also proposes higher allowances for selected public employees, including doubling the monthly special duty allowance for nurses and staff in paramedical and supplementary medical services to Rs. 12,000.

For public servants who retired before 2020, the final phase of an earlier pension adjustment is due next year. Those who retired from 2020 through 2024 would receive an interim allowance of Rs. 2,500 a month from July, pending a phased pension revision planned to begin with the 2028 Budget.

The Government has allocated Rs. 131 billion in capital spending for public healthcare. The 1990 Suwa Seriya service is due to add 100 ambulances next year with Indian assistance, while a separate Rs. 3 billion is proposed to replace ageing ambulances in the wider health service. The Budget also proposes additional funding for equipment including MRI and CT scanners.

To bring care closer to communities, the Government has set aside Rs. 3.6 billion for Arogya Wellness Centres in 2027. It plans to build 1,000 centres with provincial councils by 2029, following a pilot involving 39 centres.

For families with children in school, the Government proposes removing VAT on exercise books and printed books from April and says the distribution of uniforms donated by China will continue next year. Education reforms already introduced in Grade 1 are due to reach Grades 2 and 6, and the Government plans to connect the remaining 750 secondary schools without reliable internet. Trainee teachers at Colleges of Education would receive Rs. 20,000 a month from April, up from Rs. 10,500.

A further Rs. 77 billion is allocated for drinking water, sanitation and water supplies for economic activity. The Budget provides Rs. 5.19 billion to start 13 drinking water projects and Rs. 2 billion to improve community water schemes. It also proposes Rs. 1 billion to tackle problems including seawater intrusion and bacterial contamination in Jaffna and the Vanni, and allocates Rs. 1.74 billion for wastewater projects in Ella, Moratuwa and Ekala.

The proposed Rs. 260 billion supplementary estimate would restore in 2027 the unused portion of a previously approved Rs. 500 billion reconstruction package. Dissanayake cited a post-disaster assessment that put Ditwah’s damage and economic losses at about US$3.4 billion, with housing, industry, railways, farms, schools and health services affected.

Including the supplementary estimate, the Treasury projects a 2027 budget deficit of 4.5% of gross domestic product, compared with 3.7% in the revised estimate for this year. The deficit is projected at 3.8% of GDP before the additional reconstruction provision.

The Government is also looking to small businesses to support jobs and incomes. It has allocated Rs. 67.69 billion for interest subsidies and refinancing, which it expects to support Rs. 174 billion in concessional lending through State and private banks. For larger projects, it expects to seek proposals within two months for a public-private logistics centre at the Port of Colombo and bring public-private partnership legislation to Parliament early next year.
