Jaffna temple festival eye injuries raise concerns over possible UV exposure

Colombo, August 22 (Daily Mirror) - Symptoms reported by 157 people including 58 men, 44 women and 55 children treated at Jaffna Teaching Hospital after attending a Hindu temple festival have raised serious concerns over possible ultraviolet (UV) eye injuries, a senior eye doctor said.

The patients developed severe eye pain, irritation, watering, sensitivity to light and difficulty opening their eyes after attending a festival at a Murugan temple in Piranpatru, where high-powered electric lamps had been installed around the temple.

A senior eye doctor said the symptoms were consistent with possible photokeratitis, an acute injury to the surface of the eye caused by excessive UV exposure. However, medical examinations and technical testing are required to establish whether the lamps caused the injuries.

Police have seized several high-powered, Chinese-made lamps from the temple and sent them for technical examination. Investigators are examining their electrical specifications, intensity, intended use and whether they were suitable for use around large crowds, including children.

Photographs show large glass lamps with arc-tube assemblies resembling high-intensity discharge (HID) lamps, although their exact type, wattage and specifications cannot be confirmed from photographs alone. Some such lamps can produce UV radiation, with protective glass, filters or appropriate fixtures needed in certain designs to prevent harmful exposure.

Photokeratitis, sometimes described as a “sunburn of the eye”, can cause severe pain, watering, a gritty sensation and sensitivity to light. Symptoms may also appear several hours after exposure.

The Jaffna District Secretary and Sandilipay Divisional Secretary have advised anyone who attended the festival on Thursday to undergo an eye examination at Jaffna Teaching Hospital.

Police have not established that the lamps caused the injuries, and the technical examination results have not yet been released. Authorities are expected to determine the lamps’ type, power and UV output, whether protective components were in place, and the duration and distance of exposure before establishing the cause.
