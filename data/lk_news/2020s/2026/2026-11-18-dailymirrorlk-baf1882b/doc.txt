Factory orders pick up, services gain pace in October

Seasonal demand gave Sri Lanka’s factories a lift in October, with stronger orders for textiles, apparel, food and beverages, while a pickup in personal services helped the services sector expand at a faster pace, the Central Bank’s latest business survey showed.

The manufacturing Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) rose to 55.3 from 53.0 in September, while the services business activity index climbed to 62.5 from 60.5. A reading above 50 indicates expansion from the previous month.

New orders led the improvement in manufacturing, with the index rising four points to 55.0. Stronger demand for textiles and apparel also helped lift the production index to 52.2 from 51.0.

Factories added to raw material stocks after inventories held steady in September, while suppliers took longer to deliver amid increased demand for inputs. Factory employment continued to rise, though the pace of hiring slowed.

Personal services made the largest contribution to the pickup in services activity, with finance and goods transport also helping drive the expansion. Growth in new business eased, however, with the index slipping to 59.8 from 60.9. Financial and personal services led the gains in new business, with accommodation, food and beverage services and goods transport also contributing.

Services firms stepped up hiring to meet operating needs, lifting the employment index to 56.1 from 54.0, while work backlogs fell at a faster pace.

Manufacturers expect festive-season demand to support activity over the next three months. Services firms also expect business to expand, helped by anticipated higher tourist arrivals and festive spending, although their expectations index eased to 74.7 from 76.2. The Central Bank cited global uncertainty as a risk to the services outlook.
