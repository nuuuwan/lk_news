SC rules police promotion lawful

Colombo, Sep. 14 (Daily Mirror) - The Supreme Court, after hearing 71 petitions filed by police officers alleging an infringement of their fundamental rights by the Police Commission, has ruled that the promotion of a police Sergeant to the rank of Sub-Inspector was lawful and carried out in accordance with the prescribed promotion procedure.

The petitioners had alleged that the promotion granted to the 11th respondent, M.L.D.W.K. Jayasekera, to the rank of Sub-Inspector, had infringed their fundamental rights.

A three-member bench comprising Chief Justice Preethi Padman Surasena and Justices Mahinda Samayawardena and Sampath K.B. Wijeratna, after considering the submissions of both the petitioners and respondents, observed that the material before the Court showed that the promotion granted to the 11th respondent was not a belated attempt to secure an undeserved advantage.

Instead, the Court observed that it was the result of the 11th respondent's consistent efforts over many years to implement recommendations originally made in his favour in 2001.

The Court further observed that the 11th respondent's claim did not originate in 2019, but had been repeatedly pursued by him and consistently supported by senior police officers over a period of two decades.

The petitioners themselves had acknowledged that special promotions could be granted to police officers who satisfied the relevant promotion criteria.

Accordingly, the Supreme Court affirmed that the promotion was lawful and had not violated the fundamental rights of the 71 petitioners.

President's Counsel Faiszer Musthapha, Senior Counsel Ian Fernando and Saheeda Barrie appeared for the 11th respondent.
