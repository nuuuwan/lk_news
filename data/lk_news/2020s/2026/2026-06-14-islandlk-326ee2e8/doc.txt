FIFA World Cup 2026 likely to become the most lucrative in football history

-Based on a report from The Business Standard The 2026 FIFA World Cup, co-hosted by the United States, Canada, and Mexico, is poised to shatter financial records and become the most lucrative tournament in football history, according to industry projections.

FIFA is targeting approximately $13 billion in total revenue for the four-year cycle culminating in the event. The tournament alone is expected to generate around $8.9 billion in 2026 – a staggering 54-72% increase over the 2022 Qatar World Cup.

What is driving this unprecedented growth? The expanded format — 48 teams and 104 matches, up from 32 and 64 respectively — is the primary engine. More matches mean more ticket sales, more broadcast hours, and more sponsorship inventory.

Ticket sales and hospitality: ~$3 billion (more than triple 2022)

However, this edition also carries unusual risks. Trade tensions among the three host nations and the largest geographical footprint in World Cup history have added political and logistical complexities unseen before.

Beyond the pitch, economists are watching closely. As one observer noted, the 2026 World Cup has become “a consequential experiment” – one that will test whether bigger truly means better for the business of global sport.

If FIFA meets its targets, the 2026 World Cup will not only redefine football but also rewrite the economics of mega-events for decades to come.
