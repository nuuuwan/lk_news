BYD building Sri Lanka’s EV ecosystem beyond the showroom

The electric vehicle revolution is entering a new phase in Sri Lanka — where the battle is no longer only about selling vehicles, but about building the confidence, technology and service ecosystem that will define the future of mobility.

As the country embraces new energy vehicles (NEVs), BYD, the world’s largest manufacturer of NEVs, is strengthening its long-term commitment to Sri Lanka through a major expansion of aftersales infrastructure, technical capability and customer support.

Within just nine months of vehicle imports resuming, BYD has secured a commanding position in Sri Lanka’s automotive market, accounting for 31.1% of all brand-new passenger vehicle registrations and 63.3% of new electric vehicle registrations, according to company data.

But industry leaders emphasise that the real test of EV adoption lies beyond the initial purchase.

“The future of electric mobility will not be decided only by the vehicle on the road. It will depend on the confidence customers have in the technology, servicing capability and long-term support, a senior official said.

However, electric vehicles introduce a completely new technology landscape involving high-voltage battery systems, advanced software, electronic controls and specialised diagnostics.

BYD Sri Lanka, represented through authorised distributor John Keells CG Auto, has focused on developing a dedicated NEV aftersales ecosystem to address these challenges.

“An EV is not simply another vehicle with a different engine. It is a technology platform on wheels. Therefore, servicing requires specialised knowledge, equipment and continuous training, an industry expert said.

BYD’s aftersales network has already completed more than 25,000 service and repair operations, while its service centres have the capacity to handle more than 150 vehicles daily across the island.

The Wattala Service Centre, opened in August 2024, represents BYD’s largest service facility in Sri Lanka.

The four-storey purpose-built centre can service up to 325 vehicles per week and includes dedicated facilities such as a high-voltage battery room, EV charging infrastructure, advanced diagnostics, precision wheel alignment and customer-focused service areas.

“Customers investing in new technology need reassurance. A world-class vehicle requires a world-class support system, a company spokesperson said.

Further strengthening its commitment, BYD Sri Lanka opened the Seeduwa Body & Paint and Accident Repair Centre in February 2026.

The facility, dedicated exclusively to BYD and DENZA vehicles, can accommodate more than 100 vehicles at a time and represents a significant step towards specialised EV accident repair capability in Sri Lanka.

By separating body repair operations from general servicing, the facility aims to reduce congestion and improve turnaround times for customers requiring accident-related repairs.

More than 33 certified technical staff have completed specialised training programmes, including overseas training in China and regional support programmes.

“This transition requires people with new skills. Developing local technicians is essential for creating a sustainable EV industry,” a company official said.

The company is also evaluating Sri Lanka as a potential technical training hub for South Asia, highlighting the country’s increasing importance in the regional EV landscape.

BYD’s 3S (Sales, Service and Spare Parts) network now extends across Kurunegala, Moratuwa, Kandy, Galle, Ratnapura and Ampara, with future expansion planned for Negombo, Anuradhapura and Jaffna.

Industry analysts believe this wider network will be vital as Sri Lanka moves towards greater electrification of transport.

“The companies that succeed in the EV era will not only be those that sell vehicles. They will be those that build trust, service networks and customer relationships, an analyst observed.
