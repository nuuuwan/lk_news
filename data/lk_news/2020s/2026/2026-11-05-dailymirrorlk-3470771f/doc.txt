Liquor excise jumps 27% to Rs.137.5bn in 1H as production rises 13%

The excise duty on liquor rose 27.1 percent to Rs.137.5 billion in the first half of 2026, from Rs.108.2 billion in the same period last year, as the liquor production climbed 13.3 percent and the new rules on the timing of the duty payments were introduced, as per the Finance Ministry data.

The production increased to 19.7 million absolute litres, from 17.4 million absolute litres a year earlier.

The ministry attributed the revenue growth to “the introduction of the new rules on the timing of the duty payments on liquor production, levying of fees and higher production volumes”.

An excise notification, effective from December 12, 2025, revised the annual licence fee and security deposit and introduced a one-time fee for entering the industry.

The revenue per absolute litre grew about 12.2 percent to roughly Rs.6,981, from Rs.6,221, indicating that the higher volumes accounted for only around half of the increase, with the balance coming from a higher yield.

The first-half collection covers 56.6 percent of the annual estimate, leaving about Rs.105 billion to be collected in the second half, 23.3 percent less than the first-half intake.

It is also equivalent to 64.4 percent of Rs.213.4 billion in excise duty earned from the locally produced liquor in 2024. Liquor accounted for 24.4 percent of total excise revenue of Rs.562.7 billion.

However, the collections lost some momentum after the first quarter, for which the Excise Department reported revenue of Rs.70.4 billion, 114.8 percent higher than a year earlier. This points to around Rs.67.6 billion for the April to June quarter, about 4 percent below the first-quarter figure.
