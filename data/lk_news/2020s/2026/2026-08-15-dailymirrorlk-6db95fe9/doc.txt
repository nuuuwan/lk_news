Decriminalising LGBTIQ, death penalty etc. as means to secure GSP+

Criminalisation of same-sex relations was introduced into our countries by the British

The European Union (EU),  it is reported, has called on Lanka to adopt comprehensive anti-discrimination laws to protect LGBTIQ persons as well as for decriminalisation of same sex relations as a means to achieve GST+ benefits. Wow!  Same sex relationships and fluid gender expressions are widely acknowledged and recorded in regional literature. They were accommodated within pre-colonial South Asian societies. As for the death sentence,  it is a dead letter.

Criminalisation of same-sex relations was introduced into our countries by the British   who introduced Section 377of the Penal Code in1860 (enacted in 1862) across British India. It later served as the legal template for criminalising same-sex acts in neighbouring territories like Sri Lanka and Pakistan. Today, the EU calls on Lanka to decriminalise what was never looked on as a criminal act prior to Europeans inflicting their narrow-minded beliefs into our part of the world.

At a given time in history, even with all economic odds stacked against it, the then Sri Lankan governments dared challenge the great economic powers of the day. The then government proceeded to do what it felt was best for the interests of this country. For instance, it began taking back its assets from the colonial powers.

It nationalised the tea estates, took over distribution of petroleum products and even closed down the Israeli embassy in the country in 1971. This despite pro-Israel groups in the United Kingdom threatening to boycott Ceylon tea if Lanka persisted with the move.

At that time,  the export of tea was Lanka’s biggest exchange earner. But the then government proceeded with the closure of the Embassy regardless of threats.

The Government’s principled stance even penetrated the field of sports. In 1979 the Sri Lankan cricket team refused to play their scheduled group match against Israel during the inaugural ICC Trophy in England (it served as the qualifying tournament for the 1979 Cricket World Cup) because of continuing Israeli Human Rights violations in Palestine.

The then government instructed the team led by Anura Tennakoon to boycott the fixture due to its foreign policy of supporting the Palestinian cause and in opposition to Israel’s continued violation of human rights in Palestine. Because Lanka refused to take the field, the match was recorded as a walkover and Israel was awarded points.

Despite losing those points, Sri Lanka topped their group on net run rate and ultimately won the ICC Trophy tournament!

Unfortunately today, our government does not seem to be in a position to even explain to these modern-day delusional Don Quixotes that these old British puritanical beliefs, once imposed on our people, have now become deeply ingrained into our religio-cultural milieu. These beliefs cannot be changed overnight with a stroke of a pen. It will take time for large numbers of people to accept these ‘new’ beliefs.

In the event the government hastily adopts the required legislation to please our new neo-colonial masters, we will see a tidal wave of opposition led by religious leaders. Soon,  opportunistic politicians  will be joining hands with religious leaders calling the decriminalisng of same-sex relations as an attack on our culture. They may even demand a referendum be held on the matter.

What is even stranger perhaps is that even in the EU countries, attacks on the LGBTIQ community are growing. Data and human rights reports indicate that harassment, hate speech, and physical attacks targeting the LGBTQ+ community are growing across parts of Europe.

According to the European Union Agency for Fundamental Rights, this rise in hostility occurs alongside a broader political backlash and an increase in anti-queer rhetoric from nationalist and extremist groups. The report adds that schools deal with LGBTIQ issues more positively and proactively, and young people feel more supported by their teachers and peers. Nevertheless, bullying, harassment and violence have reached high levels.

Before demanding that  Lankans decriminalise same-sex relations immediately to receive GSP+ benefits, it may be better if the EU invested in  programmes to convince our now blind-sighted people to accept the ‘new concept’ the EU is asking us to embrace. If not, the backlash may lead to further alienation of the community they seek to protect.
