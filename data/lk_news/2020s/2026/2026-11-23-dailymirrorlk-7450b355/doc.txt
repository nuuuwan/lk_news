Animal group seeks action over alleged dumping of airport dogs

Colombo, November 23 (Daily Mirror) - The Animal Welfare Forum (AWC) has initiated legal and administrative action over allegations that dogs removed from the Bandaranaike International Airport (BIA) and Mattala Rajapaksa International Airport (MRIA) are being abandoned in forests and garbage dumps.

In a series of complaints submitted today (23), the organisation called on authorities to investigate reports that dogs captured from the airport premises were being transported and released in forest areas in Weerawila and Thanamalwila in the Hambantota District, as well as at the Kerawalapitiya garbage dump.

The organisation said reports and photographs circulating on social media suggested that a team had been capturing dogs near SriLankan Airlines and Sri Lanka Air Force facilities at Mattala since early morning, allegedly with plans to release them in nearby forest areas later in the day.

However, following an inquiry by Attorney-at-Law Sajeewa Gamage, Mattala Airport authorities had denied that the dogs or the removal operation originated from the airport, the organisation claimed.

The forum has therefore sought official information to establish where the dogs were captured, who authorised their removal and where they were taken.

An application under the Right to Information Act was submitted to Airport and Aviation Services (Sri Lanka) today (23), with copies sent to its Chairman and the Director General of the Civil Aviation Authority of Sri Lanka.

The request covers the period from January 2024 to November 23, 2026, and seeks details of wildlife hazard management plans, airport perimeter inspections, the number of dogs captured and removed, contractors involved, locations where the animals were released and public funds spent on such operations.

The Civil Aviation Authority registered the application under reference number RTI-99 and informed the organisation that a decision would be communicated within 14 days.

The forum has also lodged a formal complaint with the Director General of the Department of Wildlife Conservation, requesting immediate action to prevent dogs from being released in wildlife areas.

It has called for wildlife officers in Weerawila, Lunugamvehera, Bundala, Thanamalwila and Hambantota to conduct patrols and road inspections. It has also requested investigations and legal action against those responsible if dogs have already been abandoned.

In a separate communication, the organisation urged airport authorities, SriLankan Airlines and the Sri Lanka Air Force to immediately stop the alleged practice and adopt proper measures to manage animals within airport premises.

The forum also raised concerns over an incident in which a mother dog was allegedly separated from her five nursing puppies and abandoned at the Kerawalapitiya garbage dump. The mother and puppies were later located and rescued, while a witness had agreed to provide evidence for legal action, it said.

The organisation acknowledged that keeping dogs away from airport runways and operational areas was essential for aviation safety. However, it said authorities should address the problem by improving airport security infrastructure rather than transporting and abandoning animals in forests or garbage dumps.

It warned that abandoning domestic dogs in wildlife habitats could expose them to starvation, attacks by wild animals and infectious diseases, including rabies, canine distemper and parvovirus.

The organisation also warned that such practices could threaten native wildlife and disturb the ecological balance, while packs of abandoned dogs could affect wild elephants and contribute to human-elephant conflict.

Citing animal welfare and wildlife protection laws, the forum said the alleged abandonment of dogs could violate the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Ordinance, the Fauna and Flora Protection Ordinance and other relevant legislation.

It also pointed to the 2006 National Policy prohibiting the culling of dogs, which promotes scientific population management through Catch, Neuter, Vaccinate and Release programmes.

As a long-term solution, the organisation has proposed repairing airport perimeter fences, securing drainage channels and installing animal-proof barriers at vehicle and pedestrian entrances.

It has also recommended CCTV monitoring, proper waste management and sterilisation and vaccination programmes within a one-to-two-kilometre radius of airports.

Dogs currently living within airport premises should be documented, given medical treatment and sterilised before being placed in suitable homes or transferred to officially recognised shelters, it proposed.

The forum has requested written confirmation within seven days that the alleged abandonment of dogs has been stopped and called for a meeting with relevant authorities within 14 days to discuss implementing the proposed measures.

It warned that it would pursue criminal proceedings and lodge complaints with national and international aviation regulatory bodies if the alleged practices continued.
