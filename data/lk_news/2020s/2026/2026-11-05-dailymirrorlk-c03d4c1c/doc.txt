Rs. 2.2 bn. hydropower cost missing from coal loss calculation: Ranawaka

Colombo, Nov. 5 (Daily Mirror) - Former Energy Minister Patali Champika Ranawaka has questioned the accuracy of calculations relating to losses caused by low-quality coal imports, claiming that the opportunity cost of hydropower used to compensate for the shortfall has not been included.

Addressing the Public Utilities Commission of Sri Lanka (PUCSL), Ranawaka said the calculations relating to the initial 19 coal shipments imported through Trident and five subsequent shipments during 2025–2026 contained a significant error.

According to the figures he cited, additional coal consumption due to poor-quality coal had resulted in an expenditure of Rs. 7.099 billion, while another Rs. 93 million had been incurred in maintenance costs, including ash-handling expenses.

A further Rs. 8.572 billion had been spent on purchasing electricity to compensate for the shortfall in coal-fired generation, he said.

However, Ranawaka pointed out that of the total 197,673 megawatt-hours (MWh) of electricity lost, 35,209 MWh attributed to hydropower had been assigned zero cost.

"This is a very serious error in the calculation," he said, arguing that the value of hydropower used during the period should also be included.

He rejected claims that reservoirs were overflowing during the period, saying they had not reached maximum capacity.

He explained that the Mahaweli and Kelani reservoir systems operated as cascade systems, allowing electricity to be generated through other reservoirs even when one reservoir reached its capacity.

Ranawaka urged the PUCSL to calculate the opportunity cost of the hydropower used to compensate for the coal generation shortfall.

He said the hydropower could otherwise have been preserved for future use, reducing the need to generate electricity using expensive diesel or furnace oil.

According to his calculations, the opportunity cost of the hydropower used amounted to approximately Rs. 2.2 billion.
