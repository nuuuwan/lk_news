Public approval of the government declines: Verité Research poll

Colombo, Aug. 16 (Daily Mirror) - Public approval of the government has declined to 50% as perceptions of Sri Lanka’s economic outlook have weakened, according to the latest ‘Mood of the Nation’ poll conducted by Verité Research.

The July 2026 poll found that government approval had fallen from 65% in February, which was a post-election high.

Perceptions of the economic outlook also deteriorated sharply. The proportion of respondents who said economic conditions were “getting better” fell to 41.63% from 64% in the previous round, while those who believed conditions were “getting worse” rose to 40.29%, from around 15%.

Meanwhile, 31.40% said they disapproved of the way the current government was working, compared to around 15% previously.

More than half of those surveyed, or 55.59%, rated the current economic conditions as “poor”, while only 38% described them as “good” or “excellent”. This marked a reversal from the previous survey, when a majority had viewed economic conditions positively.

The Economic Confidence Index, which averages net ratings on the economic outlook and the current state of the economy, also returned to negative territory, recording minus 8 in the latest poll.

The index, which ranges from minus 100 to plus 100, had stood at minus 39 in mid-2024 before moving into positive territory after February 2025.

Verité Research said the questions used in the Mood of the Nation poll differed from those used in other recent national surveys. While the Social Indicators Survey measured satisfaction with specific political figures and groups, the Mood of the Nation poll asked respondents about their approval of the government as a whole.

The latest survey was conducted from July 11 to 30 among 2,013 Sri Lankan adults selected through a nationally representative, multi-stage, randomised sample covering separate households.

The survey was designed with a maximum sampling error margin of ±2.21% for the full sample at a 95% confidence level, although the researchers noted that implementation-related factors could further affect the margin of error.
