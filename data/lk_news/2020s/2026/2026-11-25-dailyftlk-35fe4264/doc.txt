CAA tells Parliament panel it has no power to set domestic gas prices

The Consumer Affairs Authority (CAA) has told a parliamentary committee that it has no power to determine domestic gas prices, after a private distributor significantly raised the price of its domestic gas cylinder, the Parliament Secretariat said.

Officials of the Ministry of Trade, Commerce, Food Security and Cooperative Development and the CAA were summoned before the Sectoral Oversight Committee on Environment, Agriculture and Resource Sustainability on Wednesday to discuss the increase. The meeting was chaired by Committee Chair MP Hector Appuhamy.

The CAA Chairman said the Authority had stepped in because of the difficulties the increase caused consumers, although it lacked the power to set prices. He said the company had agreed to reduce the price after discussions with the Authority.

According to the CAA Chairman, the company said that because its share of the domestic gas market was relatively small, it had to buy gas at a higher price and had raised its cylinder prices in line with the higher purchase cost.

The Committee urged the CAA to ensure that decisions on domestic gas prices do not cause difficulties for consumers. It also stressed the need to ensure an uninterrupted supply of domestic gas in the future and to take the necessary steps to protect consumers from price increases, the Parliament Secretariat said.

MPs Upul Kithsiri, M.A.C.S. Chathuri Gangani, Susantha Kumara Navaratne, Sudath Balagalla, Kitnan Selvaraj and Gnanamuththu Srineshan also attended the meeting.
