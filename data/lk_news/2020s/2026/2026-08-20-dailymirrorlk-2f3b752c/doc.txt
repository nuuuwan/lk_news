New preschool guides launched under education reforms

A set of new teacher and activity guides aimed at strengthening inquiry-based and activity-based learning in preschools has been launched under the Government’s new education reform programme.

The Early Childhood Support Resource Guide, prepared in line with the Early Childhood Education Curriculum Framework, was launched recently at the Ministry of Education premises in Isurupaya under the patronage of Prime Minister Dr. Harini Amarasuriya.

The initiative seeks to ensure that children receive quality education from the early stages of life by guiding preschool teachers towards a child-centred, play-based and inquiry-based approach to learning.

The resource package, prepared under the theme “Starting Right”, consists of four main components: the Teacher Guide, Suggested Activity Guide, Teaching and Learning Material Manual and Master Material Training Manual.

The guides are designed to provide preschool teachers, trainers and other stakeholders with practical guidance on introducing activity-based learning through play and other age-appropriate learning tools.

The programme is expected to be implemented across preschools islandwide, with training programmes for trainers and preschool teachers to be conducted throughout the country.

The initiative is being implemented under the leadership of the Ministry of Education, Higher Education and Vocational Education and the Ministry of Women and Child Affairs, with support from institutions including the National Institute of Education and UNICEF.

The Prime Minister said plans had been made to successfully introduce the new education reforms beginning with early childhood education.

She said the new guidelines represented a significant change in the learning process by promoting a child-centred, inquiry-based and activity-based approach to early childhood development.

“We are trying to build an education system that evaluates what children can do with their knowledge, rather than one that merely provides knowledge,” she said.

The Prime Minister also stressed the importance of educating parents on early childhood development and creating a society that is sensitive to children's needs and safety.

Minister of Women and Child Affairs Saroja Savitri Paulraj said the guidelines represented a strong foundation for the country’s future generations.

She said the Government expected to establish an islandwide preschool system operating under a single national policy by 2027, allowing parents to send their children to preschools with confidence.

Deputy Minister of Women and Child Affairs Dr. Namal Sudarshana, Secretary to the Ministry of Education, Higher Education and Vocational Education Nalaka Kaluwewa, Director General of the National Institute of Education Senior Professor O. G. Dayaratne Banda, Additional Secretaries, provincial education authorities and UNICEF representatives were among those present at the event.
