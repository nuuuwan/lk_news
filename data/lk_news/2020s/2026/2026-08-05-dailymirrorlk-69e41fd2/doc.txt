Are Easter Sunday victims happy with the death sentences?

Former Defence Ministry secretary Hemasiri Fernando and former police inspector-general Pujith Jayasundara were sentenced to death by the Colombo High Court in a 2-1 decision over the Easter Sunday terrorist attacks of 2019

Last Friday (July 31), Sri Lankans were thunderstruck with the news that former Defence Ministry secretary Hemasiri Fernando and former police inspector-general Pujith Jayasundara were sentenced to death by the Colombo High Court in a 2-1 decision over the Easter Sunday terrorist attacks of 2019.

Fernando and Jayasundara had separately been indicted by the Attorney General on charges including criminal neglect of duty by failing to take action to prevent the Easter Sunday terrorist attacks, despite receiving prior intelligence information that such attacks were imminent.

People were dumbfounded by the sentence not because of their approval or disapproval of it, but the court’s verdict came as a bolt from the blue on them. Even the journalist who cover the courts had failed to sense such a serious decision by the three-judge bench beforehand.

Many people had expressed their views in mainstream media as well as on  social media on it. Some people, while approving the judgement and the punishment, seemed to have bewildered by the fact that others who were ordered to pay compensation for the same offence – negligence and dereliction of duty – by the Supreme Court in 2023 had not been indicted in this case.

Some others seemed to attempt to find fault with the arrest and detention of former State Intelligence Service chief, retired Major General Suresh Sallay,  using the sentence against Jayasundara and Fernando.

Underlining a legal point, the former Justice Minister Mohamed Ali Sabry, in a Facebook post, had stated “Negligence, however serious, and dereliction of duty, however catastrophic their consequences, do not ordinarily constitute the mental element required for offences such as murder or attempted murder. A failure to act, however culpable, is not the same as an intention to kill or an intention to facilitate murder.”

However, an important segment of people – the Christian community that was made victim directly by the terrorist attacks – maintained a deafening silence over the sentence of corporal punishment on the two former high officials responsible for the public security. They - especially the Catholic community led by Cardinal Malcolm Ranjith that has been in the forefront in demanding justice for the victims of the Easter Sunday carnage – did not seem to be moved by the court’s decision.

They neither issued any statement,  nor did any journalist think it fit to seek their stance on it. In a way, one could argue that a community—led by a leader who asked his followers, in the wake of the crime, to forgive the terrorists who brutally killed hundreds of men, women, and children—cannot be happy with the death sentence imposed on people who failed to protect those innocent lives.

In fact, the victim community – which was mainly concerned about who the culprits behind the terrorist attacks were and what prompted them to kill nearly 300 innocent people – was sent from pillar to post by  successive governments, giving false hopes.

President Maithripala Sirisena who was in office at the time of the terrorist attacks appointed a three-member committee headed by Supreme Court judge Vijith Malalgoda to conduct investigations into the tragedy, within 24 hours after the incident. The Committee submitted its’ report on June 10, 2019 to President Sirisena.

While the committee was conducting its probe, a nine- member Parliamentary Select Committee (PSC) headed by Deputy Speaker Ananda Kumarasiri was also appointed to inquire into the same matter on May 22, 2019. The final report of the Committee containing 272 pages was presented to the Parliament on October 23, 2019, but nothing important came out either from the Malalgoda Committee report or the PSC report.

Frustrated by the government’s actions, the Cardinal, during a service at St. Sebastian’s Church in Katuwapitiya which was devastated by Easter Sunday Bombings, stated that the leadership lacked a backbone, had failed the country, and must step down to allow capable individuals to run the government. He seemed to be turning to the then Presidential hopeful, Gotabaya Rajapaksa who was highly critical of the joint administration by President Sirisena and Prime Minister Ranil Wickremesinghe.

Rajapaksa and his party, the Sri Lanka Podujana Peramuna (SLPP) assumed office in November 2019, and Cardinal Ranjith expressed satisfaction in the new government’s actions towards revealing the mastermind of the attacks. Prominent politicians such as former Trade Minister Rishard Bathiudeen and his brother Riyad were arrested in connection with the terrorist attacks, on purported strong evidence against them.

However, the Rajapaksa government too, soon disappointed the victim community. Rishard and Riyad were released on the grounds of lack of evidence. Cardinal Malcolm Ranjith accused the government of “deal politics” which he said undermined his faith in the investigation into the Easter Sunday. The government leaders, who promised to reveal the mastermind, on its part, stated that Nawfer Mawlavi, who is in police custody since the previous administration,  was the mastermind.

By that time, a theory that accused the Rajapaksas of planning and executing the suicide bombings was developing and the leaders of the Catholic Community too were seemed to have been carried away by it. Towards the end of  the Gotabaya Rajapaksa government, which was marked by the famous ‘Aragalaya, ” they  totally lost faith in the government and that continued during the Ranil Wickremesinghe administration that was formed with the support of the SLPP. Hence, they rejected the two committees that was appointed by Wickremesinghe to look into the intelligence failures in the run up to the terrorist attacks.

In the lead up to the last Presidential election, when the victory of the National People Power (NPP) had almost been sealed, the victim community was on the same wavelength with the NPP, with regard to the mastermind of the Easter Sunday attacks. However, they were again frustrated with the NPP administration as well, due to the delay in a breakthrough in investigations, until Suresh Sallay, the former head of State Intelligence Service (SIS), was arrested on February 25, this year.

It is against this backdrop that the Cardinal filed an intervening petition in the Court of Appeal, urging the dismissal of a writ petition by Suresh Sallay, challenging his detention. Therefore, the death sentences handed to Pujith Jayasundara and Hemasiri Fernando seem to have little real impact on the victim community.
