Joint Opposition urges international partners to review Anti-Corruption Bill

The Joint Opposition has called on international development partners supporting Sri Lanka's anti-corruption reforms to examine the human rights implications of the proposed Anti-Corruption (Amendment) Bill.

In a statement issued today, Joint Opposition Convener Professor G.L. Peiris urged organisations including the United Nations Development Programme, the European Union, the Asian Development Bank and the Government of Japan to review the proposed legislation.

The Opposition noted that these partners have provided technical and financial assistance to strengthen the Commission to Investigate Allegations of Bribery or Corruption and other justice sector institutions.

It called on development partners to assess the proposed bail regime in light of Sri Lanka's prison overcrowding, engage with the government on legislation that does not worsen pre-trial detention, and ensure that continued support for anti-corruption efforts remains consistent with international human rights standards.

While reiterating its support for efforts to combat corruption, the Joint Opposition maintained that such measures should not come at the expense of fundamental rights or further burden an already overcrowded prison system.

The Opposition said it would continue raising its concerns in Parliament and other public forums while supporting what it described as balanced anti-corruption reforms that protect both accountability and individual liberties.
