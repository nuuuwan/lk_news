Sri Lanka’s exports rise 5% to US$13.6 billion in first nine months of 2026

Colombo, Oct. 27 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka’s total export earnings increased by 5.04% to US$13.61 billion during the first nine months of 2026, compared with US$12.96 billion in the same period last year, according to the Sri Lanka Export Development Board (EDB).

The growth was supported by an increase in both merchandise and services exports, with merchandise exports rising by 5.35% to US$10.76 billion and services exports growing by 3.88% to US$2.85 billion during January to September.

September recorded particularly strong growth, with total exports increasing by 17.23% year-on-year to US$1.69 billion, compared with US$1.44 billion in September 2025.

Merchandise exports rose by 21.66% to US$1.39 billion during the month, while services exports increased by 0.67% to US$306.17 million.

Commenting on the performance, EDB Chairman and Chief Executive Officer Mangala Wijesinghe said the results reflected the resilience of Sri Lanka’s export sector amid an increasingly competitive global trading environment.

He said the priority was to sustain the growth momentum by improving competitiveness, developing new products and markets, increasing value addition and strengthening the country’s integration into global supply chains.

The EDB aims to build on the latest performance through the National Export Development Plan 2026–2030, which targets annual export earnings of US$36 billion by 2030.

Among the major merchandise export sectors, rubber and rubber-based products recorded strong growth in September, with earnings increasing by 33.68% to US$99.31 million.

Exports of industrial and surgical rubber gloves rose by 34.38% to US$31.70 million, while pneumatic and retreaded tyres and tubes increased by 39.57% to US$54.95 million.

Exports of spices, essential oils and oleoresins recorded a 73.19% increase to US$74.73 million. Pepper exports surged by 165.38% to US$23.46 million, while cinnamon exports grew by 68.27% to US$42.59 million.

The food and beverages sector also performed strongly, with export earnings rising by 27.92% to US$63.77 million. Electrical and electronic components recorded an 88.35% increase to US$68.54 million, supported mainly by a 114.62% rise in insulated wires and cables.

Seafood exports grew by 4.01% to US$23.61 million, while apparel and textile exports increased by 3.46% to US$439.78 million during the month.

Transport and logistics services exports were estimated to have increased by 23.32% to US$167.9 million.

However, several key export sectors recorded declines. Tea export earnings fell by 1.38% to US$135.11 million in September, largely due to weaker demand in Middle Eastern markets. Exports to the United Arab Emirates, Iran and Iraq declined by 42.56%, 34.57% and 55.73%, respectively.

Coconut-based product exports fell by 8.88% to US$111.60 million, while ornamental fish exports declined by 35.15% to US$1.55 million.

Despite the strong monthly performance, some major sectors faced difficulties during the first nine months of the year.

Apparel and textile export earnings declined by 5.68% to US$3.78 billion, reflecting weaker demand in key markets, including the United States, the European Union and the United Kingdom.

Tea exports fell by 6.83% to US$1.08 billion, while seafood exports declined by 2.03% to US$180.81 million during the January–September period.

In contrast, several value-added manufacturing and agricultural sectors recorded substantial cumulative growth.

Electrical and electronic component exports increased by 61.05% to US$508.86 million, while food and beverage exports rose by 20.90% to US$526.31 million. Exports of spices, essential oils and oleoresins grew by 30.53% to US$430.53 million.

Rubber and rubber-based product exports increased by 8.44% to US$773.88 million, while coconut-based product exports rose by 3.68% to US$942.5 million.

Information and communications technology and business process management (ICT/BPM) exports were estimated to have increased by 3.69% to US$1.24 billion during the first nine months of 2026.

The United States remained Sri Lanka’s largest merchandise export destination, with earnings increasing by 18.06% to US$281.84 million in September. Cumulative exports to the US edged up by 0.05% to US$2.25 billion during January–September.

India strengthened its position as Sri Lanka’s second-largest export destination, overtaking the United Kingdom. Exports to India rose by 12.3% to US$889.84 million during the first nine months, while September exports increased by 29.65% to US$113.22 million.

In contrast, exports to the UK declined by 9.33% to US$654.36 million during the January–September period.

Turkey also recorded significant growth, with cumulative exports increasing by 94.01% to US$218.24 million. Exports to China rose by 16.46% to US$234.39 million, supported partly by increased demand for activated carbon.

Exports to India and Pakistan, Sri Lanka’s free trade agreement partners, also recorded positive growth. Combined exports to the two countries increased by 33.85% to US$125.14 million in September.

Exports to the European Union, which accounted for approximately 20.12% of Sri Lanka’s merchandise exports, increased by 1.92% in September. However, cumulative exports to the bloc declined marginally by 0.15% during the first nine months.

Italy remained the leading EU destination, with exports reaching US$514.28 million, up 1.48%. Exports to Germany declined by 4.27% to US$513.08 million, while shipments to the Netherlands and France fell by 5.35% and 6.54%, respectively. Exports to Belgium increased by 3.16% to US$193.42 million.

The EDB said sustaining export growth would require further market diversification, greater value addition, improved product quality and standards, and stronger coordination between the Government, exporters and industry stakeholders.

The export figures were compiled using data from Sri Lanka Customs and the Central Bank of Sri Lanka, with estimates for services, gems and jewellery, and petroleum products provided by the EDB.
