Over 749 killed in motorcycle crashes so far this year

Colombo, Aug. 8 (Daily Mirror) - More than 749 people have died in motorcycle accidents in Sri Lanka during the first seven months of this year, prompting authorities to reiterate the importance of wearing SLS-certified safety helmets.

Director of the Road Safety, Traffic and Control Division of the Sri Lanka Police, Senior Superintendent of Police Manoj Ranagala, said 1,626 road accidents had been recorded between January 1 and July 31, 2026, compared with 1,585 during the corresponding period last year.

He said 749 people had died in motorcycle accidents during the period, including 13 persons below the age of 18.

Speaking at a media briefing held yesterday at the Ministry of Transport Auditorium in Colombo, Chairman of the National Road Safety Council Manjula Kularatne said wearing a standardised, SLS-certified helmet is mandatory for motorcycle riders to ensure their safety.

He said more than 2,500 people lose their lives in road accidents in Sri Lanka each year, with more than 1,000 of these deaths reportedly linked to motorcycle accidents. Fatal head injuries are a major cause of deaths among motorcycle riders, he added.

Kularatne said the Ministry of Transport, Highways and Urban Development has launched a two-year Road Safety Action Plan comprising 96 measures aimed at reducing road accidents and promoting safer driving. Of these, 30 measures have already been physically implemented, with further action underway.

He said the Sri Lanka Standards Institution, Sri Lanka Police and Consumer Affairs Authority are conducting a joint programme to ensure compliance with helmet safety standards, while raids are also being carried out to enforce regulations.

SSP Ranagala said 271,890 cases had been filed over safety helmet-related offences between January 1 and July 31 this year, compared with 349,823 cases in 2025.

He urged motorcyclists to wear safety helmets not merely to avoid legal action or police enforcement, but to protect their own lives and safety.

Chairman of the Consumer Affairs Authority Hemantha Samarakoon and Director Asela Bandara also participated in the briefing.
