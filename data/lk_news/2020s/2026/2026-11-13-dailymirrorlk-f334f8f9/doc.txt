Budget 2027 sets stage for offshore oil exploration in Mannar Basin

Colombo, Nov. 14 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka has finally embarked on its mission to realise its offshore oil exploration potential, with the Petroleum Development Authority of Sri Lanka (PDASL) calling for bids for oil and natural gas exploration and production under the Sri Lanka Licensing Round 2026, covering four blocks in the Mannar Basin off the western coast.

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, in his Budget speech on November 12, said that bids were expected to be opened on February 2, 2027. The President outlined an ambitious plan aimed at strengthening the country's energy security.

“A transformative change in the energy sector is required for future economic transformation, national security and the improvement of the living standards of the people. The plans required for a new transformation in the energy sector, aimed at ensuring a stable and uninterrupted energy supply and reducing production costs, have been included in this budget,” he said.

The four blocks cover a combined area of approximately 33,964 sq km and comprise 2026-MB-01 (5,948.95 sq km), 2026-MB-02 (5,689.05 sq km), 2026-MB-03 (10,597.67 sq km) and 2026-MB-04 (11,728.30 sq km). They extend across water depths ranging from approximately 100 to 3,000 metres.

Previous exploration activities have demonstrated the existence of a working petroleum system and resulted in gas and condensate discoveries, providing important evidence of the basin's hydrocarbon potential. At the same time, substantial areas remain comparatively underexplored, creating opportunities for international companies to apply modern exploration technologies and interpretation techniques.

Meanwhile, former Energy Minister Udaya Gammanpila said that he was delighted to see the NPP government planning to resume oil exploration, a process he had initiated in 2020.

He said that, as the subject minister during the Gotabaya Rajapaksa presidency, he had worked out the necessary requirements for upstream petroleum development by enacting the Petroleum Resources Act, No. 21 of 2021, establishing the PDASL, recruiting Sri Lankan petroleum experts working overseas, developing a detailed exploration map and introducing a natural gas policy.

“I am happy that my efforts are going to be fruitful,” Mr Gammanpila, who is also the leader of Pivithuru Hela Urumaya, said.

He also said that he conducted an aerial exploration at that time.
