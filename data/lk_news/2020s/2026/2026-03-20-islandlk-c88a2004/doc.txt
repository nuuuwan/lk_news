IMF delegation’s oncoming visit gives bourse a regenerative boost

CSE activities yesterday recorded some bullishness following the announcement of an IMF delegation’s visit next week to Sri Lanka amid successful bond restructuring plans by SriLankan Airlines.Amid those developments both indices moved upwards. The All Share Price Index went up by 416.07 points, while the S and P SL20 rose by 105.3 points. Turnover stood at Rs 4.37 billion with 10 crossings.

Companies that had arranged transactions or crossings were; JKH where Rs 34.5 million shares crossed to the tune of Rs 628 million; its shares traded at Rs 18.30, HNB 400,000 shares crossed to the tune of Rs 156 million; its shares traded at Rs 390, Colombo Dockyards one million shares crossed for Rs 118.5 million; its shares sold at Rs 120, NTB 272,900 shares crossed to the tune of Rs 75 million; its shares traded at Rs 275, Lanka IOC 400,000 shares crossed for Rs 55.6 million, its shares traded at Rs 139, Softlogic Life 640,000 shares crossed for Rs 51.2 million; its shares sold at Rs 80, Hayleys 226,000 shares crossed for Rs 46 million, its shares sold at Rs 205.

In the retail market companies that mainly contributed to the turnover were; JKH Rs 529 million (29 million shares traded), Softlogic Capital Rs 255 million (18.5 million shares traded), LMF Rs 153 million (1.9 million shares traded), Colombo Dockyard Rs 123 million (one million shares traded), Lanka IOC Rs 121 million (874,000 shares traded), Prime Lands Residencies Rs 118 million (2.5 million shares traded) and Commercial Bank Rs 96 million (500,000 shares traded). During the day 214.8 million share volumes changed hands in 28129 transactions.

It is said that manufacturing sector counters, especially JKH, led the market, while in the banking sector, HNB and Commercial Bank performed well. In the Real estate sector, Prime Lands Residencies significantly performed during the day.

Yesterday the rupee was quoted at Rs 311.50/80 to the US dollar in the spot market, weakening from Rs 311.50/65 the previous day, dealers said, while bond yields stabilized, opened steady and offers dropped on the back of signs of de-escalation of the Middle East conflict.
