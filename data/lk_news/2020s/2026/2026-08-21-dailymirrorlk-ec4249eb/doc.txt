CTC profit expands in 2Q even as turnover contracts

Ceylon Tobacco Company PLC (CTC) posted a 4.5 percent increase in second-quarter (2Q) net profit despite weaker turnover and revenue, as lower operating expenses and a reduced tax charge helped offset rising raw material and employee costs.

Profit for the three months ended June 30 rose to Rs.7.69 billion from Rs.7.37 billion a year earlier, according to the company’s interim financial statements. Earnings per share increased to Rs.41.07 from Rs.39.32.

The bottom-line improvement came even as the cigarette maker’s top line contracted. Turnover fell 4.9 percent year-on-year to Rs.52.44 billion from Rs.55.14 billion, while revenue after government levies declined 2.4 percent to Rs.16.57 billion from Rs.16.98 billion.

The divergence between revenue and earnings reflected a sharp reduction in other operating expenses, which dropped 43.9 percent to Rs.1.03 billion from Rs.1.84 billion. That provided a buffer against significantly higher input and staff costs.

Raw material costs jumped 37.5 percent to Rs.913 million, while employee benefit expenses surged 50.3 percent to Rs.1.14 billion. Depreciation and amortisation also increased to Rs.180 million from Rs.129 million. Even with the operating cost savings, pre-tax profit slipped 1.8 percent to Rs.13.62 billion from Rs.13.88 billion. The improvement at the net-profit level was therefore driven partly by taxation, with the income tax charge falling 8.9 percent to Rs.5.93 billion from Rs.6.51 billion a year earlier.

CTC said it maintained its revenue performance against a challenging macroeconomic environment and pointed to savings initiatives and cost controls as supporting profitability during the quarter. For the first six months, profit increased 2.5 percent to Rs.14.39 billion, while revenue rose 4.8 percent to Rs.32.64 billion. First-half pre-tax profit climbed 4.4 percent to Rs.26.62 billion.

Cash generation, however, weakened during the half. Net cash generated from operating activities fell to Rs.7.30 billion from Rs.11.66 billion a year earlier, with cash generated from operations declining to Rs.21.13 billion from Rs.23.33 billion. The company ended June with Rs.25.31 billion in cash and cash equivalents, down from Rs.31.37 billion at the end of December.

The board recommended a second interim dividend of Rs.41 per share, payable on September 14.
