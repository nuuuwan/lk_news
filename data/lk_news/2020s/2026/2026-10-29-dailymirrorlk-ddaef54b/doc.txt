Hands of some members of govt top brass stained with Kawantissa’s blood: Environmentalists

Colombo, Oct. 29 (Daily Mirror) - Hands of some members of the government top brass are stained with Tusker Kawantissa’s blood, a group of environmentalists alleged yesterday.

Environmentalist Nanyanaka Ranwella said many are responsible for negligence when treating Kawantissa and therefore the hands of Agriculture Minister K. D. Lal Kantha and Minister of Environment Dhammika Patabendi are stained with the tusker’s blood.

“It essential to probe and determine the real wrong doers and accordingly it is important to find out who is responsible for negligence. We say Ministers Lal Kantha and Palabendi are responsible. Even President Anura Kumara cannot get away from it,” Ranwellla told a media briefing. He said those in the government could be forgiven only if a proper investigation is carried out to determine the real wrong doers and they are brought to book.

Coming up with another serious allegation he said a rice mill is to be constructed on land where elephants move. He said this is a corridor where around 300 elephants pass. “This plan was originally drawn up around ten years ago. Mahinda Rajapaksa who was the then President decided against this move accepting it is a crime. However, the present regime is to go ahead with it” he said.

He said there are reasons to believe that Kawantissa has been shot after he collapsed and that probes should confirm or dismiss these suspicions.

“We have drawn up a plan to safeguard Sri Lanka’s forests as a whole. This will cover the conservation of tuskers and elephants. This plan will be published soon,” he said.

Environmentalist Rukshan Jayewardene said Sri Lanka should follow Kenya in conserving tuskers and elephants. “Kenya has set up a special force to safeguard tuskers and they are kept on duty for 24 hours. Sri Lanka should learn from these countries,” he said.

Meanwhile it was announced that the Wildlife Conservation forum announced the plan to hold a photography exhibition to pay tribute to Kawantissa. The exhibition will showcase photographs of Kawantissa and other elephants. The exhibition will get off the ground at Lionel Wendt from November 2 to 4th.
