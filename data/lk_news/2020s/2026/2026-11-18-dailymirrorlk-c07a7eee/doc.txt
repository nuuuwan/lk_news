Govt. begins first state institution amalgamation with gem sector

Colombo, Nov. 18 (Daily Mirror) - The Government has officially merged the Gem and Jewellery Research and Training Institute (GJRTI) with the National Gem and Jewellery Authority (NGJA), marking its first institutional amalgamation initiative aimed at improving public sector efficiency and eliminating overlapping functions.

The merger was formalised through Extraordinary Gazette No. 2513/35 dated November 5, 2026, issued by Minister of Industry and Entrepreneurship Development Sunil Handunnetti with Cabinet approval, under Section 25 of the National Gem and Jewellery Authority Act, No. 50 of 1993.

Under the Gazette notification, the Gem and Jewellery Research and Training Institute, established on July 28, 1995, has been dissolved, with all its movable and immovable assets, rights, liabilities and obligations transferred to the NGJA.

The notification also ensures that all officers and employees of the dissolved institute will continue their employment under the NGJA without any adverse changes to their service conditions, leave entitlements or pension benefits.

Meanwhile, all ongoing courses, training programmes, examinations, certifications and research projects will continue uninterrupted under the authority. The rights of students currently enrolled in the institute's programmes will also be protected.

Existing contracts and agreements entered into by the former institute, as well as pending legal proceedings involving it, will remain valid and continue under the NGJA.

The merger is aimed at bringing regulation, research and training activities within Sri Lanka's gem and jewellery sector under a single institutional framework, while streamlining operations, optimising state resources and supporting the development and export promotion of the industry.
