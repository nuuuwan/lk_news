Oil rises on supply concerns as Iran claims mine strikes on ships

Reuters - Crude oil prices rose on Thursday, extending gains from the previous session on supply concerns after Yemen's Houthis said they had hit Saudi Arabia's largest oil ​refinery and at least two ships hit Iranian mines in the Strait of Hormuz.

Brent ‌crude futures were 10 cents, or 0.1%, higher at $94.71 a barrel by 0640 GMT. US West Texas Intermediate crude futures rose 31 cents, or 0.4%, to $83.99 a barrel. There will be no settlement for WTI on Thursday due ​to the US Thanksgiving holiday.

Prices rose for the third straight session on Thursday and ​are up about 5% this week, after plunging by around a quarter in ⁠the three weeks since the beginning of November. Middle East exports have been increasing despite continued ​attacks on shipping, which triggered expectations of a global oil surplus this year.

Brent finished 3.5% higher in the ​previous session, while WTI climbed 2% after Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis said they had attacked Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura oil refinery and Riyadh's financial district.

The refinery, Saudi Arabia's largest, is part of a complex that also serves as a major ​export terminal for Saudi crude, and analysts expect the latest Houthi attacks to increase the geopolitical ​risk premium.

At the same time, shipping has been disrupted through the Strait of Hormuz, where oil supply had ‌been recovering ⁠to near pre-war levels.

An oil tanker hit a naval mine and caught fire in a "restricted zone" in the strait on Wednesday, the semi-official Fars news agency cited an Iranian Army statement as saying. That report came after state media said two oil tankers struck mines and exploded in the strait ​on Wednesday.

"The broadening of the ​Middle East conflict ⁠threatens to halt the recovery in oil flows in recent weeks. Persian Gulf crude exports rose last week but remain on a declining trend," said ​ANZ analyst Daniel Hynes.

OPEC+ ministers will hold a series of meetings on ​November 29 to ⁠decide on their 2027 output policy, facing the challenge of balancing output with the United Arab Emirates no longer in the group, and with Iraq and Kazakhstan pressing OPEC+ to raise its estimate of ⁠their oil ​production capacity.

In the US, crude stocks rose by 1.1 ​million barrels to 418.9 million barrels in the week ended November 20, the Energy Information Administration said, also supporting prices as ​analysts had expected a 1.5 million-barrel rise.
