Kalawana bus stand made gateway for Sinharaja conservation awareness

The main bus stand in Kalawana has been transformed into a public information hub dedicated to the conservation of the Sinharaja rainforest, aimed at educating both local communities and visiting tourists about the ecological significance of Sri Lanka’s last remaining tropical rainforest.

The programme, which was officially opened on May 24, has been implemented by the Movement for Land and Agricultural Reform (MONLAR) in collaboration with local authorities, environmental groups and transport institutions.

Environmental activist Sajeewa Chamikara said the project was designed to raise awareness among the thousands of commuters and travellers who pass through Kalawana on their way to the Sinharaja World Heritage Forest Reserve.

“Kalawana is the principal town used by people living around Sinharaja as well as by local and foreign tourists visiting the rainforest. We wanted to transform the bus stand into a space where people could understand the biodiversity, environmental importance and conservation needs of Sinharaja,” Chamikara said.

He noted that the initiative was also intended to promote environmentally sustainable agricultural practices among communities living around the rainforest ecosystem.

The project has been carried out under MONLAR’s tropical rainforest conservation programme, which advocates ecological farming and agroforestry concepts to gradually convert monocrop tea lands into diversified multi-crop cultivation systems.

According to Chamikara, protecting the Sinharaja ecosystem requires not only forest conservation measures but also greater awareness among surrounding communities regarding sustainable land use and ecological farming practices.

The walls and display areas of the Kalawana bus stand have been fitted with educational exhibition boards featuring photographs, illustrations and explanatory notes highlighting Sinharaja’s biodiversity, endemic wildlife and environmental value.

The original proposal for the project was put forward by Aravinda Lakmal, terminal manager of the Kalawana Depot of the Sabaragamuwa Road Passenger Transport Authority; Rajitha Chathuranga Pitigala, chairman of the Kalawana Urban Development Foundation; and environmental advocate Upali Jayawardena of the Pitigalakanda and Mannawatta areas.

The initiative received support from the Kalawana Pradeshiya Sabha headed by Chairman Sumith Priyantha Somadasa, the Sabaragamuwa Road Passenger Transport Authority, the Kalawana Urban Development Foundation and the Kalawana Bus Friends Association.

Photographs used in the exhibition were contributed by MONLAR, reptile and amphibian researcher Udaya Chanaka, and researcher Shalith Karunaratne of the Ambagahakanda Sinharaja Friends Organisation. Artwork by Inoka Dilrukshi D. Herath, an art teacher at Rambukkana Sujatha Vidyalaya, was also used in the displays.

MONLAR provided financial support for the preparation of exhibition content, graphic design, printing and installation work.

Chamikara called on schoolchildren, tourists and the general public visiting Sinharaja to stop at the Kalawana bus stand and view the displays.

“We hope these exhibition boards will help people better understand why rainforests such as Sinharaja must be protected and encourage the public to actively contribute towards conservation efforts,” he said.
