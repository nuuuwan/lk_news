Votes from workers, land for investors: TPA’s Barath questions Govt. ahead of Budget

Colombo, Nov. 9 (Daily Mirror) - The Tamil Progressive Alliance (TPA) has urged the Government to put its plantation leasing decision on hold and first honour the housing and livelihood land commitments made to the Malaiyaha Tamil community through the Hatton Declaration.

Ahead of the 2027 Budget, DPF Vice President for International Affairs and Communications and TPA Politburo Member Barath Arullsamy said the community’s land rights were “hanging between the Hatton promise and the Cabinet’s leasing decision”.

“We are asking for what you promised. The Hatton Declaration pledged land rights, dignified housing and the use of uncultivated plantation lands for our youth’s livelihoods. Those assurances helped you win substantial support in the plantation region. Now honour them,” he said.

The declaration, launched under Anura Kumara Dissanayake’s leadership in October 2023, committed to addressing land ownership and housing problems and using uncultivated and abandoned plantation lands to improve unemployed Malaiyaha youth’s livelihoods.

Arullsamy contrasted this with the Cabinet’s October 26 approval to lease 15 properties belonging to the Janatha Estates Development Board, Sri Lanka State Plantations Corporation and Elkaduwa Plantations Limited to investors.

“Governement representatives describe the leasing programme as national policy. Then explain how that policy honours your own declaration. Secure housing land with legal deeds and livelihood land for our youth before signing commercial agreements,” he said.

He stressed that the TPA welcomed private investment and economic development, but insisted that these must proceed after the community’s promised land rights were secured.

As examples of the implementation gap, he said over 1,000 Ditwah-affected families remained in disaster-prone areas without permanent resettlement more than ten months later, while the Indian-assisted 10,000-house programme and infrastructure projects continued to face delays.

Citing the World Bank’s October assessment, he noted that only about 8% of the Rs. 500 billion national reconstruction allocation had been spent by mid-2026.

“Budget allocations and media spectacles cannot shelter a family from a landslide. People need land, completed homes and livelihoods,” he said.

Arullsamy recalled that the TPA, under Leader Mano Ganesan MP, supported the previous Budget’s Second Reading in good faith. He said Ganesan had written to President Dissanayake the previous week and tabled his ten-point letter in Parliament, seeking gazetted wage protections, land rights, equitable development and the repeal of the PTA without similarly restrictive replacements.

“The Government can find a Cabinet route for investors and draft new powers under proposals such as the PSTA, yet a plantation worker’s land deed apparently requires a miracle. Is this the promised transformation, new powers to suppress minority voices, old excuses for landlessness? Hold the leasing decision. Honour Hatton Declaration first. Give our people housing and livelihood land, then bring forward investment proposals. We will support development that delivers on those promises, why we are demanding land rights, because we have proved that we can during our tenure” he said.
