Deloitte Sri Lanka examines Budget 2027 as nation looks beyond stabilisation towards sustainable growth

As Sri Lanka moves beyond economic stabilisation towards its next phase of growth, attention is turning to how fiscal reforms, investment priorities and policy initiatives can translate into meaningful economic progress. Bringing together perspectives from Government, industry and economic policy, Deloitte Sri Lanka hosted its Budget 2027 webinar, “Beyond Stabilization: Building for Growth,” on 12 November, 2026, following the presentation of the National Budget in Parliament.

The webinar provided a platform for policymakers, economists and business leaders to examine the Budget through two complementary discussions, “Fiscal Perspective” and “Trade & Economy.” Moderated by Partner and Head of Tax Charmaine Tillekeratne and Head of Strategy, Risk & Transactions Ruvini Fernando, respectively, the sessions explored the implications of fiscal measures, investment, trade, energy and economic reforms. The discussions highlighted the importance of moving beyond policy announcements towards effective implementation and tangible outcomes for businesses and the wider economy.

Budget 2027 reflects Sri Lanka’s continued emphasis on fiscal discipline while seeking to create greater opportunities for investment and economic expansion. According to Budget 2027, the Government targets a primary surplus of 2.3% of GDP and an overall fiscal deficit of 4.5% in 2027, with tax revenue projected at 14.6% of GDP. Total budgeted revenue stands at Rs. 6.15 trillion against an expenditure of Rs. 7.93 trillion, reflecting the continued importance of balancing fiscal commitments with national development priorities.

Taxation remains a key area of focus, with proposals aimed at providing relief to individuals, encouraging investment and strengthening the tax framework. The proposed increase in annual personal income tax relief from Rs. 1.8 million to Rs. 1.95 million, together with revised progressive tax bands, is expected to reduce the tax burden on qualifying individuals. For businesses, the proposed 200% enhanced capital allowance for qualifying BOI-approved investments of at least $ 3 million in export-oriented manufacturing and tourism introduces a further incentive for investment in priority sectors.

Other notable measures include the proposed expansion of capital gains tax coverage to certain digital assets and commercial rights, legal recognition of electronic VAT invoices and changes to VAT refund eligibility for new exporters. The proposed abolition of the Tourism Development Levy from April 2027 also forms part of the Budget’s revenue-related measures.

Beyond taxation, public investment and private-sector development feature prominently in the Government’s growth agenda. Budget 2027 allocates Rs. 2 trillion for public investment, with substantial provisions for highways, railways, healthcare, irrigation and water supply. Support for micro, small and medium enterprises also remains a priority, with Rs. 67.69 billion allocated towards interest subsidies and bank refinancing to support Rs. 174 billion in concessionary lending.

The importance of these measures was reflected in Deloitte’s webinar discussions, where panellists examined the challenges of improving access to finance, strengthening investment facilitation and reducing administrative barriers. The conversations highlighted that financial allocations alone may not be sufficient without stronger institutional capabilities and practical mechanisms to support implementation.

Energy security, digital transformation and governance reforms represent further priorities within Budget 2027. Proposed initiatives relating to renewable energy, efficient power markets and digital infrastructure sit alongside plans to strengthen investment protection, public-private partnerships and State-owned enterprise management. The anticipated Digital Economy Bill and proposed Cyber Security Authority also reflect the increasing importance of digital governance in Sri Lanka’s economic development.

The wider discussion on trade and the economy also drew attention to power-sector efficiency, particularly the need to achieve a lower unit cost of electricity and address policy challenges that influence business competitiveness over the longer term.

Taken together, Budget 2027 outlines a range of fiscal and structural measures intended to support Sri Lanka’s transition towards sustainable growth. Their longer-term significance will depend on effective implementation, institutional co-ordination and the ability to translate policy priorities into meaningful economic outcomes.
