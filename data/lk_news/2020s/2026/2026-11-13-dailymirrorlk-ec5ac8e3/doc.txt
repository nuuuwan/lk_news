Budget Speech 2027

Our government presents its third Budget with the honest and genuine determination of making the story, cherished over many generations, of building a thriving nation to a reality. Every Budget we have presented bears witness to our collective resilience, and has established a firm conviction that, as a nation, we are emerging from the deepest crisis and are on a correct and well-planned trajectory.

We have been able to kindle an unfading dawn of hope in the hearts of hundreds of thousands of our people. I wish to recall that we are unconditionally fulfilling the expectations of the noble trust that the entire people of this country placed in us two years ago.

Eradicating poverty, building a just society, uprooting bribery and corruption, accelerating economic development, restoring the trust of the people and providing every citizen with the opportunity to build a decent life are among our key objectives. However, the foundation for all of these must be the creation, through education, of citizens who are equipped with knowledge, endowed with virtue, think critically and act creatively, as well as visionary leaders. What we need is to build an educated Sri Lankan generation that is rich in knowledge and intellect, contributes to the world through new ideas and new inventions, and is capable of facing any challenge at the international level. Our aim is to create a situation where, wherever in the world a Sri Lankan is recognized, he or she is accepted as a person endowed with knowledge, intellect, creativity and good virtues.

It is to make the Sri Lankan identity a symbol of intelligence, virtue and excellence. Creating a true renaissance through all of these is the core objective of our Budget. This is because ushering in a renaissance era is not merely a change in economic indicators or statistics. It is a broad social transformation that brings about a change in people’s thinking, knowledge, attitudes and behaviour, and breathes new life into the cultural, intellectual and artistic life of the country.

We must all come together and, through a collective people’s intervention, take this country forward towards the development that we, as Sri Lanka in the 21st century, should have already achieved.

On this journey, the first thing we had to do was to bring about a fundamental transformation in the political culture. For the first time in history since independence, the people of this country gave us the mandate to establish a genuine people’s governance in place of a government administration that had been in the hands of a few corrupt elite families.

What we need is not merely a Government that collects revenue, but a Government that delivers value to the people for every tax rupee it collects. What we need is not a large Government machinery, but an efficient and transparent Government machinery that is accountable to the people.

Our policy is to recognize, at every level of governance, that the true owners of public money are the citizens of this country.

Today, we have the confidence, experience, economic foundation and stability required to take our country’s economy into a transformative era. The Budget we present for the year 2027 is the next step built on those foundations. Our expectation is to make it a Budget that strengthens the production economy, protects small and medium scale industrialists, makes room for investment, connects knowledge and technology to the economy through digital economic activities, protects the environment and ensures social justice.

Every Budget proposal we present is part of a continuous programme moving towards an interconnected common goal. Moreover, this Budget has not been prepared based solely on the views of one individual or one party. It is a programme prepared collectively, incorporating the views, proposals and experiences of various stakeholders. Due attention has been paid to every proposal submitted to us. This Budget has been prepared based on our policy priorities, available resources, fiscal space and implementation capacity.

It is no exaggeration to say that this economic stability of our country was built during a most difficult period. The war raging in the Middle East is becoming more prolonged than ever before, creating great uncertainty in the world. The closure of the Strait of Hormuz has affected nearly 20 percent of global petroleum shipments. Accordingly, the increase in fuel prices, premiums and shipping and insurance charges had a very adverse impact on our energy market. We have still not been able to free ourselves from the pressure this created on our economy and on people’s lives. As a result, more than Rs. 147 billion was spent to minimize the impact on the people and on economic stability.

Further, according to the Post-Disaster Needs Assessment (PDNA) report, the damage caused by Cyclone Ditwah to housing, industries, railway systems, agriculture, education and the health system has been estimated at USD 2 billion, which, together with economic losses of USD 1.4 billion, amounts to a total of about USD 3.4 billion (Rs.1.022 trillion).

We have been able to build economic stability while facing all these adverse circumstances.

As forecast by the World Meteorological Organization, it has been confirmed that there is a 100 percent probability that the El Niño weather condition that has now developed will reach its peak in February 2027. This El Niño weather condition poses a risk of adverse impacts on agriculture, food prices, water resources and hydropower. We consider it one of our foremost responsibilities to identify such risks that may arise both globally and domestically in advance, and to further stabilize the economy so that it is capable of facing them.

Our target is not only to manage today’s economic challenges, but also to transform the country into one where future generations will never again have to face an economic crisis such as the one that prevailed before. The essence of our policy statement, “A Thriving Nation – A Beautiful Life”, is not merely to improve the performance of economic indicators, but to create a society in which people can live with dignity and pride, and a country that has confidence in its future.

The main objective of this Budget is to move from economic stability towards transformation. It is to achieve a progressive transformation in every sphere, including investment, exports, productive employment, social welfare and sustainability.

During the past two years, our government has succeeded in achieving remarkable macroeconomic stability. Accordingly;

Sustaining the economic growth of 5 percent achieved in 2025, an economic growth of 5.1 percent was achieved in the first quarter of 2026, even amidst the impact of Cyclone Ditwah. In the second quarter, a growth of 4.2 percent was achieved, despite the war situation in the Middle East region.

The negative primary balance, which had recorded over a long period, reported the highest positive balance of 5.4 percent in 2025 in its history. Against the positive primary balance target of Rs. 360 billion for end-2026, a positive primary balance of Rs. 1,844 billion was recorded by end-September.

In 2025, the budget deficit recorded its lowest value in 68 years at 2.3 percent.

Government revenue, which was at a historic low of 8.2 percent of GDP in 2022, reached 16.6 percent in 2025, the highest value recorded in 23 years, amounting to about 110 percent of the expected revenue.

In 2025, we were able to record the highest-ever current account surplus in history of USD 1,719 million.

In 2025, workers’ remittances recorded the highest value in history at USD 8.1 billion. Further, by October this year, it amounted to USD 7.6 billion. Accordingly, remittances exceeding USD 9 billion are expected this year.

Central Government debt, which was 114 percent of GDP at the end of 2022, was reduced to 95 percent by the end of 2025 as per the records of Public Debt Management Office (PDMO).

In 2022, 79.1 percent of Government revenue was spent on interest payments, which declined further to 45.9 percent by 2025 and to 39.6 percent by September 2026.

The total net loss before tax of Rs. 774.6 billion recorded by 52 key State-Owned Enterprises in 2022 was turned into a profit of Rs. 444.4 billion in 2025. This reduced the pressure on the Treasury. We turned loss-making institutions into profit-making institutions. By the end of August 2026, a profit before tax of Rs.367 billion was recorded. The accumulated loss of SriLankan Airlines from the date it was taken back in 2008 up to 2025 amounts to Rs. 528 billion. Accordingly, SriLankan Airlines has become a burden on the General Treasury. The Cabinet of Ministers has already appointed an expert committee to find the necessary solutions in this regard.

Even though total fuel import expenditure increased to USD 4,487 million by September 2026 compared to USD 4,042 million in 2025 due to the increase in world market fuel prices, and amidst the opening of the vehicle import market, foreign reserves were recorded at over USD 7 billion by October 2026.

Nevertheless, annual inflation, which was recorded at 2.1 percent in December 2025, increased to 8.3 percent by October 2026 due to the rise in fuel prices in the face of the Middle East conflict and other related factors.

Credit extended by commercial banks to the private sector increased by 25.2 percent, from Rs. 8,156 billion in 2024 to Rs. 10,212 billion in 2025.

During the past two years, Sri Lanka has been able to achieve significant progress in global rankings, including economic and social rankings.

According to the 2026 Fitch Ratings, Sri Lanka has been upgraded from ‘CCC+’ to ‘B-’.

According to the Corruption Perceptions Index (2025/2026) issued by Transparency International, Sri Lanka has advanced 14 places in the global rankings to 107th place.

According to the Global Peace Index (2026), Sri Lanka advanced 14 places to be ranked 67th globally.

According to GlobeTrotter & Flash Pack, in 2025, Sri Lanka was ranked first in the world index under the solo travellers category.

The World Economic Forum has named Sri Lanka 10th in the world among the countries that have shown the fastest progress among tourism economies.

The international magazine Condé Nast has named Sri Lanka the best destination in the world for the whole family to travel together.

According to the assessment carried out by the Institute of International Finance (IIF), among 57 emerging market and developingeconomies, Sri Lanka secured fourth place for progress in improving investor relations and fifth place for progress in improving transparency on public debt.

The World Bank had classified Sri Lanka as a lower middle-income country in 2020, down from the status of an upper middle-income country. Reflecting the recovery of the economy, the classification was upgraded again in 2026 to an upper middle-income country.

The above facts are not the only criteria that measure the success of our fiscal policy. The Government’s support for and care of distressed children, the elderly, persons with disabilities, those suffering from chronic illnesses, low-income households and families who have lost their livelihoods due to disasters are equally important.

Through the 2025 Budget, we laid the foundation for economic stability and social justice. Through the 2026 Budget, greater attention was paid to production, investment, digitalization and institutional reforms. What we propose through the 2027 Budget is to implement the reforms and proposals for a new economic transformation required, based on the economic stability we have achieved so far, to increase production, to involve the people as stakeholders engaged in the economy, to ensure that the gains achieved in the economy are distributed fairly among the people, and to achieve the sovereignty of the national economy.

Economic transformation does not mean presenting yet another list of expenditures. It means that every investment made by the Government, every tax concession granted, every borrowing undertaken and every social protection programme proposed will be assessed on the basis of the economic and social benefits generated through them.

It is not our objective to make all the sacrifices that the people bore during the economic crisis, and continue to bear even at this moment, into permanent sacrifices. It is our duty to build economic stability, better jobs, fair incomes, a quality public service, a safe social environment and a sustainable future for future generations.

When the Budget for 2026 was presented to this House, we emphasized the need to build an economy that can move forward unshaken in the face of internal and external challenges. At the end of 2025, Cyclone Ditwah severely affected the lives of our people, production activities and infrastructure. Due to the Middle East conflict that escalated in the first half of 2026, new pressure exerted on energy prices, import costs and supply chains.

In facing a disaster such as Cyclone Ditwah, what was important for a Government was that we had built fiscal space in advance. Through this, we were able to strengthen the financial capacity required to provide relief to the people affected by the disaster, to maintain essential services and to deploy resources for reconstruction activities according to priorities.

The fundamental structural macroeconomic problem that Sri Lanka has faced for a long time has been the deficits in the budget, the balance of payments and the current account. Although we were able to reduce the budget deficit significantly through correct revenue and expenditure policies, the external sector has not yet been strengthened as required. While a current account surplus of USD 1,853 million was recorded in the first nine months of 2025, a deficit of USD 420 million was recorded in the corresponding period of 2026. The progress achieved in the fiscal sector must be made the foundation for a structural transformation that strengthens the external sector.

This need becomes even clearer when we look at the current state of the world economy. According to the World Economic Outlook issued by the International Monetary Fund in October 2026, global economic growth is expected to slow down to 3.1 percent in 2026. Risks such as the escalation of conflicts, rising energy and food prices and uncertainty in international trade still persist.

We need a long-term plan capable of withstanding these conditions.

2.1 Safeguarding Macroeconomic Stability and Strengthening the Resilience of the Economy

While safeguarding macroeconomic and fiscal stability, our main objective is to ensure debt sustainability and to prepare Sri Lanka to face future internal and external shocks. For this, sustainable Government revenue, prudent expenditure management, planned debt repayments, adequate fiscal space and strong foreign reserves are essential. It is also important to maintain adequate coordination between fiscal and monetary policies, while respecting the legal mandate of the Central Bank of Sri Lanka in maintaining price stability.

Central Government debt stood at 114 percent in 2022, the year in which the economy was plunged into crisis. What is taken into consideration in debt sustainability is the capacity to repay debt relative to GDP and Government revenue, and whether there is adequate liquidity to settle the debt servicing obligations falling due in the near term.

The target of the IMF-Extended Fund Facility programme is to bring public debt down to a level of 95 percent by 2032. Based on the progress in public financial management, the IMF 6th Review has forecast that this will decline to 87 percent. Annual borrowing plans and strategies are being implemented under the leadership of the Public Debt Management Office to maintain debt sustainability. In 2025, the Central Government debt ratio had reached 95 percent. Total foreign debt service amounted to USD 3,707 million in 2025 and USD 3,591 million in 2026. Going forward, it will be USD 2,986 million and USD 3,574 million in 2027 and 2028, respectively. We have the stability required for this debt servicing.

2.2 Accelerating Economic Growth Driven by Investment, Production and Exports

Our target is to accelerate the structural transformation towards a high productivity, export-competitive production economy. Expanding domestic and foreign private investment, diversifying export products and markets, integrating with international value chains and promoting industries and services with higher value addition are the main areas of this.

We are taking steps to broaden access to finance for small and medium-scale enterprises and to establish a clear and transparent investment environment for investors.

We give priority to enhancing national productivity by strengthening education, competency development, research, innovation and digital transformation, modernizing public services, expanding digital infrastructure, encouraging the use of new technologies and strengthening collaboration among educational institutions, research institutions and industries.

In particular, it is essential to equip our younger generation with the competencies required for the changing domestic and global job market, and to transform knowledge and innovation into income-generating enterprises and services. Creating a technologically capable workforce and a more productive economy will also enhance export competitiveness and the capacity to earn foreign exchange.

The agri-food sector has very high potential for export and rural income growth. Instead of excessive reliance on broad-based and untargeted subsidies that do not contribute to raising productivity, high priority should be given to directing unproductive lands to productive cultivation, agricultural research, climate-smart technology, water management, storage and cold storage facilities, product quality certification and digital systems. It is also essential to resolve, after proper study, the existing barriers to land use, rural access to finance and connecting producers to markets. Then, it will be possible to make the empowerment of the livelihoods of rural people, food security and foreign exchange earnings the outcomes of a single development process.

A new economic transformation at the rural level can be brought about by encouraging small and medium-scale entrepreneurs, creating new economic opportunities for rural people, and developing the technical, vocational and linguistic skills required for the modern job market.

Rising international fuel prices, supply chain disruptions and climatic changes form the basis of instability in the energy sector in our country. Our objective is to change these underlying conditions that we face with regard to energy. For this, it is also necessary to expand renewable energy, and to improve electricity transmission networks, energy storage and energy efficiency. Our target is to minimize the long-term pressure on foreign exchange spent on imported fuel, while safeguarding the continuity of essential energy supply through the use of diverse supply routes and energy sources.

It is important to follow standards capable of withstanding future disasters, early warnings and proper maintenance plans. Along with this, food supply chains, storage, public transport and the mechanisms for distributing essential goods must be strengthened. The continuity of services such as essential communication facilities and electricity must be ensured. Although the risks arising from natural disasters that may occur in the future cannot be completely eliminated, our objective is to significantly minimize the resulting paralysis of economic activities and the pressure on people’s lives.

It is essential to build an efficient Government machinery that provides a quality service to the people, facilitates investment and production activities, and is accountable to the law and transparent. Modernizing public financial management, State-Owned Enterprise and asset management, tax administration and the procurement process are essential elements of this. Minimizing corruption and unnecessary administrative delays, while strengthening the rule of law and accountability, is equally important to enhance public trust and to safeguard public resources.

Our medium-term economic target is to move towards a sustainable economic growth of 7 percent or higher. A strong and credible fiscal foundation is essential for the economic transformation we envisage. Accordingly, we expect to continue fiscal consolidation in line with the responsibility framework established by the “Medium-Term Fiscal Framework 2027–2031” and the “Public Financial Management Act”. Maintaining a primary surplus of 2.3 percent of GDP, maintaining the budget deficit at a level below 5 percent, and placing public debt on a sustainable path are our core fiscal targets.

Our objective is to maintain Government revenue at a level not less than 16 percent of GDP in the medium term, and to gradually increase it to about 20 percent in the long term. To achieve these targets, the necessary reforms will be implemented to broaden the tax base, rationalize tax exemptions and incentives, digitalize tax administration and strengthen tax compliance.

In accordance with the Public Financial Management Act, we will take steps to maintain medium-term expenditure in line with the primary expenditure ceiling. However, due to the additional allocations required to restore the infrastructure and people’s livelihood damaged by Cyclone Ditwah, primary expenditure in 2027 is expected to increase temporarily to 13.6 percent of GDP. While managing these special expenditure requirements within the statutory fiscal framework, we also expect to bring primary expenditure back to 13 percent of GDP from 2028 onwards.

We expect to maintain public investment at a level exceeding 4 percent of GDP from 2027 onwards. In 2027, capital expenditure of Rs. 2,000 billion has been allocated, amounting to total public investment of 5.12 percent of GDP, comprising 4.48 percent through the Budget and 0.64 percent through the Supplementary Estimate for Ditwah.

The Extended Fund Facility programme entered into with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) is scheduled to conclude in March next year. We expect to successfully complete the 17th IMF programme. While the programme to stabilize the economy domestically is proceeding successfully, it is difficult to forecast the impacts arising from global and natural disasters at a time when we are preparing for an economic transformation for growth. We believe that our reserves must be further strengthened to successfully face such situations.

Therefore, the Cabinet of Ministers has appointed a committee headed by the Deputy Minister of Finance and Planning to examine and submit a report on the suitability of entering into another programme after the successful completion of the current IMF programme, and on what type of programme it should be.

For social justice and the protection of the people, targeted social protection programmes will be further strengthened to protect low-income and vulnerable households who face the highest risk in the face of economic and natural disasters.

Our people made exceptional sacrifices and endured great hardships for this economic stabilization. We must all pay our respect to them. Doing justice to these sacrifices means achieving a rapid turn from economic stabilization towards transformation. To that end, I present to this Honourable House the Budget proposals for the year 2027.

A new and strong legal framework and institutional structure are required to achieve the economic transformation we envisage and to enhance efficiency. Some of our laws are more than 100 years old. The historical role of some of our institutions has come to an end. Those laws and institutions have become shackles that bind our feet from reaching the economic transformation we envisage. Therefore, in order to achieve the new economic transformation, it is necessary toreformulate old and outdated laws and institutions so that they can face present day challenges, and to build new laws and institutions that correspond to the new circumstances that have arisen. Hence, we cannot move forward without removing these shackles. Accordingly;

The “Rescue, Rehabilitation and Insolvency (Corporate and Personal) Act, No. 12 of 2026” has been passed to provide an opportunity for businesses that collapsed due to various crises such as pandemics, natural disasters and terrorist acts to rebuild, contribute to the economy and repay their creditors.

The laws required to strengthen the prevention of money laundering have been passed through the “Prevention of Money Laundering (Amendment) Act, No. 16 of 2026”.

The “Financial Transactions Reporting (Amendment) Act, No. 17 of 2026” has made provisions to minimize the risk arising from transactions not being reported to the financial system and to enforce the law against non-compliers.

Through the “Convention on the Suppression of Terrorist Financing (Amendment) Act, No. 18 of 2026”, the financing of global terrorism using Sri Lanka, which poses a threat to national security, has been prevented by strengthening the financial system.

Many people who have obtained microfinance loans are still stranded in a severe debt trap. For their protection, the “Microfinance and Credit Regulatory Authority Act, No. 9 of 2026” has already been passed. Accordingly, the ‘Microfinance and Credit Regulatory Authority’ will be established in 2027 and steps will be taken to provide protection to business customers.

The “Public-Private Partnership (PPP) Act” has already been published in the Gazette for the initiation, development, management and monitoring of public-private partnership projects. It is expected to be presented to Parliament in early 2027.

The draft “State Owned Business Enterprises Management Bill” has already been referred to the Attorney General to incorporate a holding company for the efficient management of companies in which the State holds shares. 34 State-owned enterprises have already been identified for this purpose.

The draft “Investment Protection Bill” has already been referred to the Attorney General to promote and protect investments and assets belonging to investors, to ensure fair and equitable treatment, and to make provisions relating to the payment of compensation and the settlement of disputes.

The draft “Colombo Port City Economic Commission (Amendment) Bill” has been referred to the Attorney General to attract foreign investment to the Colombo Port City and to provide greater facilities for investors and those employed there.

The “Institute of Professional Actuaries of Sri Lanka Act” required to establish a new institute aimed at ensuring the quality of professional actuaries and improving professional training is being drafted.

Data and information are most important in policy decision-making in this era. The “National Statistics Act” is being drafted for the preparation, coordination and governance of official statistics in Sri Lanka.

The “Customs Ordinance (Amendment) Act” is expected to be passed in the first half of 2027 with the aim of making the Sri Lanka Customs more efficient and transparent and facilitating businesses. From October this year, Sri Lanka Customs has implemented a paperless programme and has digitalized the ‘Customs Long Room’, one of its divisions that had been in use for about a century.

The “Financial Reporting Regulatory (Amendment) Act” will be amended to formalize the Sri Lanka Accounting Standards, Sri Lanka Auditing Standards and the regulatory standards on quality control.

The “Excise (Special Provisions) Act” will be amended to provide solutions to long-standing issues that have arisen in its implementation.

The “Finance Business Act” will be amended to strengthen the regulation and supervision of finance companies, the investigation and prosecution of unauthorized finance businesses, and the requirements for winding up nonviable finance businesses.

The “Public Asset Management Act” will be introduced to meet the requirements of public asset management.

The Cabinet of Ministers has appointed an expert committee to reform labour laws that have been in place for decades, so as to align them with current social and economic needs. This initiative aims to create a conducive working environment required for investment and production processes, to be carriedout in an effective and efficient manner and to promote employer-employee relations in which professional responsibility and accountability are clearly defined. This committee is in the process of formulating recommendations, taking into account the views and proposals of relevant stakeholders, including the National Labour Advisory Council.

An officials’ committee has been appointed by the Cabinet of Ministers to look into minimizing delays in providing Government lands to investors, and to submit recommendations on the concessions to be granted according to the national importance of the project, the number of jobs generated and the area in which the investment is made.

It is expected to establish a company, 100 percent owned by the Treasury, comprising professionals with private sector and international experience, for the promotion of investment. It is expected to provide this company too with market-competitive salaries and allowances, as was considered in the establishment of the GovTech institution.

The Gambling Regulatory Authority has already been established to regulate casinos, online gambling and other games that fall into the category of gambling. The necessary regulations have already been finalized with expert assistance.

The National Single Window for Trade, which has been launched as a pilot project from the fifth of this month for the export of several selected goods, is expected to connect exporters as the first phase in the first half of 2027, and importers and re-exports in the second half of 2027. Rs. 1,990 million has been allocated for this purpose.

Bids have been called to obtain a consultancy firm for the creation of the National Single Window for Investment, and these bids are currently being evaluated. Rs. 100 million has been allocated for its establishment.

It is proposed to allocate Rs. 1,000 million for the procurement of body scanners and container scanners, aimed at expanding the scanning system to strengthen the border protection process of Sri Lanka Customs.

It is proposed to modernize the existing “Temporary Importation for Export Processing” (TIEP) process, under which goods imported for export are cleared with tax concessions, in order to introduce “inter-port trading” mechanisms and to direct small and medium-scale industrialists towards exports.

We expect to update the existing Free Trade Agreements and enter into new trade agreements with more countries in order to connect Sri Lanka to the global trade network.

An officials’ committee has already been appointed by the Cabinet of Ministers to expand the existing Free Trade Agreement with India.

Attention has been paid to a new Free Trade Agreement with China.

An application has been made to accede to the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP).

It is expected to enter into Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreements with the United Arab Emirates and Oman.

The “Sri Lanka–European Union Sustainable Trade and Economic Integration Project” will be implemented under a European Union grant of Rs. 3,430 million during the period 2027-2030, to promote export competitiveness, export diversification and sustainable production.

Further, we will work to enter into new Free Trade Agreements with other countries following a feasibility study.

A transformative change in the energy sector is required for the future economic transformation, national security and the improvement of the living standards of the people. The plans required for a new transformation in the energy sector, aimed at ensuring a stable and uninterrupted energy supply and reducing production costs, have been included in this Budget.

Today, around 62 percent of our installed capacity comes from renewable energy. Plans have been prepared to increase this to 69 percent by 2029.

As energy sources that vary with weather and seasonal conditions, such as solar and wind power, are increasingly added, we must pay greater attention to the stability control of the system. The national power system failures that occurred in February 2025 and October 2026 were an important lesson for us to reflect on our system protection, frequency control, power flow management and network protection mechanisms. We expect to make an investment of Rs. 26.5 billion to technically strengthen our grid system so that such incidents do not recur.

The installation of two synchronous condensers of 75 MVA (megavoltamperes) each at the new Habarana and Mannar locations, which will help maintain grid stability when renewable energy is added to the national system at a high percentage, and the installation of STATCOM facilities with a capacity of 300 MVAr at the Padukka 220 kV grid substation, are expected to commence in 2027 and be completed in 2029 with the financial assistance of the World Bank.

A Wide Area Monitoring System will be established under the “World Bank Technical Assistance Project” to identify unstable conditions in the system in advance and to make prompt and effective decisions in emergencies.

We have already introduced Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) to store the surplus electricity we receive during the daytime. In 2026, 160 MW is received by the system through BESS. Plans have been made for this quantity to be 560 MW in 2027 and for the total quantity to be 1,245 MW by 2029.

The price selected in the first phase (160 MW) of calling bids for the BESS system in 2026 was Rs. 2.033 million per megawatt per month. In the second phase in 2026, when bids were called for 250 megawatts, the bid received had decreased to Rs. 1.808 million per megawatt per month. Accordingly, through competitive bidding, it has been possible to reduce the price by around 11 percent within this year itself.

With the aim of promoting renewable energy generation, enhancing energy security, and ensuring stability by minimizing imbalances in the national power system, it is proposed to provide a subsidy for the purchase of the batteries required for energy storage to domestic consumers who install rooftop solar power systems equipped with battery storage facilities under the Net Plus scheme.

Domestic consumers who newly install rooftop solar power systems will be provided a subsidy of Rs. 25,000 per kilowatt of battery storage capacity. It is proposed to provide this subsidy for up to 10 kilowatts, subject to a maximum of Rs. 250,000. It is proposed to provide Rs. 3,500 million for this purpose. It is also proposed to remove the Ports and Airports Development Levy (PAL) charged on the importation of batteries used for the storage of solar energy.

Although the need for supplementary power supply in the event of an emergency at the Norochcholai Lakvijaya Power Plant has been discussed for a long time, no appropriate steps have been taken to address it. Measures are now being taken to establish a BESS with a capacity of 30 MW to provide this supplementary power.

Rs. 500 million has been allocated for the preliminary work of the 400 kV Northern main transmission network from Kilinochchi to New Habarana, with the aim of completing it by 2030. We expect to obtain financial assistance of USD 150 million from the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) to construct the transmission line.

It is expected to invite private investments for the construction of 2 transmission lines of 220 kV from Norochcholai to Wariyapola and from Moneragala to Hambantota, and the New Habarana–Kirindiwela 400 kV transmission line.

In 2027, smart meters will be provided free of charge to 300,000 electricity consumers who use more than 180 units, on a grant of USD 12 million from the Asian Development Bank (ADB). Smart meters will be provided to all electricity consumers who use more than 120 units in the areas served by Lanka Electricity Company (LECO).

A new capacity of 370 MW generated from wind power will be added to the system by 2029. Of this, steps will be taken to add 50 MW in 2027. By the end of 2029, the installed capacity generated from wind power will increase to 674 MW.

Ten reservoirs, including Randenigala, Deduru Oya, Ridiyagama, Ambalan Oya, Castlereigh, Chandrika Wewa and Kiri Ibban Ara, have been selected for the installation of floating solar panels. We expect to generate around 500 MW of electricity by installing solar panels using 10 percent or less of the water surface of these reservoirs. These projects will be carried out by the Government and the private sector. Steps will be taken in January 2027 to call for investment proposals from the private sector.

Four Distribution Control Centres will be established in Kuliyapitiya, Peradeniya, Battaramulla and Dehiwala to manage power failures, optimize the power grid, manage electricity demand, integrate and manage solar energy resources and other distributed energy resources into the power system, and strengthen data-driven planning of electricity distribution systems.

The Petroleum Development Authority of Sri Lanka (PDASL) has called for bids for oil and natural gas exploration and production (Sri Lanka Licencing Round 2026) in 4 blocks in the Mannar Basin, located off the western coast. The bids are expected to be opened on February 2, 2027.

To increase fuel storage capacity, 6 tanks with a capacity of 64,000 m3 are being constructed at the Kolonnawa Petroleum Complex with an investment of Rs. 3,746 million in 2025, 2 tanks with a capacity of 22,000 m3 with an investment of Rs. 1,446 million at the end of this year, and 3 tanks with a capacity of 40,000 m3 at the Muthurajawela Storage Terminal with an investment of Rs. 3,786 million in September 2026. Accordingly, our storage capacity will increase by 126,000 m3, up to the requirement for 45 days.

Bids have been called for the construction of a pipeline system from Kolonnawa to the Port with an investment of Rs. 16,332 million, with the aim of reducing the time taken to unload oil from ships from 96 hours to 48 hours. This is expected to be completed within 18 months.

In line with the proposal to develop the Bandaranaike International Airport, Katunayake as a hub serving the international aviation sector, construction work has already commenced on a tank complex with a capacity of 92,000 m3 for Jet A1 fuel at the Muthurajawela Petroleum Terminal and a pipeline to carry aviation fuel from Muthurajawela to the Bandaranaike International Airport, Katunayake, with an investment of Rs. 22,340 million.

The Sapugaskanda Oil Refinery will be upgraded from its current limit of 50,000 barrels per day to a capacity of 100,000 barrels under a public-private partnership. For this, Requests for Proposals (RFP) will be called in 2027 based on the Expressions of Interest (EOI) already called.

Digital transformation is no longer merely a technological initiative, but a fundamental requirement for improving the efficiency of the public sector, strengthening governance, facilitating economic growth, and ensuring that every citizen can access Government services. Digitalisation has been identified as a fundamental tool for modernizing public administration, improving the efficiency and transparency of Government activities, and strengthening institutional capacity.

Reaching the level of development this sector has attained in today’s world is a challenging and multifaceted process. At the same time, this sector is constantly evolving. Therefore, in order to reap its true benefits, the necessary legal and institutional structure must first be built.

The main livelihoods of rural people are bound up with farming. Although agriculture accounts for 23.9 percent of the labour force and around 39 percent of land use, and more than Rs. 200 billion has been spent annually on public investment and subsidies over the past few years, its contribution to the national economy is close to 8.4 percent. These data themselves demonstrate the problem of farming life. It will not be possible to emerge from this crisis without subjecting this sector to a radical new transformation.

Private sector investment will be encouraged to increase large-scale agricultural production and its quality, improve the efficiency of agricultural value chains and expand markets.

It is proposed to give paddy land owners the freedom to use uncultivated or low-productivity paddy lands, after they have been identified through an accurate survey, for field crops or permanent crops. Rs. 1,550 million has been allocated to encourage cultivation on these selected lands.

A collective “Yaya” production approach will be introduced, instead of individual cultivation, for purchasing agricultural inputs in bulk, carrying out pest and disease control jointly, using water resources with maximum efficiency, facing price monopolies in the market and increasing productivity. Rs. 400 million has been allocated as an initial step.

For rural agricultural development, Agrarian Service Centres will be transformed into ‘One-Stop Shops’ through the establishment of digital infrastructure, the creation of machinery pools, the empowerment of farming communities and the establishment of a digital platform through which realtime information can be exchanged. Rs. 250 million has been allocated to launch this as a pilot project in 5 main agricultural districts.

It is proposed to allocate an additional sum of Rs. 300 million in addition to Rs. 700 million already allocated for food security to enable cooperative societies which have been already selected to receive paddy dryers in 2026 and possess the necessary machinery capacity to purchase paddy from the Paddy Marketing Board and release it to the market. Furthermore, priority will be given to meeting the working capital requirements of these societies, under an approved framework to facilitate the immediate commencement of these operations. This allocation is also intended to assist these cooperative societies in purchasing the machinery required for rice production.

An Integrated Plant Nutrient Management System (IPNM) is required to promote the integrated use of chemical fertilizer and organic fertilizer, increase crop productivity and strengthen soil quality and nutrient status. It is proposed to implement a 4-year project for this purpose, and Rs. 47 million has been allocated for the year 2027 as an initial step.

It is expected to provide the fertilizer subsidy of Rs. 30,000 per hectare for paddy cultivation, and Rs. 44,000 million has been allocated for this purpose. This subsidy of Rs. 30,000 per hectare of paddy cultivation will also be provided for other crops cultivated in paddy fields from the 2026/27 Maha season.

Rs. 400 million has been allocated for the development of the entire value chain, aimed at enhancing the productivity, quality and global market access of the cinnamon industry.

It is expected to develop and maintain identified farms of the National Livestock Development Board as “Breeding Farms”. Rs. 1,800 million has been allocated for the importation of foundation breeding animal stocks of high genetic quality required to establish specialized Nucleus Herds of “dairy cattle and buffaloes” at the Nikaweratiya Farm, “pigs” at the Horakele Farm, and “sheep” at the Siringapatha Farm.

Provisions have been allocated for a feasibility study to establish a special economic zone, centred on the Western and North Western Provinces, for the export of chicken and chicken-related value-added products to foreign markets.

Provisions of Rs. 3,000 million were provided in 2026 to recommence the construction of the new Milco milk processing factory at Badalgama, on which EUR 57 million had been spent but which had been suspended for a long time. Rs. 3,000 million has been allocated for the year 2027 to complete its work and establish the Milco factory there.

This project, commenced in 2015 and was to be completed in 2019, has given rise to many problems due to its suspension for a long period, such as damage to machinery and construction, additional rehabilitation costs, increases in the prices of machinery parts, and the submission of delay claims by the contractor. Milco has already commenced discussions with the contractor, DESMI of Denmark, to resolve these issues.

It is proposed to allocate a further Rs. 300 million, in addition to the Rs. 200 million currently allocated, to provide concessions to farmers for theestablishment of goat farms in districts including Puttalam, Mannar and Batticaloa.

Developing and maintaining a strong irrigation system is essential to increase agricultural production, ensure food security, strengthen the livelihoods of rural people and efficiently manage water resources in the face of climate change. Through this Budget, Rs. 77,000 million has been allocated, providing adequate and prioritized financial provisions for irrigation development, to accelerate ongoing irrigation development activities and to commence new projects.

Rs. 21,000 million has been estimated for the rehabilitation of the cascaded tank systems damaged by “Ditwah” and the associated minor irrigation infrastructure and agricultural roads. These programmes commenced in 2026 with the Ditwah provisions, and the remaining provisions required will be provided in 2027.

Although a feasibility study on the Jaffna Irrigation Project was carried out in 2018 to improve the water security of the people of the Jaffna Peninsula, it has not been implemented to date. Rs. 75 million has been allocated for further feasibility studies, including on this project.

Rs. 7,500 million has been allocated to accelerate the work of the Lower Malwathu Oya Project, construction of which has already commenced, for the agricultural and social development of the Anuradhapura, Vavuniya and Mannar districts.

It is proposed to allocate Rs. 1,000 million to meet the water requirements of the Batticaloa and Ampara districts, minimize flood risk and commence the Mundeni Aru River Basin Development Project.

The water potential of the Hambantota industrial zone and its associated areas will be around 115 million cubic metres by 2040. There is an additional water requirement of approximately 115 million cubic metres (MCM) per year for domestic and industrial needs. To find solutions to this requirement, water sources including the Rakwana Ganga, the additional effective water capacity of the Samanalawewa Reservoir and the Uma Oya water transfer to the Kirindi Oya basin have been identified, and the necessary provisions have beenallocated to carry out an integrated water balance and pre-feasibility study on them.

Rs. 5,000 million has been allocated for irrigation projects such as the implementation of the Wayamba Canal Project to divert surplus water from the Weemedilla Reservoir to the Mee Oya basin in the Kurunegala district, the modernization of the over 50-year-old Rajanganaya lift irrigation scheme, the completion of work on the halted feeder canal from the Siyambalangamuwa Oya to the Mahagalgamuwa Oya, and the mitigation of floods in Gampaha town.

Rs. 19,500 million has been allocated for the Mahaweli Water Security and Investment Programme, including the North Central Province Greater Canal Project.

It is expected to carry out feasibility studies to recommence the Kumbukkan Oya Reservoir Project aimed at meeting the water requirements of the Moneragala district and the Heda Oya Reservoir Development Project aimed at improving the water supply of the Ampara district. It is proposed to allocate an additional Rs. 200 million for the preliminary work of such irrigation projects.

It is our duty to safeguard environmental balance for sustainable economic development. It is one of our fundamental responsibilities to secure the natural resources of the country for future generations by facing the challenges of climate change, protecting biodiversity and moving towards a green economy approach.

Our Government, which has understood the importance of the wildlife sector, has recruited 313 wildlife guards and 3,451 electric fence maintenance assistants to strengthen the services of the Department of Wildlife Conservation for its protection and management. Provisions have been allocated for the recruitment of 114 wildlife rangers. Vehicles and equipment, including 16 double cabs, 84 single cabs, 181 motorcycles and 09 tractors, have been added in 2026 for field operations to deliver wildlife services efficiently.

The Government is implementing a phased pension revision programme with the objective of ensuring a fair and structured pension system for all pensioners by systematically eliminating the disparities that exist among the pensions of retired public servants.

Once this revision is applied to the relevant pensioners and the payment of the revised pension commences, the payment of the above mentioned monthly interim allowance of Rs. 2,500 proposed for those who retired during the period 2020–2024 will cease.

The overall objective of all these measures is to provide fair relief to public servants who retired at different times by systematically narrowing the pension disparities among them, and to establish a sustainable pension system in the long term.

36.1 Increasing the Personal Income Tax Relief and Revising the Progressive Tax Slabs

Our Government increased the annual personal income tax free threshold from Rs. 1.2 million to Rs. 1.8 million in 2025. Through this measure, individual taxpayers gained a monthly tax relief ranging from a minimum of Rs. 3,500 to a maximum of Rs. 20,500.

To provide further relief to personal income taxpayers, it is proposed to further increase the annual personal income tax free threshold and to revise the personal income tax structure by expanding the taxable income brackets.

We were able to increase the tax-free threshold from Rs. 100,000 per month to Rs. 150,000. Through the proposed revisions, it will be further increased to Rs. 162,500. Accordingly, a person receiving a monthly salary and allowances of up to Rs. 162,500 will not be required to pay tax on his or her salary.

The tax slab to which the 6 percent tax rate applies will be Rs. 70,833 per month, and thereafter the tax slabs will be widened from Rs. 41,667 per month to Rs. 50,000 per month. The tax rates will be set at 6 percent, 12 percent, 18 percent, 24 percent, 30 percent and 36 percent, applying a rate of 12 percent instead of 18 percent after the 6 percent rate.

These revisions will raise the monthly income threshold at which the maximum personal income tax rate applies from Rs. 358,000 to Rs. 433,000. Consequently, each individual taxpayer will receive an additional monthly tax relief ranging from a minimum of Rs. 750 to a maximum of Rs. 15,750.

Accordingly, as a combined result of the tax reforms introduced in 2025 and the revisions proposed for 2027, the total monthly tax relief provided by our Government to each individual taxpayer will increase ranging from a minimum of Rs. 4,250 to a maximum of Rs. 36,250.

36.2 Enhanced Capital Allowances for Export-Oriented Manufacturing and Tourism Businesses Approved by the Board of Investment (BOI)

With the aim of encouraging private sector investment, promoting export-oriented manufacturing and strengthening the tourism sector, it is proposed to introduce enhanced capital allowances as an investment incentive for enterprises operating under agreements with the Board of Investment of Sri Lanka.

Accordingly, BOI-approved enterprises engaged in export-oriented manufacturing or tourism development activities with a minimum qualifying investment of USD 3 million will be permitted subject to meeting the relevant requirements to opt for an enhanced capital allowance equivalent to 200 percent of qualifying capital expenditure in place of the existing allowance regime.

36.3 Exemption of Exercise Books and Printed Books from Value Added Tax (VAT)

Printed books and other printed materials became subject to Value Added Tax (VAT) effective from January 2024, while exercise books had been subject to VAT even before that date.

Recognising the importance of education and the necessity of ensuring that essential educational materials are available at an affordable price, and with the objective of promoting access to education, knowledge and reading, we propose to exempt textbooks, dictionaries, encyclopaedias, children’s drawing and colouring books, educational publications and other printed books, as well as exercise books, from Value Added Tax (VAT).

36.4 Revising the Basis for Calculating VAT Liability on Cinema Ticket Revenue

With the aim of ensuring the fair and equitable application of Value Added Tax within the film industry, we propose to revise the basis for calculating the VAT liability arising from the sale of cinema tickets.

Accordingly, the VAT liability of film producers, distributors, the National Film Corporation and cinema hall operators will be determined on the basis of the share of cinema ticket revenue received by or allocated to each party at each stage, rather than calculating VAT on the full ticket value.

36.5 Facilitating Earlier Qualification of Newly VAT Registered Exporters as “Eligible Exporters”

With the aim of facilitating export-oriented businesses, I propose to revise the criteria under which newly VAT registered Exporters are recognized as Eligible Exporters.

Accordingly, newly VAT registered persons will be recognized as Eligible Exporters where more than 80 percent of their turnover during the preceding six months has been derived from exports, without having to satisfy the existing requirement relating to the preceding Calander year.

36.6 Use of the Indicative Exchange Rate of the Central Bank of Sri Lanka for Value Added Tax on Foreign Currency Supplies

In order to ensure greater transparency, consistency and fairness in valuing taxable supplies denominated in foreign currencies, it is proposed to use the Indicative Exchange Rate published by the Central Bank of Sri Lanka for the conversion of foreign currencies into Sri Lanka Rupees for VAT purposes. The Commissioner General of Inland Revenue (CGIR) will specify by way of a circular the manner in which such exchange rate is to be applied.

36.7 Social Security Contribution Levy (SSCL) Exemption for Bunker Fuel, Supplied to Ships Engaged in International Transportation

The supply of bunker fuel to ships engaged in international transport is an important component of Sri Lanka’s maritime services sector. This industry operates in a highly competitive international market.

With the aim of safeguarding the competitiveness of the industry and strengthening Sri Lanka’s position as a regional maritime hub, we propose to exempt the supply of fuel to ships engaged in international transport from the Social Security Contribution Levy.

36.8 Review and Rationalization of the Tax Structure applicable to the Betting and Gaming Industry

Addressing the long-standing need for an effective regulatory framework for the betting and gaming industry, the Government has established the Gambling Regulatory Authority (GRA) and commenced its operations.

Given that a regulatory framework is now in place, it is proposed to conduct a comprehensive review of the taxes, fees, and charges applicable to this industry with the view to eliminate instances of double taxation and inconsistencies, align the tax structure with international best practices, and ensure a fair and balanced approach that supports the sustainable development of the industry while safeguarding government revenue.

This review aims to establish a simple, transparent, and integrated tax structure that is easy to administer and facilitates compliance.

With the objective of strengthening tax administration, enhancing legal certainty, and facilitating tax compliance for taxpayers, we propose to remove ambiguities present in certain provisions of existing tax laws. Furthermore, we propose to review and amend these provisions to align them with evolving business practices and new forms of transactions.

Additionally, we propose to review certain stringent procedural requirements which could lead to taxpayers denied legitimate tax refunds or input tax credits due technical or procedural noncompliance while ensuring that adequate safeguards remain in place to protect government revenue.

These measures aim to establish a fair, transparent, and efficient tax administration system that encourages voluntary tax compliance, safeguards the taxpayers’ legitimate entitlements, and protects government revenue.

36.10 Revision of the Special Commodity Levy (SCL) on Several Essential Food Items

With the aim of minimizing the cost-of-living pressure on consumers caused by rising prices of essential food items, it is proposed to reduce the Special Commodity Levy on imported sprats from Rs. 100 to Rs. 10 per kilogram, on Maldive fish from Rs. 302 to Rs. 10 per kilogram, and on powdered and unpowdered dried chillies by Rs. 50 per kilogram.

These tax revisions are scheduled to be implemented with effect from November 13, 2026.

It is proposed to reduce by 25 percent, the existing CESS rates applicable to 800 selected Harmonized System (HS) code lines, under the Government’s programme of phased reduction of import tariffs, with the aim of simplifying the import tax structure, reducing trade-related costs and enhancing the competitiveness of the economy.

It is proposed to remove the 10 percent Ports and Airports Development Levy (PAL) currently charged on the importation of newsprint classified under specified HS codes, with effect from January 1, 2027.

36.12 Supporting the Domestic Sugar Industry to Compete in the Market

To protect the domestic sugar industry, it is also proposed to increase the Special Commodity Levy imposed on imported sugar by Rs. 10 per kilogram, in order to minimize the tax disparity between domestically produced sugar and imported sugar, create equal competitive opportunities and a fair competitive environment for both parties, and ensure a more neutral and equitable tax treatment.

With the aim of reducing the tax burden on the tourism industry, enhancing its competitiveness and ensuring sustainable financing for tourism development, it is proposed to abolish the Tourism Development Levy currently charged on the turnover of tourism establishments.

In order to ensure continued funding for tourism promotion and development, steps will be taken in the future to formulate a suitable financing mechanism. Until then, the required funds will be provided by the Treasury on requests made by the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority.

The 2025 Budget proposed to introduce a new Excise Act in place of the centuryold Excise Ordinance with the aim of improving revenue management.

However, until a new Act is introduced, we propose to bring amendments to the Excise Ordinance by further simplifying this complex process and incorporating amendments into the existing Ordinance itself.
