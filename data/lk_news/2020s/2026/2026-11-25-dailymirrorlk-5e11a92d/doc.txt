Inflation climbs to 8.5% in October on higher transport costs

Annual inflation picks up after holding at 8.1% for 2 months

Non-food inflation reaches 9.5%, with petrol and bus fares driving transport costs

Food inflation rises to 7.2%, while monthly increase in overall prices quickens to 0.41%

The nationwide inflation accelerated to 8.5 percent in October, its highest reading this year, with the higher petrol prices and bus fares driving up the transport costs and households paying more for vegetables and milk powder, the official data showed.

The annual inflation had held at 8.1 percent in August and September, after climbing from 1.6 percent in February. The October rate was more than three times the 2.7 percent recorded a year earlier, according to the Census and Statistics Department.

The non-food inflation rose to 9.5 percent, from 9.1 percent in September and accounted for more than three-fifths of the annual increase in consumer prices. Transport was the largest single contributor, adding 2.45 percentage points to headline inflation. Petrol alone added 1.21 points, followed by the bus fares at 0.36 points, diesel at 0.20 points and school and preschool transport at 0.19 points.

Housing and utilities were the next-largest non-food contributor at 1.17 percentage points, reflecting higher rents, maintenance costs, cooking gas prices and electricity bills.

Restaurants and hotels added 0.52 points, while education contributed 0.39 points, mostly due to the higher tuition fees.

The health costs also rose, with the fees at private medical practices accounting for the largest share of that category’s contribution.

The food inflation rose to 7.2 percent, from 6.9 percent in September, more than double the 3.3 percent recorded in June. Vegetables made the largest contribution to the annual rise in food prices, followed by fresh fish, dried fish and big onions.

Milk powder, fruit, chicken and eggs were also more expensive than a year earlier, while the lower coconut and rice prices offset part of the increase.

The monthly figures showed a reversal in the transport costs. The consumer prices rose 0.41 percent from September, up from 0.11 percent the previous month.

Transport added 0.19 percentage points to the October increase after subtracting 0.15 points in September. Petrol accounted for most of the turnaround, while the vehicle servicing charges and three-wheeler fares also rose. The airfares fell over the month.

The non-food items contributed 0.33 percentage points to the monthly increase, with the tuition fees and restaurant and hotel prices adding to the pressure from transport. Food contributed 0.08 points as higher prices for vegetables, limes, milk powder and coconuts outweighed the declines in fresh fish, green chillies, fruit, eggs, rice and chicken.

The National Consumer Price Index rose to 225.1, from 224.2 in September. The cost of the 485-item basket used to calculate the index increased by Rs.466 from September and Rs.8,951 from a year earlier. The 12-month average inflation climbed to 5.0 percent, from 4.5 percent.
