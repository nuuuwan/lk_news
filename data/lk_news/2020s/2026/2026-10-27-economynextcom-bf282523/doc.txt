Sri Lanka to lease 15 plantation properties to investors

ECONOMYNEXT – Sri Lanka’s Cabinet has approved the long-term leasing of 15 state plantation properties to selected local investors, minister Nalinda Jayatissa said.

The assets are owned by the Sri Lanka State Plantations Corporation, the Janatha Estates Development Board and Elkaduwa Plantations Company.

The government had earlier called for expressions of interest from investors for 1,514 hectares of land, 10 tourist bungalows and scenic locations, and 15 inactive factories managed by the three plantation entities.

A total of 470 investors submitted expressions of interest, Jayatissa said, after which shortlisted applicants were invited to submit detailed proposals.

“The government received 44 proposals covering 50 land and property lots.”

“Cabinet approved the proposal to lease 15 selected land and property plots to investors on a long-term basis.”

The government expects the initiative to attract private investment and improve the utilization of underused plantation-sector assets, he said.

He did not respond to reporters’ questions on how long the lease periods are, or how much the government hopes to raise through the leasing, or if the lands and properties will be distributed among parties loyal to the government.

Historically, most lands under the SLSPC and JEDB originated from the 1972 and 1975 land reform laws, when large privately owned plantations were taken over by the state. Many tea, rubber and coconut estates were nationalized and later placed under state plantation entities. (Colombo/Oct27/2026)
