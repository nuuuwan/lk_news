Amnesty calls UN report a reminder of decades of impunity

AMNESTY INTERNATIONAL - Responding to the publication of a new report by the UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) on crimes under international law committed during Sri Lanka’s 25-year internal armed conflict, Smriti Singh, Amnesty International’s South Asia Director, said:

“This report shines a light on the fact that no progress has been made in addressing crimes under international law in Sri Lanka and that truth, justice and reparation remain elusive for hundreds and thousands of victims. Its findings reflect the unwillingness of successive Sri Lankan governments to investigate allegations and ensure accountability for abuses committed during the internal armed conflict that ended in 2009. Impunity persists even as mass graves continue to be identified, such as in Chemmani where recent exhumations found the remains of more than 583 individuals, including children.

“At a time when conflicts across the world have seen humanitarian assistance and infrastructure come under rampant attack amid a clear disregard for international law, this report is particularly timely. It is significant that it highlights that attacks on humanitarian activities in Sri Lanka became a model and a precursor to what we are seeing in today’s conflicts. This should act as a powerful reminder of how a lack of accountability and continuing impunity in Sri Lanka can be a source of encouragement for those who commit crimes and undermine international law elsewhere.

“This report and the Sri Lankan government’s decades-long failure to address justice, truth and reparation underscore the clear need for continued international scrutiny. This further reinforces the importance of continuing the valuable work of OHCHR’s Sri Lanka Accountability Project and for UN member states to use the project’s resources to advance accountability including through the principles of universal jurisdiction.”

The report,’I DIED MANY TIMES” Attacks on humanitarian assistance in Sri Lanka, 20 years on’ is available here. The report addresses attacks on humanitarian personnel and operations, and the restriction and denial of humanitarian assistance by all parties, during the final phase of the armed conflict in Sri Lanka.

The OHCHR Sri Lanka Accountability Project (OSLAP) was established by OHCHR to implement the accountability mandate set out in UN Human Rights Council Resolution 46/1 (2021), which requested the Office to “collect, consolidate, analyse and preserve evidence of serious human rights violations and abuses and related crimes in Sri Lanka”.
