New Disability Rights Bill moves forward after 30 years

Colombo, Oct. 6 (Daily Mirror) - A long-awaited overhaul of Sri Lanka’s disability rights laws has moved a step forward, with the Parliamentary Forum for Persons with Disabilities holding extensive discussions on a new Bill to replace the 30-year-old legislation.

The proposed Bill seeks to repeal the Protection of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, No. 28 of 1996, and introduce a new legal framework to strengthen the rights and protections of persons with disabilities.

The Parliamentary Forum for Persons with Disabilities met recently at Parliament under the chairmanship of MP Sugath Wasantha de Silva to discuss the proposed legislation.

Addressing the forum, de Silva said the need for new legislation had been discussed continuously over the past three decades since the enactment of the 1996 Act.

He noted that although Sri Lanka ratified the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UN CRPD) in 2016, the country has yet to establish domestic legislation fully aligned with the Convention.

Several Cabinet decisions have been taken since 2016 to formulate new legislation. However, differences have emerged between the original objectives of the proposed law and provisions contained in subsequent drafts.

The Chairman stressed the need to resolve these issues through discussions with all relevant stakeholders before the legislative process is taken forward.

Ministry officials informed the forum that a model Bill prepared by a subcommittee comprising members of the National Council for Persons with Disabilities, incorporating their observations, had been submitted to the Legal Draftsman's Department.

Following revisions by the Legal Draftsman's Department, the draft has been returned to the Ministry, while the process of obtaining the views of the National Council for Persons with Disabilities is currently underway.

The forum also considered observations previously made by the Attorney General’s Department and recommendations of the Human Rights Commission in determining how the legislative process should proceed.

Participants discussed the need to establish an independent monitoring mechanism in line with the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.

Representatives of the National Council for Persons with Disabilities stressed that persons with disabilities must have direct representation and meaningful participation in the formulation of laws and policies affecting them, based on the principle “Nothing About Us Without Us.”

The meeting also focused on employment rights, including suitable employment opportunities, equal conditions of service and remuneration, and reasonable accommodation for persons with disabilities in workplaces.

The meeting was attended by Deputy Co-Chairman of the Parliamentary Forum Dr. Sathyalingam Padmanathan and MPs Dr. Nihal Abeysinghe, Attorney-at-Law Susantha Dodawatta and Padmini Bandara, among others.
