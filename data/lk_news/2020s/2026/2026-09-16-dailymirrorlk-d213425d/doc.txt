Shortage of crepe bandages reported at Colombo National Hospital

Colombo, Sep 16 (Daily Mirror) - A shortage of essential surgical supplies, including crepe bandages, at the Colombo National Hospital and other major hospitals has sparked concern over the country’s medical supply system, with patients reportedly being asked to purchase items needed for their treatment from outside pharmacies.

Specialist Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa, President of the Government Medical Officers' Forum (GMOF), claimed that patients requiring wound dressing, post-operative wound coverage and orthopaedic support were facing difficulties due to shortages of the required bandages.

He said the shortages were linked to delays in the procurement process of the Medical Supplies Division, while arrangements allowing hospital directors to purchase certain essential items locally from approved pharmacies had not been functioning effectively in some hospitals.

The situation has reportedly placed an additional burden on patients and their families, particularly those without caregivers who can leave the hospital to purchase medical supplies.

Dr. Sanjeewa questioned how patients requiring hospital-based treatment could be expected to obtain basic surgical supplies from outside when such items are needed as part of their care.

He also raised concerns over the procurement process for the 2027 health sector, claiming that it has stalled and warning that prolonged delays could result in a recurrence of shortages of medicines and medical equipment experienced in recent years.

The GMOF President urged the Health Minister to intervene immediately and address weaknesses in the procurement and distribution system.

He further claimed that the wider health sector crisis that emerged in 2022 had not yet been fully resolved, raising concerns over whether the country’s health system is adequately prepared to prevent another disruption in the supply of essential medical items.
