40,000 unqualified individuals dispensing medicines in Sri Lanka

Colombo, Nov. 19 (Daily Mirror) - Around 40,000 individuals without recognised medical qualifications are involved in dispensing medicines in Sri Lanka, raising serious concerns over public health and the misuse of antibiotics, the Association of Medical Specialists (AMS) has warned.

AMS President and Consultant Community Physician Dr. Ruwan Ferdinando said the practice was contributing to the growing risk of antimicrobial resistance, particularly among bacteria, as antibiotics were being used excessively and inappropriately.

Addressing a media briefing at the Health Promotion Bureau, Dr. Ferdinando identified several factors behind the excessive use of antibiotics, including patients obtaining them without medical advice, requesting antibiotics from doctors and doctors frequently prescribing them, sometimes in anticipation of patients' demands.

He also raised concerns over the involvement of an estimated 40,000 individuals who are not qualified medical practitioners in dispensing medicines, noting that antibiotics were also being supplied by them.

Responding to a question about the identity of these individuals, Dr. Ferdinando said one category consisted of people without formal medical qualifications who had acquired knowledge of medicines through working in pharmacies.

He warned that continued misuse and overuse of antibiotics could accelerate drug resistance, eventually making medicines ineffective in treating infections.

Dr. Ferdinando further noted that, under the law, Western medicines could be prescribed by medical practitioners registered with the Sri Lanka Medical Council.
