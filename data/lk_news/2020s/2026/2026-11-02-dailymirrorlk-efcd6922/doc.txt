Government open for discussion on Terrorism Bill: Bimal

Colombo, Nov. 2 (Daily Mirror) - Amid concerns raised by local and international organisations over the proposed Protection of the State from Terrorism Bill (PSTB), Transport Minister Bimal Rathnayake said yesterday that the government remains open to discussions on the proposed legislation, which was recently tabled in Parliament.

The Minister said that any specific concerns or shortcomings identified in the Bill could be addressed through discussions.

He noted that the Prevention of Terrorism Act (PTA) had faced criticism for nearly four decades over its alleged undemocratic nature, adding that the National People's Power (NPP) government had pledged to repeal the existing legislation.

Rathnayake said the government was taking steps to abolish the PTA and stressed the need for a replacement law that would be more democratic, effective and consistent with internationally accepted principles.

He further pointed out that a new legal framework was necessary to address ongoing threats posed by organised crime and international terrorism.

"We need an Act to protect the State. Any specific concerns regarding the Bill can be addressed through discussion. We are open to discussions," he said.

Several organisations, including the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR), have expressed concerns over the proposed legislation, warning that it retains or expands several shortcomings found in the existing Prevention of Terrorism Act and previous counter-terrorism legislative proposals.
