Govt. eyes AI platform to plug OTA commission leak from unregistered rooms

Sri Lanka could retain the 20 to 25 percent commission currently paid to global online travel agencies (OTAs) on tens of thousands of unregistered rooms by bringing the country’s largely informal tourism sector onto an artificial intelligence-based platform, Deputy Minister of Tourism Prof. Ruwan Ranasinghe said.

Addressing the national celebration of World Tourism Day 2026, hosted by the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA) at Taj Samudra, Colombo on Friday (25) under the theme ‘Digital Agenda and Artificial Intelligence to Redesign Tourism’, Deputy Minister pointed out that a large part of the industry remains informal and outside the direct purview of the regulator. “We can have a more regulated and even governed tourism sector if we absorb that into an AI platform. I think it is an investment that we need to work on,” he told the gathering, adding that harnessing AI to take the industry to the next level must now find a place on SLTDA’s agenda.

He argued that the commissions paid to OTAs for rooms not registered with the authority represent money the country need not lose. “If we can bring these onto that platform, we can save that money within our country in its entirety,” he noted. On his figures, every US$ 100 of room revenue booked through such channels sends between US$ 20 and 25 out of the country.

However, the Deputy Minister suggested the gains extend beyond commissions to pricing itself. Referring to an example from Dr. Subasinghe’s keynote on AI in hospitality, in which an average daily rate (ADR) of about US$ 225 served as the starting point for a negotiated rate, he observed that Colombo’s ADR sits at roughly US$ 100 to 125. The keynote benchmark is thus 80 to 125 percent above the Colombo range, a gap he believed AI-driven negotiation could help hotels narrow during the high season.

He also cited the government’s decision to establish a dedicated ministry for the digital economy as evidence of a policy view that digitisation is critical to efficiency, regulation, legislation and governance. The SLTDA and the Sri Lanka Tourism Promotion Bureau have already launched a digital promotion campaign that such a platform could run alongside, he added.

Meanwhile, he stressed that the discussion must reach the communities that practise tourism on the ground, with several parallel programmes, particularly those aimed at school students, being run alongside the national event.

The celebration also featured the Tourism Start-Up Competition 2026, themed ‘AI-Driven Innovation for the Future of Tourism’, which drew 52 applications across the Tertiary Level and Commercial Level categories. According to SLTDA, 25 entries were shortlisted to present before an expert panel, with six winners chosen from each category, meaning fewer than one in four applicants walked away with an award. (NF)
