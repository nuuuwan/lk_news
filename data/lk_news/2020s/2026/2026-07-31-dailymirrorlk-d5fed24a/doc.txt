Sri Lanka to face vegetable shortage?

Colombo, July 31 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka is beginning to face shortage of vegetables with the dry spell that is prevailing, traders said yesterday.

President Dambulla Economic Centre Traders Association C. A. Siriwardene told media that the supply of vegetables in the economic centre has been reduced by 200,00kgs of vegetables daily. The daily supply has reduced from 600,00o kgs to 400,000 kgs during the last few days. He said the dry spell and non-availability of fertilizer have resulted in this situation.

The wholesale price of capsicum ranges from Rs 500 to Rs 550 a kilo, while the price of carrot ranges from Rs 230 to R 250. A kilo of brinjal is Rs 350, price of drumsticks ranges from Rs 150 to Rs 200 and pumpkin R 115 a kilo.

Meanwhile, former President Manning Market Traders Association S. M. Upasena said the country seemed to be beginning to face a vegetable shortage. He also blamed the dry spell for the shortage. “The farmers have become reluctant to cultivate vegetables at the moment as water levels of reservoirs and tanks are dropping,” he told Daily Mirror.

However he said the prices are not likely to increase because of the low demand. “The demand for vegetable are on the lower side as people do not have much cash even to spend for their essentials,” Upasena said.

Also, he said the issue with the vegetables is that there is no possibility of keeping buffer stocks. “The issue pertaining to vegetables could reach serious proportions,” he added.
