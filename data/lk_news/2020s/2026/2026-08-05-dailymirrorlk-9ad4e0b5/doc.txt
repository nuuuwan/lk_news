Has the Consumer Affairs Authority Gone into Hibernation?

Every day, Sri Lankan consumers are being taken for a ride, and the Consumer Affairs Authority appears to be watching from the sidelines.

I recently ordered a meal from an internationally renowned fast-food chain. The advertised menu price was Rs. 2,990.00. Yet, when the bill was presented, I found an additional Rs. 386.00 described as a “handling fee”  Rs. 76.50 as a “convenience fee and  29.00 as fuel surcharge. I accept the fuel surcharge. The amount I finally had to pay was an astonishing Rs. 511.50 more than the advertised price.

The obvious question is: What is the real price of the meal? Is it the price displayed on the menu, or the inflated amount extracted from the customer at the point of payment? These charges may have impressive names, but to the ordinary consumer they are nothing more than hidden costs that distort the advertised price. What is even more disturbing is the deafening silence of the Consumer Affairs Authority. Complaints about these questionable pricing practices have repeatedly appeared in newspapers and across social media, yet the Authority seems content to remain a passive observer.

The Consumer Affairs Authority was created to safeguard consumers—not to preside over their exploitation. Its responsibility is to ensure transparent pricing, prevent unfair trade practices, and restore public confidence in the marketplace. The Authority must immediately investigate these pricing practices and require that the price displayed to the public is the final price payable, except for taxes or charges clearly disclosed before the order is confirmed. Anything less makes a mockery of consumer protection.

Sri Lankan consumers are not asking for charity. The time has come for the Consumer Affairs Authority to wake from its deep slumber. If it continues to remain inactive while consumers are systematically overcharged, the public is entitled to ask a simple but uncomfortable question: Who, in reality, is the Consumer Affairs Authority protecting?
