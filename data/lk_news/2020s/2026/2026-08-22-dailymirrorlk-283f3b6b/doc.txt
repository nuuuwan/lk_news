Hayleys Advantis-National Council for Road Safety launch ‘100 for 100’ rider safety initiative

From left: Hayleys Advantis Group Managing Director Ruwan Waidyaratne, Transport, Highways and Urban Development Minister Bimal Rathnayake and Citypak Chief Executive Officer Vaseeharan Thiagarajah

Hayleys Advantis in collaboration with the National Council for Road Safety (NCRS), recently launched ‘100 for 100’, a national rider safety movement, aimed at strengthening motorcycle safety across Sri Lanka through defensive riding training and practical road safety interventions.

The programme has a national ambition of mobilising 100 organisations, with each engaging 100 motorcycle riders, creating a potential reach of 10,000 riders across the country.

The launch comes at a time when motorcycle safety remains a critical national concern. In 2024, Sri Lanka recorded 2,403 fatal road accident cases, with motorcycles accounting for 34 percent of vehicles involved in road accidents. Motorcycle riders represented 31 percent of road user fatalities, making them the second-largest road user fatality group after pedestrians.

As a transportation and logistics company, Hayleys Advantis recognises its responsibility to contribute to safer roads for the people and communities connected to its operations. This led the company to initiate ‘100 for 100’ as a practical and scalable approach to reaching motorcycle riders with safety education and training.

Under the programme, each participating organisation will identify 100 riders from among its employees, operational riders, delivery partners or regular motorcycle commuters. These riders will receive defensive riding education and related safety guidance covering areas such as hazard anticipation, road discipline, responsible riding behaviour, helmet and personal protective equipment awareness and crash prevention.

Advantis is leading by example, commencing the initiative with its domestic express subsidiary, Citypak, which was the first to put the programme into practice. With over 30 years of experience in Sri Lanka’s domestic express sector, 45+ branches and more than two million shipments delivered annually, Citypak provides a strong starting point for Advantis’ commitment to embedding safer riding practices within its own operations.

Hayleys Advantis Group Managing Director Ruwan Waidyaratne said, “Motorcycle riders are a large and varied group of road users but unlike many other vehicle categories, there is no single network through which we can consistently reach them with road safety education and training. We saw an opportunity to use workplaces as practical touchpoints to reach riders within their own networks. This led us to develop ‘100 for 100’ in collaboration with the NCRS, enabling organisations to take an active role in promoting safer riding. We hope to see organisations across the country take up the challenge and help us build a movement for safer riding across Sri Lanka.”

NCRS Chairman Manjula Kularatne stated, “‘100 for 100’ is a strong example of how the private sector can complement national road safety efforts through practical action. We welcome the collaboration with Hayleys Advantis and encourage more organisations to come forward, engage their riders and become active partners in creating safer roads across Sri Lanka. When the government, businesses and communities work together, we can extend the reach of road safety initiatives and create a lasting impact.”
