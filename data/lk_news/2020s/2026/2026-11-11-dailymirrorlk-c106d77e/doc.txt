Only 20km of Colombo’s 469km drainage network upgraded since 2014 – Minister

Colombo, Nov 11 (Daily Mirror) - Only around 20 kilometres of Colombo’s 469-kilometre canal and drainage network have been upgraded since 2014 to cope with heavy rainfall, Cabinet Spokesman and Minister Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa said.

Speaking at the weekly Cabinet decisions press briefing, the Minister said the recent flooding in several parts of Colombo was mainly caused by exceptionally heavy rainfall, with between 129mm and 150mm of rain falling within one to two hours.

The Minister explained that Colombo's existing drainage network was designed to accommodate rainfall of around 30mm per hour.

Of the city's total 469-kilometre canal and drainage network, only around 20 kilometres have been upgraded since 2014 to withstand rainfall intensities of up to 102mm per hour. He said the upgraded sections had helped water recede within a few hours in some locations, whereas those areas had previously remained flooded for several days.

The Minister also identified unauthorised structures, irregular developments and improper waste disposal as factors contributing to drainage blockages and urban flooding.

Referring to the ongoing cleaning of the Norris Canal near the National Hospital, he said eight households had yet to be relocated, although a compensation package worth Rs. 11 million had been prepared for them.

He said that addressing urban flooding was not a responsibility that could be handled by the Colombo Municipal Council alone, adding that a rapid action programme was underway under the direction of the Presidential Secretariat.

The Minister also urged the public to refrain from dumping non-biodegradable waste into drains and canals, stressing that public cooperation was essential to maintaining the city's drainage network and preventing future flooding.
