High-value crossings lift turnover as CSE ends week on a soft note

The Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) concluded the week with another subdued trading session, as both benchmark indices edged marginally lower amid cautious investor sentiment. Price movements remained largely range-bound throughout the day, while trading activity was dominated by high-value crossing transactions rather than broad-based market participation. Although turnover and share volume increased sharply compared to the previous session, retail participation remained relatively muted, reflecting the market's continued wait-and-see stance.

The strong contribution from negotiated crossings suggests that value-oriented investors are gradually accumulating positions in selected counters despite the prevailing weakness in the broader market. This selective institutional participation could provide a constructive signal for market sentiment in the coming weeks, particularly as investors look ahead to the corporate earnings season.

The All Share Price Index (ASPI) declined by 19.50 points, or 0.09%, to close at 21,129.21, while the S&P SL20 Index slipped 6.05 points, or 0.10%, to settle at 5,939.98. Total market turnover rose to LKR 4.34 Bn, with 117.10 Mn shares traded, largely driven by crossing transactions.

The Capital Goods sector emerged as the largest contributor to market turnover, generating LKR 1.35 Bn with 11.50 Mn shares traded. HAYL.N led sector activity, recording LKR 1.04 Bn in turnover on 4.67 Mn shares traded. Crossing transactions amounted to LKR 2.50 Bn, accounting for 57% of total market turnover, with the largest crossing recorded in UML.N amounting to LKR 1.02 Bn involving 38.00 Mn shares.

Market breadth remained weak, with 109 decliners outnumbering 73 gainers, indicating that selling pressure continued to outweigh buying interest despite the sharp increase in turnover. HHL.N was the largest positive contributor to the ASPI, adding 6.49 points, followed by HAYC.N, MELS.N, VONE.N, and DOCK.N. On the downside, HNB.N exerted the biggest drag on the index, reducing it by 7.05 points, while CFIN.N, DFCC.N, HAYL.N, and LOLC.N also weighed on overall market performance.

Among notable trades, UML.N attracted the highest investor interest, generating LKR 1.03 Bn in turnover on 38.16 Mn shares traded, although the counter declined by 0.74% during the session.

Overall, the market ended the week on a cautious note, with institutional participation through sizeable crossing transactions offsetting subdued retail activity. While the broader market remained under pressure, continued accumulation in selected fundamentally strong counters suggests investors are positioning selectively ahead of stronger market catalysts.
