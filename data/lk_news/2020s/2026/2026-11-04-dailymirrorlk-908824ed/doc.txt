JKH triples shareholder profit

Lower tax charge and changing earnings mix boost bottom line

Premier blue chip John Keells Holdings PLC reported a sharp rise in earnings attributable to shareholders in the September quarter, with profit more than tripling to Rs.5.17 billion and earnings per share rising to 29 cents from 9 cents.

The performance of JKH in the second quarter of FY2026/27 reflected stronger contributions from transportation, financial services and property, alongside a lower Group tax charge. However, the scale of shareholder profit growth also reflected a change in the composition of earnings, with a reduced contribution from its 50 percent-owned John Keells CG Auto business lowering the share allocated to minority shareholders.

Total Group profit after tax grew 39 percent to Rs.5.82 billion, while profit attributable to equity holders increased 213 percent. Earnings allocated to non-controlling interests declined 74 percent to Rs.653 million.

“The higher flow through to attributable PAT primarily reflects the lower effective contribution from JKCG, in which the Group has an ownership of 50 percent,” Chairperson and CEO Krishan Balendra said in a commentary accompanying the interim financial statements.

The improvement in shareholder returns came despite revenue declining 2 percent to Rs.142.09 billion and Group earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation remaining broadly unchanged at Rs.18.30 billion.

Pretax profit edged up 2 percent to Rs.7.95 billion. Excluding exchange movements, however, it declined by about 5 percent to Rs.7.61 billion.

Transportation was the standout operational contributor, with EBITDA increasing 153 percent to Rs.4.89 billion, driven by bunkering business Lanka Marine Services and Colombo West International Terminal.

Lanka Marine Services recorded a 31 percent increase in volumes and higher margins. Management said demand benefited from supply-chain disruptions associated with the Middle East conflict, while energy price volatility supported margins.

Colombo West International Terminal handled 502,351 twenty-foot equivalent units during the quarter, operating beyond full utilisation of its first-phase capacity. The terminal remains on track for full operations in December 2026, providing a further source of capacity as commissioning progresses.

These gains helped offset a 53 percent decline in retail EBITDA to Rs.4.75 billion. Within retail, the combined contribution from John Keells CG Auto and John Keells Office Automation fell 70 percent to Rs.2.34 billion.

The decline was principally associated with the vehicle business, which faced an exceptionally strong comparative quarter following the resumption of imports and the release of pent-up demand.

Demand was also affected by the temporary surcharge on vehicle duties and tighter vehicle financing limits introduced in May, while a higher proportion of lower-priced models weighed on the revenue mix.

JKCG handed over more than 2,400 vehicles during the quarter and reported approximately 1,000 vehicles scheduled for delivery in the coming months.

At Keells supermarkets, stronger sales translated into only a marginal improvement in earnings as rising costs absorbed much of the benefit.

Supermarket revenue increased 17 percent, with same-store sales growing 13 percent on higher customer footfall and basket values. EBITDA rose just 1 percent to Rs.2.40 billion, reflecting pressure from electricity and distribution costs.

The increase in basket values reflected higher selling prices, particularly across food products, as well as more units purchased per basket. This points to an improvement in purchasing activity, although the earnings outcome shows the challenge of protecting margins as operating costs rise.

“Consumer demand continued to improve, although discretionary spending remained measured across certain segments,” Balendra said.

Consumer foods delivered a stronger earnings increase, with EBITDA rising 17 percent to Rs.2 billion. Carbonated soft drink volumes increased 10 percent and confectionery volumes grew 7 percent, while convenience foods also improved.

Nevertheless, higher input, fuel and electricity costs continued to pressure margins, limiting the extent to which volume growth could flow through to profit.

City of Dreams Sri Lanka recorded a further improvement in operating performance, generating EBITDA of Rs.340 million against a loss of Rs.64 million a year earlier.

The integrated resort’s pretax loss narrowed to Rs.1.19 billion from Rs.3.39 billion. However, the improvement included a Rs.896 million exchange gain, compared with an exchange loss of Rs.523 million in the corresponding quarter.

Depreciation, amortisation and interest expenses totalled Rs.2.43 billion. Interest costs benefited from refinancing and the reduction of the syndicated loan balance to $150 million.

Higher occupancy and average room rates supported hotel earnings, although electricity and other costs associated with scaling up operations affected momentum.

JKH recognised fixed rental income from the casino during the quarter. Variable rental income will become applicable once the relevant performance threshold is reached, making this an additional earnings opportunity dependent on the casino’s operating performance.

Despite the improvement at City of Dreams, overall leisure EBITDA declined 10 percent to Rs.1.28 billion, with weaker contributions from Colombo hotels and Maldivian resorts partly offset by improved profitability at Sri Lankan resorts.

JKH cited disruptions to travel and air connectivity arising from the Middle East conflict. Maldivian resorts also faced lower occupancy and average room rates, alongside higher fuel costs.

Forward bookings across Sri Lanka and the Maldives remained encouraging ahead of the winter season, although continued connectivity through regional transit hubs remains a factor in the outlook.

Financial services EBITDA rose 44 percent to Rs.3.62 billion, supported by a higher contribution from Nations Trust Bank. This included a one-off tax credit arising from the acquisition of HSBC Sri Lanka’s retail banking business, partly offset by integration expenses.

The size of the credit’s contribution was not separately quantified in the chairperson’s review. Its presence means the financial services earnings increase includes a benefit that should be distinguished from recurring business growth.

Property EBITDA rose to Rs.1.11 billion from Rs.196 million, driven by profit recognition from residential and commercial sales at Cinnamon Life and residential sales at TRI-ZEN.

At Group level, a lower tax expense was a major factor behind the increase in after-tax earnings. The charge declined 41 percent to Rs.2.13 billion, compared with Rs.3.60 billion a year earlier.

Currency movements also supported reported pretax profit, with a net exchange gain of Rs.340 million replacing a loss of Rs.245 million. The gain primarily reflected rupee appreciation on foreign currency borrowings at City of Dreams, partly offset by losses on dollar cash holdings at the holding company.

For the first half, Group revenue increased 10 percent to Rs.283.74 billion and EBITDA grew 11 percent to Rs.34.65 billion. Pretax profit rose 10 percent to Rs.12.03 billion, while total profit after tax increased 34 percent to Rs.6.60 billion.

Profit attributable to shareholders reached Rs.5.23 billion, against Rs.849 million a year earlier, with basic earnings per share increasing to 30 cents from 5 cents. The September quarter accounted for almost all first-half attributable profit.

Operating cash flow also strengthened, with a net inflow of Rs.18.83 billion against Rs.2.85 billion in the corresponding six months, helped by lower inventories and a release of funds from receivables.

Financing costs remain significant. First-half finance expenses increased 11 percent to Rs.13.25 billion, while bank overdrafts stood at Rs.61.12 billion at September-end.

The board declared a first interim dividend of 10 cents per share, payable on or before 2 December.
