TPA’s Barath questions JVP’s ‘Indian Expansion’ narrative

May 25 (Daily Mirror) - A rare and politically significant acknowledgement on the historical injustices faced by the Indian Origin Malayaga Tamil community was made in Parliament last week during a special adjournment motion brought by Deputy Leaders of the Tamil Progressive Alliance (TPA), MPs V. Radhakrishnan and Palani Thigambaram.

Speaking during the debate, Minister Bimal Rathnayake openly acknowledged that political decisions, discriminatory state policies and historical political narratives contributed to the suffering of the IOT Malayaha people after independence.

The Vice President of the DPF and the politburo member of the Tamil Progressive Alliance figure Barath Arullsamy described the Minister’s speech as “one of the most honest acknowledgements ever made inside Parliament by a Sinhala mainstream political leader regarding the historical treatment of the Indian Origin Tamil community.”

“For decades, our community carried a painful historical burden that many refused to openly acknowledge,” Arullsamy stated.

He noted that before 1948, all communities in Ceylon were recognised as British subjects. However, after independence, the very people who helped build Sri Lanka’s plantation economy and infrastructure became stateless in the very land they helped develop.

“Our people cleared jungles, built roads, railways, plantations and contributed immensely to the national economy. Yet citizenship itself became a struggle that continued until 2003,” he said.

Arullsamy further highlighted that Minister Rathnayake had made a striking observation in Parliament by pointing out that Tamil communities taken by the British to countries such as Malaysia, Mauritius and Fiji had progressed socially and economically, while those brought to Sri Lanka endured generations of statelessness, disenfranchisement, exclusion and political suppression.

According to Arullsamy, the Minister had also acknowledged that prominent Tamil political leaders as well as sections of the then UNP leadership supported the post-independence citizenship framework that rendered the Indian Origin Tamil community voteless and stateless.

However, Arullsamy noted, somewhat ironically, that while the Minister courageously acknowledged many uncomfortable truths, he stopped just short of addressing another painful chapter: how generations of Malayaga Tamils were politically vilified under the banner of resisting so-called “Indian expansionism.”

“For decades, a poor plantation worker carrying nothing more dangerous than a tea basket was politically portrayed as part of an Indian expansion project,” Arullsamy remarked.

He stated that extremist political propaganda during past decades, particularly narratives aggressively amplified during earlier political periods by the Janatha Vimukthi Peramuna, helped cultivate suspicion and hostility toward the Indian Origin Tamil community.

“While our people were struggling for citizenship, land, education and survival, they were simultaneously treated as a demographic threat. That contradiction is one of the great political ironies of post-independence Sri Lanka,” he added.

Arullsamy nevertheless praised Minister Rathnayake and the present government for allowing such difficult discussions to emerge openly in Parliament, noting that many political leaders in the past lacked the courage to publicly address these realities.

“I have great respect for Minister Bimal Rathnayake for honestly bringing these issues into the national political conversation inside Parliament. Acknowledgement is the beginning of reconciliation,” he said.

Meanwhile Arullsamy added that, Mano Ganesan MP, Leader of the Tamil Progressive Alliance, addressing Parliament and later through a video shared on social media, stressed that the history of the Malayaha Tamil people must never again be erased or politically manipulated.

Arullsamy stated that while acknowledgement was welcome, the next phase must focus on practical reforms including land rights, housing security, economic independence, educational transformation, equal state investment and greater representation for the Indian Origin Malayaga Tamil community.

“History must not divide us again. But history must also never be forgotten,” he said.
