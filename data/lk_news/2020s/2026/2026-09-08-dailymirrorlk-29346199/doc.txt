El Niño may increase human-elephant conflict, Wildlife DG warns

Colombo, Sep. 8 (Daily Mirror) - The ongoing El Niño conditions could increase the risk of wild elephants roaming into human settlements in search of food and water, Director General of Wildlife Ranjan Marasinghe has warned.

Marasinghe said the prevailing weather conditions were having an impact on wildlife and could increase the likelihood of wild elephants moving closer to human settlements.

He urged the public to remain vigilant, particularly when travelling at night, and to exercise greater caution in areas affected by human-elephant conflict.

The Director General also said the effects of El Niño were being felt in marine ecosystems, with the situation raising concerns over the condition of coral reefs.

He said a programme had already been initiated to conserve and restore coral reefs affected by environmental changes.

Meanwhile, several zones of the Yala National Park have been considered for temporary closure due to the prevailing weather conditions, he said.

Marasinghe also commented on recent wild elephant attacks and the reported entry of poachers into the Yala National Park.

He said that statistics compiled so far indicate that the number of elephants killed since January this year is lower than the corresponding figure recorded during the previous year.

The Wildlife Department is continuing to monitor the impact of the prevailing climatic conditions on wildlife and take measures to minimise threats to animals and their habitats.
