Sri Lanka calls for greater global investment in children at Paris Peace Forum

Colombo, Nov. 12 (Daily Mirror) - Minister of Women and Child Affairs Saroja Savithri Paulraj has called for greater investment in children, stressing that their well-being should remain a global priority despite growing financial constraints.

Addressing a special roundtable discussion titled “Making the Case for Investing in Children” at the 9th Paris Peace Forum in Paris, France, the Minister said investments in children were essential responsibilities that could not be postponed.

The Minister represented Sri Lanka at the Forum held on November 10 and 11, where discussions focused on child protection and care, safe digital environments, child health and nutrition, and quality early childhood development.

Addressing the gathering, she said Sri Lanka's vision for children was to create “A Safer World for Children – A Creative Future Generation”, as outlined in the country's National Policy Statement.

She stressed that every child, regardless of where they were born, deserved equal opportunities to survive, grow up healthy, receive an education and realise their full potential.

The Minister outlined three key areas in which Sri Lanka was making investments: ensuring equitable and quality early childhood care and development, strengthening child protection and psychosocial well-being, and ensuring the inclusion of all children while maximising their human potential.

She said investing in children was not merely a moral obligation but also a means of building a stronger economy, reducing the burden of unpaid care work on women and strengthening social cohesion.

She also highlighted the importance of partnerships among international organisations, development partners, the private sector, civil society and communities, noting that governments could not fulfil these responsibilities alone.

Sri Lanka was ready to share its experiences, learn from international best practices and work with international organisations to ensure children's well-being remained a global priority, she added.
