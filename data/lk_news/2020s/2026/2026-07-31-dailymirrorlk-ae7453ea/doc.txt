Uga Resorts launches community elephant coexistence project 2nd phase

New fencing conservation initiative at Uga Ulagalla will protect 65 farming families while helping safeguard Sri Lanka’s elephant population

Uga Resorts, the award-winning Sri Lankan hospitality group renowned for its authentic luxury experiences, has reached another significant conservation milestone with the completion of a second community elephant fence near its five-star Uga Ulagalla resort in Anuradhapura.

Officially opened on July 15, 2026, the five-kilometre electric fence has been developed in partnership with the local community and the Centre for Conservation and Research (CCR).

The initiative will help protect 65 farming families and around 132 acres of agricultural land while supporting peaceful coexistence with Sri Lanka’s endangered elephant population.

Representing a total investment of approximately Rs.4,500,000, Uga Resorts contributed more than Rs.3,175,000, with the local community contributing Rs.1,324,500 towards the project.

Construction began in February 2026 and forms part of Uga Ulagalla’s long-term commitment to reducing human-wildlife conflict through practical conservation initiatives.

Unlike the traditional boundary fencing, the community elephant fences are strategically positioned around villages and cultivated land, helping to prevent elephants from entering the settlements while allowing them to continue moving naturally through their habitat.

Rather than creating an impenetrable physical barrier, the fences act primarily as a psychological deterrent, reducing conflict without causing harm to the elephants and creating a sustainable solution that benefits both the wildlife and local communities.

Commenting on the initiative, Uga Resorts Head Naturalist and Uga Ulagalla Elephant Research Centre Founder Katharina Raaben said, “Living and working in this region every day, we’ve seen first-hand how important practical conservation measures are for both local communities and elephants. This new fence is the result of months of collaboration and demonstrates what can be achieved when resorts, conservationists and villagers work together. It’s about creating safer spaces for people while protecting one of Sri Lanka’s most iconic species for generations to come.”

The new fence builds upon the work of the Elephant Research Centre at Uga Ulagalla, established in 2020 by Raaben in partnership with renowned Sri Lankan elephant conservationists Dr. Prithiviraj Fernando and Dr. Jennifer Pastorini of the CCR.

The centre supports elephant conservation and research while helping to reduce human-elephant conflict in the Anuradhapura region, with its team continuing to monitor elephant movements and share insights with guests through visits to the centre.

Uga’s primary electric fencing project at Pittala Konderama and Karambe villages, where Uga Ulagalla is located, spans 3.4km and has transformed everyday life for the 70 families in these village communities since being installed in January 2023.

Guests staying at Uga Ulagalla can gain a deeper understanding of Sri Lanka’s elephants through visits to the resort’s Elephant Research Centre, where Katharina and her team share insights into Sri Lanka’s elephant conservation efforts.

Uga Resorts owner and Managing Director Priyanjith Weerasooria said, “At Uga Resorts, we believe tourism has a responsibility to create a positive and lasting impact on the destinations where we operate. Supporting initiatives like this community elephant fence allows us to protect local livelihoods while safeguarding one of Sri Lanka’s most iconic species. It’s a powerful example of how conservation, communities and tourism can work together to create meaningful change.”
