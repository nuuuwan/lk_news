Financial literacy rises to 58%: PM

Colombo, Oct. 5 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka’s financial literacy rate has increased from 35% to 58% due to positive measures taken by the Government, Prime Minister Dr. Harini Amarasuriya said today.

She made these remarks while attending the 40th anniversary celebrations of the Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) at the World Trade Center in Colombo as the chief guest.

The Prime Minister said the Government aimed to ensure that knowledge of money and the financial system was not limited to a select group, but was accessible to all citizens.

“Our objective is to empower every citizen with the awareness needed to manage their earnings effectively, save properly, invest with an understanding of risks, and avoid falling victim to fraudulent financial schemes,” she said.

She said the Government was particularly focusing on schoolchildren and was taking steps to introduce “Entrepreneurship and Financial Literacy” as a new subject area for Grades 6 to 9 under the revised school curriculum.

She also highlighted initiatives such as the “Stock Market Game” and school-based capital market clubs, which she said would help children and young people gain practical knowledge of entrepreneurship and investment from an early age.

Amarasuriya said the Government was committed to strengthening the country’s capital market and creating new investment opportunities for the younger generation, moving beyond traditional savings methods.

She urged young people to think differently and create opportunities rather than wait for them.

“Let us foster a culture of sound financial literacy within society, not merely to secure marks in an examination, but to benefit throughout one’s entire life,” she said.

The event also featured the launch of the “Stock Market Game” programme, while a commemorative cover and stamp marking the CSE’s 40th anniversary were unveiled. The Prime Minister also rang the bell to commence market trading.

CSE Chief Executive Officer Rajeeva Bandaranaike and officials led by Securities and Exchange Commission Chairman Senior Professor D.B.P.H. Dissabandara were among those present.
