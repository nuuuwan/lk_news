Sri Lanka to conduct first passenger drone trial at Port City today

Sri Lanka is set to conduct its first official trial of an electric-powered passenger drone service today (November 11) at the Colombo Port City, marking an initial step towards introducing a new mode of air transport to the country.

The trial will use an EHang E216 electric vertical take-off and landing (eVTOL) aircraft, which can carry two passengers without requiring a runway.

The Chinese company EHang, which specialises in passenger drone technology, held discussions with the Ministry of Ports and Civil Aviation in August on the possibility of introducing the service to Sri Lanka.

The meeting was attended by Minister Anura Karunathilaka, Deputy Minister Janitha Ruwan Kodithuwakku, Ministry Secretary W.W.S. Mangala and officials of the Civil Aviation Authority of Sri Lanka.

Discussions focused on the potential use of passenger drones in tourism, passenger transport and emergency services.

The proposed project will initially operate as a trial rather than a commercial service. Testing is expected to continue at the Port City premises for approximately six months.

Several institutions, including the Civil Aviation Authority, Ministry of Defence, Sri Lanka Air Force, Telecommunications Regulatory Commission, Colombo Port City Commission, Sri Lanka Tourism Promotion Authority, Board of Investment and airport authorities, will need to coordinate to implement the project.

The E216 aircraft, recently brought to Sri Lanka for testing, is equipped with 16 propellers, 12 battery packs, three flight control systems and a computer system.

The aircraft is operated remotely from a central control centre. It is also designed to take automated safety measures in the event of an emergency or communication failure.

If successfully introduced, passenger drone services could open up new opportunities for aerial tourism in Sri Lanka, particularly by offering tourists flights over popular destinations such as Sigiriya, Pidurangala and surrounding islands.

The proposed service is expected to create new opportunities in the tourism sector while exploring the potential of electric-powered air transport in the country.
