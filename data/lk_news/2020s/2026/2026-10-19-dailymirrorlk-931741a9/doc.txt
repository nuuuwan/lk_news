CBSL Governor confident of US$ 8.2bn reserves by year-end as buffer against ME, El Niño shocks

Reserves at US$ 6.85bn by end-September, needing US$ 1.35bn build in three months

Sri Lanka’s gross official reserves are expected to climb to around US$ 8.2 billion by year-end on the back of earmarked inflows, creating a buffer against a prolonged Middle East conflict and El Niño-driven drought next year, Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) Governor Dr. P. Nandalal Weerasinghe told CNBC’s Squawk Box Asia on Friday.

“We have earmarked inflows, so we will build up to that level by the end of the year,” he noted, putting current reserves at close to US$ 7 billion.

As per CBSL data, reserves stood at US$ 6.85 billion at end-September, little changed from US$ 6.83 billion at end-2025, meaning the target requires a net addition of around US$ 1.35 billion within three months, a 19.7 percent increase. The IMF’s seventh review would unlock about US$ 345 million, roughly a quarter of that sum, pending Executive Board approval.

If achieved, year-end reserves would be the highest since 2014, when they closed at US$ 8.2 billion. However, the reserve figure includes about US$ 1.4 billion from the swap with the People’s Bank of China (PBOC), which remains subject to conditionalities on usability.

Dr. Weerasinghe expects a small current account deficit this year, ending three straight years of surpluses, as the energy shock lifted the import bill. The trade deficit widened 68.4 percent to US$ 7.18 billion in January to August when compared to the same period last year.

“Building buffers is the only way to protect the economy,” the Governor stressed, pointing to consecutive primary surpluses. The primary surplus rose 28.8 percent year-on-year to Rs.1,644.6 billion in the first eight months of 2026, while the overall budget swung to a Rs.35.4 billion surplus from a Rs.411.0 billion deficit, CBSL data showed.

He maintained that the CBSL retains ample policy space following its proactive 100 basis point hike in May, with CCPI-based inflation at 8.0 percent in September against 1.5 percent a year earlier. “Obviously, we are ready to take any actions if inflation is going to go out of control,” he warned.

Meanwhile, he cautioned that while El Niño has brought wetter weather this year, projections point to drought in 2027 that could hit agriculture and hydropower generation.

“That’s where we are building buffers in terms of renewable energy, solar,” he explained.

Hydropower supplied 49.8 percent of electricity generated from October 12 to 15, against 15.7 percent from solar, as per CBSL data.
