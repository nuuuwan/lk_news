Public service shrinks, but salary bill keeps rising

Colombo, Nov. 27 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka's public service lost more than 23,000 employees in the year to June 2026, and vacancies rose to 236,967, yet the state's salary bill kept climbing, official figures show.

According to the Department of Management Services' cadre reports for 2025 and 2026, the public service employed 893,797 people as at June 30, 2026, compared with 917,137 a year earlier. During the same period, the approved cadre increased from 1,117,713 to 1,130,764. As a result, vacancies rose by 36,391 in a single year, and the share of approved posts that were filled declined from 82 percent to 79 percent.

Although the public service has shrunk, the cost of employing it has continued to rise. The Treasury's Budget Economic and Fiscal Position Report 2027 shows that spending on the salaries and wages of public servants, including Provincial Council employees, increased by 8.7 percent to Rs. 827 billion in the first eight months of 2026, from Rs. 760.7 billion a year earlier. The Treasury attributed the increase to the second phase of the public sector salary revision, which took effect in January. Pension payments rose by 17.5 percent to Rs. 332.4 billion over the same period, and the Treasury's 2027 pension estimates are based on 762,570 pensioners.

Around two-thirds of the increase in vacancies stemmed from the net loss of employees. The remaining third came from new posts added to the approved cadre.

The figures contrast with the government's recruitment announcements. Presenting Budget 2027, the President told Parliament that approval had been granted for recruitment to a total of 148,940 vacancies. In July, Public Administration Minister Prof. Chandana Abeyratne said more than 120,000 people would be recruited to the public service this year. However, the Treasury's report states that its salary estimates assume the number of public sector employees will not increase considerably through new recruitment in 2027.

The Treasury bases those estimates on a public sector workforce of approximately 1.3 million employees. That figure is well above the 1,028,861 recorded in the cadre report, which includes state-owned enterprises but excludes the uniformed cadre of the tri-forces.

The losses were concentrated in the lower grades. Employees in the secondary and primary grades, which include clerical staff, teachers and minor employees, fell by 26,723, whereas the senior grade expanded by 2,912. As a result, the number of frontline employees for every senior officer fell from almost 20 a year ago to fewer than 18.

All nine Provincial Councils recorded a drop in staff, with the Western Province losing the most at 2,989 employees. The Northern Provincial Council remains the most understaffed. It had 31,509 employees against 41,433 approved posts, leaving almost one in four posts vacant, and its primary grade was only 63 percent filled. The Western Provincial Council has the largest number of vacancies at 19,284. North Central recorded the sharpest deterioration, as its share of filled posts fell from 88 percent to 82 percent. The Southern Provincial Council remains the most fully staffed, at 94 percent of its approved cadre.

Several essential services also have fewer staff than a year ago. The Police had 73,875 uniformed officers against 102,112 approved posts, 856 fewer than in 2025, even though the Cabinet said in March that the bulk of the 15,839 public sector vacancies it had approved would be absorbed by the defence and public security sectors. National schools lost 1,208 employees and the Samurdhi Department 1,448. The Sri Lanka Ports Authority's staff fell by 441 in a year when Colombo port faced congestion.

The Commission to Investigate Allegations of Bribery or Corruption (CIABOC) had 223 employees against an approved cadre of 972, down from 238 a year earlier, and had filled only four of its 463 tertiary-level posts. This is despite the Treasury's report committing to strengthen the "institutional capacity and independence" of the commission under the National Anti-Corruption Action Plan 2025-2029. The Attorney General's Department had 218 senior officers against 548 approved posts.

While several key institutions operate well below their approved strength, others employ more people than they are permitted. According to the 2026 cadre report, 15 state institutions, excluding the Rural Development Ministry, together employ 973 people above their approved cadre.

The over-staffing is not confined to state firms. The Ministry of Transport, Highways and Urban Development had 531 employees against 457 approved posts, and the Court of Appeal had 139 against 132.

Lanka Sathosa had 608 more employees than approved posts in 2025. Its approved cadre has since been increased by 1,238 to 4,246.

However, two other sets of figures remain unexplained. The Judicial Service Commission's staff fell from 9,251 to 5,256, even though its approved cadre rose from 11,820 to 15,058, and its primary-grade staff declined from 4,002 to 557. The Presidential Secretariat had 602 employees against 602 approved posts in 2025 and now has an approved cadre of 1,130, but this year's report does not give a staff figure. These two institutions account for about 4,600 of the overall decline. Even if both are excluded, the public service still lost around 18,700 employees.

At the same time, newly created regulatory bodies remain without personnel. The Gambling Regulatory Authority shows no reported staff despite having 23 approved positions, even as the Treasury's report highlights plans to bolster its operation in the upcoming year. Similarly, the Data Protection Authority left all 41 of its positions vacant in 2025, with the latest report providing no updated figure.
