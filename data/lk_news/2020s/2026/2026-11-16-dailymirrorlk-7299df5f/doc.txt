Hayleys becomes first listed company to post US$ 1bn half-year revenue

Hayleys PLC has become the first Sri Lankan listed company to cross US$ 1 billion in revenue in a six-month period, with consolidated revenue from continuing operations for the half year ended September 30, 2026 rising 33 percent to Rs.358.01 billion and profit before tax (PBT) up 49 percent to Rs.21.31 billion, the group said.

The achievement comes eight years after Hayleys became the first listed Sri Lankan company to surpass US$ 1 billion in annual turnover, in the 2017/18 financial year. Half-year revenue was Rs.268.96 billion in the same period last year. At the Central Bank’s end-September rate of Rs.330.83 to the dollar, this year’s figure is roughly US$ 1.08 billion, marking what the group called a significant milestone in its growth trajectory.

The performance was supported by significant contributions from the key export-oriented sectors, as well as the transportation and logistics and consumer and retail sectors. The group attributed this to the strength of its diversified portfolio and its ability to capitalise on opportunities across domestic and international markets.

The consumer and retail sector, the group’s largest, grew revenue 37 percent to Rs.95.45 billion, while transportation and logistics revenue climbed 53 percent to Rs.74.80 billion; together, these two made up close to 48 percent of group revenue.

Transportation and logistics also more than doubled its segment result to Rs.7.24 billion from Rs.3.09 billion. Among the export-oriented businesses, purification products lifted its result 91 percent to Rs.4.61 billion and hand protection 49 percent to Rs.3.25 billion.

However, revenue in the agriculture and plantations sectors slipped 5 percent and 4 percent respectively, with the plantations segment result falling 19 percent to Rs.572.05 million.

According to the group, the domestic operating environment remained broadly resilient on the back of continued economic recovery, although rising inflation and cost pressures presented challenges. Globally, geopolitical uncertainties, commodity price volatility and evolving trade dynamics continued to influence business conditions. Against this backdrop, the group’s diversified portfolio, its focus on operational efficiencies and its strategic investments supported its ability to navigate the evolving operating environment.

Consolidated EBITDA increased by approximately 46 percent to Rs.39.13 billion. Despite a 64 percent rise in net finance cost to Rs.9.24 billion, PBT grew 49 percent, while profit after tax (PAT) from continuing operations increased 60 percent to Rs.13.63 billion. Finance costs alone climbed 89 percent to Rs.16.18 billion. Over the same period, short-term interest-bearing borrowings rose to Rs.190.05 billion at end-September from Rs.152.86 billion at end-March.

Profit attributable to shareholders of the parent rose 93 percent to Rs.7.53 billion from Rs.3.90 billion. Earnings per share improved to Rs.9.55 from Rs.5.19, even though the share base grew to 795 million shares after the Rs.9 billion rights issue completed in April.

In the July to September quarter alone, revenue grew 29 percent to Rs.178.69 billion, PBT rose 40 percent to Rs.11.17 billion and profit attributable to shareholders increased 66 percent to Rs.3.96 billion when compared to the same quarter last year.

Investment also stepped up during the period, with spending on the purchase and construction of property, plant and equipment rising more than fourfold to Rs.43.64 billion from Rs.10.38 billion a year earlier.

Meanwhile, the group completed the transfer of the head lease and operating assets of its Maldives resort operation, Luxury Resort (Pvt) Ltd, in June. The loss from discontinued operations narrowed to Rs.415.89 million from Rs.964.45 million.
