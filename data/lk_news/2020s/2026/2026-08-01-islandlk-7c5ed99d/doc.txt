OPA Facilitates Brainstorming Session on Harnessing Digital Transformation for Inclusive Development

The Organisation of Professional Associations of Sri Lanka (OPA), under the patronage of Mr. Jayantha Gallehewa, President of OPA, through its National Issues Committee (NIC), convened a high-level brainstorming session on “Digitalization for Societal Upliftment” as part of its continued commitment to advancing national dialogue on digital transformation. The initiative was spearheaded by Mr. Bhanu Wijayaratne, Vice President of OPA and Chairman of the National Issues Committee.

The session served as a multidisciplinary platform bringing together government officials, academics, technology specialists, and professionals to deliberate on the strategic role of digital transformation in advancing Sri Lanka’s socio-economic development agenda.

Recognizing that digitalization has evolved beyond the realm of technology into a key enabler of national progress, the discussion explored how digital solutions can strengthen governance systems, enhance public service delivery, foster innovation, promote social inclusion, and create sustainable opportunities for citizens.

In his address, the President of OPA emphasized that Sri Lanka’s digital transformation journey requires a collective and forward-looking approach driven by professional expertise, collaborative partnerships, and citizen-focused solutions. He highlighted OPA’s continued commitment to facilitating constructive dialogue on national priorities by bringing together professionals from diverse disciplines to contribute knowledge, insights, and practical recommendations.

Speaking at the session, Mr. Bhanu Wijayaratne, Vice President of OPA and Chairman of the National Issues Committee, emphasized that Sri Lanka’s long-term economic growth and global competitiveness will increasingly depend on the successful digital transformation of the country. He noted that digitalization is no longer merely a technological advancement but a strategic enabler of economic resilience, productivity, innovation, and inclusive development.

The session featured an eminent panel of experts representing the Government, academia, industry, and the technology sector, including Mr. Waruna Sri Dhanapala, Secretary, Ministry of Digital Economy; Mr. Sumudu Rathnayaka, Adviser, Ministry of Digital Economy; Dr. Shanta Rajapaksha Yapa, Director and Senior Academic Fellow, SLIIT Business School; Mr. Dulip Somiratna, Senior Assistant Secretary to the President; Mr. Thilak Pathirage, Chairman, Sri Lanka Computer Emergency Readiness Team (SLCERT); Mr. Sanjaya Karunasena, Executive Director and Acting CEO, GovTech Sri Lanka; Mr. Indika De Zoysa, Strategic Adviser and Vice President, Huawei Technologies Lanka (Pvt) Ltd and Dr. Aparrajitha Ariyadasa.

Experts at the session highlighted the urgent need for Sri Lanka to accelerate digital adoption and strengthen citizen-focused digital services to achieve the vision of Society 5.0. They emphasized that while Sri Lanka has made considerable progress in developing digital infrastructure, introducing technology solutions, and encouraging innovation, the utilization and adoption of these digital facilities remain comparatively low when compared with several countries in the region.

The experts noted that the future of digital transformation should not be measured only by the availability of technology but by how effectively citizens are able to access and benefit from digital services. They stressed that technology must move beyond infrastructure development and become a practical tool to improve the daily lives of people.

Highlighting the importance of citizen-centered digital platforms, the experts pointed out that Sri Lanka has the opportunity to expand digital services across key areas, including government services and public administration; healthcare and medical services; education and skills development; public transportation; financial services; and citizen complaint and support mechanisms.

They observed that many countries have successfully used integrated digital platforms to provide faster, more transparent, and accessible services to citizens. However, Sri Lanka still faces challenges in ensuring widespread public adoption and effective utilization of available digital facilities.

The session was moderated by Mr. Heshan Karunaratne, President of the Computer Society of Sri Lanka (CSSL) and Mr. Udesh I W Seneviratna, Secretary, Computer Society of Sri Lanka (CSSL) and Member of the National Issues Committee (NIC) of OPA who guided the deliberations towards identifying opportunities, challenges, and strategic pathways for advancing digital transformation in Sri Lanka. The proceedings were compered by Mr. B.A.H.S. Preena, NIC Committee Member.

The event was attended by Mr. Tisara De Silva, President-Elect of OPA, Eng. Ravi Rupasinghe, General Secretary, Dr. Sunil Abeyaratne, Vice President of OPA and Chairman of the Law and Order and OPA Constitutional Amendments Committee, Past Presidents, members of the Executive Council, representatives of the General Forum and professionals representing a wide range of disciplines.
