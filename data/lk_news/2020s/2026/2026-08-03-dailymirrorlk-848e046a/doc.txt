Communist Party urges President to act on Indian trawler issue affecting Jaffna fishermen

Colombo, Aug. 3 (Daily Mirror) - The Communist Party of Sri Lanka (CPSL) has urged President Anura Kumara Dissanayake to take immediate steps to address the longstanding issue of Indian fishing trawlers entering Sri Lankan waters and affecting the livelihoods of fishermen in the Jaffna District.

In a letter to the President, CPSL General Secretary Dr. G. Weerasinghe said the issue was raised during discussions held with fishing community leaders in Jaffna during the party’s 83rd anniversary celebrations.

The party said Indian high-powered trawlers equipped with bottom-trawling technology were entering Sri Lanka’s Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) and engaging in destructive fishing practices, causing serious damage to marine resources and local fishing activities.

According to the letter, the arrival of large numbers of such vessels, their powerful engines and iron hulls had created difficulties for traditional fishermen operating wooden boats. It said fishing nets belonging to local communities were frequently damaged or destroyed as a result.

The CPSL warned that the problem was not limited to economic losses faced by fishermen but also posed a threat to the sustainability of marine ecosystems.

“These destructive and illegal fishing practices are damaging the biological activities of the seabed and could lead to the depletion of future fish stocks,” the party said.

It said the fishing industry remained one of the main livelihoods in the Northern Province and that communities dependent on it were facing increasing hardships due to unregulated fishing activities.

The party also raised concerns over possible links between illegal maritime activities and the movement of narcotics and other illicit goods through unregulated vessels.

The CPSL proposed the establishment of legal agreements and regulatory mechanisms between Sri Lanka and India to prevent illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing practices, particularly bottom trawling.

It also called for discussions between the two countries based on the principles of the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations’ Code of Conduct for Responsible Fisheries adopted in 1995.

The party stressed the need for fishing communities from both countries to be included in negotiations and monitoring mechanisms established by the two governments.

It further proposed the use of satellite-based vessel monitoring systems to regulate fishing activities and sought technical assistance from international organisations to protect marine resources.

The CPSL said it was willing to assist efforts aimed at finding a sustainable solution to the fisheries dispute between Sri Lanka and India.
