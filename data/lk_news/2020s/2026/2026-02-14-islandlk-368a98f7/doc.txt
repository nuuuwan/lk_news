HSBC Ceylon Literary and Arts Festival Opens with Call to Inspire a New Generation

The third edition of the HSBC Ceylon Literary and Arts Festival opened on the banks of Beira Lake in Colombo (Cinnamon Lakeside) on Friday with a strong call to harness the power of arts and literature to inspire Sri Lanka’s next generation.

Delivering the welcome address, Festival Director and Co-founder Ajai Vir Singh said the event was conceptualised three years ago at a time when the country was grappling with multiple crises and social strain.

He added:”Sri Lanka has gone through a tremendously tough time over the last five years,” he said. “This festival was created to provide a platform for mainstream art, literature, music and film — to give voice to our creatives and to encourage youth engagement at a time when it was most needed.”

Singh noted that the festival aimed to bring communities back together through cultural exchange, while also enhancing Sri Lanka’s global cultural footprint.

He thanked title sponsor HSBC and partners including the Indian High Commission, the Swiss Embassy, the British Council and other cultural institutions for supporting the initiative.

The festival featured more than 45 authors, writers and personalities, alongside art exhibitions, film screenings and a jazz performance by an international ensemble from Milan. An art exhibition by the John Keells Foundation is also on display at the venue.

Chief Executive Officer of HSBC Sri Lanka, Mark Surgenor, addressing the gathering, said the festival had grown significantly since its inception.

“This is the third edition, bigger than before, with more authors and more countries represented,” he said. “The more voices we bring together, the richer and more interesting our conversations become.”

Surgenor emphasised that beyond celebrating the arts, the festival was also about nurturing future talent. This year, 300 complimentary tickets were distributed to schoolchildren, continuing a tradition established at the inaugural event.

He recounted the journey of a young participant who attended the first edition as a student, later entered and won the Future Writers Competition, received mentorship and subsequently authored a book that won a literary prize.

“That is what truly matters,” Surgenor said. “It’s not just about what we do today, but about inspiring future generations to contribute and to love literature and the arts as much as we do.”

HSBC also showcased its parallel community initiatives, including spoken English programmes in hill country communities and a Female Entrepreneurs Programme supporting women-led small businesses. Ten entrepreneurs are exhibiting their products at the festival site.

Highlighting environmental concerns, Surgenor drew attention to efforts to clean Beira Lake, where two waste-collection boats remove nearly 200 kilogrammes of debris daily as part of a broader awareness campaign.

The opening ceremony concluded with a traditional Sri Lankan dance performance by the Saranda Dance Academy, underscoring the festival’s commitment to celebrating local cultural heritage alongside international voices.

Organisers expect around 500 visitors each day, as the festival continues with panel discussions on history, food, sport, fiction, health and contemporary issues, reaffirming Colombo’s place on the region’s cultural calendar.
