Extreme weather events: advanced alert system not yet in place

Colombo, Aug. 24 (Daily Mirror) - In the wake of frequency of seismic jolts in the Indonesian region with tsunami warnings and other forms of extreme weather events, Sri Lanka is yet to activate an automatic mobile alert system for warnings on all kinds of disasters such as Cyclone Ditwah, Daily Mirror learns.

An earthquake of magnitude 7.4 struck in the Northern Molucca Sea off Indonesia's Ternate island recently damaging some buildings and triggering tsunami waves, authorities and witnesses said. The United States Geological Survey reported aftershocks of magnitude as high as 5, and Indonesia's meteorology agency BMKG reported tsunami waves in West Halmahera at 0.3m high and Bitung at 0.2m high.

Earthquakes in the region trigger tsunami threats to Sri Lanka. In 2004, Sri Lanka suffered a massive loss of lives and damages to property and infrastructure as tsunami waves struck the coastal areas.

Sri Lanka's Department of Meteorology is in touch round the clock with the official tsunami warning centres for Australia, India, and Indonesia to get updates on threats in case of any quake in the region.

The Disaster Management Centre (DMC) has also activated an alert system.

Climatologist Malith Fernando told Daily Mirror that the Department gets warnings since the system is in place round the clock, and therefore there was nothing to be concerned as such.

He said that the activation of an advanced automatic mobile phone alert system with the support of service providers for people in the coastal areas should be considered.

Sri Lanka is currently in discussion with India to get bilateral assistance in this regard. India has launched a nationwide mobile-based emergency alert system that delivers real-time, geo-targeted disaster warnings instantly to mobile phones across all 36 states and union territories.
