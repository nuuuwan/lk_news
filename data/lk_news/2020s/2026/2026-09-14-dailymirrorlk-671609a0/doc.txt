Foreign reserves climb to five-month high of US$ 6.9bn on record Central Bank dollar purchases

Sri Lanka’s gross official reserves rose to a provisional US$ 6,905 million by end-August 2026, the highest level in five months, as the Central Bank purchased a record volume of dollars from the domestic market amid strong remittance inflows.

The Central Bank’s latest data showed that the stock increased by US$ 305 million or 4.6 percent from US$ 6,600 million at end-July, marking the strongest reading since end-March. The figure includes proceeds from the People’s Bank of China swap arrangement, which the Central Bank has noted remains subject to conditionalities on usability. Reserves nevertheless remain US$ 365 million below the February peak of US$ 7,270 million, when the buffer crossed the US$ 7 billion mark for the first time in five and a half years.

Foreign currency reserves accounted for US$ 6,690 million of the August total, while gold holdings were valued at US$ 210 million, up from US$ 192 million in July.

The stock covers about 3.3 months of imports, measured against average monthly merchandise imports of US$ 2,092 million during the first seven months of the year.

The month’s accumulation was driven largely by intervention and remittances. The Central Bank purchased US$ 579 million from the domestic foreign exchange market in August without recording any sales, compared with US$ 348.6 million in July and US$ 356 million in August 2025. Workers’ remittances amounted to US$ 748.6 million during the month against US$ 680.8 million a year earlier, taking the eight month total to US$ 6,131 million, a rise of 19.8 percent. The erosion of the reserve position since February has been driven mainly by the fuel bill, as expenditure on fuel imports reached approximately US$ 3,168 million in the first half of the year, an increase of 58.8 percent, after the conflict in the Middle East pushed crude prices sharply higher. Total imports expanded 25.8 percent to US$ 14,643.2 million in the seven months to July while exports grew 4.4 percent to US$ 8,136.4 million, widening the merchandise trade deficit to US$ 6,506.9 million from US$ 3,850.1 million a year earlier.

The rupee has depreciated 5.7 percent against the US dollar so far this year, averaging Rs. 328.56 in the week ending 11 September compared with Rs. 302.02 a year earlier.

The pace of accumulation recorded in August may prove difficult to sustain, as crude prices resumed climbing during the week ending 11 September, with Brent and West Texas Intermediate both ending the period above US$ 100 per barrel after gaining US$ 12.00 and US$ 10.77 respectively over the week. The increases followed attacks on Saudi energy facilities and the precautionary shutdown of the kingdom’s East-West pipeline, which serves as its main bypass route to the Red Sea.
