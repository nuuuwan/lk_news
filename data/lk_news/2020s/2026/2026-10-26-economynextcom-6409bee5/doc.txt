Fitch assigns Asia Asset Finance’s Rs3bn debenture issue ‘A-(EXP)(lka)’

Fitch Ratings – Singapore/Colombo – 26 Oct 2026: Fitch Ratings has assigned Asia Asset Finance PLC’s (AAF, A+(lka)/Stable) proposed Sri Lankan rupee-denominated subordinated listed debentures of up to LKR3 billion an expected National Long-Term Rating of ‘A-(EXP)(lka)’.

The proposed debentures are likely to have a five-year maturity and will be listed on the Colombo Stock Exchange. The company plans to use the proceeds to strengthen its Tier 2 capital base and support loan-book expansion.

The final rating is subject to the receipt of final documentation conforming to information already received.

The proposed debentures are rated two notches below AAF’s National Long-Term Rating. We applied the Bank Rating Criteria in rating this instrument, as we believe the prudential capital framework for Sri Lankan finance companies is closer to that for banks.

The expected rating reflects our baseline notching for loss severity for this debt class and expectation of poor recoveries in the event of default. There is no additional notching for non-performance risk, as the proposed debentures do not contain going-concern loss-absorption features.

AAF’s National Long-Term Rating reflects our expectation that its majority shareholder, India-based Muthoot Finance Ltd (MFL, BB+/Stable), would provide extraordinary support to AAF if needed. This view is based on MFL’s 78.4% shareholding, record of ordinary equity support and the strategic and operational alignment between the parent and subsidiary in their core product – gold-backed loans.

Even so, we assess AAF’s strategic importance to MFL as limited, given the subsidiary’s small size, modest role and contribution, limited brand sharing and operations in a different jurisdiction. AAF’s standalone credit profile does not drive the rating, but we assess it as significantly weaker than its support-driven rating.

See our commentary Fitch Affirms Asia Asset Finance at ‘A+(lka)’; Outlook Stable for details.

Factors that Could, Individually or Collectively, Lead to Negative Rating Action/Downgrade

A downgrade of AAF’s National Long-Term Rating would lead to a downgrade of the expected subordinated debt rating.

Factors that Could, Individually or Collectively, Lead to Positive Rating Action/Upgrade

An upgrade of AAF’s National Long-Term Rating would lead to an upgrade of the expected subordinated debt rating.
