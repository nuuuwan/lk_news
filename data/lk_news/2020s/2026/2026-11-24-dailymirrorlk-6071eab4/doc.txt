Il Poya Day today marks end of Kathina season

Colombo, November 24 (Daily Mirror) - The Il Full Moon Poya Day, which marks the end of the annual Kathina robe offering season, is being observed today (24) by Buddhists across the country with religious activities at temples.

The day is particularly significant as it marks the conclusion of the period during which devotees offer Kathina robes to Buddhist monks who have completed the three-month Vas retreat.

During the Vas season, Buddhist monks traditionally remain in their temples, devoting themselves to meditation, religious observances and the study of the Dhamma. The Kathina ceremony is held after the conclusion of this retreat, allowing devotees to express their gratitude and support for the Sangha.

The Il Full Moon Poya Day also commemorates several important events in Buddhist history.

According to Buddhist tradition, it was on a Poya day such as this that the Buddha dispatched the first 60 Arahants to spread the Dhamma for the benefit of the world. This marked an important milestone in the early propagation of Buddhism.

The day is also associated with the Buddha's visit to Uruvela to subdue the three Jatila brothers, who were ascetics practising religious austerities.

Another significant event commemorated on this day is the Bodhisattva Maitreya receiving the Niyatha Vivarana, the definite assurance of his future attainment of Buddhahood.

The Parinirvana, or final passing away, of Arahant Sariputta, the Buddha's Chief Disciple, is also believed to have taken place on an Il Full Moon Poya Day.

Furthermore, the day is associated with Pavāraṇa, the formal conclusion of the Vas retreat, during which Buddhist monks invite one another to point out any shortcomings in their conduct during the retreat.
