Hambantota solar projects: Allegations of bribery and environmental destruction –– Sajeewa Chamikara speaks out

Environmental activist Sajeewa Chamikara has alleged that several solar power projects in the Hambantota District have resumed operations following the payment of bribes, resulting in extensive destruction within a designated wildlife management reserve.

Chamikara, representing the Movement for Land and Agricultural Reform (MONLAR), said that projects previously halted by the Hambantota District Development Committee have now recommenced after what he described as irregular financial transactions amounting to Rs. 14 million.

According to him, five companies had jointly provided the funds to facilitate the construction of an electric fence spanning approximately 12 km from Nagarawawa to Mayurapura and Usagala within the Hambantota Wildlife Management Reserve.

He alleged that around Rs. 11 million had been channelled for the fencing work, while the remaining Rs. 3 million had been distributed in a manner that has not been properly disclosed.

He alleged that after providing the funds, these companies resumed clearing forest lands within the wildlife reserve to construct solar power facilities.

“Nearly 1,000 acres of forest land within the elephant management reserve have been cleared using heavy machinery and in some instances by setting fires,” Chamikara charged. “This has caused severe and irreversible environmental damage in areas where elephants and other wildlife live.”

He further claimed that the projects had initially been suspended following a decision taken at the District Development Committee meeting held on January 29. However, the projects were later recommenced without the required fresh approval or review by the committee.

Chamikara alleged that the payment of funds for the electric fence had effectively paved the way for the projects to move forward once again. “It is clear that these projects were restarted after money was provided for the electric fence. This amounts to a bribe,” he said.

He also questioned whether the required environmental approvals had been properly obtained under the National Environmental Act. Citing provisions under Section 23B of the Act, he noted that large-scale development projects and power generation facilities exceeding the stipulated capacity must undergo a formal Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) process and secure prior written approval.

“Any development exceeding one hectare or power projects above the prescribed capacity must obtain clearance following a proper environmental impact assessment,” he said. “Carrying out such projects without adhering to these legal requirements is a violation of the law.”

Chamikara further alleged that the resumption of the projects had taken place without subjecting them to a renewed environmental review, despite the earlier suspension decision.

He warned that the continued destruction of forest areas within wildlife management zones would aggravate human-elephant conflict and undermine conservation efforts in the Hambantota District.

“The elephant management reserve exists to protect wildlife and maintain ecological balance. Clearing these lands for commercial gain not only violates environmental law but also threatens biodiversity and local livelihoods,” he stressed.

Chamikara called for a transparent investigation into the alleged financial transactions and the environmental damage caused within the reserve. He urged authorities to ensure strict enforcement of environmental regulations and to hold accountable those responsible for any illegal activities.

“The law is clear. Environmental protection cannot be bypassed for private interests,” he said. “What is at stake here is not just forest land, but the integrity of our environmental governance.”
