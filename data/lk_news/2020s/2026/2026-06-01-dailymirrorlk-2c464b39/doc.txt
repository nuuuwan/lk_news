Businessman files bribery complaint over alleged Rs. 3.2 mn Ports Authority fraud

Colombo, June 1 (Daily Mirror) - A businessman from Boralesgamuwa has lodged a complaint with the Commission to Investigate Allegations of Bribery or Corruption seeking an investigation into an alleged fraud involving more than Rs. 3.2 million connected to officials of the Sri Lanka Ports Authority.

According to the complaint, the businessman alleged that he was misled into believing that a tender for the supply of goods had been awarded to the Ports Authority and subsequently paid large sums of money to several individuals connected to the institution.

The complainant, identified as Damseth Dineth Amaratunga, claimed that the alleged fraud had taken place within the office of the Managing Director of the Ports Authority on Darley Road, Colombo, with the involvement of an officer attached to that office.

He further alleged that the Managing Director of the Ports Authority had refused to meet him when he attempted to raise concerns regarding the incident.

According to the complaint, Amaratunga said that an officer attached to the Managing Director’s office had introduced him to the Managing Director’s secretary and that he had handed over Rs. 600,000 on three separate occasions at the office premises.

He also claimed that he transferred Rs. 1,697,600 to the personal bank account of a female Ports Authority officer on six occasions and paid a further Rs. 368,000 to the Ports Finance Division.

The complaint further said that attempts to seek redress from port officials had been unsuccessful as the individuals involved allegedly avoided him.

Amaratunga also claimed that, through the intervention of another senior port official, the matter was brought to the attention of Deputy Minister of Ports Janitha Kodituwakku, who had instructed him to lodge a complaint with the Criminal Investigation Department.

The complaint said that several officials, including the Managing Director of the Ports Authority, had allegedly been present during the meeting with the Deputy Minister.

The businessman further alleged that the CID, the Mirihana Financial Crimes Division, the Fort Police, the Peliyagoda Financial Crimes Division and the Central Crimes Investigation Division had all refused to accept his complaint.

He has therefore requested the Bribery Commission to conduct a full investigation into all individuals allegedly connected to the incident, including officials attached to the Managing Director’s office, the Supply Division and the Finance Division of the Ports Authority.

Copies of the complaint have also been forwarded to the President, the Minister of Ports and Aviation Services, the Chairman of the Ports Authority and seven other parties, the complaint stated.
