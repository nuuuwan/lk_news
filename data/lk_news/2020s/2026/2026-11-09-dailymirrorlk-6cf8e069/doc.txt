Stray animals are innocent souls that deserve love understanding and compassion : Dr. Janey Lowes

Sarnie wrapped in a towel after being rescued from the street

Dr. Janey enjoying her run in Point Pedro Images courtesy of WECare Worldwide

Dr. Janey Lowes, British veterinarian and founder of WECare Worldwide completed an extraordinary 621km run across the island titled ‘Dog Jog’. The goal is to raise £1.6 million to build a fully furbished animal hospital with modern facilities. Dr. Lowes from Barnard Castle, County Durham gave up her life in UK to care for injured street animals in Sri Lanka. In 2014, she founded WeCare Worldwide, a UK charity with the support from Westway Veterinary Group to provide veterinary care for the estimated three million street dogs in Sri Lanka as well as many street cats. During her jog, Dr. Lowes rescued several injured street dogs including Sarnie and Petal which made her realise the importance of this initiative.  In a candid interview with the Daily Mirror, Dr. Lowes shared her experience jogging across a country like Sri Lanka, her encounter with Sarnie, the goal of her fundraising campaign, activities carried out by the WECare Animal Hospital and why people shouldn’t frown at stray animals. Excerpts :

Q How challenging or fun has it been to jog around a tropical country like Sri Lanka ?

I hadn’t run since school (and I’m now 38!) before embarking on my Dog Jog training. So starting my running ‘career’ in extreme heat and humidity was a shock to the system! It didn’t help that I don’t enjoy running, so I had to really push myself to get going some days. I also injured my knee six weeks before starting the Dog Jog, which was really worrying as I was petrified I wouldn’t be able to finish (or start…!). Running more than a half marathon every day for 22 days was really tough. There were days where it felt like my body was giving up, but I knew I had to plough on. However, on the flip side, getting to see parts of the country that I’d never visited before was absolutely incredible- the hardest part of running Devil’s Staircase was actually stopping to take pictures every few minutes because the scenery was so beautiful. This island really does take your breath away and I feel so lucky to live here.

I found Sarnie by chance on day five of my Dog Jog (October 13) as I had to change my route at the last minute due to reports of elephant attacks in the area. She didn’t move as my support tuk drove past her, which was a huge red flag, and that’s when I realised she was paralysed and couldn’t use her back legs. She was super skinny and I knew if we left her there, she would end up starving to death by the side of the road. So we asked around if anyone knew her (they didn’t) and took her with us. We were really far from the hospital at this stage. So we snuck Sarnie into our hotels for a few nights before managing to get her down to the WECare hospital for treatment. The X-rays showed that she had an old spinal fracture and an old femoral fracture as well as issues with her bladder.

She’s been at WECare for a few weeks now and has had treatment for a urinary infection, as well as physiotherapy to try to get her walking again. Her fractures are old, which makes things incredibly difficult, but she’s shown improvement in her mobility. Her future remains uncertain, but we’re doing everything we possibly can for her, and we remain hopeful!

Q Your mission is to treat the many street dogs in Sri Lanka. But challenges are aplenty. What is the ultimate goal you plan to achieve through the Dog Jog?

The goal was to raise money to start building a new WECare street dog hospital, and raise awareness about why it’s so desperately needed. We need £1.5 million (LKR 671,270,850) to build our new street dog hospital, rehabilitation area, and veterinary training centre, which is a huge amount of money. So I knew I needed to do something crazy that would hopefully catch people’s attention. There are so many amazing animal lovers in the world and I truly believe that we can achieve this dream together.

I still can’t quite believe that I managed to complete this run. While I’m so proud of myself and incredibly thankful for all the amazing people who supported me, we still have a long way to go to reach our £1.5 million (LKR 671,270,850) fundraising goal. We’ve raised £260,000 (LKR 116,353,614) so far, which is incredible. But we need a lot more. So please continue to share, donate and support to give these street animals the hospital they not only need, but deserve.

Q Could you share any other unforgettable incident(s) along the way, other than Sarnie’s incident?

On day 15 (October 24) in the Hill Country, we had a bit of a fright only an hour into the day’s run when an electricity pylon almost fell on me!! A bus drove past and hit the power line, pulling the pylon over right in front of me; it was pretty scary how close we were to disaster! Thankfully, everybody was ok, but we were a bit shaken.

Two days before the end of my run (October 30) we came across Petal, a seriously ill puppy who’d been hit by a car two weeks prior, so we transferred her down to the WECare hospital. Petal is now recovering after emergency surgery to repair a life-threatening diaphragmatic hernia (the impact of the accident had pushed her abdominal organs into her chest cavity!). The team stayed all night on Friday to look after her as she crashed after surgery- she’s now doing so much better, but isn’t out of the woods yet. We’re doing everything we can to get her back on her feet!

Our current hospital is in Gandara in the south of Sri Lanka and is the only fully-equipped vet hospital on the island that’s dedicated to street dogs (and cats!). We have 27 permanent kennels for inpatients (plus many pop-up kennels), X-ray, ultrasound machines, an in-house laboratory, isolation facilities (for potentially infectious animals), and a surgical suite.

We’ve treated 6,156 animals at the hospital since it was built in 2016, and currently have 30 inpatients (and 11 recovered dogs living in our garden who are awaiting adoption. Some have been waiting for years as people are often reluctant to adopt a dog with three legs). I’m so grateful to have this hospital, but it’s in an old rented building that’s falling apart and costing us a lot of money in ongoing repairs. Our hospital is constantly full and we have a huge waitlist of seriously ill/injured animals– it breaks our hearts knowing that we could help more of them if we only had a better facility.

The new hospital will have a rehabilitation area for recovering animals. This will free up kennels in the main hospital, allowing us to treat an extra 3,000 patients per year, which will have an incredible impact on the street animal community. It will also have a veterinary training centre that will offer free courses for Sri Lankan vets, as well as Sri Lanka’s first-ever veterinary nursing qualification. I’m really excited about this as it’s our way of extending our impact around the island, and ensuring community animals receive a fantastic level of care wherever they are in the country.

We also have an incredible CNVR (Catch-Neuter-Vaccinate-Release) outreach programme, with two neutering and vaccination lorries out in the community five days a week in Matara District. To date, we’ve neutered 27,114 dogs and vaccinated 41,000 against rabies. CNVR is the only sustainable way of managing the street dog population and keeping the current dogs happy and healthy.

I understand why people might be wary of street dogs and cats because rabies is  a very real risk here. But they’re just innocent souls trying to get through life the same way we are, and they deserve love, understanding, and compassion. One of WECare’s areas of focus is rabies vaccination as vaccinating dogs is the only humane and sustainable way of eliminating the virus on the island.  We’ve vaccinated 41,000 animals against rabies since 2014. We also educate local communities about rabies prevention, to help people live more harmoniously with street animals.

Dr. Janey extended a request to the animal loving community to support her endeavour in whichever way possible to build a hospital that these street animals deserve.
