Drug shortage puts cancer patients’ lives at stake

Colombo, Aug. 18 (Daily Mirror) - Cancer patients receiving treatment at Apeksha Hospital in Maharagama have appealed to the authorities to urgently address shortages of essential medicines, warning that delays in treatment could put their lives at risk.

In a letter addressed to the President, Prime Minister, Health Minister and health authorities, the patients said there was a serious shortage of Neupogen, a medicine needed by patients undergoing chemotherapy.

They said the shortage was affecting their ability to receive chemotherapy on time and could lead to their health conditions worsening.

According to the patients, a Neupogen injection purchased privately can cost more than Rs. 20,000, while some patients require several doses. They said many cancer patients already face difficulties meeting the costs of food, transport and other basic needs, making it difficult to buy the medicine privately.

The patients also said other medicines needed for chemotherapy were sometimes unavailable at the hospital.

They claimed that when limited stocks arrived, they were not enough to meet the needs of all patients, leaving some without the medicines required for their treatment.

Furthermore, the patients said the shortage was adding to the financial and emotional difficulties they already face while undergoing cancer treatment, particularly amid rising living costs.

They warned that delays in treatment could worsen their conditions and pose a serious threat to their lives.

Several patients also expressed concern about their young children and families, saying their children’s future depended on their survival.

As such, the patients urged the authorities to take immediate action to ensure a continuous supply of essential cancer medicines at Apeksha Hospital.

“What we ask for is not merely sympathy, but the protection of our right to live,” they said.
