Let Our Roadsides Breathe

Every year, the Government and local authorities spend public money painting and maintaining electricity and telephone poles, walls and other structures along our roads. Yet, soon after they are cleaned and painted, they are covered with handbills, posters and advertising banners.

Private tuition classes, property agents, pawn brokers and even some financial institutions are among those responsible. The result is particularly visible in urban areas, where once-clean roadsides quickly become an eyesore.

There is, however, a simple solution. Municipal Councils, Urban Councils and other local authorities should provide designated public advertising boards where individuals and businesses can legally display handbills, posters and banners.

At the same time, regulations should be introduced, or existing laws strictly enforced, to prohibit unauthorised advertising on electricity poles, telephone poles, walls and other public property. Offenders should be fined. Enforcement should not be difficult, as most advertisements carry contact details, making it relatively easy to identify those responsible.

Making Sri Lanka clean and beautiful does not always require expensive projects. A little discipline, proper regulation and effective enforcement can make a significant difference.

Clean roadsides are the responsibility of both the authorities and the public. Let us keep our towns and cities clean, green and beautiful.
