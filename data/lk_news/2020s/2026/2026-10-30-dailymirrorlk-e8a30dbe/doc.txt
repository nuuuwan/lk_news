LAUGFS consumers gasping for gas for months

Colombo, October 30 (Daily Mirror) - For nearly three months, customers have faced difficulties securing LAUGFS Gas cylinders, leaving households frustrated and placing additional pressure on distributors and dealers struggling to meet demand.

The shortage has raised concerns about the reliability of domestic gas supplies, particularly among families that depend on liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) for their daily cooking needs. Consumers who have traditionally relied on LAUGFS Gas are now uncertain about when regular supplies will resume.

The Consumer Affairs Authority (CAA) is considering legal action against LAUGFS Gas PLC over the prolonged disruption, according to an official familiar with the matter.

The official said the company had been repeatedly informed of the need to ensure an adequate supply of gas cylinders before legal action was considered.

However, the company had responded on several occasions that shipments were on their way but had been delayed, the official said.

The continuing shortage has also affected the wider domestic gas market, forcing consumers to seek alternatives and placing additional pressure on the country's other major LPG supplier, Litro Gas Lanka Ltd.

Cabinet Spokesman and Health Minister Dr Nalinda Jayatissa said the LAUGFS Gas shortage had affected the market since September, pushing daily demand for Litro Gas from approximately 1,100 metric tonnes to 1,600 metric tonnes.

He said a shipment carrying 3,000 metric tonnes of Litro Gas had arrived in Sri Lanka, with unloading operations underway. Another vessel carrying 3,900 metric tonnes was expected to arrive by October 31.

Litro Gas Lanka Ltd has increased its daily distribution to between 90,000 and 100,000 cylinders to address the shortage and meet rising demand.

The company normally distributes around 74,000 cylinders a day. The increase represents an expansion of approximately 22% to 35% in daily distribution as it attempts to accommodate consumers affected by supply disruptions.

The developments highlight the pressure on the country's LPG distribution network, with a disruption affecting one supplier resulting in a significant increase in demand for the other.

The shortage has raised questions about the impact of prolonged supply disruptions at LAUGFS Gas, which has a significant presence in Sri Lanka's energy market.

According to company figures, LAUGFS Gas accounts for approximately 30% of the domestic and commercial LPG market and around 60% of the industrial bulk LPG market. The company also holds an estimated 85% share of the vehicle autogas retail market.

Its cylinder-filling facility uses an automated process and has storage capacity for large quantities of empty and filled cylinders. The plant is designed to fill approximately 30,000 cylinders of different sizes a day.

Against this backdrop, consumers are questioning how a company with an established distribution network and a substantial market presence has been unable to maintain regular supplies of domestic gas cylinders.

The reasons for the prolonged disruption, including the status of pending shipments and the expected timeline for restoring normal distribution, remain key concerns for affected consumers.

Meanwhile, the Government has said it is increasing the supply of Litro Gas cylinders to meet rising demand. However, a LAUGFS Gas consumer pointed out that customers using LAUGFS cylinders cannot simply switch to Litro Gas, leaving them affected by the shortage despite the increase in Litro's distribution.
