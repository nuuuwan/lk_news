Rising inflation and narrowing buffers demand policy action now: IMF

ECONOMYNEXT – Global economic growth is expected to slow to 3.1 percent in 2026 before rebounding to 3.4 percent in 2027, with inflation rising amid geopolitical disruptions, the International Monetary Fund has said.

The IMF said the global economy has remained resilient, helped by a technology-sector upturn, policy buffers and adjustments by businesses and households to geopolitical shocks.

Global headline inflation is projected to rise to 4.7 percent in 2026 before easing to 4.1 percent in 2027, while energy-importing economies outside global technology supply chains face the weakest outlook.

However, the IMF warned that the factors supporting resilience are not unlimited and that risks remain tilted to the downside.

An escalation of conflicts, renewed trade tensions, fiscal vulnerabilities or a reassessment of expectations around artificial intelligence could weaken growth, it said.

Stronger AI investment, productivity gains from new technologies and a sustained easing of geopolitical and trade tensions could provide upside to the outlook.

The IMF also called for stronger fiscal frameworks, saying governments should rely on targeted support for vulnerable groups during cost-of-living crises while preserving market price signals and rebuilding fiscal space through revenue measures. (Colombo/Oct27/2026)
