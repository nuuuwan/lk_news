FBI team in Sri Lanka to help probe scam centres

A team from the US Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) is in Sri Lanka this week to help local law enforcement officials investigate scam centres operating in the country, the US Embassy in Colombo said.

The team, from the FBI’s Charlotte Field Office, is in Colombo from 16 to 22 November at the request of Sri Lankan authorities.

The FBI has set up a dedicated team to support ongoing investigations, provide specialised technical expertise and strengthen the ability of US and Sri Lankan authorities to identify and disrupt transnational fraud networks.

During the visit, FBI personnel will work with Sri Lankan law enforcement on investigative techniques, digital forensics and case development. The Embassy said this would help investigators identify victims, trace criminal activity and build stronger cases against those responsible for online scams. It would also strengthen Sri Lanka’s ability to prevent transnational criminal networks from using its territory for cyber-enabled crime.

“Criminal networks operating overseas are stealing billions of dollars and we are committed to stopping them,” US Ambassador to Sri Lanka Eric Meyer said.

“By bringing FBI investigative expertise and advanced technology together with Sri Lankan law enforcement, we can identify victims, pursue criminals and prevent more Americans and Sri Lankans from falling prey to these schemes,” he added.

The Embassy said the visit marks the beginning of sustained FBI engagement with Sri Lankan authorities. The initiative is supported by the US Department of State, working in coordination with the FBI.

“The United States remains committed to pursuing transnational criminal networks wherever they operate, protecting American citizens from financial exploitation and working with international law enforcement partners to hold perpetrators accountable,” the Embassy said.
