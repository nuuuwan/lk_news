Police told to stop playing hide and seek when checking for traffic offences

Colombo, August 14 (Daily Mirror) - When a vehicle needs to be stopped for a traffic offence, police officers must carry out the interception openly and professionally at a location visible to other road users and should not hide in isolated locations or behind bushes while attempting to stop motorists, DIG in charge of the Traffic and Road Safety Range, W.P.J. Senadheera said.

The DIG also reminded traffic officers that departmental circulars and guidelines clearly set out how roadside duties should be performed. Officers should not arbitrarily stop vehicles or conduct traffic operations in a manner that could create suspicion of soliciting money from motorists.

He said vehicles requiring interception should be stopped in a transparent manner, such as at a bus stop or another suitable location where the process is visible to the public. Officers may also pursue a vehicle where necessary, provided the police identity and purpose of the interception are clearly visible.

Senadheera further reminded motorists that they are not required to make payments directly to police officers. Where a temporary driving permit is issued, fines should be paid through legally authorised channels, including post offices where applicable.

He warned that roadside payments made outside the approved procedure could amount to bribery or other offences, exposing those involved to legal proceedings.

The DIG urged motorists not to offer money to police officers and stressed that no police officer has the right to demand or accept a bribe under any circumstances.

His remarks came following the circulation of a video on social media allegedly showing a police officer accepting a bribe, with the incident reportedly linked to the Highway Unit in Dambulla.

DIG Senadheera said investigations into the incident are being conducted by the relevant senior police officers.

Pending the outcome of the investigations, the officer involved has been removed from traffic duties and transferred to another police station. Further disciplinary and legal action is also being pursued, particularly under provisions of the Bribery Act.

Senadheera said that the Police Department maintains zero tolerance towards bribery and corruption and that officers found engaging in such conduct would face strict departmental, legal and anti-corruption action.

He said the conduct of a small number of officers should not undermine the work of the majority of traffic police personnel who carry out their duties honestly and diligently.
