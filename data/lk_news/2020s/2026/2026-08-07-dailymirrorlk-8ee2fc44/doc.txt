10,000 schools to be linked online under digital transformation plan

Colombo, Aug. 7 (Daily Mirror) - The government plans to connect all 10,000 schools across Sri Lanka to the internet through fibre, 4G, 5G or satellite technology as part of an ambitious digital transformation drive aimed at modernising the education sector, Additional Secretary to the Prime Minister A.B.M. Ashraf said.

Addressing a ceremony where 50 Lenovo laptops were distributed to outstanding but underprivileged G.C.E. Advanced Level students, Ashraf said the initiative forms part of a national plan to bridge the digital divide and ensure equal access to technology in schools.

He said the Government had appointed a Task Force on Digital Transformation in Education last year and had since been working with the private sector, academia and professional associations to develop a comprehensive digital education strategy.

"As we are living in a digital world, without digitalising and connecting our schools, we risk becoming digitally illiterate," he said.

Ashraf said fibre connectivity would be provided wherever commercially feasible, while schools in remote areas would be connected through 4G, 5G or satellite technology.

He added that the Government plans to upgrade school computer laboratories and local area networks while ensuring that every secondary school without a computer lab receives one next year.

According to him, more than 1,500 schools, mainly in remote and underserved areas, still lack computer laboratories, and these schools will be prioritised under the programme.

Ashraf said the Government had already fulfilled its commitment to provide at least one digital classroom in every one of the country's approximately 6,200 secondary schools, eliminating disparities where around 2,500 schools previously lacked even a single digital board.

He also noted that more than 500 secondary schools which had no functioning computers were equipped with new systems last year, while around 100 divisional education offices also received functional computers.

The Additional Secretary said all divisional, zonal and provincial education offices have now been connected under the digital network, while an Education Management Information System (EMIS) has already been introduced.

He said teacher human resource management systems are currently being deployed in all nine provinces, enabling online management of transfers, leave and personnel records.

Ashraf added that a Student Information System is expected to be introduced by the end of this year, allowing school attendance to be recorded online from the middle of next year.

He further revealed that the Government has launched a pilot Learning Management System (LMS) for students from Grades 6 to 13. Around 1,000 schools are expected to use the system by the end of this year, with all 6,000 secondary schools expected to adopt it by the end of next year.

Highlighting the Government's commitment to education despite economic challenges, Ashraf said Sri Lanka has continued to invest in human development even during periods of financial hardship.

He said the country had never regretted investing in education and health, stressing that the Government had increased investment in education despite the economic crisis because the country's future depends on its younger generation.

Ashraf also commended Technocity, Laptop.lk and Lenovo for supporting the Government's digital education initiatives, including restoring computer laboratories in schools and donating laptops to high-achieving students from underprivileged backgrounds. He urged the students to use the laptops to become innovative, productive and patriotic citizens who would contribute to the country's future.
