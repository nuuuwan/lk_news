Filing of court cases on the decline in Sri Lanka: MP

Colombo, Nov. 27 (Daily Mirror) - Filing of court cases in Sri Lanka is on a declining trend, an opposition MP told Parliament yesterday.

“Filing of court cases is on the declining trend as people seem to think it is not worth spending their resources, " Opposition MP Chithral Fernando revealed yesterday during the committee stage debate on the budget.

“People seemed to have lost faith in the judiciary. It seems that the people have completely lost faith in submitting fundamental rights applications when looking at statistics during the last three hours. As many as 350 human rights applications have been submitted to the judiciary in 2024 while 226 have been submitted in 2025. However, only 89 human rights applications have been filed this year,” the MP said.

“The government might turn this situation to its advantage and will claim that the backlog of court cases is on the decline,” he added.

Also he said the expenditure progress of the Justice Ministry is low just like other ministries. He said the Justice Ministry has spent only 24 percent of the funds allocated for construction purposes.

The MP then referred to a remark that had been made by Justice Minister Harshana Nanayakkara who said lawyers are using courthouses to earn money. “The Judiciary is not established for the lawyers to earn money but there are other important purposes such as meting out justice to people.

However, the minister accepted that the capital expenditure of his ministry has been low this year.
