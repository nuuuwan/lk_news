West Asian crisis lands cash strapped SriLankan Airlines in further turbulence

Colombo, November 6 (Daily Mirror)- The West Asian crisis, coupled with the resulting surge in fuel prices, has placed the cash-strapped national carrier, SriLankan Airlines (SLA), under further strain, with the authorities compiling a report on financial losses caused by the conflict alone, an official said.

The company recorded a loss before tax of Rs. 35.6 billion during the first quarter of the 2026/27 financial year, according to the mid-year financial report submitted by the Finance Ministry.

According to the unaudited management accounts submitted by the company, total revenue during the period amounted to Rs. 82.8 billion, compared to Rs. 62.6 billion in the corresponding period of 2025/26.

During the period under review, the escalation of the conflict in the Middle East disrupted air connectivity, with SriLankan Airlines suspending flights to several destinations in the region and subsequently resuming services as airspace restrictions were eased.

The disruption posed a significant risk to passenger and cargo revenue, particularly given the importance of Middle Eastern destinations to the airline's network. At the same time, the temporary diversion and rerouting of regional air traffic to Sri Lanka generated some additional aviation activity and passenger movements, partially mitigating the adverse impact on the sector.

Despite the increase in revenue, the company continued to incur losses due to its operating deficit, high fuel and maintenance costs, foreign exchange losses and finance costs. These factors continued to constrain its ability to generate sufficient internal cash flows to meet operational and financial obligations.

However, owing to an improved net income of Rs. 3.4 billion from SriLankan Catering, the net loss recorded by the group was reduced to Rs. 32.2 billion.

The company's total assets amounted to Rs. 209.9 billion as at June 2026, while its liabilities stood at Rs. 561.4 billion. It continued to experience a serious erosion of capital during the period under review.

During the first six months of 2026, the Government provided financial support to service the first and second instalments of selected Bank of Ceylon and People's Bank loans under the relevant tripartite arrangements. In addition, US$30 million was released to meet the airline's short-term funding requirements.

Further, following the restructuring of the US$175 million guaranteed bonds, the restructured bond value of US$126.5 million was absorbed into the Government's debt stock with effect from March 20, 2026.

In June 2026, the Cabinet approved the establishment of a Strategy and Restructuring Committee to undertake a comprehensive review of SriLankan Airlines, with the objective of strengthening its financial sustainability.

Asked for comment, SriLankan Airlines Chief Executive Officer Vipul Misra said the West Asian crisis had also resulted in reduced availability of aviation fuel.

“When crude oil prices increase, refineries prioritise turning out more and more petrol and diesel. There is less focus on aviation fuel. When aviation fuel availability is reduced, it creates further price hikes, affecting the industry. It is not merely the rise in crude oil prices that has an impact,” he said.

He said an assessment of the losses arising solely from the Middle East crisis had been compiled, and his office was awaiting its vetting.
