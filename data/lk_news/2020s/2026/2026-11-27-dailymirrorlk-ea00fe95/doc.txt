FIFA hit by cyberattack as hackers gain access to personal and business data

ESPN - FIFA has been hit by a criminal cyberattack in which hackers gained access to personal and business information.

Sources told ESPN the hack accounted for less than 0.1% of data held by FIFA and the documents taken did not include anything related to the controversial and ultimately doomed FIFA Forward Enterprise proposal.

The breach was not a result of systems or technology failure on FIFA's part, sources told ESPN, adding that hackers gained access by impersonating staff.

A FIFA spokesperson told ESPN: "FIFA can confirm that it has been the victim of a criminal cyberattack resulting in unauthorised access to a limited set of personal and business information. FIFA is working closely with the relevant authorities, including law enforcement.

"FIFA condemns in the strongest possible terms the unlawful acquisition and publication of information, particularly personal data. Such actions constitute a serious and unacceptable violation of privacy and can have significant consequences for the individuals concerned."

Sources told ESPN that the attack targeted FIFA as an organization rather than specific individuals.

A group called Handsome Boy Hacking Club appeared to claim responsibility for the attack after publishing screenshots of what appeared to be a FIFA computer desktop on its X account.
