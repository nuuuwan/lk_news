The new currency of leadership

As the world becomes more complex, interconnected and uncertain, the qualities required of leaders will continue to evolve. The future belongs to leaders who combine meaningful contribution through purposefulness with meaningful connection through empathy. Such leaders recognise that success is not only about what we achieve, but also about how we influence the lives of others. They understand that flourishing emerges when contribution and connection reinforce one another

Disengagement costs the global economy $8.9 trillion a year, equal to 9 percent of the world’s GDP. People are connected through technology around the clock, yet loneliness and isolation are part of everyday life for millions. Organisations have sophisticated tools for productivity and growth, yet many people are still searching for meaning in their work and lives.

Modern leadership often emphasises one of two priorities. Some leaders focus primarily on achieving goals, driving performance and delivering results. Others place greater emphasis on understanding people, creating engagement and building relationships. Both are important. Yet neither is sufficient on its own. Human flourishing requires both meaningful contribution and meaningful connection.

It is this shared conviction that has brought together Purposeful Leadership and Empathy Everywhere. While emerging from different traditions and experiences, both recognise that flourishing individuals, organisations and societies are built when people are able to contribute meaningfully and connect meaningfully.

Purpose helps us contribute meaningfully. Empathy helps us connect meaningfully. Flourishing requires both.

Many leadership models begin with targets, objectives and desired outcomes. While goals are important, they are not the deepest source of motivation or meaning. Purposeful Leadership begins by asking a more fundamental set of questions: Why does this goal matter? What contribution does it make? How does it support the flourishing of life?

A sales target, market expansion or strategic initiative may be impressive in itself. Yet its true significance lies in the value it creates for people and society. When leaders understand the deeper purpose behind goals, they often find greater commitment, resilience and clarity in pursuing them.

Likewise, many leaders believe they understand people because they understand job descriptions, qualifications, personality profiles or demographic characteristics. While such information has value, it only provides a partial picture of the human experience.

Empathy Everywhere invites leaders to look past labels and categories and step into people’s reality: their hopes, fears, aspirations, challenges and sense of belonging. Behind every performance indicator and organisational chart sits a person with a story.

Knowing someone is not the same as understanding them, and understanding people begins with listening to them. The challenge for leaders today is to understand two things well: the purpose of their work and the people they serve.

Purposefulness is often misunderstood as finding a single, fixed purpose that remains unchanged throughout life. My own experience and doctoral research suggest something different.

Purposefulness is not about having all the answers. It is an ongoing process of seeking a clearer sense of purpose, acting on our convictions, reflecting on our experiences, gaining new insights and refining our actions. Each cycle teaches us something about ourselves, others and the world around us. As we act, we learn. As we learn, our understanding deepens. As our understanding deepens, our purpose becomes clearer and our actions more purposeful. Purposefulness is therefore not a destination, but a continuous journey of clearer purpose, purposeful action, reflection, insight and growth.

Within the Purposeful Leadership framework, flourishing is viewed through nine interconnected spheres of life: spiritual, emotional, relational, intellectual, physical, financial, temporal, social and ecological. Purposefulness encourages individuals to consider how their actions contribute not only to personal success but also to flourishing across these broader dimensions of life.

The pursuit of purpose is therefore not a destination. It is a way of living. It is an intentional commitment to making meaningful contributions while remaining open to learning, growth and refinement throughout life's journey.

When people find meaning in their work and connect their personal purpose to their organisation's purpose, performance follows. Employees who feel connected to purpose are far more engaged (50 percent versus 9 percent), far less likely to experience burnout (13 percent versus 38 percent), and significantly less likely to be seeking another job (41 percent versus 68 percent) than those who do not. Organisations that strengthen employees' connection to purpose by just 10 percent also see a 4.4 percent increase in profitability. Having worked with thousands of leaders over three decades, Ranjan believes purposefulness helps people stay longer, work smarter, contribute more and grow faster.

Mimi’s experience echoes this. Her purpose found her through connection. As she writes in her upcoming book The Connection Prescription (Out 2027): “Start with empathy again and again, and your purpose will find you.”

At its simplest, empathy is the ability to recognise and respond to the reality or viewpoint of another. It is the skill of understanding the experiences of the people we live and work with. Neuroscience tells us that empathy is a skill set we are born with, not a personality trait reserved for a few. Far from being a soft attribute, empathy is a measurable human skill with measurable organisational outcomes. Like any skill, it can be practised, strengthened and applied to create measurable results."

Listening is the backbone of all empathy, and curiosity is the backbone of all listening. Empathy asks us to set our assumptions aside, ask questions and stay curious about how another person experiences their work and their world. If we don’t ask, how will we ever know?

When people are heard and understood, performance follows. They are 48 percent more innovative, 64 percent more engaged and 17 percent more efficient, and they drive 23 percent higher profits. Having worked with over twenty thousand people, Mimi believes listening alone may solve up to 80 percent of the disconnection issues in the workplace.

This is the survival of the connected. Leaders who listen are leaders of longevity.

Human flourishing can emerge through many pathways. Purposefulness creates enormous value by helping people find meaning, direction and contribution, leading to higher engagement, lower burnout, stronger retention and higher profitability. Empathy creates enormous value by helping people build understanding, belonging and connection, leading to higher engagement, greater innovation, improved efficiency and higher profitability. Although they travel different paths, both demonstrate the powerful relationship between human flourishing and organisational success.

What is particularly interesting is that the two approaches often lead to remarkably similar outcomes through different mechanisms:

Purpose helps people understand why their work matters. Empathy helps people feel that they matter.

Purpose creates alignment between personal aspirations and organisational contribution. Empathy creates connection between individual experiences and collective success.

Together they offer an even richer perspective on flourishing. Purposefulness encourages us to ask, “How can I contribute meaningfully?” Empathy encourages us to ask, “How can I connect meaningfully?” Both move people beyond self-interest towards a greater concern for others, and both can be consciously developed throughout life. In many ways, each contains elements of the other. Purposefulness naturally encourages consideration of others, while empathy naturally inspires contribution to others. Whether pursued independently or in combination, both help individuals, organisations and societies flourish. When meaningful contribution and meaningful connection reinforce one another, that potential becomes even greater.

While purposefulness provides meaningful direction and energy, empathy enhances the quality and relevance of that direction and energy. Empathy begins with ourselves. Self-empathy helps us understand our own needs, motives and limits, and our interactions with others often reveal what we might otherwise overlook. Empathy helps us understand human needs accurately. It allows us to anticipate the consequences of our decisions. It keeps us grounded in reality. Most importantly, it helps ensure our contributions benefit the people we seek to serve.

A purposeful individual may ask, “What contribution should I make?” Empathy encourages an equally important question: “How is that contribution experienced by others?” Mimi’s first question to leaders is always the same: Have you actually asked your team?

Ranjan’s doctoral research identified four recurring complexities associated with purposeful living and leadership. Empathy has the potential to help individuals navigate each of these complexities more effectively.

Cognitive complexity arises because decisions about purpose rarely involve a single perspective. Listening to a range of voices broadens understanding and brings competing interpretations of reality into view.

Psychosocial complexity emerges because purpose unfolds within relationships and communities. Listening creates environments where people are valued enough to be heard, and relationships grow healthier as a result.

Practical complexity reflects the challenge of translating purpose into everyday realities. Empathy helps us understand what people can realistically embrace and sustain within organisational and social constraints.

Contextual complexity arises because people operate within diverse cultures, systems and environments. Empathy encourages sensitivity to those differences and reduces the likelihood of imposing one-size-fits-all solutions.

An empathetic approach enriches purposefulness. It keeps purpose grounded in people’s reality and responsive to their lives.

Empathy allows us to understand people. Mimi teaches acknowledgement over action: listen first, recognise what you have heard, and then decide how to respond. Stop. Recognise. Progress. Understanding alone does not automatically create positive impact, and this is where purposefulness guides what comes next.

It encourages us to ask: What should we do with what we have understood? How can understanding lead to positive change? How can connection contribute to flourishing?

Purposefulness transforms empathy from understanding into action. It helps leaders convert insight into action and compassion into meaningful outcomes.

Empathy helps us recognise what matters to people. Purposefulness helps us decide how to respond in ways that generate value and contribute to flourishing.

A purposeful approach enriches empathy. It helps empathy become more impactful.

The leadership challenges of the future may require a deeper integration of purposefulness and empathy than has traditionally been recognised.

Purposefulness asks: How should I conduct myself for the flourishing of life?

Empathy asks: Whom am I serving and how do they experience the world?

Together these questions help leaders understand themselves and the people around them. They encourage leaders to contribute meaningfully and connect authentically. They support flourishing organisations where performance and humanity grow together.

Such leaders are not only capable of achieving results. They are capable of building environments where people can grow, belong and contribute. They strengthen organisations while also strengthening communities and society.

Perhaps the most powerful insight emerging from this partnership is that empathy helps purposefulness become wiser, while purposefulness helps empathy become more impactful.

Together they create the conditions for meaningful contribution, meaningful connection and human flourishing.

The integration of purposefulness and empathy is not merely a philosophy; it is a set of practices that leaders can cultivate every day. Here are a few practical ways to strengthen both meaningful contribution and meaningful connection.

The Purpose Conversation: Take time to understand what matters most to the people you lead. What strengths do they most enjoy using to create value? What contribution do they hope to make? What gives them a sense of meaning?

Ask Why, Then Ask Who: Before making an important decision, ask: Why does this matter? Then ask: Who will be affected, and how will they experience it?

Ask Before Deciding: Before making an important decision, ask your people. Listen not only for their opinions, but also for what gives them meaning, energy and purpose.

Ask Those Who Matter Most: When you revisit an important decision, invite the people affected to help shape its refinement.

Purpose becomes more meaningful when those impacted by it have a voice in it.

The Double Ask: When someone tells you what they enjoy doing, ask again.

The first answer is often about what people do. The second answer often reveals the why: the motivations, values, contribution - the seeds of purpose itself.

The Acknowledgement Pause: Before responding, pause and reflect back what you heard to ensure you have understood correctly.

Be Fully Present: Put the phone down and give people your attention. Listening requires authenticity.

As Ranjan often reminds leaders, meaningful change rarely comes from a single insight. It comes from persistent practice, reflection, learning and refinement over time. And as Mimi teaches leaders around the world, “practice creates permanence.” Together, these principles remind us that flourishing leadership is built one conversation, one action and one practice at a time.

As the world becomes more complex, interconnected and uncertain, the qualities required of leaders will continue to evolve.

The future belongs to leaders who combine meaningful contribution through purposefulness with meaningful connection through empathy.

Such leaders recognise that success is not only about what we achieve, but also about how we influence the lives of others. They understand that flourishing emerges when contribution and connection reinforce one another.

When purposefulness guides our contribution, and empathy shapes our connection, we move closer to what every person, organisation and society seeks: the flourishing of life. Start with empathy, and purpose will follow.
