Ministry directs CAA to intensifies action against overpriced blood tests at private hospitals

Colombo, July 30 (Daily Mirror) - While expressing concern over reports that some private hospitals are charging patients above the government-approved prices for Full Blood Count (FBC) tests, the Ministry of Health has directed relevant authorities to intensify inspections and raids on private healthcare institutions.

This happens against the backdrop, where the country continues to grapple with a surge in dengue cases.

Sources said the Ministry is concerned that patients suspected of having dengue, who often require repeated FBC tests to monitor their platelet counts and overall condition, could face an unnecessary financial burden if hospitals fail to adhere to the regulated prices.

In response, the Ministry has requested the Consumer Affairs Authority (CAA) and other relevant authorities to intensify inspections and raids on private healthcare institutions to ensure compliance with the government-controlled pricing, the sources added.

The move follows the recent conviction of a private hospital in Vavuniya for overcharging patients for an FBC test.

According to the CAA, the violation was detected during a raid conducted by its investigation officers, who found that the hospital had charged patients more than the government-approved price for the laboratory test.

Following the investigation, the Authority instituted legal proceedings before the Vavuniya Magistrate’s Court, which subsequently imposed a fine of Rs. 500,000 on the hospital for violating the government-controlled price regulations.

the CAA said that under Order No. 67 issued in terms of Section 20(5) of the Consumer Affairs Authority Act, No. 09 of 2003, the maximum charge for a Full Blood Count (FBC) test, including the report, is Rs. 400, while the maximum charge for a Dengue NS1 Antigen test, including the report, is Rs. 1,200.
