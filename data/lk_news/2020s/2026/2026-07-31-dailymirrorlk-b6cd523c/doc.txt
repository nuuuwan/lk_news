El Niño threat grows as Sri Lanka braces for climate risks

Colombo, July 31 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka must prepare for possible severe weather impacts from the developing El Niño climate phenomenon, which could trigger extreme weather conditions globally, a senior climate expert has warned.

Speaking to media, Senior Professor Buddhi Marambe of the University of Peradeniya said the current El Niño event developing in the Pacific Ocean is presently at a weak level but is expected to intensify in the coming months.

El Niño is a natural climate pattern caused by the abnormal warming of surface waters in the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean. It can disrupt normal weather patterns worldwide, often leading to droughts in some regions, excessive rainfall and flooding in others, as well as impacts on agriculture, water resources and ecosystems.

Professor Marambe said several parts of Sri Lanka are already experiencing severe dry weather conditions, with farmers reporting that water sources have dried up, affecting cultivation lands.

Meanwhile, Disaster Management Centre (DMC) Director General Major General (Retd.) Sampath Kotuwegoda said authorities are preparing plans to address possible disasters arising from El Niño-related climate changes.

He made these remarks while responding to questions before the Committee on Public Finance regarding Sri Lanka’s preparedness to face potential disaster situations.

During the committee meeting, it was also revealed that the Disaster Management Fund, established under an Act of Parliament, has not yet been fully operationalized.

Meanwhile, Environment Minister Dr. Dammika Patabendi said Sri Lanka could experience significant weather changes in the coming months as the El Niño phenomenon continues to strengthen, with its peak expected earlier than previously forecast.
