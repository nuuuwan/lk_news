14-year sentence for monk convicted of abusing novice monk upheld

Colombo, Nov. 18 (Daily Mirror) - The Court of Appeal yesterday upheld the 14-year rigorous imprisonment sentence imposed on a Buddhist monk convicted of sexually abusing a child who had been ordained as a novice monk at a temple in Ampara.

A two-judge Bench comprising Justices P. Kumararatnam and Pradeep Hettiarachchi held that there were no grounds to interfere with the conviction and sentence imposed by the Ampara High Court, observing that the accused had abused his position of trust and authority over the child to gain access to him and perpetrate the offences.

The accused had been convicted of one count of grave sexual abuse under Section 365B(2)(b) of the Penal Code. The victim was approximately 11 years old at the time the offence was committed. The accused was sentenced to 14 years’ rigorous imprisonment, fined Rs.40,000 and ordered to pay Rs.500,000 as compensation to the victim.

According to the victim’s evidence, he had visited the Temple with his parents on a Poya day, where the accused, who was the chief incumbent, had inquired whether any of the children wished to enter the Buddhist priesthood. Following his mother's initial reluctance, the victim was eventually permitted to be ordained and stayed at the temple with the accused and another monk.

The victim testified that the accused had subjected him to various forms of sexual abuse over approximately ten months.

The incident came to light following a complaint made by a police officer who was on duty at the temple. The Judicial Medical Officer attached to the Ampara District Hospital, who examined the victim, had found positive reflex anal dilation, which was suggestive of chronic anal penetration.

In dismissing the appeal, Justice Kumararatnam emphasised that the credibility of child witnesses must be carefully assessed, while their evidence should not be judged by applying standards that disregard the particular characteristics of childhood.

The Court referred to several judicial decisions from England, India and Canada concerning the assessment of evidence given by child witnesses, observing that a child's age alone does not determine his or her ability to provide truthful and accurate testimony.
