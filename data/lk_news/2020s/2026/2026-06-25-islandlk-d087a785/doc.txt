Institutional investors, falling oil prices help boost stock trading

The CSE yesterday picked up with some significant participation by institutional investors. Factors that helped in the process were impressive export earnings and falling world oil prices, market analysts said.

Amid those developments both indices moved upwards. The All Share Price Index was up by 198.46 points, while the S and P SL20 rose by 73.9 points.

Turnover stood at Rs 2.73 billion with seven crossings. Those crossings were : Commercial Bank crossed 219,680 shares to the tune of Rs 46 million, its shares traded at Rs 209, Singer Sri Lanka 250,000 shares crossed to the tune of Rs 40 million; its shares traded at Rs 77, HNB (Non-Voting) 100,000 shares crossed for Rs 33.7 million; its shares sold at Rs 337, CIC Holdings 750,000 shares crossed tfor Rs 24 million; its share s sold at Rs 32.50.

Tokyo Cement (Non-Voting) 360,000 shares crossed for Rs 24 million; its shares traded at Rs 56, Tokyo Cement 265,000 shares crossed for Rs 23 million; its shares sold at Rs 87.30 and Aitken Spence 130,000 shares crossed for Rs 21 million; its shares fetched Rs 158.

In the retail market top seven companies that mainly contributed to the turnover were; Commercial Credit Rs 197 million (1.6 million shares traded), Digital Mobility Solutions Rs 172 million (992,000 shares traded), Samson International Rs 116 million (714,000 ,shares traded), Dipped Products Rs 90 million (1.5 million shares traded), Royal Ceramics Rs 81 million (1.7 million shares traded) and Hayleys Rs 73 million (306,000 shares traded). During the day 67.8 million share volumes changed hands in 19136 transactions.

It is said that the banking and financial sectors performed well, especially Commercial Bank, while construction sector counters, especially Tokyo Cement, performed well too. The manufacturing sector, especially Haycarb and Dipped Products, were impressive.

Yesterday the rupee was quoted at Rs 336.75/337.50 to the US dollar in the spot market, from Rs 337.00/75 the previous day, while bond yields edged lower slightly, dealers said.

The telegraphic transfer rate for Sri Lanka’s rupee against the US dollar was Rs 333.00 buying, 342.00 selling; the euro was Rs 375.7198 selling, Rs 389.6368 buying; and the pound was Rs 437.5636 buying, Rs 451.6092 selling.
