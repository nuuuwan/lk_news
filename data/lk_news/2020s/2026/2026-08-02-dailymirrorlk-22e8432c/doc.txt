‘There is no agreement’: Iran rejects Trump claim of Strait of Hormuz reopening deal

First Post - Iran on Sunday denied reports that it had agreed to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, rejecting US President Donald Trump’s claim that an outline of a deal with Tehran included the “immediate, complete, and total” reopening of the strategic waterway.

Iranian state media also dismissed reports of an agreement as false, while officials warned that the strait would remain closed as long as the United States continued what Tehran described as hostile actions.

The semi-official Fars news agency, citing sources close to Iran’s negotiating team and military, said there was no agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz.

“There is no agreement on reopening the Strait of Hormuz,” Fars reported, quoting an informed source.

The source said the waterway would remain closed while US “hostile actions” continued.

Iranian officials also rejected reports that Tehran had approved any plan to change its policy on the strategically important shipping route.

According to Fars, vessels would only be permitted to pass through a designated route after coordinating with and receiving approval from the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy.

The agency quoted a military source as warning that vessels attempting to use other routes could face “incidents”.

The comments underscore Tehran’s continued control over access to the waterway amid heightened tensions with Washington.

Iranian military officials also rejected Trump’s claim that Tehran had asked him to halt a planned US attack.

Mehr News Agency quoted officials as describing the claim as “another lie” and an attempt to pressure Gulf countries through threats.

The officials said Iran’s armed forces remained at the “highest level of readiness” and were prepared for any scenario.

“If confrontation becomes inevitable, the battlefield will be decisive,” they said, warning that the outcome would determine “who holds the power and who will have the final say”.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi reiterated Tehran’s readiness to respond to any US or Israeli military action during a phone call with Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan on Saturday.

Araghchi said Iran was prepared to defend its sovereignty, territorial integrity and national security, adding that any aggression would face a “decisive and proportionate response” from Iranian forces.
