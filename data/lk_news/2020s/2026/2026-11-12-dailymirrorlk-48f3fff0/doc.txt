Streamline road safety and save people’s lives

It is said that the most prudent way would be to prioritise non-infrastructure initiatives for enhancing road safety

Despite repeated awareness programmes and setting up of committees, road accidents continue to claim the lives of Sri Lankans. According to Sri Lanka Police, over 2000 road a cidents have been recorded for 2026 alone, claiming the lives of over 2300 people. On Tuesday, a fatal bus crash in Rambewa, Anuradhapura claimed the lives of five individuals while over 50 people were injured.

Eye witness accounts reveal that two buses had collided head-on when a third bus had tried to overtake the bus heading from Akkaraipattu to Jaffna. This bus has collided with another bus heading towards Colombo in the opposite direction. Subsequently all three buses had collided and the intensity of the crash had made it difficult to rescue the injured individuals inside the bus caught in the middle.

At its outset, the incumbent government launched an islandwide campaign to remove decorations inside buses and three-wheelers as part of the Clean Sri Lanka programme. The objective was to reduce road accidents, especially those involving buses and three wheelers. However, this campaign didn’t continue for long and many of these vehicles continue to have various kinds of décor that obstruct windscreens and side mirrors. This décor ranges from plastic fruits to different types of flashy items. Recently, a social media user posted a photo of a Bo tree sapling kept at the front of a bus that he had travelled in!

In April this year, Transport Minister Bimal Ratnayake said that the government is taking measures to reduce road accidents under the National Road Safety Action Plan. He said that the Government is prioritising passenger safety by introducing standardised buses and vehicles, and aims to significantly reduce road fatalities by 2029 through sustained safety reforms.

In October, the government announced a driver de-merit points system pilot programme which aimed at deducting points of drivers based on the severity and frequency of traffic violations. The government also introduced the mandatory seat belt rule for passengers traveling in vehicles via expressways following a spike in accidents on expressways.

Many of these interventions have been introduced as short term or immediate remedies to respond to a situation. But what is needed are long term solutions to protect the lives of passengers. In October, a bus carrying school children collided with an express train at a railway crossing between Habaraduwa and Koggala. Around 10 students sustained injuries. The government could start an initiative to inspect the number of unsafe railway crossings, places where there are no gates and where signals either don’t work or tend to malfunction. There are many such railways crossings that fall along the southern railway line.

Despite speed limits and restrictions, accidents happen quite frequently along the Southern expressway. Apart from mandatory seat belt rules the reason for these accidents need to be investigated immediately.

Observations made by the Asian Development Bank indicate that in order to improve road safety in Sri Lanka, the government should investigate two main pathways: Improve road infrastructure for safer roads; and expand the capacity of road users and road management systems in terms of road safety behaviours.

It is said that the most prudent way would be to prioritise non-infrastructure initiatives for enhancing road safety. These include strengthening road and transport agencies, implementing road safety education and bringing about reforms to governance and legal frameworks.

Passengers use public transport mainly due to affordability. Some may also use public transport for convenience in commuting. But people seem to be losing their trust in public transport due to the spike in road accidents. Due to under-investments, there are mechanical failures including faulty brakes and driver fatigue is rampant among drivers who work long hours for extra bucks. Without streamlining these core issues, this problem cannot be resolved. Ultimately it is the citizens who have to pay the price.

Therefore, restoring people’s trust in the public transport system and ensuring road safety should be a top priority for this government.
