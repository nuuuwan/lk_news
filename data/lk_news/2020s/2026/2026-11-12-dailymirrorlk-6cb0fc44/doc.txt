Colombo’s Deluge:  Will We Ever Learn?

The recent deluge that submerged Colombo will soon be forgotten, only to be remembered when the next disaster strikes. We seem to have little faith in the adage, “A stitch in time saves nine.” History provides ample evidence that, whether man-made or natural, we act only after disaster has struck. Despite repeated warnings, we were caught napping by the tsunami and Cyclone Ditwah, while the Easter Sunday attacks exposed our failure to act on clear intelligence.

As Colombo begins to recover from the floods, the familiar blame game has already begun and will probably continue until the next deluge. Improper garbage disposal is being blamed for blocking the city’s drainage system. This is a nationwide problem, particularly in urban areas.

Proposed solutions invariably focus on harsher penalties, hefty fines and even imprisonment, without addressing the root causes. Poverty and unequal access to education are important factors that cannot be ignored. Can we realistically expect those struggling below the poverty line to prioritise civic responsibility when their daily struggle is simply to survive? Even developed countries face similar problems in poorer and marginalised communities.

The lasting solution lies not merely in punishment but in addressing social and economic inequalities through equal educational opportunities and a fairer distribution of income. Unless we tackle these underlying issues, we will continue to clean up after every disaster without ever preventing the next one.
