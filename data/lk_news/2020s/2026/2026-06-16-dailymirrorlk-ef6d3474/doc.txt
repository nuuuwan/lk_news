Authorities examine crypto regulations as digital economy drive gathers pace

Sri Lanka is moving closer to establishing a regulatory framework for virtual assets, as the policymakers and financial regulators grapple with the growing risks posed by the unregulated cryptocurrency trading, offshore platforms and cross-border digital transactions.

The issue took centre stage yesterday as the Digital Economy Ministry and Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka (SEC) brought together the regulators, policymakers and industry experts to discuss the contours of a regulatory roadmap for virtual assets, a market that continues to operate largely outside the country’s formal financial regulatory framework.

The governments across the world accelerate efforts to regulate digital assets amid the rising retail participation, increasing institutional interest and concerns over money laundering, fraud and investor protection.

The experts addressing the forum warned that a significant portion of Sri Lankan virtual asset activity currently takes place through peer-to-peer channels and offshore platforms, creating challenges for the regulators seeking to monitor the transactions and safeguard investors.

The Digital Economy Ministry noted that the absence of a regulatory framework leaves the participants exposed to operational, financial and compliance risks while limiting the country’s ability to benefit from the emerging opportunities in the digital asset ecosystem.

The awareness session, jointly organised by the Digital Economy Ministry and SEC, under the theme ‘Let’s Together for a Safer Digital Future’, focused on the lessons that could be drawn from the jurisdictions that have already established frameworks for virtual assets.

Presentations by experts Nisal Chandrasekara and Dinindu Thevarananthri examined the regulatory models adopted by Singapore, Hong Kong, New Zealand and Malaysia, highlighting how those jurisdictions have sought to balance innovation with investor protection and financial stability.

Particular attention was paid to the compliance frameworks including the know your customer requirements, anti-money laundering controls and transaction monitoring systems, areas viewed as essential to any future regulatory regime.

The experts also highlighted the role of blockchain technology in improving transparency and traceability, noting that the technology itself could assist the regulators in monitoring the transactions and strengthening oversight.

The participants were further briefed on how virtual assets are created, traded and stored, as well as their growing use in investment products such as exchange-traded funds, self-custody holdings and international payment systems.

The focus was also on investor protection, corporate accountability and development of a regulatory framework suited to Sri Lanka’s financial system and digital economy ambitions.

Senior Advisor to President Dr. Hans Wijesuriya, Digital Economy Ministry Secretary Waruna Sri Dhanapala, Digital Economy Deputy Minister Eranga Weeraratne, SEC Chairman Senior Professor Hareendra Dissabandara, Financial Intelligence Unit Director Dr. Subhani Keerthirathna and senior officials from the Colombo Stock Exchange, Central Bank of Sri Lanka and Digital Economy Ministry were among those attending.
