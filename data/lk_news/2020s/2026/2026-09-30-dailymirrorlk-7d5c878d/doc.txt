Aesthetic education vital for empathetic professionals: PM

Colombo, Sep. 30 (Daily Mirror) - Aesthetic education plays an important role in developing not only talented artists but also professionals with empathy, creativity and a strong sense of humanity, Prime Minister Dr. Harini Amarasuriya said.

She made these remarks while attending the opening of the Galle Bataduwa Sri Rathanajothi Vidyalaya, a specialised school offering aesthetic subjects to identify and nurture the artistic talents of children in the Southern Province.

The Prime Minister said the Government aimed to create a generation capable of contributing to society in diverse fields, including medicine and engineering.

“We need doctors who can understand the pain of their patients and engineers who have a strong sense of humanity,” she said, stressing that aesthetic education could help develop such qualities.

She said education should not be limited to passing examinations and obtaining certificates, but should produce sensitive and responsible citizens who understand society and contribute to positive change.

According to the Prime Minister, aesthetic education develops creativity while fostering values such as empathy, respect for diversity and an understanding of others’ experiences.

Dr. Amarasuriya also highlighted the growing economic importance of the creative sector, saying education reforms would create opportunities in emerging fields such as digital art, new media, film, music technology and other creative industries.

She said the reforms were aimed at preparing Sri Lankan students for changing global employment and professional opportunities.

The Prime Minister announced that arrangements had been made to implement education reforms for Grades 2 and 6 next year. Cabinet approval was granted on September 28 for the 13-year curriculum framework, the concept paper of the National Education Commission and the student assessment framework linked to the reforms.

Referring to recent achievements by Sri Lankans in film and sport, Dr. Amarasuriya said the country was undergoing a period of transformation and awakening.

She noted that a Sri Lankan film had, for the first time, been selected for submission to the Academy Awards, while athlete Rumesh Tharanga had won a gold medal at the Asian Games.

The Prime Minister expressed hope that the newly opened Bataduwa Rathanajothi School of Aesthetic Education would become a starting point for Sri Lankan students to achieve further success internationally.

She encouraged students to make use of the growing global opportunities and described the establishment of the school as an important beginning.
