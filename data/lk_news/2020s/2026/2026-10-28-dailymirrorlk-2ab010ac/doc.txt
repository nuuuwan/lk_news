House Arrest Law still at concept stage: Minister

Colombo, Oct. 28 (Daily Mirror) - Justice Minister Harshana Nanayakkara said the proposed “House Arrest” law, which is still at the conceptual stage, is expected to help ease severe overcrowding in the country’s remand facilities once implemented.

“We are trying to introduce a Bill titled ‘House Arrest.’ Instead of someone being remanded in prison, the proposal is to place them under supervision or in home confinement,” he said.

The Minister said the proposed legislation was among several measures being considered to address prison congestion, which has increased amid ongoing law enforcement operations and the arrest of many suspects.

He said other measures included expediting reports from the Government Analyst’s Department, expanding prison infrastructure and introducing necessary legislative amendments.

However, Nanayakkara said that reducing the number of inmates alone would not resolve the underlying problem, adding that the justice system must strike a balance between punishing criminal offences and ensuring that people who do not need to remain in custody are released.

He said a special committee had been appointed under the direction of the President and himself to examine prison overcrowding and recommend practical measures to address the issue.

The Minister also referred to the responsibilities of Public Security Minister Ananda Wijepala, whose portfolio includes Law enforcement and the apprehension of suspects, while the Justice Ministry was concerned with ensuring basic humanitarian facilities for those in custody.

“Fundamentally, an increase in the number of people inside a prison itself shows that we have failed as a society,” he said.

Nanayakkara said the long-term solution was not simply to build more prisons to accommodate a growing inmate population, but to address the entrenched criminal culture that draws impoverished young people into crime.

He said that the ultimate goal should be to create socio-economic opportunities, provide people with second chances and reduce the prison population, rather than continually expanding detention facilities.
