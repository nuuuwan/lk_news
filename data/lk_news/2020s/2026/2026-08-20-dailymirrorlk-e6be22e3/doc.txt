Amana Bank 2Q profit rises 33% on financing, trading gains

Amana Bank PLC’s second-quarter profit rose 33 percent, as the growth in its core financing business and a threefold increase in trading income outweighed the higher operating costs and impairment charges.

The net profit increased to Rs.622.7 million in the three months ended June 30, from Rs.467.2 million a year earlier, according to the bank’s interim financial statements. Earnings per share rose to Rs.1.13, from 85 cents.

The net financing income climbed 18 percent to Rs.2.23 billion, as the financing income grew faster than the related expenses. The net trading income surged to Rs.275.3 million, from Rs.91.4 million, providing a significant boost to the quarterly earnings.

Those gains lifted the total operating income by 22 percent to Rs.2.84 billion. The net fee and commission income, however, fell 2 percent to Rs.331.4 million, as the commission expenses increased 43 percent.

The credit costs also rose sharply, with the impairment charges reaching Rs.67.5 million, from Rs.9.5 million a year earlier. The net operating income increased 20 percent to Rs.2.78 billion after accounting for the impairments.

The operating expenses climbed 15 percent to Rs.1.41 billion, led by the higher personnel, depreciation and other operating costs. Even so, the operating profit before the financial-sector taxes and levies advanced 25 percent to Rs.1.37 billion.

The pre-tax profit rose 29 percent to Rs.1.03 billion, while the tax charge increased 24 percent to Rs.409.9 million.

The balance sheet expanded during the first half, with the net customer financing growing 4 percent from end-December to Rs.157.01 billion and the deposits rising 5 percent to Rs.180.39 billion. Total assets increased 5 percent to Rs.215.23 billion, partly reflecting the purchase of a new corporate office for Rs.2.7 billion during the quarter.

The gross stage three financing ratio was unchanged at 2.8 percent, while the net ratio improved to 1.1 percent, from 1.2 percent.

The bank’s total capital ratio declined to 13.8 percent, from 14.7 percent at end-2025, narrowing its buffer over the 12.5 percent regulatory minimum. Its common equity Tier 1 capital ratio fell to 12.4 percent, from 13 percent.

For the six months through June, the net profit increased 25 percent to Rs.1.12 billion, while the pre-tax profit rose 22 percent to Rs.1.87 billion.
