More national parks may close as heat wave threatens wildlife

Colombo, Sep. 14 (Daily Mirror) - More national parks could be temporarily closed if the prevailing heatwave and prolonged dry conditions intensify, as wildlife authorities struggle to protect animals from increasingly harsh weather conditions.

The warning comes after the Wildlife Department decided to close Blocks 1 and 2 of Yala National Park from September 14 to October 15, while keeping Blocks 3, 4, 5 and 6 open to visitors.

A senior Wildlife Department official said authorities were closely monitoring the weather and could consider temporarily closing additional national parks if temperatures continue to rise, particularly under an El Niño-related weather pattern.

The official said the prevailing heat and dry conditions were creating increasingly difficult conditions for wildlife, particularly in areas where water sources were drying up.

“Although we cannot control the prevailing temperatures, we can provide water and other essential facilities to help wildlife cope with the conditions,” the official said.

The official said any decision to close additional parks would be based on prevailing weather conditions and the need to protect wildlife and their habitats.

The possible expansion of park closures comes at a time when Sri Lanka's wildlife tourism sector is generating billions of rupees in revenue.

According to official figures, national parks and other wildlife attractions recorded 1,381,079 visitors between January and June 2026, generating Rs.4,279,609,307.37 in revenue.

Of the total visitors, 759,411 were locals, while 621,668 were foreigners. The foreign visitors included 615,022 tourists from outside the SAARC region and 6,646 visitors from SAARC countries.

Yala National Park recorded the highest number of visitors during the six-month period, with more than 459,000 arrivals, followed by Udawalawe with 219,465, Horton Plains with 196,651 and the Elephant Transit Home with 123,273.

Yala was also the country's biggest wildlife tourism revenue earner, generating approximately Rs.2.10 billion during the first six months of the year.

The park recorded 56,165 visitors in June alone, including 26,378 local visitors and 29,681 foreign visitors. Foreign visitors generated approximately Rs.305.27 million in revenue during the month.

Foreign visitor fees accounted for the largest share of wildlife tourism revenue during the first six months, generating approximately Rs.4.06 billion. Local visitor fees generated around Rs.94.5 million, while SAARC visitor fees contributed approximately Rs.27.5 million.

Udawalawe National Park was the second-highest revenue earner, generating approximately Rs.927.5 million, while Minneriya generated around Rs.297.9 million.

Horton Plains and Mirissa generated approximately Rs.264.3 million and Rs.233.4 million, respectively.

The Wildlife Department said the decision to close Yala Blocks 1 and 2 was aimed at reducing pressure on wildlife and the ecosystem during the difficult weather conditions.

Sri Lanka has 23 national parks, many of which attract large numbers of local and foreign visitors throughout the year. Several of the country's most popular wildlife destinations are located in dry-zone areas that are particularly vulnerable to prolonged dry weather and high temperatures.

With the dry season continuing, authorities face the challenge of maintaining tourism activities while ensuring that wildlife has adequate access to water and other essential resources.

Officials stressed that the protection of wildlife and fragile ecosystems would remain the priority, even if worsening weather conditions require wider temporary closures of national parks.
