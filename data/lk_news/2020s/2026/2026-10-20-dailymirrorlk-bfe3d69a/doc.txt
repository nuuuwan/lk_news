The raid on Congresswoman Ilhan Omar: Trump uses executive power to cripple political opponents

Ilhan Omar has a 59% approval rating among Democrats in Minnesota

The  Justice Department’s October 12 raid on Congresswoman Ilhan Omar (Democrat-Minnesota) is the latest example of US President Donald Trump’s politically motivated use of executive power to silence his critics, destroy their political careers, or even deport them.

His Presidency bristles with such cases,  points out Sara Pequeno in www.usatoday.com She even goes on to say that “it has become the Republican party’s way of politics.”

She points out that exactly a year ago, a federal jury in Virginia indicted New York Attorney General Letitia James for legal questions around a second home she owns in the State. But the case was dismissed. Further, former FBI Director James Comey was recently indicted for an Instagram post.

Pequeno quotes CNN to say that the Justice Department had gone after at least 13 Democrats in the Congress since the start of Trump’s second term, while nine of the last 11 Republican Congressional members convicted of federal crimes have been pardoned or had their sentences commuted by the President.

“Trump has made it clear that he will use his position of power to go after anyone, whether it be journalists or politicians. As long as you’re standing in the way of what he wants, he will unleash a campaign of vengeance,” Pequeno adds.

Omar (44),  a Somali-origin Congresswoman, has been a relentless and sharp critic of Trump’s right wing and racist policies. And Trump has been lashing out at her and her country of origin, Somalia, and threatening to strip her of her citizenship and deport her.

The raid took place less than a month before the November mid-term elections for the US Congress. To evade criticism, it was dressed up as an investigation into Omar’s alleged financial and immigration frauds. But given the failure of investigations into these charges in the past decade, few would believe in the veracity of the Trump regime’s accusations.

On the contrary, fingers are pointed at Trump’s tendency to use executive power to harass opponents into submission. In the normal course, the Justice Department does not make big investigative moves during 60 days of an election, so as to avoid swaying the results. Such actions are banned under the Hatch Act.

The Hatch Act, passed by the US Congress in 1939, bans the use of federal administrative and investigative agencies to influence voting. Congresswoman Omar, who won the primaries, is expected to win the Minnesota seat again convincingly, as she is popular and Minnesota is a Democratic state.

Trump has consistently branded Omar as a cheat who had submitted false accounts of her income and had fraudulently obtained US citizenship. He has threatened to cancel her citizenship and deport her to Somalia. “I think she’s a terrible person. Just a complainer. She comes from a country that is a non-country. And then she comes over here, and she complains about our Constitution,” Trump said.

Omar’s son, born in the United States, was pulled over by Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers. He was let go after he produced documentation showing he was a U.S. citizen.

Omar’s immigration and financial history had been investigated by federal authorities for over a decade, but these had not led to charges, the New York Times points out. However, US Attorney Jeanine Pirro said that Omar’s financial disclosures had shown that the reported value of businesses tied to her husband, Tim Mynett, had surged from $51,000 in 2023 to $30 million in 2024.

Omar subsequently amended her financial disclosures, reducing the couple’s combined reported assets to between $18,004 and $95,000. The revised filing listed her husband’s two businesses as having no net value after accounting for liabilities. Omar’s office added that the original disclosures had failed to properly account for liabilities associated with her husband’s businesses, creating what it described as a misleading picture of the couple’s finances.

Subsequently, the Office of Congressional Conduct voted 5-1 to recommend that the House Ethics Committee dismiss misconduct allegations stemming from the financial disclosures, as the watchdog found there was not substantial reason to believe that Omar had reported false or incomplete information in violation of federal law or House rules.

Omar has two ex-husbands, Ahmed Abdisalan Hirsi and Ahmed Nur Said Elmi. US authorities had insinuated that Ahmed Nur Said Elmi was her brother. But Omar had described the accusation as “absurd and offensive.”

Omar legally married Ahmed Abdisalan Hirsi in January 2018, a month after she dissolved her marriage with Ahmed Nur Said Elmi, a UK citizen. Omar has described her relationship with Elmi as “brief,” between 2009 and 2011. There’s no record of the two having children.

Allegations that Omar and Elmi were sister and brother first surfaced on a Somali politics discussion board called SomaliSpot. But they were deleted.

Minneapolis-based Somali blogger and community leader Abdihakim Osman later told the Daily Mail in 2020 that she had introduced Elmi as her “brother” from London to Somali community members in the late 2000s.

It was also alleged that Omar would say that Elmi was looking for immigration documents for a green card. This, according to the allegation, was an immigration fraud (which attracts five years in prison and a $250,000 fine).

It is said that Omar might have used the term “brother” in a Somali social gathering, as among Somali Muslims, cousins are referred to as “brothers”. And marriage in Somalia (and in many other Eastern societies) is allowed between patrilateral parallel cousins.

In agricultural communities like the Somalis, there is a strong preference for endogamous (inter-cousin) marriages to reinforce family ties and inheritance patterns, Anthropologists say.

Therefore, Elmi might well be a perfectly marriageable “cousin” of Omar’s, who she could have referred to as her “brother” in a Somali social context – a description misinterpreted by Westerners who tend to be ignorant about Afro-Asian kinship terminologies.

Her resilience in the face of his administration’s pointed attack is going to endear her to her community – especially fellow immigrants who see themselves in her.”

In August, The Marshall Project reported that more than 610,000 people had been detained by Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) since Trump’s second inauguration, according to ProPublica. The Marshall Project is a nonpartisan, nonprofit news organisation that seeks to create and sustain a sense of national urgency about the U.S. criminal justice system.

“More than a hundred Americans held after agents questioned their citizenship. Nearly all were people of colour. At least 70 American children and teens detained, some of whom were handcuffed. Of the more than 300 Americans we tracked as detained for allegedly assaulting or obstructing agents, we found fewer than half were actually charged in federal court, and many of those cases were dropped or dismissed. Collectively, these cases show that  the administration has relentlessly pursued immigrants. Federal agents have run roughshod over the constitutional rights of citizens too,” ProPublica says.

But the Trump administration says none of this is happening. “DHS is NOT arresting U.S. citizens by mistake. There are no ‘indiscriminate’ stops. Allegations of racial profiling are disgusting, reckless and categorically FALSE.”
