Taking money to sign bail costs two years in jail

Colombo, Aug. 19 (Daily Mirror) - Two men from Sanchi Arachchiwatta, Colombo, who were found guilty of accepting money to sign bail for an Indian national and submitting forged documents to court, were sentenced to two years’ rigorous imprisonment by Colombo Chief Magistrate Asanga S. Bodaragama today.

The two accused, Hussein Mohamed and Hasith Mohamed, were convicted following a lengthy trial on charges of accepting money to sign bail for an unidentified person, submitting forged documents to court, and aiding and abetting the offences.

The case was instituted by the Colombo Fraud Investigation Bureau following a complaint made by the Registrar of the court that the accused had submitted a forged affidavit stating that they were prepared to furnish bail.

No counsel appeared for the accused. Appearing in the dock, the two suspects pleaded with the court to do justice to them. The first accused also requested the court to take into consideration that he has a young child.

Police officers of the Fraud Investigation Bureau who prosecuted the case told court that persons who come forward to furnish bail in exchange for money by submitting forged documents should be given the maximum punishment.

They urged the court to impose a sentence that would serve as an example to society, pointing out that individuals offering to sign bail for money should be deterred from committing such offences. They also noted that the accused had been prosecuted and found guilty following a lengthy trial.

Having considered the submissions, the Chief Magistrate sentenced both accused to two years’ rigorous imprisonment.

However, taking into consideration that the accused had already spent six months in remand custody, the Magistrate ordered that the period spent in remand be deducted from their sentences.
