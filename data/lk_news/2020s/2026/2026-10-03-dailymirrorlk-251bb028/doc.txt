Sri Lanka secures US $ 100mn ADB loan to tackle skills shortages

Sri Lanka has secured a US $ 100 million Asian Development Bank (ADB) loan to overhaul vocational training, seeking to address the skills shortages that leave the businesses struggling to recruit even as young people face high unemployment.

The Skills Development System Transformation Programme will bring the training institutions and employers closer together, modernise courses and facilities and expand women’s access to technical jobs as the country seeks to strengthen its economic recovery and competitiveness.

Approved on September 29, the nationwide programme will run from 2027 to 2031 and is expected to directly benefit more than 100,000 young people through better access to employment-oriented training.

“A skilled workforce is essential to Sri Lanka’s long-term economic transformation and competitiveness,” ADB Country Director for Sri Lanka Shannon Cowlin said.

“This programme will help create stronger pathways from education to employment by making training more responsive to industry needs, expanding opportunities for young people and women and ensuring that the graduates have the skills required by a modern and evolving economy.”

The financing comes as Sri Lanka faces a mismatch between the available skills and employers’ requirements. Despite the economic recovery, the priority sectors continue to experience skills shortages, alongside high youth unemployment and low female labour force participation, according to the ADB.

Many employers report difficulty finding workers with the skills needed in a changing economy, underscoring the need to connect training more closely with the labour market demand.

The programme will deepen the employer involvement through workplace-based learning and industry participation in assessing and certifying the trainees. It will also strengthen the mechanisms for private sector engagement and introduce industry-aligned courses in priority sectors.

Women’s employment opportunities will be expanded in nontraditional fields, including automotive technology, engineering, information and communications technology, construction and renewable energy.

To modernise training delivery, the programme will establish a centre of excellence for automotive technology, upgrade learning facilities and introduce a hub-and-spoke training model across all nine provinces.

It will also expand career guidance for secondary school students, strengthen teachers’ capabilities and improve coordination between schools and training institutions, helping young people make more informed decisions about further training and employment.

The reforms extend to oversight and funding. A national accreditation system, performance-based financing mechanisms and a unified digital training management information system will be introduced to improve quality assurance and align skills development with labour market needs.

The programme supports the government’s Technical and Vocational Education and Training Sector Strategic Framework 2026-2035.

The ADB financing comprises a US $ 50 million regular loan and a US $ 50 million concessional loan, alongside a separate US $ 500,000 technical assistance grant.
