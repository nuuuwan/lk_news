Cancer patients left waiting as Rs. 1 billion radiation units remain idle

Colombo, Nov. 18 (Daily Mirror) - Cancer patients in Badulla and Hambantota are being left waiting for life-saving radiation treatment as newly installed Linear Accelerator machines remain unused due to administrative and technical delays, a medical professional has alleged.

Consultant Physician Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa, President of the Medical Professionals' Alliance for Medical and Civil Rights, said the machines had been installed at the Badulla Provincial General Hospital and Hambantota General Hospital but radiation treatment services had still not commenced.

He said installation at Badulla was completed in April, while work at Hambantota was finalised in the first week of October. Despite this, patients in both districts are yet to benefit from the facilities.

According to Dr. Sanjeewa, the delay has been attributed to the lack of dosimetry equipment required to operate the machines. However, he questioned why the equipment could not be temporarily obtained from the Apeksha Hospital in Maharagama to start treating patients without further delay.

He said Deputy Health Minister Hansaka Wijayamuni had also instructed officials during an inspection visit to Badulla General Hospital to obtain the necessary dosimetry equipment from Apeksha Hospital.

Dr. Sanjeewa alleged that the failure to create the necessary conditions to commence treatment had raised serious concerns, particularly when cancer patients could not afford further delays.

The five Linear Accelerator machines were procured for the Badulla Provincial General Hospital, Ratnapura Teaching Hospital, Hambantota General Hospital, Kurunegala Teaching Hospital and Anuradhapura Teaching Hospital.

Each machine costs more than Rs. 600 million, while the total investment in a complete radiation therapy unit at each hospital exceeds Rs. 1 billion.

Of the five machines, three have so far been delivered to Badulla, Ratnapura and Hambantota.

At Ratnapura Teaching Hospital, the first phase of installation was completed by the first week of June, while preparations are underway for the second phase. The delay has been attributed to pending preparatory work in the room housing the equipment.

Meanwhile, the failure to commence services in Badulla and Hambantota has forced patients to seek treatment at already burdened facilities, including Apeksha Hospital in Maharagama and the National Hospital in Kandy.

Dr. Sanjeewa warned that this was adding further pressure to existing waiting lists, which already stretch for several months.

With delays in the public health system, some patients are being forced to seek radiation treatment from private hospitals, where the cost could range from Rs. 500,000 to Rs. 2 million.

The Association of Radiation Technologists has also raised concerns over the failure to utilise the newly installed machines, pointing out that the required dosimetry equipment could be temporarily obtained from Apeksha Hospital.

It has cited previous instances in which equipment from Apeksha Hospital was used to facilitate the commencement of Linear Accelerator treatment services at other hospitals.

At present, Linear Accelerator radiation therapy is available at five public institutions: Apeksha Hospital, the National Hospital in Kandy, the National Hospital in Galle, the Teaching Hospital in Jaffna and the Teaching Hospital in Batticaloa.

Dr. Sanjeewa has urged President Anura Kumara Dissanayake to intervene urgently and ensure that the newly installed machines are put into operation without further delay.

He said patients should not be made to suffer because of administrative delays when millions of rupees had already been spent on facilities intended to provide them with life-saving treatment.
