Women MPs Caucus weighs legal action against deported sex offenders

The Women Parliamentarians’ Caucus is pushing for stronger legal and institutional mechanisms to identify, monitor and prosecute Sri Lankan nationals deported after being convicted of sexual offences overseas, amid concerns they could re-enter society without scrutiny and pose a risk of repeat offences.

At a joint meeting of the Caucus and its Subcommittee on 23 November, chaired by Women and Child Affairs Minister Saroja Savithri Paulraj, discussions focused on recognising and enforcing foreign court judgments in Sri Lanka, improving international information-sharing and incorporating overseas criminal records into the local police database.

Proposals included mandatory background checks for recruitment to institutions working with children and a legally authorised, risk-based monitoring mechanism for deported offenders. The meeting also stressed the need for legislative amendments or new laws to support these measures.

The discussions also covered strengthening safeguards for children under the probation system, including possible six-monthly medical examinations and the maintenance of medical and behavioural records. The possibility of bringing Provincial Council-administered probation institutions under Central Government oversight was also considered.

The Caucus further called for expediting amendments to the Prevention of Domestic Violence Act No. 34 of 2005 and the Maintenance Act No. 37 of 1999, while highlighting delays in cases involving women and children due to pending reports from the Government Analyst’s Department.

Officials from the Attorney General’s Department, National Child Protection Authority, Sri Lanka Police, Government Analyst’s Department and Health and Mass Media Ministry participated in the discussions.
