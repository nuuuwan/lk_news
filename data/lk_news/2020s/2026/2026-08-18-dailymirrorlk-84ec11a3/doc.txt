Underground clay pipe system unearthed at ancient Yapahuwa

Colombo, Aug. 18 (Daily Mirror) - Archaeologists have uncovered important evidence of settlements and the daily lives of ordinary people during excavations at the historic Yapahuwa Kingdom under the Wayamba Project.

The most significant discovery is a previously unknown underground clay pipe system, parts of which have been unearthed in the western section of the outer city. Researchers believe the system may have been used to transport some form of liquid.

Several clay pipe structures running parallel to each other in opposite directions have been identified within the same structure. Archaeologists say a system of this nature has not previously been discovered at any other archaeological site in Sri Lanka.

The discovery is expected to provide new insights into the social, technological and urban development of ancient Sri Lanka.

Previous discoveries of foreign coins at Yapahuwa had provided evidence of the kingdom’s international trade connections. The newly discovered underground infrastructure could now offer further clues about the organisation and technological capabilities of its urban settlements.

While archaeological research in Sri Lanka has largely focused on Buddhist monastic complexes and royal palaces, researchers believe the latest findings could help reconstruct aspects of ordinary life during the medieval period.

The archaeological excavation project was launched by the Central Cultural Fund in 2025 under the direction of Professor Mangala Katugampola.

Researchers have also made extensive use of modern technology during the excavations, making the project significant in the ongoing study of Yapahuwa and Sri Lanka’s ancient urban history.
