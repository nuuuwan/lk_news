Five-day business registration pilot launched

Colombo, Nov 11 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka has launched a pilot project to simplify its 108-year-old business name registration process, enabling sole proprietorships and partnerships to complete registration within five working days.

The Simplified and Unified Business Name Registration (BNR) pilot project was launched in Anuradhapura yesterday (10) under the patronage of Deputy Minister of Industries and Entrepreneurship Development Chathuranga Abeysinghe.

The initiative is being implemented jointly by the Ministry of Industry and Entrepreneurship Development, non-profit organisation Chrysalis and Provincial Councils.

The reform aims to replace the lengthy registration procedure, which has been in place since 1918, with a simplified and standardised system requiring only basic identity and residency information.

Under the previous system, applicants had to submit documents including proof of land ownership and obtain approvals from multiple government agencies, making the process time-consuming and complicated.

The new system separates business name registration from other regulatory licensing requirements, allowing applicants to complete the process within five working days.

The initial three-month pilot will cover 20 selected Divisional Secretariat divisions across the Central, Northern, North Central and North Western provinces.

Following an evaluation of the pilot project, the Government plans to expand the system islandwide, with an online platform also expected to be introduced to facilitate digital registration.

The initiative is aimed at reducing administrative barriers for aspiring entrepreneurs and small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), while encouraging more businesses to enter the formal economy.

The launch was attended by Additional Secretary to the Ministry of Industry and Entrepreneurship Development Anoja Herath, Directors of Entrepreneurship Development Susanthi Jayasinghe and Evangeline St. Clair, Chrysalis Country Director and CEO Ashika Gunasena, Senior Consultant Padmasiri Gunawardena, and other provincial and regional administrative officials.
