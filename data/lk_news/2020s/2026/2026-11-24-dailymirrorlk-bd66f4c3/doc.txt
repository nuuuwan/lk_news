Bring online gaming into Sri Lanka’s tax net, Advocata urges

Proposes single levy on gross gaming revenue for online operators and physical casinos

Says current casino tax structure creates scope for expense shifting

Calls for independent regulator funded by licence fees or a dedicated levy

Sri Lanka should bring online gaming operators into the tax net under its Budget 2027 review of gambling taxes, the Advocata Institute said, calling for a single levy on gross gaming revenue across online platforms and physical casinos.

The think tank said a uniform levy would allow the government to collect revenue it says is being lost from unregulated online gaming. It recommended setting the rate in line with competing gaming jurisdictions. The institute gave no estimate of the revenue forgone and did not detail how the levy would be collected from offshore platforms serving Sri Lankan customers.

The proposed levy would replace the current casino tax structure, which Advocata said combines an 18 percent levy on gross gaming revenue with a 45 percent corporate income tax on profits. The institute said the two-tier system complicates tax administration and gives operators scope to lower their tax bills by classifying non-gaming expenses as gaming costs. It said the system also creates an incentive to shift costs into categories such as rent to take advantage of tax holidays under the Strategic Development Projects Act. Taxing gross gaming revenue alone would remove the benefit of shifting expenses to reduce taxable profit under the proposed regime, Advocata said.

The institute also called for the Gambling Regulatory Authority to have greater independence from the Finance Ministry. It proposed requiring Constitutional Council approval for board nominees, giving the board responsibility for hiring the director general through a competitive process, and granting the authority independent rule-making powers comparable to those of the Securities and Exchange Commission. It also sought to end the Finance Minister’s power to issue binding directives to the regulator.

Advocata said reliance on Treasury allocations had left the authority struggling to secure office space and recruit technical staff. It proposed allowing the regulator to retain operator licence fees or charge a dedicated regulatory fee to fund its work.

The institute said an independent regulator would be better placed to assess the appropriate size of the market and address money laundering, terrorism financing, problem gambling and consumer protection risks. It also proposed using the term “gaming” across tax and regulatory laws, arguing that inconsistent definitions and tax treatment across casinos, betting and lotteries could distort the market.

Advocata issued the recommendations in response to Budget 2027’s commitment to align gaming taxation with international practices. The proposals build on two policy papers published by the institute in 2024 and 2025.
