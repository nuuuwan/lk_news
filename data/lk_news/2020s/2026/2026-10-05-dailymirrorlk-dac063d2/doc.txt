Unforeseen hand trying to sabotage rabies prevention programme: Veterinary associations

Colombo, Oct. 5 (Daily Mirror) - An “unforeseen hand” is attempting to sabotage the rabies prevention programme being carried out by veterinary practitioners, three veterinary associations alleged yesterday.

The Sri Lanka Veterinary Association, Association of Companion Animal Practitioners and State Veterinary Surgeons Association said efforts were being made to undermine the programme aimed at making Sri Lanka free of rabies.

Past President of the Association of Companion Animal Practitioners Dr. Nuwan Wickramasinghe said veterinary practitioners had vaccinated 1.2 million dogs within five months.

He said the Department of Health, which had been responsible for rabies control for many years, had vaccinated only around one million dogs over a period of 50 years.

President-elect of the Sri Lanka Veterinary Association Dr. Chamari Kannagara said the Prevention of Rabies and Registration of Dogs Bill was an amalgamation of the Dogs Registration Ordinance of 1901 and the Rabies Ordinance of 1893.

She said the proposed legislation was aimed at updating the laws relating to rabies prevention.

President of the State Veterinary Surgeons Association Dr. Upul Ranjith said his association, together with other veterinary associations, had been lobbying for new legislation on dog registration for some time.

He said the vaccination programme had resulted in 1.2 million dogs being vaccinated within five months.
