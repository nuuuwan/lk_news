Fitch revises SLT to ‘AA+(lka)’ on national scale recalibration

Sri Lanka Telecom PLC (SLT) has been revised two notches higher to ‘AA+(lka)’ from ‘AA-(lka)’ with a Stable Outlook, as the recalibration of Sri Lanka’s national rating scale lifted the telecom operator, whose rating remains tied to the sovereign, Fitch Ratings said.

The recalibration followed Fitch’s upgrade of Sri Lanka’s sovereign Long-Term Local-Currency Issuer Default Rating to ‘B-’ from ‘CCC+’ on 22 September. The agency expects no other Fitch-rated corporate to see a rating change on account of the recalibration, making SLT the only one to move on the exercise.

Fitch also resolved the Rating Watch Positive on SLT after the state decided not to proceed with the sale of its stake in the company, a plan by the previous administration that was formally abandoned following a Supreme Court ruling in April. The agency believes this reflects a strategic shift by the state to “retain and strengthen SLT as a key national asset” rather than privatise it.

However, SLT’s ratings remain constrained by the weaker credit profile of the state, its parent, Fitch noted, even though the company’s standalone credit profile is stronger than that of the state. The agency attributes SLT’s rating, a notch below Dialog Axiata PLC’s ‘AAA(lka)’, mainly to this sovereign drag.

Meanwhile, Fitch forecasts SLT’s EBITDA margin to widen to 38 percent in 2026 from 34 percent in 2025, on revenue growth it expects to accelerate to 9 percent from 2 percent, along with cost controls. EBITDA net leverage is projected to fall to around 0.9x from 1.6x, while cash interest expenses are forecast to ease by about 10 percent to Rs.5.2 billion in 2026 from Rs.5.8 billion.

The agency expects SLT’s investment in national digital infrastructure to support the government’s pledge of minimum broadband speeds of around 2Mbps nationwide within the next two to three years. It also believes SLT’s financial capacity will support continued investment, including in recently acquired 3.5GHz 5G spectrum.

Fitch pointed out that SLT’s unrestricted cash of Rs.19 billion at end-June 2026, including Rs.7 billion in fixed deposits, covers its Rs.10 billion of short-term borrowings nearly twice over. The company is nonetheless likely to rely on refinancing to roll over its long-term debt, the agency added.
