Daily dengue cases drop to 350 from 1,300 amid control measures: Health Ministry

Colombo, Agu. 7 (Daily Mirror) - Although Sri Lanka remains under a high dengue alert, the number of daily dengue cases has declined from around 1,300 to 350-400 due to control measures and programmes implemented in recent months, the National Dengue Control Unit Director, Specialist Dr. Kapila Kannangara said.

He said the reduction was achieved through the collective efforts of health authorities and other stakeholders, while stressing the need to continue mosquito control activities due to ongoing risks caused by rainfall and climate changes.

Dr. Kannangara made these remarks at a special meeting held on Wednesday (05) at the Colombo National Hospital to review dengue high-risk areas and strengthen future dengue prevention programmes.

The meeting was held under the patronage of Health and Mass Media Minister Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa and organised by the National Dengue Control Programme of the Ministry of Health and Mass Media.

Officials and health sector representatives from dengue high-risk Medical Officer of Health (MOH) divisions across the country participated in the meeting to assess shortcomings in previous dengue control programmes and identify new strategies to tackle future outbreaks.

The discussions focused on strengthening mosquito control activities, identifying high-risk areas requiring special attention, improving technical support, responding to climate-related challenges, enhancing treatment facilities and reducing dengue-related deaths.

Addressing the meeting, Minister Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa said a planned programme aimed at completely eliminating dengue should be implemented over the next two years with the commitment of all stakeholders.

He praised the contribution of health workers during the recent dengue outbreak and said reducing dengue deaths was a significant achievement.

The Minister also said the need to strengthen mosquito control programmes during periods when dengue cases are lower and implement targeted programmes up to the Grama Niladhari division level under the supervision of the Health Ministry.

According to Dr. Kannangara, 87,536 dengue cases and 63 deaths had been reported as of yesterday. He warned that continued vigilance was essential due to changing weather patterns and rainfall conditions.

Health Ministry Secretary Specialist Dr. Anil Jasinghe called for a continuous, year-round dengue prevention programme focused on public participation, while Director General of Health Services Dr. Asela Gunawardena highlighted future challenges in dengue control and appreciated the efforts of health workers.

Senior health officials, provincial and regional health directors, Medical Officers of Health, Public Health Inspectors, entomology officers and other public health professionals attended the meeting.
