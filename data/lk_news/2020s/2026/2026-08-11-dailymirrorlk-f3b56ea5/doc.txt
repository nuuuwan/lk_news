Sri Lanka moves to strengthen regulation and welfare of captive elephants

Colombo, Aug. 11 (Daily Mirror) - The Government is taking steps to introduce a stronger regulatory framework for captive elephants in Sri Lanka, including their use in cultural and religious pageants.

The Ministry of Environment has appointed a committee chaired by its Additional Secretary for Policy, Legal Reforms and Parliamentary Affairs to review the proposed “2026 Registration and Licensing of Elephants Regulations.”

The draft regulations have been prepared by the Department of Wildlife Conservation.

The committee has been tasked with reviewing the proposed regulations, making recommendations and submitting a report on the welfare of captive elephants and procedures governing their deployment at cultural and religious events.

The Ministry said legal solutions would be introduced expeditiously following the committee’s recommendations.

The move comes alongside wider Government efforts to improve elephant welfare and address human-elephant conflict.

At the Pinnawala Elephant Conservation Centre, the Government has also introduced what it describes as Asia’s first “Free Elephant Sanctuary,” where musth elephants are released from chains and allowed to move freely in a natural environment.

The initiative is intended to provide improved welfare conditions for elephants during musth, a natural period in which adult male elephants experience heightened hormonal activity and can become difficult to manage.

The Government said protecting elephants requires attention not only to wild populations and their habitats but also to the welfare and management of captive elephants.

The broader national programme includes restoring elephant corridors, improving food and water sources within forest reserves, strengthening wildlife field operations and developing new measures to reduce deaths caused by railway collisions.

These initiatives are being implemented as part of a wider policy aimed at ensuring human-elephant coexistence while preserving Sri Lanka’s wildlife heritage for future generations.
