CAA conducts islandwide raids on Atapirikara, Pirikara sales

Colombo, Aug. 11 (Daily Mirror) - The Consumer Affairs Authority (CAA) has conducted a special islandwide raid targeting establishments selling Atapirikara and Pirikara items, uncovering several irregularities including incomplete sets and substandard items.

The special operation was carried out on August 10 as part of efforts to protect consumer rights and ensure traders comply with legal requirements governing the sale of religious items.

According to the CAA, 92 successful raids were conducted during the special operation, while a further 35 raids had been carried out between January and July 2026. This brings the total number of raids conducted on Atapirikara and Pirikara sales so far this year to 127.

During the August 10 operation, officers checked whether traders had complied with the requirements stipulated under Order No. 55 of Extraordinary Gazette No. 1918/20 dated June 11, 2015. Bills, invoices and other relevant documents maintained by traders were also inspected.

The majority of violations detected involved the failure to display required information on Atapirikara, Tanipata Sivuru, Adanakada, Bandapati, bowls and other Pirikara items or their packaging.

CAA officials seized 436 items, including Atapirikara sets, robes, Adanakadas, Bandapatis, bowls and other accessories in connection with the violations.

In a significant part of the operation, officers did not limit inspections to the outer packaging. They randomly opened selected Atapirikara sets to examine their contents.

The inspections uncovered several irregularities, including some sets containing only a single robe instead of a pair, incomplete sets missing some of the eight components traditionally associated with an Atapirikara, changes to items based on the stated price, and the inclusion of aluminium and damaged bowls as well as blunt or unusable razor blades.

The CAA warned that such irregularities may not be apparent from the outside packaging and urged consumers to carefully check the contents, particularly when purchasing complete Atapirikara sets for religious purposes.

The Authority said legal action will be initiated against the relevant traders before the appropriate Magistrate’s Courts for violations detected during the raids.

The CAA also stressed that traders are responsible for providing the required information on Atapirikara and Pirikara products and packaging, ensuring that the products are in proper condition and complying with all relevant legal requirements.

The Authority said random market inspections and special raids targeting the sale of Atapirikara and Pirikara products will continue islandwide.

Consumers can report suspected violations or irregularities to the CAA through the 1977 consumer hotline during office hours.
