Thilan Wijesinghe seeks probe into source of parliamentary list on corruption charges, after name withdrawn

Colombo, Oct. 28 (Daily Mirror) - Former Board of Investment (BOI) Chairman Thilan Wijesinghe has sought an investigation as to how he came to be named in a High Court case where his lawyers could not trace any reference, in a list of 50 corruption cases tabled in Parliament, after Leader of the House Bimal Rathnayake withdrew his name last week, he said.

Rathnayake, who is also the Minister of Transport, Highways and Urban Development, tabled the 10-page document on October 8 to counter claims that the Government was pursuing political revenge. The document detailed corruption, fraud and criminal cases involving former ministers, members of the Rajapaksa family, senior state officials and businessmen, with Wijesinghe appearing as the 50th and final entry in connection with alleged violations of anti-corruption legislation in the award of contracts for state development projects outside approved procedures.

Addressing Parliament on October 22, the Minister withdrew the names of Wijesinghe, former Kilinochchi District Secretary Rupavathy Ketheeswaran and former ministry additional secretary Jayantha Fernando from the list. Rathnayake explained that the three may or may not have held Government positions at the relevant time and that their inclusion was not essential to his argument, while reiterating that the cases were not being pursued out of political revenge.

"I welcome Minister Bimal Rathnayake's decision, announced in Parliament on 22 October 2026, to withdraw my name from the list he tabled on 8 October," Wijesinghe stated in a social media post.

However, Wijesinghe questioned how the case reference came to be included at all. According to him, the list cited case reference HC/9021/2018, but two independent legal teams that inquired with the Registrar of the Colombo High Court found no case under that number. In an earlier update on October 15, he noted that the lawyers had been told only around 800 cases were filed in that court in 2018, and that under its numbering system, a case numbered 9,021 could not have been filed that year. On those figures, the cited reference is more than 11 times the number of cases filed in that Court during the year.

Wijesinghe also cautioned that the consequences would not end with the withdrawal.

"Given my responsibilities on Boards of public companies listed in Sri Lanka and India, allegations of this nature can affect the confidence of regulators, shareholders and business partners. Withdrawing a name does not automatically undo those consequences," he pointed out.

Questioning why anyone would put forward information of such seriousness without first establishing its accuracy, he observed that knowingly supplying false information to a public servant could, in certain circumstances, amount to an offence under the Penal Code.

"A Minister must be able to rely on the information placed before him. I have therefore written to the Minister requesting an investigation into its source and the circumstances in which it was included," he added.

Meanwhile, in his initial response on October 9, Wijesinghe categorically denied the allegation. He noted at the time that media reports suggested it related to 2010, a period during which he held no public office and served as Group Managing Director of Overseas Realty (Ceylon) PLC, and that no law enforcement agency had sought a statement from him on the matter.
