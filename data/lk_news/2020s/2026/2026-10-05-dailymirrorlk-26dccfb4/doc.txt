‘Don’t be distracted by social media’ – IGP tells cops

Colombo, Oct. 5 (Daily Mirror) - The Inspector General of Police (IGP) Priyantha Weerasuriya has urged police officers to remain focused on their duties and not be distracted by social media, while assuring them of the support of the President, the relevant Minister and the Ministry Secretary to perform their duties without interference.

The IGP has also warned that police officers involved in criminal activities will not be tolerated within the Police Department, regardless of their rank or position.

Addressing police personnel, the IGP said strict action would be taken against officers who assist criminal activities, engage in drug trafficking or maintain links with organized crime groups.

He said that discipline was the foundation of an effective police service and that there was no place in the department for officers who compromise the law.

The IGP also announced plans to establish in-service training units at district and divisional levels to improve the professional knowledge and capabilities of police officers.

He said improving police infrastructure was also essential, noting that a conducive living environment was important for the mental well-being of officers. Although facilities had improved significantly compared to the war period, further improvements would be made to police barracks, canteens and kitchens across all ranks.

According to the IGP, financial allocations for such improvements would be decentralized from next year, with Senior Deputy Inspectors General (SDIGs) receiving funds directly instead of all allocations being managed through Police Headquarters.

He said infrastructure and human resource development would be ineffective without maintaining strict discipline within the Police Department.

Addressing concerns over police salaries, IGP Weerasuriya acknowledged that the existing salary structure did not adequately reflect the demanding and risky nature of police duties.

He said a Cabinet sub-committee had already reviewed the matter and that the President had given assurances that necessary steps would be taken to implement salary reforms by next year.

The IGP also reminded officers that their salaries and facilities were funded by public money collected through taxes.

He urged every police officer to take responsibility for their duties and engage in daily self-reflection to ensure they provide the highest possible standard of service to the public.
