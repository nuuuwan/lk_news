A Cost-Free Solution to Railway Level-Crossing Accidents

The recent tragedy at the Weligama level crossing should serve as an eye-opener to the Government and prompt immediate preventive action, not only to minimise accidents at unsafe railway level crossings but also to prevent the continuing slaughter of elephants on railway tracks.

According to the Auditor General’s Report for 2024, approximately 400 level crossings in the country are classified as unsafe. This is hardly a new problem. During President Premadasa’s tenure, recognising that the country lacked the financial resources to install mechanical gates at every unprotected crossing, a practical solution was adopted.

Following the Ahungalla level-crossing disaster, which claimed many lives, three gatekeepers were appointed to operate bamboo gates eight hours per shift to cover 24 hours at each unprotected crossing. Remarkably, accidents were avoided until this system was subsequently withdrawn.

Ironically, the recent Weligama accident involving a Special Task Force (STF) jeep raises serious questions about the observance of basic safety precautions.

Here is a simple, cost-free suggestion that deserves serious consideration. Until proper safety measures are installed at all unprotected crossings, trains should be required to slow down considerably, or even stop and proceed cautiously, before crossing these dangerous points. This would require only a revision of railway timetables and operating procedures.

Prevention is always better than compensation, and human lives are worth far more than a few minutes of railway travel time.
