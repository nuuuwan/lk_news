Above-band inflation prompts call for Treasury-CBSL talks

Wants the CBA Coordination Council to meet fortnightly instead of quarterly

Says CBSL’s “external shocks” explanations amount to a policy of inactivity

The Treasury and the Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) should jointly fight inflation through a fortnightly coordination mechanism, as monetary policy alone cannot rein in prices above the 7 percent ceiling without pushing the economy into contraction, former CBSL Deputy Governor Dr. W.A. Wijewardena urged.

Delivering the keynote address on Monday at the Budget Highlights 2027 forum organised by the Institute of Certified Management Accountants of Sri Lanka (CMA Sri Lanka), Dr. Wijewardena proposed that the Coordination Council established under the Central Bank of Sri Lanka Act of 2023 be given revised terms of reference and convened every two weeks. He elaborated thus  would allow Treasury and CBSL officials to work out relief measures together whenever inflation breaches the upper limit of the target band.

The Council is chaired by the CBSL Governor and includes the Secretary to the Treasury as a member. It was set up as a platform for policy dialogue after the new Act removed the Treasury Secretary from the CBSL’s decision-making bodies, and it is required to meet once a quarter.

“We have provided independence to the Central Bank, which is the correct thing, but of course we must now set up a mechanism where the Treasury officials and the Central Bank officials will meet at least once in two weeks and discuss how they should jointly provide relief to the people when inflation is above 7 percent,” Dr. Wijewardena said.

His call comes as headline inflation, measured by the Colombo Consumer Price Index (CCPI), accelerated to 8.3 percent in October from 8 percent in September, as per CBSL data. This kept inflation above the CBSL’s 5 percent target band of plus or minus 2 percentage points for a fourth consecutive month. The latest reading is nearly four times the 2.1 percent recorded in October 2025, while the CBSL expects inflation to remain in high single digits through the first quarter of 2027.

However, the former central banker questioned the CBSL’s tendency to attribute inflation to external shocks whenever it strays outside the band in either direction.

“When the rate is below 3 percent, that is due to external shocks. When the rate is above 7 percent, it’s also due to external shocks. So it means the Central Bank had been following the policy of inactivity,” he observed.

At the same time, he conceded that the CBSL had little room to act alone. He pointed out that dragging inflation down from around 8 percent to 3 percent by curbing aggregate demand would trigger a massive contraction in the economy, leading to unemployment and recession. Such a downturn, he warned, would itself become “public enemy number one”.

Meanwhile, Dr. Wijewardena acknowledged that he had since rethought the monetarist doctrine he upheld during his years at the CBSL. That doctrine held that inflation was “always and everywhere a monetary phenomenon” and the economy’s foremost threat.

“As an outsider today when I look at it, I might think differently. Inflation had been public enemy number one, but today there are other things which are public enemy number one, and inflation might be reduced to, say, public enemy number two,” he noted.

Drawing on ‘Inflation: A Guide for Users and Losers’ by political economists Mark Blyth and Nicolò Fraccaroli, he contended that once inflation breaches the upper limit and the central bank is unable to rein it in, the responsibility falls on the fiscal authorities. In his view, budgetary policy then has to be geared towards providing relief to the people.

He welcomed the Rs.1.5 trillion allocated in Budget 2027 for subsidies to bring down prices, adding that the budget would now have to be re-geared to take charge of the inflation fight.

“It’s a matter for the Treasury as well as the Central Bank to do this job jointly,” he stressed. He expressed hope that Industry and Entrepreneurship Development Deputy Minister Chathuranga Abeysinghe, who was present at the forum, would take up the proposal seriously and arrange for the Council to meet fortnightly.

Dr. Wijewardena also listed a joint inflation defence by the Finance Ministry and the CBSL among the benchmarks he set for the forum’s panel discussion. The other benchmarks were a seamless handover from the IMF programme and the ignition of economic take-off.
