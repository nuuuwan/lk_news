Statutory rape is not a love story!

The recent incident of a 15-year old girl found in a forest cave with a two-month old baby has gained undue attention for mostly the wrong reasons.

The girl was reported missing to the Ududumbara Police in July. Following a tip-off about a man who had been purchasing small quantities of rice, officers at the Ududumbara Police station had followed him into the dense forest. According to statements given to the police, the baby was born in the cave without a doctor or midwife. The 23-year old man had told police that he cut the umbilical cord with a knife used for tapping kithul palms, and that he treated the mother and baby with herbal remedies from the forest.

Police arrested the 23-year-old man, identified as the baby’s father, and a 36-year-old relative accused of helping him. According to police reports, the older suspect allegedly first sexually abused the girl in 2023. Police said the younger suspect later allegedly abused her and that both men were suspected of sexually abusing her on multiple occasions.

Many so-called analysts on social media romanticised this incident by even comparing it to the mythical tale ‘Sinhabahu’! At least one individual praised the suspect for his innovative approach to cut the umbilical cord much to the dismay of a majority of social media users!

Under Sri Lanka’s Penal Code, sexual intercourse with a girl under 16 is rape whether or not she consents. The offence carries a sentence of 10 to 20 years’ imprisonment. Following the incident, Minister of Women and Child Affairs, Saroja Savithri Paulraj, said that children should be taught sex education and the basics of the country’s laws from an early age. She described this incident as a ‘clear case of statutory rape.’

This is one among many incidents that happen rather frequently in this country. What is even worse is that many such incidents often go unreported. Many causative factors such as lack of sex education and poverty are identified as main reasons behind these incidents.

However, the discussion around sex education has always come to a standstill. It had faced severe backlash by religious and cultural groups. But it is ironical that many individuals in religious institutions have been accused of alleged sexual abuse particularly concerning minors. The victims include both girls and boys below consenting age. A major religious figure who opposed the introduction of sex education in school curricula claimed that topics such as same-sex relationships and birth control are “allegedly being funded and influenced by international agencies.”

Even though the incumbent had broader educational reforms as part of its elections manifesto, critics including the Opposition have raised concerns regarding the clarity of these reforms. According to Prime Minister Harini Amarasuriya, discussions have been initiated to introduce age-appropriate sex education for children.

But sex education alone won’t resolve this issue which has spread like a cancer, especially in rural areas of the country. Children need to be aware of the law and what it means to give consent. Parents need to be aware of their children’s whereabouts. Many children are abandoned or neglected in rural settings as both parents go to work. They are left with their guardians such as grandparents or uncles and aunts. As a result, many children and adolescents and vulnerable to sexual abuse, rape and harassment most often done by a known individual.

These issues have been identified, discussed and spoken in public platforms for many years. But the recurring issue of maintaining a dangerous silence regarding incidents of sexual abuse, especially when it concerns perpetrators with power and authority is worrying. It sets a dangerous precedent to enforce the law on other perpetrators that need to be apprehended and punished for similar crimes.

If incidents of sexual abuse, rape, assault and harassment concerning minors become normalised due to dangerous social media trends it would become increasingly difficult to control the situation at grassroots level.

Without a secure future for children, Sri Lanka as a society would continue to undergo profound psychological, social and structural decay.
