Govt. shifts export strategy towards higher volumes and scale

The government is shifting its export strategy towards increasing the volumes and scaling the commercially viable producers, rather than measuring the progress by the number of exporters entering the international markets, Industry and Entrepreneurship Development Minister Sunil Hadunnetti said yesterday.

However, the minister did not identify any new incentives, funding commitments or policy measures to support the shift, which comes as the government pursues a steep increase in export earnings over the next four years.

“The government’s primary target is to increase the export volume rather than merely the number of exporters,” Hadunnetti told the 26th Exporters’ Forum organised by the Export Development Board (EDB).

The government aims to lift the export earnings to US $ 36 billion by 2030, from a record US $ 17.3 billion in 2025. According to EDB Chairman and Chief Executive Officer Mangala Wijesinghe, achieving that goal would require the export earnings to more than double within five years.

For 2026, the government is targeting US $ 19 billion in export earnings, an increase of US $ 1.7 billion or nearly 10 percent, from last year.

Hadunnetti reiterated that efforts are underway to remove the barriers preventing the rural producers from entering international markets, including the shortcomings in market knowledge, packaging and technology.

He said the exporter constraints were not limited to Colombo and that discussions were being conducted with the state institutions at provincial and district levels.

No details were provided on how the producers would be financed to invest in technology and packaging or how the government planned to connect them with the overseas buyers.

The minister also did not specify whether the strategy would include tax concessions, subsidised credit, grants or targeted support for the priority industries.

The Sri Lankan exporters have repeatedly raised concerns over trade-related and operational barriers that weaken competitiveness and delay access to the overseas markets.

The EDB said a significant number of issues raised by the exporters had been referred to the relevant state institutions through the Export Facilitation Task Force. The outstanding matters were discussed at yesterday’s forum, although neither the issues nor the solutions agreed upon were disclosed.

Wijesinghe said the public and private sectors were working together under the National Export Development Plan to address the practical and trade-related constraints.

Hadunnetti said the government intended to simplify the policy decisions and create a more favourable operating environment for the exporters. An implementation timetable for the proposed changes is yet to be shared.
