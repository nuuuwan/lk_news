President warns against salary protests, assures Rs. 150Bn from 2028 budget

Colombo, Nov. 28 (Daily Mirror) - Claiming that a planned conspiracy is behind ongoing public sector protests aimed at pushing the country into another economic crisis, President Anura Kumara Dissanayake stressed that the government would not give in to demands that could place an additional burden on public finances while assuring that Rs. 150 billion would be allocated for public sector salaries and allowances in the 2028 Budget.

Addressing a gathering of trade union representatives under the theme “The Budget and the Working People”, the President explained that granting further salary increases and allowances would force the government to either increase borrowings or reduce allocations for capital expenditure, healthcare and other essential sectors.

He said the government was not prepared to cut capital expenditure, as its objective was to strengthen and expand the national economy rather than maintain a mere hand-to-mouth economic existence.

Highlighting the country's financial position, Dissanayake said government revenue is projected to stand at Rs. 6,230 billion in 2027, while total expenditure, including salaries, pensions and debt servicing, amounts to Rs. 7,035 billion.

He noted that government revenue was insufficient even to cover salary payments and debt repayments, leaving the Treasury with a funding gap of Rs. 805 billion that must be met through borrowing.

“We have set the borrowing limit at Rs. 3,800 billion for 2027. Allocations for capital expenditure and social welfare programmes must also be financed through borrowings,” he said.

Comparing the current financial situation with the economic crisis in 2022, the President said the government had been operating with an overdraft of Rs. 700 billion at the time, whereas the Treasury now has deposits amounting to Rs. 700 billion.

Responding to claims that the country could face another economic crisis, he said such a situation could arise if the government gave in to protests and granted all salary increases and allowances without considering their impact on the national budget.

“There is a planned conspiracy to push the country into a crisis by making demands and placing an additional burden on the budget. We will not fall into that trap,” he said.

The President further revealed that the government expects to make an additional allocation of Rs. 400 billion through the 2028 Budget. Of this amount, Rs. 250 billion will be directed towards capital expenditure, while Rs. 150 billion will be allocated for public sector salaries and allowances.

He also announced plans to increase the monthly income threshold for the highest personal income tax rate of 36% to Rs. 500,000 under the 2028 Budget.

Under the existing tax structure, the 36% rate applies to monthly incomes exceeding Rs. 358,333. The 2027 Budget proposes raising this threshold to Rs. 433,333.

Dissanayake also alleged that opposition political forces were attempting to use religious groups and working people to advance their political objectives.
