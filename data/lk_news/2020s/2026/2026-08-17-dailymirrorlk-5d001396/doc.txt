Govt. to appoint internal interim CEO for SriLankan this week amidst restructuring drive

​The government is set to appoint an internal candidate as the interim Chief Executive Officer of loss-making  SriLankan Airlines this week on a contract basis, ensuring management continuity as the national carrier navigates a crucial restructuring phase.

​While the specific identity of the new interim chief has not been officially disclosed, Deputy Minister of Civil Aviation Janitha Kodithuwakku confirmed that the stopgap appointment will be drawn from within the organisation. This move aims to steer daily operations while the state finalises a permanent corporate model. The airline’s top executive post has remained largely in an acting capacity for over a year, following two unsuccessful recruitment drives that targeted both local and foreign professionals.

​“We advertised for the CEO position on two occasions, opening it to both local and foreign applicants, but the interview panel concluded that none of the candidates met the required criteria or there were complications in reaching agreements,” Kodithuwakku stated. “Instead of getting stuck on this, we are looking to appoint an internal CEO on a contract basis this week to manage operations until this transition period ends. Once the new model is established, we plan to make a more long-term appointment.”

​The management transition aligns with the government’s broader policy on the national carrier. Despite the airline’s historic financial difficulties, state policy firmly emphasises the necessity of maintaining a national airline due to its critical role in supporting the local economy, driving the tourism sector, and serving vital national interests during emergencies.

​To determine the most financially sustainable path forward, the government recently appointed a dedicated strategy and restructuring committee led by Dr. Hans Wijayasuriya. This committee is tasked with evaluating external expertise and proposing the most viable corporate model for the airline’s future.

​“We have appointed a group led by Dr. Wijayasuriya to analyse how this restructuring model should function,” Kodithuwakku explained. “They are looking deeply into whether we retain the majority shareholding under the government while exploring options for capital injections, or whether we should pursue a management contract. They have about five months to submit their report, and the final restructuring process for SriLankan Airlines will be decided based on those findings.”

​The government has already begun receiving strategic restructuring proposals from foreign entities, including a recent submission from Hong Kong-based Transwell.  . These plans are being forwarded directly to the committee for evaluation. Kodithuwakku claimed that despite ongoing financial constraints and the lack of a permanent CEO, the airline’s staff have successfully managed operations and maintained critical flight slots, highlighting the urgent need for a stabilised, long-term corporate framework.
