Mechanism needed to address changing electoral landscape shaped by social media, AI: PAFFREL

Colombo, Nov. 2 (Daily Mirror) - People's Action for Free and Fair Elections (PAFFREL) Executive Director Rohana Hettiarachchi has called for strong dialogue among all stakeholders to address the changing electoral landscape in Sri Lanka, which is increasingly dominated by disinformation disseminated through social media posts.

Speaking at a forum organised by Democracy Reporting International, Hettiarachchi said all stakeholders should initiate a serious discussion on the issue. "It is difficult to control social media platforms, but some mechanism should be in place to prevent the spread of false information," he said.

Meanwhile, research conducted by Democracy Reporting International has revealed that increased voter exposure to deceptive disinformation generated by generative AI (GenAI), limited capacity among independent media to counter or correct AI-generated misleading content, and the risk of distorted voter decision-making are key threats to Sri Lanka's electoral integrity.

The research found that incomplete and inaccurate responses were consistently observed across all four large language models (LLMs) assessed. Gemini performed best, with only 10.4 per cent of its answers classified as false and misleading, followed by Copilot at 16.7 per cent, ChatGPT at 18.8 per cent, and DeepSeek at 35.4 per cent during the local government election. This is considered a risk as a new generation of voters is increasingly turning to AI for electoral information.

The research also revealed that political campaigning and promotion remained prominent during the 48-hour campaign silence period, including during the 2024 presidential election.

"Posts that featured campaigning, promotion and demotion of candidates and parties were 38.6 per cent of posts during the silent period", the research report revealed.

According to the report, there were 97 posts related to campaigning and promotion, while 45 posts featured political commentary, analysis and predictions during the presidential election.

Another finding was that 30 different currencies had been used to pay for political advertisements during recent elections. Payments had been made through diverse and non-local payment arrangements. "It calls into question whether all advertisers targeting Sri Lanka were effectively locally authorised, as required under platform policies," the report also said.
