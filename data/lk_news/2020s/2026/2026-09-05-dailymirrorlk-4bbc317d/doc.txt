Rs.3bn allocated to cushion drought impact on agricultural sector

The government has allocated Rs.3 billion for agricultural insurance compensation, to cushion the financial impact of the drought-related crop losses and help the affected farmers sustain production ahead of the next cultivation season.

The compensation programme comes as the prolonged dry weather, attributed to the El Niño conditions, has affected agricultural production in several major farming districts, with potential implications for farmer incomes, rural economic activity and food supply.

The preliminary assessments have identified damage to around 8,000 acres of farmland, cultivated by approximately 7,000 farmers in districts including Monaragala, Polonnaruwa, Hambantota, Ampara and Anuradhapura.

The reported damage covers 1,401 acres in Monaragala, 1,259 acres in Polonnaruwa, 783 acres in Hambantota, 1,055 acres in Ampara and 1,923 acres in Anuradhapura, while the assessments are continuing in the other affected areas.

The officials of the Agricultural and Agrarian Insurance Board and Agrarian Development Department are conducting field inspections to quantify the crop losses and facilitate the release of compensation.

Under the insurance scheme, the farmers affected by damage to paddy, maize, big onion, potato, soybean and chilli can receive compensation of up to Rs.100,000 per hectare, subject to a maximum coverage of five acres.

The government has instructed the authorities to accelerate the compensation payments, with the financial assistance expected to help the farmers meet the cultivation costs and maintain their capacity to invest in the next production cycle.

The move is also aimed at limiting the wider economic impact of the drought-related agricultural losses by supporting the income of the farming communities and helping restore the cultivation in the affected areas.

The farmers who have not yet reported crop damage have been urged to contact the agricultural research and production assistants of the Agrarian Development Department or development and field officers of the Agricultural and Agrarian Insurance Board.

The crop losses can also be recorded in the damage report books maintained at the Agrarian Development Centres. Further information is available through the Agricultural and Agrarian Insurance Board’s 1918 hotline.
