IFAD-backed SARP offers up to Rs.900,000 grants to spur youth agribusiness ventures

The Agriculture, Livestock, Lands and Irrigation Ministry has called for business proposals from young entrepreneurs, under its Youth Entrepreneurship Development Programme, offering matching grants of up to Rs.900,000 to support new agribusiness ventures in the climate-vulnerable regions of the country.

The initiative is implemented through the Smallholder Agribusiness and Resilience Project (SARP), which is funded by the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) and government of Sri Lanka.

The project seeks to reduce poverty among the small-scale farmers while strengthening food security and nutrition by improving resilience and market access in the dry-zone areas.

The programme targets youth aged 18 to 40 in selected project areas spanning the Anuradhapura, Vavuniya, Mannar, Puttalam, Kurunegala and Matale districts, covering the Deduru Oya, Mee Oya and Malwathu Oya river basins.

The eligible proposals include investments in on-farm production, post-harvest processing and other agribusiness-related activities.

In addition to providing matching grants covering up to 45 percent of an approved investment plan, the SARP will offer technical training and support to access credit facilities where required, with the aim of encouraging youth-led agribusiness entrepreneurship.

Applications for the first round of funding close on August 14, 2026. According to the SARP, the eligible proposals will be selected through a comprehensive evaluation process and considered on a first-come, first-served basis.
