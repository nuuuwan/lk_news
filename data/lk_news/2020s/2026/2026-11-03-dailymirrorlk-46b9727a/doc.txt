CEJ urges Wildlife Department to enforce court orders on Hakwatunawa reserve

Colombo, November 3 (Daily Mirror) - The Centre for Environmental Justice (CEJ) has urged the Department of Wildlife Conservation to immediately implement court orders to recover nearly 60 hectares of land illegally cultivated within the Hakwatunawa Reservoir area, which is part of the Kahalla-Pallekele Wildlife Reserve.

The CEJ expressed concern over the Department's failure to enforce the court orders issued to restore the protected land to the Department's control.

According to the CEJ, wildlife officer Chathura Gunaratne, who was serving in the area at the time, obtained several court orders in November 2025 to recover the illegally occupied land.

However, the organisation claims that the current wildlife officer has delayed implementing the orders for nearly a year.

The CEJ has also raised concerns over alleged political connections involving the officer, claiming that his close association with a local politician affiliated with the National People's Power (NPP) may have influenced the delay. However, these allegations have not been independently verified.

The organisation warned that the delay could result in the loss of nearly 60 hectares of protected land, particularly as the legal validity period of the court orders is reportedly nearing its end.

The CEJ called on the Department of Wildlife Conservation to take immediate action to enforce the court orders and prevent further encroachment on the Hakwatunawa Reservoir Reserve.
