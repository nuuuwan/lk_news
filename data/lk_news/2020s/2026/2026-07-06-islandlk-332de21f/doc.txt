The night that went wrong …

Yes, it was supposed to be waves of sound, but it ended up being waves of frustration.

The headliners took the stage, as promised, and the crowd showed up, but they were thoroughly disappointed.

By mid-evening the complaints were louder than the music. Too many people, too little space to move. Food and drinks? A marathon, not a sip. Some fans, with valid tickets, couldn’t even get past the entry point.

Overseas, and out-of-town supporters, flew, or drove in… only to spend the evening uncomfortable, boxed in, and disappointed.

The seating arrangement was a complete mess, with people standing throughout the event, and the only way many guests could see the stage was by climbing onto chairs.

More importantly, what if there had been an emergency or a fire?

Then came the last verse no one wrote: a personal clash in the crowd. Security stepped in. The show ended early … abruptly.

According to the stars of the show, the night was meant to be memorable for music, not for mayhem.

They’re reviewing everything, and promising that any future shows, under their banner, will hit the standard fans actually deserve — comfort, safety, organisation, the whole package.

Fans are already debating it. Some say “Apology accepted, we’ll be back.”

Others, who were greatly inconvenienced, say: “We need to think twice before attending such events!”
