Why not use drones to catch poachers?

There have been recent social media reports, including photos and videos, of poachers killing thirsty animals looking for water inside the Yala National Park. The Wildlife department is short staffed and can’t be expected to monitor water holes round the clock. If they post rangers, poachers will avoid those places and shoot elsewhere.

The best solution is to provide Wildlife with surveillance drones. Unlike military drones, these do not cost the earth. Poaching isn’t limited to Yala. In the dry zone, large numbers of deer and other protected species are still hunted. Then there are the elephants. A few drones watching round the cloth in high risk areas could help catch those who kill elephants, and also monitor rampaging elephants and move them away.

Drones will help reduce the number of injured elephants and reduce the large amounts of money spent by Wildlife to track and treat injured elephants. They could also watch over where elephants habitually cross rail tracks, causing accidents. An early warning system using mobile phones or radio could warn engine drivers if there are elephants on the track.

There are several types of civilian drones available for prices ranging from Rs. 75,000 to Rs. 600,000 or so, from beginner camera drones to mid-range ‘prosumer’ drones costing up to Rs. 550,000. The Wild Life Department could research and select the most cost-effective type, and save itself much time and money, and save our wildlife from ruthless poachers.
