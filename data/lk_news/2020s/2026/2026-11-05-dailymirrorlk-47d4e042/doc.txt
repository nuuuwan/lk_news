Drug-related arrests among children raise alarm as under-18 arrests reach 1,874

Colombo, Nov. 5 (Daily Mirror) - Drug-related arrests among children have increased sharply, with 1,874 people under the age of 18 arrested in connection with 'Ice' by 2025, compared to just one arrest recorded in 2022, President Anura Kumara Dissanayake said today.

Addressing a religious summit at Temple Trees, the President said the figures highlighted the growing threat drugs posed to children and the country's younger generation.

He said authorities had also arrested 11,737 young people over the age of 21, while 10,889 of those arrested were between 18 and 21 years of age.

The figures also included 1,068 children between the ages of 14 and 18 and 13 children between five and 14 years of age.

More than 200 girls had also been arrested in connection with drug-related activities, he said.

The President warned that the drug menace was destroying the younger generation and threatening the country's future.

He said parents had a major responsibility to identify changes in their children's behaviour and prevent them from falling victim to drugs.

The Government was developing a programme through the Ministry of Education to educate parents about the dangers of drug addiction and the warning signs they should look out for.

The President said parents were often the first to recognise changes in their children's behaviour and were in a better position to identify whether they were becoming involved in drug use or criminal activities.

He also called on religious leaders to support families facing drug-related problems, saying parents often approached religious leaders before seeking assistance from the police.

The Government, he said, had organised 259,000 community awareness programmes on drug prevention with the involvement of the Health Department and other relevant institutions.

He said the Government would continue strengthening rehabilitation facilities and other support services to prevent young people from becoming victims of the drug menace.
