India to overhaul tourist visa rules with wider visitor access by year-end

travalmole - India is making it easier for international travelers to visit, with a major overhaul of its visa system that broadens the scope of tourist permits, simplifies applications and introduces more flexible entry conditions.

The government is replacing the traditional Tourist Visa with a new Visitor Visa category, allowing foreign nationals to combine leisure travel with a wider range of short-term activities. This includes business meetings, medical consultations, wellness retreats and cultural courses.

Announced by India Ministry of Home Affairs, the reform reduces India’s main visa categories from 13 to nine and cuts subcategories from 49 to 23. Application forms will also become substantially shorter, with the number of fields reduced by between 65% and 70%.

For tourists, the new Visitor Visa is divided into two categories, V-1 and V-2.

The V-1 permit will be issued for 30 days with multiple entries. Beyond conventional sightseeing, it covers short-term business meetings, medical consultations, wellness retreats, short medical treatments and courses in Indian languages, music, art and crafts. Short-duration yoga programs are also included.

The V-2 category is designed for longer stays, with validity options of one, five or 10 years, depending on eligibility. It covers the activities permitted under V-1, as well as mountaineering. A separate six-month multiple-entry provision will also be available for mountaineering visitors.

The changes could benefit international travelers seeking to combine holidays with India’s growing wellness, cultural and specialist tourism offerings, without having to apply for separate visa categories for certain short-term activities.

India is simultaneously making its medical visa system more flexible. Conventional medical treatment will fall under the M-1 category, while M-3 will cover treatment through AYUSH traditional medicine systems.

Both visas, including their electronic versions, will normally be issued for one year with multiple entries. Separate permits will be available for accompanying attendants.

The measures support India’s broader “Heal in India” initiative, targeting international patients requiring several consultations or extended treatment.

Other reforms streamline visas for foreign students, researchers, investors and business professionals.

India currently offers electronic visas to nationals of 172 countries. Its authorities are updating the technology supporting the new framework, with implementation expected by the end of 2026.

For international tourism operators, the wider visitor provisions could make India a more attractive destination for travelers combining leisure with wellness, education and business activities.
