Houthis claim attack on Saudi oil refinery

ASIA TODAY - Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels have claimed to have attacked a major oil refinery on Saudi Arabia's Red Sea coast. As military tensions rise across the Middle East amid the war between the United States and Iran, Saudi energy infrastructure is also coming under threat.

According to Reuters on November 22 (local time), the Houthis said they had carried out a military operation targeting the Rabigh oil refinery on Saudi Arabia's western Red Sea coast and had directly hit the facility. The extent of any damage and whether operations at the facility have been disrupted remain unconfirmed. Saudi authorities have not commented on the claim.

The Rabigh area is home to Petro Rabigh, an integrated refining and petrochemical complex developed by Saudi state oil company Aramco and Japan's Sumitomo Chemical. The complex also has facilities for exporting petroleum products through a deepwater port. Located on the Red Sea coast, approximately 165 kilometers north of Jeddah, it is a major industrial hub for both oil refining and petrochemical production. Any confirmed strike would therefore cause significant damage to Saudi Arabia's energy industry.

The Houthis have claimed a series of attacks on Saudi energy facilities in recent months. Reported targets include the country's East-West Pipeline and refineries in Jazan in the south and Ras Tanura in the east. However, Saudi authorities have yet to officially confirm attacks on these refineries.

Armed clashes between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia have flared up again since July as the war involving the United States, Israel and Iran has intensified. According to Reuters, the Houthis have seized additional territory controlled by Yemen's internationally recognized government. They have also stepped up missile and drone attacks targeting Saudi Arabia's "85 Airport" and energy facilities. Saudi Arabia, meanwhile, has intensified airstrikes on Houthi strongholds to support its allies in Yemen.

The Red Sea shipping routes near Saudi Arabia's energy facilities are major maritime trade corridors linking Europe and Asia. Analysts warn that if damage from attacks on energy facilities and other infrastructure is confirmed, both crude oil and petroleum product shipping rates and international oil prices could fluctuate sharply.
