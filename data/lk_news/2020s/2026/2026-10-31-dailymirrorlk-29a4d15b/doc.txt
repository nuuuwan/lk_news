Sri Lanka and Türkiye reaffirm commitment to strengthening bilateral relations

Labour Minister and Finance and Planning Deputy Minister Dr. Anil Jayantha Fernando reaffirmed Sri Lanka’s commitment to further strengthening bilateral relations with the Republic of Türkiye, at the celebration of the 103rd anniversary of the Proclamation of the Republic of Türkiye, held on October 29, 2026.

Speaking as the chief guest at the event, Dr. Fernando extended warm congratulations to President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and the government and people of Türkiye on behalf of President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, the government and people of Sri Lanka.

He highlighted the long-standing friendship between Sri Lanka and Türkiye, noting that although formal diplomatic relations were established in 1948, the historical connections between the two countries date back centuries through trade networks linking Asia, Europe and the Indian Ocean region.

Dr. Fernando recalled Türkiye’s swift and compassionate assistance to Sri Lanka following the devastating Indian Ocean tsunami in 2004 and its continued support during Sri Lanka’s economic recovery.

He also expressed appreciation for Türkiye’s consideration of support for reconstruction following Cyclone Dithwah. In turn, Sri Lanka stood in solidarity with Türkiye following the devastating earthquakes of 2023, reflecting the mutual understanding and solidarity between the two nations.

Emphasising the potential for further economic cooperation, Dr. Fernando identified trade, investment, tourism, employment, renewable energy, hospitality and tourism, agro-processing, textiles and maritime logistics as key areas for collaboration. He encouraged Turkish investors and enterprises to explore opportunities in Sri Lanka, including those available through the Colombo Port City and dedicated investment zones.

He noted that the resumption and expansion of direct flights by Turkish Airlines had strengthened tourism and enhanced business-to-business connectivity between the two countries. He also highlighted the importance of private-sector partnerships in promoting joint ventures and creating economic opportunities for both nations.

In this regard, he welcomed the recent visit by the Penthol Group to explore investment opportunities in renewable energy, petroleum, boat building, shipping and textiles, describing it as an encouraging indication of growing interest among Turkish investors in Sri Lanka.

Referring to continued high-level political engagement, Dr. Fernando recalled the meeting between the two countries’ Foreign Ministers on the sidelines of the Seventh Conference on Interaction and Confidence Building Measures in Asia (CICA), held in Baku, Azerbaijan, on October 15, 2026.

He also highlighted the recent visit of Sri Lanka’s Deputy Minister of Foreign Affairs and Foreign Employment to Türkiye, during which productive discussions were held with Turkish Deputy Ministers responsible for Foreign Affairs, Trade, Tourism and Labour. These engagements, he noted, had further strengthened bilateral relations and opened new avenues for cooperation.

Dr. Fernando further noted that the Second Round of Sri Lanka-Türkiye Political Consultations was successfully held in Ankara on May 5, 2026. The consultations provided an opportunity to review progress in bilateral relations and identify new areas for collaboration.

He expressed appreciation for Türkiye’s support for capacity-building and knowledge-sharing, including the recent two-week International Collaboration Study Programme in Türkiye undertaken by a 20-member delegation from Sri Lanka’s Defence Services Command and Staff College. He also conveyed Sri Lanka’s sincere gratitude to the Turkish Ministries of Foreign Affairs and National Defence for their coordination and facilitation of the programme.

In addition, he welcomed the provision of government-funded scholarships for Sri Lankan students to pursue higher education at leading institutions in Türkiye. He noted that such opportunities contribute to human resource development and further strengthen the bonds between the two countries.

Dr. Fernando also acknowledged Türkiye’s consideration of a substantial, long-term reconstruction initiative through the Turkish Cooperation and Coordination Agency (TİKA). He encouraged continued engagement between TİKA and the relevant Sri Lankan authorities to identify priority areas for assistance, taking into account the scale and regional impact of the disaster.

Highlighting the broad scope of bilateral cooperation, he stated that the relationship between Sri Lanka and Türkiye extends across political consultations, trade, investment, tourism, education, capacity-building, humanitarian assistance, defence and multilateral cooperation.

He reaffirmed Sri Lanka’s commitment to creating an environment in which the private sectors of both countries could collaborate effectively through partnerships and joint ventures that generate shared prosperity.
