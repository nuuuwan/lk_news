Inside Sri Lanka’s strategic blueprint for global tourism marketing promotion

For more than a decade, Sri Lanka’s hospitality and tourism sector navigated one of the most turbulent operating environments in modern travel history without a unified, sustained global brand campaign. From unprecedented macroeconomic realignments to the lingering aftereffects of global disruption, the island’s tourism trade relied heavily on private sector resilience, organic word-of-mouth, and tactical crisis management.

Yesterday, that decade-long promotional void officially came to an end. Gathered before an audience of hoteliers, inbound tour operators, airline executives, and international media, the Ministry of Tourism and the Sri Lanka Tourism Promotion Bureau (SLTPB) unveiled a comprehensive, two-stage promotional offensive. Anchored under the thematic identity that Sri Lanka has more to say, with the global declaration that the world is listening, the initiative marks a decisive transition from reactive crisis marketing to a structured, data-driven promotional architecture.

Detailed by SLTPB Chairman Buddhika Hewawasam, the blueprint establishes an immediate Rs. 1.5 billion interim digital and public relations campaign running from August 2026 through April 2027 across six primary source markets, followed by an expansive, multi-year Rs. 5 billion global branding campaign scheduled to roll out in March 2027 and run through February 2029.

“This is not just promises made in the air; it is very much solid, very much structured, very much planned,” Hewawasam said while Deputy Minister of Tourism Prof. Ruwan Ranasinghe reinforced the milestone, noting that “this has been a day that all were waiting for years.”

“Whenever we sit for a press conference, the question that is asked is, ‘Where is the global campaign?’ So it is right here now, and we are launching it here today,” he said.

Sri Lanka enters this promotional era from a position of renewed macroeconomic momentum. In 2025, the destination surpassed its historical arrival benchmarks by welcoming a record 2.36 million visitors and generating 3.2 billion US dollars in tourism revenue. Crucially, that performance was achieved organically across five years of disruption without an active international umbrella campaign. For 2026, the performance trajectory has accelerated significantly, with visitor arrivals crossing 1.4 million by early August. Backed by the immediate boost expected from the interim campaign and expanding flight frequencies, state authorities have formally revised their annual targets upward, projecting over 2.7 million tourist arrivals and 4.2 billion US dollars in foreign exchange revenue by the end of the year.

Looking ahead, the launch of the subsequent global campaign is designed to propel arrivals past the 3 million mark in 2027, yielding over 5 billion US dollars in earnings. Ultimately, this framework builds toward a long-term vision of 5 million arrivals and an annual revenue contribution of 8 billion to 10 billion US dollars by 2030. The core objective, however, extends far beyond headline volume. The primary structural challenge confronting the industry has long been the imbalance between volume and yield. As articulated during the launch, the central priority is attracting quality, high-spending visitors whose daily expenditure and dwell time reflect the true value of the island’s offering, shifting the marketing narrative away from generic destination postcards toward high-yield, experiential engagements.

In his comprehensive presentation, Hewawasam outlined the strategic conversion funnel governing the campaign architecture. The framework operates on a four-step execution sequence: first, defining the target segment in each source market; second, prioritizing the core product across leisure, culture, nature, adventure, wellness, luxury, and MICE; third, mapping the specific Sri Lankan locations that deliver those assets; and fourth, curating the emotional experiences that turn initial traveler interest into a confirmed booking. This conversion funnel directly feeds into four overarching national objectives: generating critical arrival volumes to support hotel occupancies and employment, maximizing per-day tourist spending, expanding geographic distribution into untapped frontiers across the Northern and Eastern provinces, and diversifying the product portfolio beyond traditional sun-and-sand packages.

By addressing these four pillars, the state promotional apparatus aims to dismantle seasonal limitations that have traditionally created sharp peaks and troughs in tourist arrivals. While the southern and western coastlines experience monsoon conditions during specific months, the eastern shores provide nine consecutive months of calm seas and favorable marine conditions, a geographic asset that has historically suffered from inadequate international promotion.

Rather than presenting static historical landmarks or generic landscapes, the creative direction of the campaign pivots entirely around immersive human experiences. Under the concept of seven experiences within one island, the presentation mapped out how distinct pillars align directly with global travel demand.

The leisure pillar focuses on boutique seaside escapes and unhurried coastal living; culture highlights living traditions, folklore, sacred shrines, and culinary storytelling; nature centers on biodiversity, national parks, and marine mammal migrations; adventure covers surfing, white-water rafting, kite surfing in Kalpitiya, and trekking the Pekoe Trail; wellness promotes authentic Ayurvedic retreats and indigenous Hela Wedakama; luxury showcases world-acclaimed boutique villas, heritage tea bungalows, and fine dining; while the MICE pillar positions high-capacity urban convention facilities and incentive retreats.

This creative campaign device is structured to highlight more of culture, flavor, and wildlife, alongside more of delight, connection, and nature, echoing the message that Sri Lanka has more to say. Each pillar is dynamically tailored to source markets, ensuring that Western European campaigns emphasize wellness, nature, and heritage, while campaigns in India and East Asia highlight MICE, destination weddings, and luxury leisure. Unexplored destinations such as Dunhinda, Panama, the Knuckles Mountain Range, and pristine northern locations like Kilinochchi are brought to the forefront to deliver authentic, uncrowded travel experiences.

The execution timeline presented by the SLTPB has been structured to maintain year-round international engagement, avoiding the fragmented, short-burst advertising attempts of the past. The Rs. 1.5 billion interim phase focuses on six primary source markets, beginning immediately in Australia with a six-month digital blitz and public relations campaign running from August 2026 to February 2027.

The Australian rollout serves as the initial operational proof point, combining targeted digital activations with physical trade roadshows across four major Australian cities, Melbourne, Sydney, Perth, and Adelaide, before crossing into Auckland and Christchurch in New Zealand. In September 2026, synchronized eight-month digital and public relations campaigns will launch in the United Kingdom and Germany, coinciding with major global travel trade platforms including the World Travel Market in London and ITB in Berlin. Concurrently, digital and public relations activations will roll out across China and Russia for seven months, integrating platforms like Yandex.

Meanwhile, India will be engaged with a high-frequency, programmatic digital activation starting in October 2026 and running for seven months to capture tech-savvy, weekend-break, and wedding travelers. By April 2027, the digital and public relations machinery will expand into a wider secondary tier of high-value markets, establishing footprints in France, Italy, Spain, the Middle East, the Scandinavian nations, the Benelux region, Poland, Japan, and South Korea. Operating on a dedicated Rs. 5 billion budget, the full-scale global branding campaign will launch in March 2027 following Cabinet clearance and international procurement tenders, deploying a rolling 24-month master brand identity across international broadcast networks, global transit hubs, and digital platforms through February 2029.

To demonstrate the quantitative rigor underpinning the campaign, the presentation highlighted the specific key performance indicators established for the phase one Australia activation. The projected volume and engagement scope target 16 million to 18 million total ad impressions across all channels. The campaign aims for a combined reach of 5 million to 8 million individuals across YouTube, Meta, and TikTok, alongside driving 125,000 to 205,000 web traffic clicks directly to the campaign microsite and securing 4 million to 5 million full video content views. These performance benchmarks establish an operational discipline where every promotional rupee is tied directly to measurable reach, engagement, and referral traffic into destination booking platforms.

While the state blueprint is ambitious, the country’s private hospitality leaders made it clear that the success of the promotional drive will depend on rigorous execution, transparency, and relentless monitoring. Delivering an address on behalf of the hotel sector, The Hotels Association of Sri Lanka (THASL) President Ashok Hettigoda contextualized the massive private capital backing the island’s tourism infrastructure.

“THASL is the biggest investor in Sri Lanka tourism. Over the last several decades, we have invested more than 15 billion US dollars,” Hettigoda stated, noting that member properties span from Jaffna in the north to Dondra Point in the south, ranging from five-room boutique villas to 500-room resorts. Reflecting on the industry’s decade-long wait for a state-led global campaign, he highlighted the fiduciary stakes involved: “Today you have embarked on a dream that we have had for the last 10 years. This dream has been so elusive that sometimes I think you were ignoring our questions or sometimes not meeting us. Honorable Deputy Minister, sometimes we call the Chairman, he is in the office, but he doesn’t come to the phone because he knows exactly what question we are going to ask him! Because as investors, we are responsible for the shareholders to give a return, and that return can only come if the pillars that you mentioned, high-spending tourists, long-stay tourists, are met.”

Hettigoda warned against post-launch complacency, urging authorities to adopt a culture of daily optimization rather than resting on promotional announcements.

“Excellence is not an award; it’s a daily activity, how to do better every day. So this campaign, yes, we have waited for all these years, but we cannot just start napping, thinking that we have launched it. What do we want? We want the best outcome on a daily basis,” Hettigoda stressed. To ensure accountability and track return on investment, the association tabled specific oversight proposals.

“I suggest to the Deputy Minister that we should plan to have a monthly briefing, exactly where we stand. In Australia, this is what we have done; this is the position before, this is the position after. In other countries, are we going in the direction that we expect? What are the other tweaks that we need to do?” he proposed.

Hettigoda also called on the bureau to maintain momentum by hosting a follow-up press conference next month to formally disclose clear tender timelines and structural allocations for the upcoming Rs. 5 billion global campaign.

A central theme of the launch was the understanding that digital advertising cannot function in isolation from commercial connectivity and trade partnerships. Addressing this operational nexus, Sri Lanka Association of Inbound Tour Operators (SLAITO) President Nalin Jayasundera underscored the indispensable role of international airlines and global tour operators. While welcoming the interim launch, Jayasundera offered a concrete industry suggestion to expand the campaign’s immediate reach: “Starting with six important source markets is a strong step, but I believe we should expand this to at least 10 countries without waiting for next year’s global campaign, ensuring our top 10 source markets are actively addressed.”

Jayasundera emphasized that destination marketing must actively obligate global travel distributors to promote Sri Lanka: “The tour operators, of course, promote many different destinations globally, so we need to get the support of the tour operators. We have to work together and carry out joint marketing with the tour operators. Because Sri Lanka is not the only country that is an option as a destination, if we want to get the support of the tour operators, we need to support the tour operator and make the tour operator obliged to support the destination.”

Jayasundera also highlighted the expanding aviation landscape, pointing out that several international carriers are entering or resuming operations to Colombo.

“In the next season, we will have about five new airlines coming into Sri Lanka. We have already, I think started yesterday, we have VietJet, Thai Airways, French Bee, Jetstar, and of course British Airways will start their operations to Sri Lanka,” he noted, urging the state to implement joint promotional mechanisms that support seat capacity and encourage airlines to increase flight frequencies. Bilateral strategic marketing pacts have already been executed with Emirates, with discussions underway to finalize agreements with IndiGo, Turkish Airlines, and Qatar Airways. Simultaneously, Jayasundera emphasized that reaching 5 million arrivals and generating 8 billion to 10 billion US dollars in revenue by 2030 requires unlocking domestic air travel to seamlessly connect coastal resorts with cultural and eastern aerodromes, transforming Sri Lanka into a true year-round destination.

Recognizing the fundamental evolution in how modern journeys are researched and booked, the promotional architecture presented by Hewawasam integrates a sophisticated digital distribution and monitoring ecosystem. The bureau has appointed a production agency to master campaign films alongside digital and public relations agencies for Australia, the United Kingdom, and Germany, with tenders underway for remaining markets. Strategic execution partners include direct alliances with global tech platforms: Meta and TikTok campaigns are deployed via authorized sales partner Roar Global; Google and YouTube search campaigns are managed via Aleph Holdings; and a direct partnership with Yandex provides search coverage across Russia and the CIS region.

To ensure accountability, the Market Development Facility, funded by the Australian Government, is providing local agency oversight, training, and independent campaign monitoring and evaluation systems. Furthermore, the Digital Marketing Association of Sri Lanka is assisting with campaign optimization frameworks and staff training, building on the digital momentum established during the DICTA initiative.

The campaign is also engineered to future-proof Sri Lanka’s search presence as travelers increasingly rely on artificial intelligence assistants, generative chatbots, and short-form video algorithms to plan travel. Hewawasam explained that destination data and regional itineraries are being optimized so that AI platforms accurately index Sri Lanka’s offerings.

“We need the AI platforms to scrape those kind of details, link the right itineraries to the globe. We know all other kind of platforms like ChatGPT and all, right now are being the main source of people to be designing their trips. If somebody is somewhere going to Kilinochchi and if he is typing in ChatGPT, ‘what can I do in Kilinochchi in one day?’, that has to come in those platforms,” Hewawasam noted. Beyond advertising, the strategy integrates a digital nomad promotion supported by a dedicated visa regime, an AI-powered web platform with API scraping capabilities, a creator incubation program empowering 100 local content creators to produce globally relevant visual stories, specialized senior residence visas to drive long-dwell stays, and digital payment integrations via LANKAQR to enable seamless tourist spending at local enterprises.

Perhaps the most significant strategic evolution articulated at the launch is the expansion of the destination brand into a unified nation brand architecture. For decades, Sri Lanka’s international promotion operated in institutional silos, with tourism, the Sri Lanka Tea Board, the Export Development Board, the Board of Investment, and Sri Lanka Cricket marketing the country independently. The new promotional framework brings these entities together under one national story, aligning tourism, tea, trade, investment, culture, and sports into a single brand identity.

As Hewawasam concluded, “The destination brand might be the key nation branding thing in the country. Nation branding is how Sri Lanka positions itself to the world by connecting its distinctive assets, sectors, and institutions into one compelling national story.”
