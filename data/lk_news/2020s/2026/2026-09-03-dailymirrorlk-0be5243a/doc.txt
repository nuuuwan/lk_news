5,000 solar technicians needed annually to meet 2030 renewable energy target: PUCSL

Colombo, Sep. 3 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka needs to produce around 5,000 qualified solar power technicians annually to support the installation of approximately 600 megawatts of solar power capacity each year and achieve its target of generating 70% of electricity from renewable sources by 2030, Public Utilities Commission of Sri Lanka (PUCSL) Chairman Prof. K.P.L. Chandralal said.

He said the rapid expansion of the solar power industry had created an urgent need for a properly trained and professionally qualified workforce.

More than 1,000 companies have obtained licences from the Sri Lanka Sustainable Energy Authority (SLSEA) to import and install solar power systems, while around 10,000 people are currently employed in the sector.

However, Prof. Chandralal said a significant proportion of those working in the industry do not possess recognised professional qualifications.

He warned that the shortage of qualified technicians, limited training facilities and the small number of consultants specialising in solar technology could become major obstacles to the country's renewable energy expansion if not addressed.

To tackle the issue, the SLSEA and PUCSL have launched a programme aimed at producing 5,000 solar technicians, along with 100 instructors and inspectors.

Around 1,500 solar technicians have already been trained, while a special programme is being implemented to provide NVQ Level 3 qualifications in solar technology through the Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) system.

Under the programme, experienced technicians with more than two years of experience in the solar power sector will be able to obtain the NVQ qualification free of charge.

Meanwhile, regulations gazetted by the Sri Lanka Engineering Council will introduce mandatory professional qualifications for solar energy technicians.

Prof. Chandralal said NVQ Level 3 would become the minimum qualification required to practise as a solar energy technician from January 1, 2028, to December 31, 2029.

From January 1, 2030, technicians will be required to possess NVQ Level 4 in Solar Energy Technology and be registered with the Sri Lanka Engineering Council as Engineering Technicians.

He said the requirements would help improve the quality and safety of solar installations while raising professional standards in the rapidly expanding renewable energy sector.

“The expansion of renewable energy must be supported by a workforce with the necessary technical skills and professional qualifications. Ensuring that technicians are properly trained is essential not only for the quality and reliability of solar installations, but also for electrical safety and the long-term sustainability of the renewable energy sector,” Prof. Chandralal said.

Prof. Chandralal also highlighted the introduction of mandatory qualifications for refrigeration, air-conditioning and vehicle air-conditioning technicians.

The National Ozone Unit of the Ministry of Environment and the PUCSL are implementing an island-wide programme to provide free NVQ Level 3 and Level 4 qualifications to technicians in these fields.

The Sri Lanka Engineering Council issued regulations on July 7, 2026, governing the practice of refrigeration and air-conditioning technicians, mobile air-conditioning technicians and qualified technicians in these fields.

Under the regulations, NVQ Level 3 will be the minimum qualification required to practise as a refrigeration, air-conditioning or vehicle air-conditioning technician from January 1, 2028, to December 31, 2029.

From January 1, 2030, technicians will be required to possess the relevant NVQ Level 4 qualification and be registered with the Sri Lanka Engineering Council as Engineering Technicians.

Prof. Chandralal said strengthening professional qualifications in these sectors would improve technical standards and electrical safety while also contributing to Sri Lanka's environmental protection commitments.

He said the programmes would provide experienced technicians with an opportunity to formally recognise their existing skills and experience through nationally recognised vocational qualifications.
