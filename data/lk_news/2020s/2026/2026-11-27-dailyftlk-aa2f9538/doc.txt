AICPA - CIMA Sri Lanka Post Budget Forum charts priorities for reform, investment and growth

Panel discussion moderated by BDO Sri Lanka Managing Partner Ashane Jayasekara which included Jetwing Travels Managing Director Shiromal Cooray, Brandix Group Managing Director Hasitha Premaratne, Finance and Planning Ministry Tax Policy Adviser Thanuja Perera, First Capital Holdings PLC Managing Director/CEO and Janashakthi Group Deputy CEO Dilshan Wirasekara

Govt., business and economic leaders explore implications of Budget 2027 and Sri Lanka’s economic outlook

As Sri Lanka moves from economic stabilisation towards its next phase of growth, the focus is increasingly shifting from policy commitments to implementation, with investment, competitiveness and consistency in the policy environment emerging as key priorities.

These themes took centre stage at the CIMA Post Budget Forum 2027, which brought together senior Government officials, business leaders, economists and finance professionals to examine the implications of the Budget for businesses, investors and Sri Lanka’s broader economic outlook.

Held under the theme “The 2027 Budget and Beyond: Navigating the Future of Business and Growth”, the forum explored the Government’s fiscal direction, the evolving business landscape and the reforms required to build on Sri Lanka’s economic recovery.

Setting the context for the forum, Treasury Secretary Dr. Harshana Suriyapperuma, FCMA (UK), CGMA, delivered the keynote address, outlining the Government’s economic priorities, fiscal direction and broader reform agenda under Budget 2027.

The role of industry and entrepreneurship in driving the next phase of economic growth was addressed by Industry and Entrepreneurship Development Deputy Minister Chathuranga Abeysinghe, FCMA (UK), CGMA. His contribution focused on strengthening domestic enterprise, attracting investment and enhancing industrial competitiveness as Sri Lanka seeks to create a stronger platform for sustainable growth.

Providing a tax and regulatory perspective, KPMG Sri Lanka Principal – Tax and Regulatory Suresh R. I. Perera, FCMA (UK), CGMA, examined proposed changes relating to personal income tax, VAT administration and investment incentives, highlighting their practical implications for businesses, compliance and financial planning.

Against the backdrop of Sri Lanka’s progress towards economic stabilisation, Advocata CEO Dhananath Fernando focused on the structural reforms needed to sustain this momentum. His presentation addressed public-private partnerships, State-owned enterprise reform and investment protection as important elements in strengthening the country’s long-term economic prospects. Bringing the corporate perspective to the discussion, John Keells Holdings Deputy Chairperson and Group Finance Director Gihan Cooray, FCMA (UK), CGMA, examined the commercial implications of Budget 2027, with particular focus on business confidence, capital allocation and long-term investment decisions.

The program culminated in a high-level panel discussion moderated by BDO Sri Lanka Managing Partner Ashane Jayasekara, FCMA (UK), CGMA.

Joining the keynote speaker and presenters were Jetwing Travels Managing Director Shiromal Cooray, FCMA (UK), CGMA; Brandix Group Managing Director Hasitha Premaratne, FCMA (UK), CGMA; Ministry of Finance and Planning Tax Policy Advisor Thanuja Perera; and First Capital Holdings PLC Managing Director and CEO and Janashakthi Group (JXG) Deputy CEO Dilshan Wirasekara.

With representation spanning Government, tourism, manufacturing, financial markets and professional services, the discussion brought a cross-sector perspective to both the opportunities presented by Budget 2027 and the practical challenges surrounding reform implementation.

The conversation reinforced a central message: the impact of Budget 2027 will ultimately depend on how effectively policy commitments are translated into measures that strengthen investor confidence, productivity and competitiveness.

Policy consistency and predictability also remain important as businesses make decisions on capital allocation, expansion and long-term investment.

Ahead of the Budget, AICPA and CIMA submitted recommendations across eight priority areas aimed at strengthening Sri Lanka’s productivity, investment environment and overall economic competitiveness.

Several of these priorities are reflected in measures announced under Budget 2027.

Among the key areas of alignment is State-owned enterprise reform, including the proposed holding-company structure for 34 enterprises. Measures relating to public asset management and public-private partnerships, supported by planned legislation, also reflect areas highlighted through the Association’s pre-Budget advocacy.

Trade and investment facilitation represents another area of convergence, including funding for the National Single Window for Trade, while efforts to pursue RCEP membership and expand trade agreements are expected to support greater international market access.

Support for the SME sector is also prominent, with Rs. 67.6 billion announced for interest subsidies and refinancing, alongside taxation measures addressing areas including high-value assets and cross-border digital services. The inclusion of these measures is encouraging. However, achieving their full economic potential will depend on effective implementation, institutional accountability and sustained policy consistency.

Through its pre-Budget advocacy and Post-Budget Forum, AICPA and CIMA continues to bring the expertise of the accounting and finance profession into Sri Lanka’s economic policy dialogue, connecting policymakers with the practical perspectives of businesses, investors and finance professionals.

The post-Budget forum provides a platform to move the conversation beyond the measures announced in the Budget to consider what they mean in practice for enterprise, investment and economic growth. As Sri Lanka looks beyond stabilisation, the challenge is increasingly one of execution. Turning reform into results will be critical to building business confidence, attracting investment and strengthening Sri Lanka’s competitiveness on the path towards sustainable growth.
