CAASL to amend Finance Act to recover Rs. 27.6 bn  in unpaid embarkation levies

The Civil Aviation Authority of Sri Lanka (CAASL) is taking steps to amend the Finance Act to regain the statutory authority needed to recover Rs. 27.6 billion in outstanding embarkation taxes from defaulting airlines.

The embarkation levy is collected directly from departing passengers, with the authority charging a fee of US$ 60 for departures from the Bandaranaike International Airport and US$ 30 for departures from the Colombo International Airport, Ratmalana, and the Jaffna International Airport.

According to a government progress report titled Parliamentary Series No. 394, which details measures taken regarding recommendations by the Committee on Public Enterprises (COPE), six out of the twenty-three airlines required to pay the tax have consistently failed to remit these passenger-collected funds since 2022.

The national carrier, SriLankan Airlines, accounts for the vast majority of this shortfall, owing Rs. 24.6 billion. The remaining defaulters include Fits Aviation with Rs. 2,126 million in arrears, Aeroflot Russia owing Rs. 508 million, Thai AirAsia with Rs. 221 million, AirAsia Berhad owing Rs. 88 million, and Air India with Rs. 57 million.

The regulatory body recently discovered a critical legislative loophole hindering its recovery efforts when it sought legal advice to determine its power to initiate proceedings against the non-compliant carriers. Highlighting this legal hurdle, the official report states that the Attorney General informed the authority that “neither the Civil Aviation Authority Act nor the Finance Act, No. 25 of 2003 contains provisions enabling the Authority to take such legal action”.

The relevant legal mechanisms for recovery that previously existed were removed through amendments made to the Finance Act in 2003.

In light of this situation, the regulatory authority, in collaboration with the General Treasury, has initiated action to reintroduce the necessary statutory recovery provisions into law. A draft bill required to amend the Finance Act was submitted to the Fiscal Policy Department of the General Treasury in August 2025 for further action.

In the interim, the authority has sought a firm policy decision from the line ministry, in consultation with the Treasury, regarding the massive outstanding dues payable by SriLankan Airlines. Concurrently, Fits Aviation has submitted a proposal outlining a plan to settle its outstanding balances, while final administrative reminders have been issued to all defaulting carriers warning of the pending regulatory shifts.
