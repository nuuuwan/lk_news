Plantation sector steps up investment drive to boost productivity

The plantation sector is stepping up investments in replanting, processing capacity and renewable energy as the Regional Plantation Companies (RPCs) push to improve productivity and strengthen their competitiveness in the global markets.

The investment drive also extends to environmental certification and solar power installations, reflecting the efforts by the plantation companies to adapt to the changing international market requirements, according to the Planters’ Association of Ceylon (PAC).

The industry seeks to expand investment, improve productivity and strengthen the policy framework governing the sector, priorities expected to feature at the PAC’s 172nd Annual General Meeting on September 19.

Alongside the investments aimed at strengthening the existing plantation operations, according to the PA, the RPCs are increasingly diversifying beyond the traditional crops, in search of higher-value opportunities.

While tea remains the backbone of Sri Lanka’s agricultural exports, the plantation companies are expanding into oil palm, coffee, avocado, berries, pepper, cinnamon, cardamom and cocoa.

The PAC said the diversification is opening opportunities in premium international markets while reducing the reliance on traditional commodities.

The developments point to a broader repositioning of the plantation industry, combining the productivity-enhancing investments with crop diversification and greater exposure to higher-value export markets.

Policy will also form part of the industry’s longer-term agenda. The PAC said it continues to engage with the government and other stakeholders on the policies affecting the sector’s long-term viability.
