Logistics carries Aitken Spence as tourism sinks deeper into loss

Aitken Spence PLC’s logistics and strategic investment businesses carried the conglomerate through a difficult quarter for tourism, helping the attributable profit nearly triple even as the group revenue slipped.

The profit attributable to the shareholders surged 172 percent year-on-year to Rs.1.45 billion in the three months ended June 30, 2026, from Rs.533.7 million, according to the group’s interim financial statements.

Overall group profit, including the losses attributable to the minority shareholders, increased 158 percent to Rs.1.04 billion. The difference reflected a Rs.407.1 million loss attributable to the non-controlling interests, which widened from Rs.128.4 million a year earlier.

The stronger bottom line came despite the group revenue declining 1.2 percent to Rs.18.96 billion, from Rs.19.19 billion. Net revenue eased to Rs.18.53 billion, from Rs.18.71 billion.

The earnings were lifted by a sharp increase in other operating income to Rs.1.98 billion, from Rs.223.8 million. While the filing did not provide a breakdown of the increase, the group’s cash-flow disclosures showed a net foreign-exchange gain of Rs.784.9 million, compared with Rs.167.6 million a year earlier.

The operating profit rose 33 percent to Rs.2.05 billion, even as the employee costs increased 14 percent to Rs.5.10 billion and the indirect operating expenses climbed 8.8 percent to Rs.4.27 billion.

A higher contribution from the equity-accounted investees also supported the earnings. The group’s share of profit from these businesses increased 74 percent to Rs.941.9 million, from Rs.540.3 million.

The gains outweighed a 16 percent increase in finance expenses to Rs.1.75 billion. The net finance costs rose to Rs.1.25 billion, from Rs.1.08 billion.

Profit before tax climbed 74 percent to Rs.1.74 billion, while the income-tax charge rose at a slower 17 percent to Rs.699.7 million.

Tourism remained the group’s weakest-performing segment during the quarter. The external revenue from the business fell 10 percent to Rs.11.57 billion, while the segment recorded an operating loss of Rs.161.6 million, reversing an operating profit of Rs.539.2 million a year earlier.

After the finance costs and tax, the tourism segment’s loss widened to Rs.1.27 billion, from Rs.447.8 million.

Maritime and freight logistics emerged as the largest operating profit contributor. The external revenue increased 28 percent to Rs.4.61 billion, while the operating profit rose 56 percent to Rs.1.26 billion. Profit after tax from the segment climbed 68 percent to Rs.1.32 billion.

Strategic investments recorded the sharpest improvement, with the operating profit rising to Rs.836.1 million, from Rs.20.1 million. The segment posted a profit after tax of Rs.568.2 million, reversing a loss of Rs.161.9 million a year earlier, despite the external revenue growing by only 3.1 percent to Rs.2.31 billion.

The services segment’s profit after tax increased 90 percent to Rs.425.9 million, helped by higher earnings from the equity-accounted investees. Its operating profit, however, declined 35 percent to Rs.111.5 million.

The improvement in the reported profit did not translate into stronger cash generation. The net cash used in the operating activities widened to Rs.2.54 billion, from Rs.1.16 billion, as the higher interest and tax payments compounded the working-capital outflows.

Total assets rose 1.8 percent from end-March to Rs.231.76 billion, while total equity increased 2 percent to Rs.106.37 billion.

The interest-bearing borrowings, including the bank overdrafts and other short-term facilities, increased to Rs.67.25 billion, from Rs.64.34 billion at end-March.
