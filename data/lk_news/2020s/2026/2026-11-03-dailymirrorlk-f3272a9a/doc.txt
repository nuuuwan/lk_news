Should Chief Prelates Intervene to Secure Gnanasara Thera’s Release?

According to reports, the protest by monks at Vihara Maha Devi Park was called off following assurances attributed to the Chief Prelates that steps would be taken to secure the release of the imprisoned Gnanasara Thera.

This raises an important question: Why should the Chief Prelates seek special intervention for one monk when other Buddhist monks are also serving prison sentences? If compassion and clemency are warranted, should they not be extended fairly, without favouritism?

It is understandable that religious leaders may appeal for mercy. However, such appeals should not be perceived as attempts to secure preferential treatment for an individual because of his prominence or influence. The principle that the law must apply equally to all is fundamental to a democratic society.

If there are legitimate grounds for a pardon, sentence reduction or other legal relief, the appropriate authorities should consider them in accordance with established procedures. Any such decision must be based on the merits of the case, not public pressure or religious status.

Why has Gnanasara Thera’s imprisonment attracted such intense attention, while comparable concern appears less evident in the cases of other imprisoned monks? Compassion should not be selective, and justice should not operate by double standards.

As the saying goes, what is sauce for the goose is sauce for the gander. The credibility of religious leadership, as well as public confidence in the justice system, depends on consistency, fairness and respect for the law.
