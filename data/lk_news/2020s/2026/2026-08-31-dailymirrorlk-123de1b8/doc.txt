Atlas Angel II brings US, Sri Lankan forces together

Colombo, Aug. 31 (Daily Mirror) - The United States and Sri Lanka have commenced Atlas Angel II, a joint military exercise aimed at strengthening cooperation and preparedness for humanitarian assistance and disaster response.

The exercise, being held from August 31 to September 4, brings together U.S. and Sri Lankan military personnel and experts for training in C-130 aircraft maintenance, urban search and rescue, incident command systems, construction engineering, landslide response and chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear and explosive hazard response.

Montana National Guard teams, led by Assistant Adjutant General for Air Brigadier General Trace Thomas and Assistant Adjutant General for Army Brigadier General Renea Dorvall, are participating alongside personnel from the Sri Lanka Air Force and U.S. Pacific Air Forces. Observers from the United Kingdom and Bangladesh are also taking part.

The exercise is being conducted at Sri Lanka Air Force Base Katunayake and in Kandy, with a focus on improving communication, coordination and interoperability during real-world disasters.

U.S. Ambassador to Sri Lanka Eric Meyer said the exercise reflects the strong partnership between the two countries and will help strengthen their ability to respond effectively when disasters occur.

Sri Lanka Air Force Commander Air Marshal Bandu Edirisinghe said natural disasters know no borders and highlighted the importance of countries working together to assist affected communities.

The exercise builds on the State Partnership between the Montana National Guard and Sri Lanka established in 2021. Cooperation was further strengthened through a memorandum of understanding signed in November 2025 covering disaster response, aviation and maritime security.

Atlas Angel II will also feature joint performances involving musicians from the U.S. Air Forces Band of the Pacific and their Sri Lankan counterparts, supporting people-to-people ties between the two countries.
