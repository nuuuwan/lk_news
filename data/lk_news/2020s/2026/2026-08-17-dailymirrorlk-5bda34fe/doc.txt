Yala national park to get major development boost to ease tourist congestion

Colombo, Aug. 17 (Daily Mirror) - A special development project is to be implemented at the Yala National Park to improve its physical and human resources while reducing congestion and improving facilities for local and foreign tourists.

Minister of Environment Dr. Dammika Patabendi held a discussion yesterday (16) with officials of the Department of Wildlife Conservation on the proposed project, which aims to introduce a new physical development plan for the park in line with international standards.

The discussion was held at the Palatupana Entrance in Zone 2 of the Yala National Park.

Secretary to the Ministry of Environment K.R. Uduwawala, Additional Director General of the Department of Wildlife Conservation (Operations) Manjula Amaranath, Additional Secretary of the Wildlife Division Udaya Kumara and senior ministry officials participated in the discussion.

Officials presented a physical development plan incorporating both short- and long-term objectives. The plan focuses on improving the park's infrastructure and making it more attractive, efficient and sustainable.

Particular attention was given to improving parking facilities for vehicles entering the park, upgrading service centres and developing facilities that can better cater to the growing number of visitors.

The proposed improvements include the park entrance, information centre, additional ticket counters, museum, sanitary facilities, souvenir shops and accommodation facilities for park staff.

The Department of Wildlife Conservation is also focusing on filling existing vacancies and strengthening its human resources to support the implementation of the development plan.

Minister Patabendi instructed officials to address the issues identified under the plan without delay and to commence the relevant projects as soon as the necessary approvals are obtained.

Several locations within the national park were also inspected during the minister's visit, with attention given to existing infrastructure problems and the need to improve facilities for both local and foreign tourists.
