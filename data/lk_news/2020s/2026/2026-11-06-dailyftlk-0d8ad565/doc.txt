2027 Budget: From economic recovery to prosperity – what the nation should expect

The 12 November Budget must go beyond fiscal consolidation and revenue collection to deliver meaningful relief to households, strengthen businesses, stimulate investment and translate economic recovery into

Sri Lanka’s 2027 Budget arrives at a pivotal stage of economic recovery. Since the 2022 crisis, the country has restored macroeconomic stability, strengthened public finances and resumed growth. However, recovery remains incomplete, with households and businesses continuing to face financial pressures. Presented on 12 November, 2026, the Budget 2027 offers an opportunity to move beyond fiscal stabilisation towards inclusive growth, employment, productivity and improved living standards.

The challenge is to transform economic recovery into tangible prosperity for the nation.

A national Budget is the Government’s annual financial and economic policy statement, outlining revenue, expenditure, borrowing and development priorities. It reflects the Government’s economic direction and commitment to national development. Through taxation, public spending, subsidies and investment, the Budget directly affects individuals, businesses and the wider economy—shaping living costs, public services, investment, employment and economic growth. Ultimately, a Budget’s success should be measured not merely by revenue collection or fiscal surpluses, but by its ability to transform public resources into tangible economic and social benefits.

Sri Lanka’s present circumstances demand a careful balance between economic discipline and public welfare.

According to the World Bank’s October 2026 Sri Lanka Development Update, the economy grew by 4.7% in the first half of 2026, with projected growth of 4.4% in 2026 and 4.2% in 2027. Although real GDP has returned to pre-crisis levels, poverty remains elevated, labour-market recovery is incomplete and household welfare improvements remain uneven. Budget 2027 must therefore move beyond macroeconomic stabilisation to strengthen sustainable growth, employment generation and inclusive economic development.

Despite improved economic stability, households continue to face financial pressures from food, transport, electricity, healthcare, education and housing costs. The cumulative impact of the economic crisis and fiscal adjustments has weakened purchasing power. Budget 2027 should therefore prioritise household affordability, improved public services and targeted social protection while maintaining fiscal sustainability.

Businesses continue to face higher taxation, compliance costs, financing constraints and operational uncertainties. Private-sector investment is vital for employment, exports, innovation and productivity. Budget 2027 should therefore promote a predictable, business-friendly environment that encourages investment, expansion, technological advancement and sustainable economic growth.

Sri Lanka’s recovery remains closely linked to its IMF-supported program and debt restructuring commitments. Maintaining fiscal discipline, strengthening revenue mobilisation and ensuring sustainable expenditure are essential. However, excessive taxation without improved public expenditure efficiency could weaken consumption, investment and public confidence. Budget 2027 must therefore balance fiscal responsibility with inclusive economic growth and improved public welfare.

The primary surplus reached 5.4% of GDP, significantly exceeding the 2.3% program target, while revenue and grants rose to 16.7% against the projected 14.7%. These achievements reflect improved fiscal management and revenue mobilisation (IMF, Sri Lanka Fifth and Sixth Reviews under the Extended Fund Facility, 2026).

Government revenue improved through economic recovery, stronger tax compliance and increased motor vehicle imports in 2025, which contributed approximately 2.8% of GDP in revenue, according to the IMF. However, this exceptional revenue reflects pent-up demand following import restrictions and may not be sustainable. Budget 2027 must therefore strengthen long-term revenue sustainability through broader tax compliance, economic expansion and a more productive tax base, rather than excessive reliance on exceptional or consumption-driven taxation.

The Government has continued modernising tax administration through expanded taxpayer registration, improved compliance and digital revenue collection. RAMIS 2.0 and enhanced VAT compliance are important developments. However, their effectiveness depends on efficient implementation, taxpayer accessibility and improved service delivery. Budget 2027 should further strengthen a transparent, efficient and taxpayer-friendly system that encourages voluntary compliance, economic activity and public confidence.

Taxation will be a key focus of Budget 2027, with households and businesses expecting tax relief, greater policy predictability and improved administration. However, significant tax reductions must be balanced against revenue requirements, debt sustainability and IMF commitments. Beyond taxation: What should the Budget deliver to the general public?

The Budget should examine the affordability of essential goods and services and the efficiency of existing social support mechanisms. Targeted support for low-income households, vulnerable groups and economically disadvantaged communities would be more sustainable than poorly targeted subsidies.

Adequate allocations for healthcare, education and public infrastructure are essential for long-term development. The Government should prioritise improvements in service quality, accessibility and efficiency rather than focusing exclusively on increasing expenditure.

Sri Lanka requires employment opportunities that provide sustainable incomes and improve household economic security. The Budget should encourage private-sector investment, vocational training, entrepreneurship and productive employment opportunities.

Agriculture remains essential for livelihoods, food security and rural development. Investment in irrigation, agricultural technology, storage facilities, value addition, logistics and market access could reduce wastage and improve farmer incomes.

Affordable housing, transport infrastructure, urban development and regional connectivity are important components of inclusive growth. Well-designed infrastructure investment can create employment while improving the productive capacity of the economy.

A successful Budget should be assessed against measurable outcomes rather than the number of proposals announced. The following indicators would provide a meaningful framework for evaluating its performance:

Economic growth: Sustaining growth through productive investment and private-sector expansion.

Household welfare: Improving real incomes, purchasing power and access to essential services.

Public expenditure efficiency: Improving the quality and effectiveness of Government spending.

Export competitiveness: Supporting diversification, value addition and foreign exchange earnings.

Social protection: Ensuring that vulnerable communities benefit from economic recovery.

Conclusion: A Budget that must deliver more than economic stability

Sri Lanka’s economic recovery provides an opportunity for national transformation. Budget 2027 must convert fiscal stability into tangible benefits through improved living standards, stronger public services, investment, employment and business growth. For citizens, the priority is affordability and economic security; for businesses, predictable taxation and investment opportunities; and for the nation, sustainable growth and prosperity.

The true success of Budget 2027 will be measured not by revenue collected, but by how effectively public resources are transformed into prosperity and opportunity for Sri Lanka.
