State research institutions face shortage of skilled staff, Committee told

Colombo, Nov. 12 (Daily Mirror) - State research and development institutions are facing shortages of qualified human resources, with officials highlighting the need for improved salary and allowance schemes to attract and retain engineers and researchers.

The issue was discussed at a recent meeting of the Sectoral Oversight Committee on Science, Technology and Digital Transformation held in Parliament under the chairmanship of MP Dr. Janaka Senarathna.

Officials stressed the importance of improving the performance of State research institutions, addressing existing shortcomings and introducing appropriate measures to manage research outcomes more effectively.

The Committee also discussed the possibility of consolidating institutions carrying out similar functions to improve efficiency and avoid duplication.

The need to provide better incentives for qualified professionals was highlighted, with officials pointing out that suitable remuneration schemes were essential to retain skilled personnel in the research and development sector.

The Committee reviewed several institutional reports, including the Annual Report of the National Engineering Research and Development Centre for 2022, the National Science and Technology Commission for 2023, the Sri Lanka Inventors Commission for 2024 and the Ministry of Science and Technology's Annual Performance Report for 2025.

The discussions also covered the need to strengthen research institutions and improve their contribution to national development.
