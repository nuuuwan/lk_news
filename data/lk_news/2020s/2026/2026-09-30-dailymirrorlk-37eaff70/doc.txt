New Bill to reform governance structure of Sri Lanka Cricket

Colombo, Sep. 30 (Daily Mirror) - The Government is preparing to introduce a new Bill aimed at completely reforming the governance structure of Sri Lanka Cricket, the Ministerial Consultative Committee on Youth Affairs and Sports was informed.

The matter was discussed at a recent meeting of the Committee, chaired by Youth Affairs and Sports Minister Sunil Kumara Gamage. It was revealed that arrangements were underway to gazette the proposed legislation and present it to Parliament.

The proposed governance structure will include independent directors, while District Cricket Associations will no longer participate directly in decision-making with Sri Lanka Cricket. Instead, they will operate through Provincial Cricket Associations.

The Committee was also informed that Sri Lanka Cricket allocates around Rs. 900 million annually, along with equipment, for school cricket.

Plans are also being formulated in consultation with the Ministry of Education, Higher Education and Vocational Education to formalise the recruitment of coaches and the player selection process.

The Committee reviewed progress on implementing recommendations made by the Auditor General concerning sports associations. Of the associations reviewed, 33 have completed the relevant recommendations, while reports are awaited from another 33 associations.

Meanwhile, the Ministry has instructed that fees charged for the use of walking tracks and sports complexes for public health and exercise activities be suspended with immediate effect.

The Committee was told that sports facilities should be maintained as a government responsibility to promote public health and community welfare, rather than being operated as profit-making ventures.

The Ministry also plans to provide at least one synthetic running track in each province, with the next track scheduled to be constructed in Anuradhapura. It was noted that Sri Lanka currently has only two synthetic running tracks.

The Committee also discussed the development of sports facilities in Ampara, the upgrading of Weber Stadium in Batticaloa and the Hingurakgoda Sports Complex, improvements to sports facilities in Hali-Ela, Badulla, and issues concerning the Bandaragama Public Grounds in Kalutara.

It was further informed that government funding for international competitions would be provided only to official national teams representing Sri Lanka and for officially recognised international tournaments.

The Committee also stressed that there would be no discrimination based on race or region in selecting national teams or sending athletes and teams to overseas competitions.
