Subsidy, price caps or hike? Govt. weighs fuel options

Colombo, Sep. 15 (Daily Mirror) - The government is considering several measures to address pressure on domestic fuel prices amid rising global crude oil costs, Power and Energy Minister Anura Karunathilaka has said.

The options include providing state fuel subsidies, similar to measures introduced in April and May, setting upper and lower price limits for private fuel distributors, or revising retail fuel prices.

The Minister said the government is assessing the available options before reaching a final decision.

“The Middle East conflict has pushed global oil prices up, while domestic prices remain significantly lower. Several private fuel distributors say they cannot sustain operations under the current pricing structure, and the government is considering future measures,” the Minister said.

During periods of sharp price increases in April and May, the government provided fuel subsidies of Rs. 100 per litre for diesel and Rs. 20 per litre for petrol.

“Therefore, we may either adjust prices directly or allow CPC to sell at a lower price while setting upper and lower price limits for other fuel distributors,” the minister said.

According to the Minister, several private fuel companies have informed authorities that they are unable to sustain operations under the current pricing structure. The companies have reportedly cited losses ranging from Rs. 140 to Rs. 170 per litre from diesel and requested revisions to fuel prices.

“Companies have submitted different figures, but no final decision has been made. Our priority remains ensuring consumers get fuel at fair, competitive prices,” he said.

The Minister said the government also recognises the financial difficulties faced by private distributors while seeking to maintain competitive prices for consumers.

He said that the government's priority is to prevent a sudden financial burden on consumers while ensuring an uninterrupted fuel supply.

Meanwhile, the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC) said that it is currently making no loss on petrol sales but is incurring a loss of around Rs. 60 per litre on diesel, CPC Chairman D.J. Rajakaruna said.

Speaking to the media, Rajakaruna said diesel pricing had emerged as a key challenge for the state-owned petroleum distributor, but said that the CPC was able to absorb the losses through profits generated from its refinery operations and the sale of jet fuel and other petroleum products.

He said the CPC’s refinery operations had recently generated significant profits, providing the corporation with the financial capacity to offset losses incurred on diesel.

The Chairman also highlighted the CPC’s competitive procurement process. According to Rajakaruna, the corporation obtains fuel through competitive tenders involving around 70 suppliers, helping it secure supplies at competitive prices and maintain tighter cost margins.

Rajakaruna also said there was no immediate need for a sudden fuel price increase. He explained that the fuel currently being sold had been ordered during the previous month, allowing the CPC to manage its costs under existing pricing conditions.

Despite the losses on diesel, Rajakaruna maintained that the CPC remains capable of balancing its overall finances through revenue generated from its petroleum operations.
