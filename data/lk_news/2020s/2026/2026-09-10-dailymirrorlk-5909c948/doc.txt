Let the Courts Decide, not social media

Sri Lanka appears to have developed an unhealthy habit of turning matters before the courts into political battles on social media. Depending on their political loyalties, people openly take sides—some defending the accused while others condemn them even before the courts have reached a decision.

This is deeply concerning. A courtroom should not have a parallel courtroom on social media. When a matter is sub judice, public commentary that seeks to influence opinion or prejudge the outcome can undermine confidence in the administration of justice.

Freedom of expression is a fundamental right, but it must be exercised responsibly. Political allegiance should never determine whether we respect the independence and authority of the judiciary.

It is time for the Supreme Court and the relevant authorities to examine this growing practice and take appropriate steps, within the law, to prevent social media from becoming a platform for trying cases before the courts have done so.

Let the accused be judged by the courts—not by political camps on social media.
