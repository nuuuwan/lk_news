Compassion is the Essence of Buddhism -- A Reply to ‘Citizen’

This is with reference to the letter by ‘CITIZEN’ in the Daily Mirror replying to Mr. Upali Weerasinghe’s  proposal (DM) to pardon Ven. Gnanasara Thero.

While I respect the views of ‘CITIZEN’, his argument misses the core of Buddhist philosophy. The writer himself admits Angulimala killed 999 people. The fact that he attained Arahanthship after his crimes is precisely the point Mr. Weerasinghe made. Buddhism believes in transformation. If Buddha had judged Angulimala only by his past, there would be no Angulimala Thero. The question is not whether Ven. Gnanasara is a saint. The question is, what does Buddhism teach us to do? The Dhammapada says “Na hi verena verani sammantidha - Hatred is never appeased by hatred, but by non-hatred alone.”

Yes, the Thero’s behaviour inside the court concerning Mrs. Ekneligoda and his remarks on other religions were unbecoming of a Bhikkhu. The Courts have rightly punished him for that. No one is asking to condone his actions.

But punishment in Buddhism is for correction, not for vengeance. The concepts of Karuna, Metta, and Muditha are not shown only to the perfect. They must be shown precisely when it is difficult. A Presidential pardon does not declare him innocent. It declares that as Buddhists, we still believe a Bhikkhu can be reformed, just as Angulimala was reformed.

If we close the door to compassion, are we following the Buddha or the citizen? It is sad that such an interesting view point is written by hiding his true name to public.

“STRIVE RELENTLESSLY/BE WISE” – these are the Buddha’s own words.

The government should Be Wise as true Buddhists and be flexible on this important issue which is being watched by the global Buddhists community.
