Minister questions PUCSL public hearing despite no tariff hike proposal

Colombo, November 06 (Daily Mirror)- Energy Minister Anura Karunathilaka yesterday questioned the Public Utilities Commission of Sri Lanka (PUCSL) decision to hold public hearing on electricity tariffs, despite the National System Operator (NSO) having decided not to seek a tariff revision for the fourth quarter of 2026.

Addressing Parliament, the Minister assured that electricity tariffs would not need to be increased this year, based on available data and forecasts.

He described the PUCSL's decision to proceed with public hearing, despite the NSO's position, as a waste of public funds.

The Minister said the hearing would merely provide an opportunity for certain opposition politicians, defeated politicians and some YouTubers to express their views.

"We wonder whether the decision to hold public consultations was taken by the members of the Commission or its officials. People with vested interests may express various opinions. However, the Government and institutions in the electricity sector work for the benefit of the people. Based on available data and forecasts, we see no need for another electricity tariff increase this year," he said.

Explaining the procedure, Karunathilaka said the NSO normally submits its projected electricity generation costs to the PUCSL on a quarterly basis.

He said the NSO had informed the PUCSL in a letter dated October 6 that the projected generation cost had declined from Rs.129 billion to Rs.125 billion, following its routine submission, thereby removing the need for a tariff revision.

While acknowledging that the PUCSL Act provided for public hearing in the public interest, the Minister questioned the need for a public hearing when the NSO had already indicated that no tariff increase was necessary.

Meanwhile, at a stakeholder consultation held yesterday on the proposed electricity tariff revision for the fourth quarter of 2026, the NSO (Pvt) Ltd reiterated that it would not seek a tariff increase for the October–December period.

The NSO said its decision was based on prevailing reservoir storage levels and forecasts of above-normal rainfall under the prevailing El Niño conditions.
