A call to regulate small scale food outlets

Food safety concerns have been highlighted in multiple food outlets across the island. The recent incident reported in the Daily Mirror included three restaurants in Mannar. A fine of Rs. 95,000 was imposed on the business owners. Charges including preparing food without valid food and medical certificates. In addition wastewater was pumped into the main drainage system. One thing that most restaurants forget is that hygiene is of utmost importance. One stale item or unhygienic move would ultimately impact another person’s health. Many believe that the food business is a lucrative one where providing substandard food at higher prices could earn them profits. But having a hygienic meal is a right of every citizen. The fact that many restaurants fail to adhere to hygiene compliances, sometimes infested with flies and rats, stray animals such as cats expose harrowing realities in the food industry. Many of these restaurants fall into the category of fast food outlets that serve fried rice and kottu on their menu. Therefore it is the duty of the Consumer Affairs Authority and other relevant public institutions to regulate these food outlets and ensure that they provide a quality service to customers.
