Colombo launches joint operation to tackle flooding; 60 drainage blockages identified

A special joint operation involving several State institutions has been launched to address flooding and flash floods in Colombo and its suburbs, with 60 locations where water drainage is obstructed identified for urgent action.

The programme was launched today (8) under the leadership of Colombo Mayor Vrai Kelly Balthazar and Deputy Minister of Urban Development Eranga Gunasekara.

The initiative covers five electoral divisions under the Colombo Municipal Council (CMC), namely North Colombo, East Colombo, West Colombo, Borella and Central Colombo.

Authorities have begun clearing drainage blockages, cleaning canals and restoring drainage systems, giving priority to areas facing a high risk of flooding.

Initial operations are being carried out at several locations, including Kirimandala Mawatha in Narahenpita, Haig Road in Bambalapitiya, Collingwood Place and Parakum Road in Wellawatte, the Cooray Urban Housing Scheme, Stafford Road in Kirulapone and the adjoining canal.

Other locations include Heen Ela near Baseline Road, Aramaya Road in Dematagoda, the New Bazaar area in Central Colombo, De Mel Watta and Paradise Road.

Personnel from the Sri Lanka Army, Colombo Municipal Council, Sri Lanka Land Development Corporation, Urban Development Authority and Road Development Authority are participating in the operation.

Officials said the programme was not limited to removing immediate blockages but would also address long-standing drainage issues through repairs, regular maintenance and better coordination among State institutions.

The initiative comes after heavy rainfall on November 6 left several parts of Colombo and its suburbs inundated for hours, disrupting traffic and affecting daily activities.

Pettah, Dematagoda, Orugodawatte, Kollupitiya, Bambalapitiya and Kompanna Veediya were among the areas affected by the flooding.

Authorities have identified several factors contributing to the problem, including poor maintenance of drainage systems, unauthorised constructions and land reclamation, unplanned urban development, the dumping of waste into canals and drainage networks, and inadequate coordination among relevant institutions.

The Government plans to bring the maintenance, rehabilitation and monitoring of drainage systems under a coordinated approach instead of allowing individual institutions to operate separately.

The programme aims to provide immediate relief from flash floods while establishing a long-term mechanism to reduce Colombo's vulnerability to flooding.

Deputy Minister of Public Security and Parliamentary Affairs Sunil Watagala, MP for Colombo District, was also present to oversee the field operations.

CMC Deputy Mayor Hemantha Weerakoon, local government representatives, officials from participating institutions, work crews and residents also attended the programme.
