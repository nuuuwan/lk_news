Guardian completes Rs.657mn buyback, but NAV discount widens to 48%

Ceylon Guardian Investment Trust PLC has completed its Rs.657 million share repurchase, with around 70 percent of the payout flowing to parent Carson Cumberbatch PLC, even as the stock’s discount to net asset value (NAV), the gap the board cited in launching the buyback, widened to about 48 percent, as per filings and market data.

A Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) notification dated November 5 confirmed the listed investment company bought back the maximum 1,510,529 ordinary shares in its fourth repurchase since 2022, trimming its ordinary share count by 1.9 percent to 78,547,462. At Rs.406.43 a share, equal to its audited NAV as at end-March, the ordinary shares cost Rs.613.9 million, while 105,609 unlisted deferred shares accounted for a further Rs.42.9 million.

Carson Cumberbatch, which holds about 68 percent of the ordinary shares across two accounts, tendered exactly its pro-rata entitlement of 1,031,167 shares for Rs.419.1 million, directors’ disclosures filed with the CSE on November 6 revealed.

The remaining 479,362 ordinary shares, worth Rs.194.8 million, came from other shareholders. That tally is marginally above their combined 1-for-53 entitlement of about 479,360 shares, which indicates that requests to sell additional shares helped fill the offer.

However, the exercise has so far done little to close the valuation gap. Guardian’s shares closed at Rs.214.25 on Friday, 7 percent below the end-June close of Rs.230.25 and 48 percent below its June 30 NAV of Rs.412.10, leaving the repurchase price almost 90 percent above the current market price. The discount had narrowed from 62 percent in March 2024 to 34 percent by September 2025, before widening again to 44 percent by June, as per company data.

In its offer document, the board described regular repurchases as a means of offering liquidity and “potentially narrowing the gap between market price and NAV”.

Meanwhile, Guardian, which holds 66.6 percent of Ceylon Investment PLC, likewise tendered exactly its 1-for-54 entitlement of 1,198,372 shares into its subsidiary’s parallel buyback at Rs.203.33 a share, a separate filing showed. The Rs.243.7 million it received, two-thirds of Ceylon Investment’s Rs.366 million payout was equivalent to 37 percent of Guardian’s own payout, with cash flowing up the Carsons group.

Funded from retained earnings, the repurchase leaves stated capital unchanged at Rs.1.13 billion. Including Rs.33.4 million in withholding tax, the total outlay of Rs.690.3 million was equivalent to about 22 percent of the company’s Rs.3.18 billion in cash and cash equivalents at end-June.
