Hoteliers praise budget’s tourism direction

THASL expects TDL collections to reach Rs.4.5-5bn this year, while the budget estimates annual cost of abolishing the levy at Rs.40mn

The association wants the US$ 3mn entry threshold for the 200% capital allowance lowered to US$ 500,000 so that boutique hotels can benefit

SLTDA and SLTPB will fund Rs.1.4bn of Rs.1.9bn in new tourism measures in the budget

The Hotels Association of Sri Lanka (THASL) praised the 2027 budget’s direction for tourism, while seeking talks with authorities on bridging the funding gap left by the abolition of the Tourism Development Levy (TDL) and on lowering the US$ 3 million threshold for a new capital allowance, THASL President Asoka Hettigoda told Mirror Business.

Hettigoda acknowledged that Many of the association’s proposals had been captured in the budget, citing the expansion of Bandaranaike International Airport (BIA) and its planned domestic terminal, a cruise terminal, hotel schools, domestic airports and digital ticketing for attractions, all of which he expected to help draw high-value travellers to the island. “At least from our side, we feel that it’s a very progressive budget,” he added, noting that tourism had been duly recognised as a key industry.

He also described the budget as a much bigger investment in tourism than last year’s, pointing to World Bank support to develop heritage sites, including historic forts, as attractions, which he said the sector had not received from donor agencies in the past. Among these is a US$ 77 million World Bank-funded project to restore historic buildings around Colombo Fort and develop the area as a tourism zone.

However, Hettigoda acknowledged that the abolition of the TDL, charged at up to 1 percent on the turnover of licensed tourism establishments, had come as a surprise to the industry. He estimated that private sector TDL contributions had reached about Rs.3.5 billion so far this year and would climb to between Rs.4.5 billion and Rs.5 billion by year-end, supported by peak occupancies and room rates in December. That would be nearly double the Rs.2.58 billion collected in 2024, as per figures presented to Parliament by Foreign Affairs, Foreign Employment and Tourism Minister Vijitha Herath.

“We want to have a dialogue with the authorities to see how the gap is going to be bridged,” Hettigoda stressed, adding that the association was also pressing for a global marketing campaign. He pointed out that small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) had been the main beneficiaries of TDL-funded promotions such as overseas travel fairs and familiarisation tours for foreign journalists, since larger companies maintain their own marketing networks.

“Large companies don’t need travel shows,” he observed, noting that between 65 and 70 of the 84 or so Sri Lankan companies at the recent World Travel Market (WTM) in London were SMEs, many of which were now enquiring whether such support would continue.

The budget’s revenue annex costs the abolition, effective April 1, 2027, at only Rs.40 million a year, or less than 1 percent of the industry’s estimate, as the levy is credited to the Tourism Development Fund rather than the Consolidated Fund. President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, who is also the Minister of Finance, Planning and Economic Development, told Parliament the move was aimed at “reducing the tax burden on the tourism industry, enhancing its competitiveness and ensuring sustainable financing for tourism development,” while the Treasury will release funds on requests made by the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA) until a new financing mechanism is formulated.

THASL will also seek to lower the minimum investment for the 200 percent enhanced capital allowance offered to BOI-approved tourism projects to about US$ 500,000 from US$ 3 million, a threshold equivalent to nearly Rs.1 billion at current exchange rates.

Hettigoda remarked that the existing threshold was “rather steep” for SMEs, given that a five- to 10-room boutique hotel requires far less capital, adding that travellers were increasingly favouring unique, smaller and high-value properties.

Meanwhile, budget documents show that the SLTDA and the Sri Lanka Tourism Promotion Bureau (SLTPB), both self-financing bodies funded through the Tourism Development Fund, will finance Rs.1,413 million of the Rs.1,948 million in new measures in the budget’s tourism chapter, leaving the Treasury’s direct share at Rs.535 million. The chapter’s total amounts to about a quarter of the Rs.7.8 billion in destination, promotion and training proposals in the 2026 budget, while the tourism development programme under the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Foreign Employment and Tourism has been cut by 41 percent to Rs.618 million.

The SLTPB also remitted Rs.2 billion in levies to the Treasury in the first eight months of 2026, double its payment for the whole of 2025, Finance Ministry data showed. (NF)
