Colombo’s highest landmarks turned orange to promote the elimination of GBV

Colombo, November 27, 2026 – Two of Colombo’s most prominent landmarks, the iconic Lotus Tower and the Colombo Municipal Council (CMC), were illuminated in orange from November 25 to 27, sending a powerful message of solidarity and commitment to ending Gender-Based Violence (GBV).

The initiative marked Sri Lanka’s participation in the global 16 Days of Activism Against Gender-Based Violence 2026, under the international theme “UNiTE to End Violence Against Women and Girls: Ensure Justice, Protect Survivors, and End Impunity.” In Sri Lanka, the campaign is being carried out under the national theme “#JusticeIsMyRight: UNITE Towards Ending GBV.”

This year, the Ministry of Women and Child Affairs and civil society organisations have joined hands to observe the 16 Days of Activism through a collaborative approach, bringing together government institutions, international partners, diplomatic missions and civil society organisations to strengthen collective action against GBV.

Commenting on the significance of this collaboration, Minister of Women and Child Affairs Saroja Savithri Paulraj emphasised that ending gender-based violence requires a united and sustained commitment from all sectors of society. She highlighted the importance of the Ministry working collaboratively with civil society organisations and other partners to strengthen prevention, protection and access to justice for women and girls.

The 2026 campaign is being observed collaboratively with the participation of the Ministry of Women and Child Affairs, UNFPA Sri Lanka, the High Commission of Canada in Sri Lanka, the Parliament of Sri Lanka, the Embassy of the Kingdom of the Netherlands in Sri Lanka, and Women In Need (WIN), reflecting a shared commitment to preventing violence and strengthening protection and access to justice for survivors.

Orange, the colour of the global campaign, symbolises hope and a future free from violence. By illuminating these prominent landmarks, the initiative brought the message of the campaign into public spaces, encouraging citizens to recognise their responsibility in ending GBV.

While the special illumination of the Lotus Tower and the Colombo Municipal Council concluded on November 27, the 16 Days of Activism campaign continues until December 10, Human Rights Day, with ongoing efforts to raise awareness, promote accountability and inspire collective action against gender-based violence.

As Colombo's landmarks shone orange, they carried a message that extends far beyond the city: ending gender-based violence is a shared responsibility, and justice must be everyone's right.
