Tourism authorities widen visitor map as super El Niño meets peak season

Met Dept. puts 69% probability on the strongest event in 75 years; NOAA sees an 81% chance of a strong El Niño in October-December, with effects running into February 2027

September-December must deliver 965,098 arrivals for the 2.5mn conservative scenario, against 795,998 in the same months of 2025

Wevita remains confident of surpassing 2.5mn, with extreme weather now treated as the norm rather than the exception

Sri Lanka’s tourism authorities are working with wildlife, irrigation and provincial agencies on a set of measures to hold the visitor experience steady through a super El Niño forecast to peak in the island’s busiest travel months, while remaining confident that arrivals will surpass 2.5 million this year, Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA) Chairman Suranjith Wevita told Mirror Business.

The Department of Meteorology has placed a 69 percent probability on the developing event becoming stronger than any experienced in the past 75 years, while the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration puts the chance of a strong El Niño forming during October-December at 81 percent, with effects capable of persisting into February 2027 — a window falling squarely across the winter peak.

Forecasts point to below-normal rainfall across parts of the Northern, North Central, Eastern and Uva regions, with heavier rain possible elsewhere later in the season.

The SLTDA’s revised growth scenarios, published last month, state that adverse weather associated with El Niño adds “another layer of uncertainty to the tourism outlook”, given that extreme weather can disrupt transport, affect coastal and nature-based attractions and shape visitor perceptions of destination safety.

“Nobody can predict what the exact impact of El Niño is going to be,” Wevita said, adding that the authority was acting on the information currently available rather than waiting for the season to declare itself. He acknowledged that extreme weather events were becoming the norm rather than the exception, making adaptation a permanent requirement for the industry.

The Department of Wildlife Conservation has already closed Blocks I and II of Yala National Park from September 14 to October 15 on prevailing drought conditions, with the remaining blocks and Lunugamvehera staying open.

Wevita noted that the traffic would also be directed to Maduru Oya National Park and other national parks , where elephant visibility is high, and that the move relieves the overtourism pressure built up at Yala and Wilpattu. Maduru Oya spans more than 58,800 hectares across the Eastern and Uva Provinces, takes in five reservoirs and holds close to 150 elephants in the reservoir catchment, alongside sloth bear, leopard and over 100 bird species, with a Vedda community resident at Henanigala.

Wevita said the authority was also working on the Yan Oya water resources and Kuchchaveli, where the SLTDA holds several properties it intends to release on 30-year leases, together with the Moragahakanda and Pahala Malwathu Oya schemes. The Yan Oya is dammed at Angurugaswewa in Anuradhapura and reaches the sea at Pulmoddai, north of the Kuchchaveli-Nilaveli stretch, where the SLTDA land bank lists 74 acres in the Kuchchaveli Beach Resort Zone and Pigeon Island National Park lies a kilometre offshore.

Moragahakanda, at Elahera in Matale, is the last reservoir of the Mahaweli programme and sits within an hour of the Sigiriya-Dambulla circuit, while the Lower Malwathu Oya scheme at Thanthirimale is being positioned to carry eco-tourism and freshwater fisheries alongside irrigation.

Meanwhile, August delivered 191,704 arrivals against 198,235 a year earlier, following 196,845 in July, taking the eight-month tally to 1,535,122 compared with 1,566,523 in the same period of 2025, a shortfall of 31,401 or 2.0 percent. The revised scenarios put the full year at 2,377,370 in a lower case, 2,500,220 in a conservative case and 2,603,311 in an optimistic case, against initial projections of up to three million drawn up before the West Asia escalation.

Meeting the conservative scenario therefore requires 965,098 arrivals over the last four months of the year, against 795,998 in the same period last year. The task is weighted towards the close of the year, with November and December alone expected to account for 541,717 arrivals, or 56 percent of what is left to be delivered.

However, the acceleration in headline inflation is feeding through hotel, transport, food and logistics costs, according to the report, with the IMF’s assumptions of US$ 89 a barrel and a 32 percent rise in crude prices over 2025 working into long-haul airfares. Colombo Consumer Price Index inflation reached 8 percent in August from 7.3 percent in July, the highest in over three years, with restaurants and hotels up 11.1 percent.

The report concludes that higher arrivals will not translate proportionately into earnings without longer stays and greater local spending. Wevita stressed that the response lies in attracting higher-spending visitors through segments such as experience-based tourism, lifting yield per visitor while spreading demand away from the destinations carrying the heaviest load.
