G.L. Peiris files another petition against 22nd Amendment

Colombo, Aug. 18 (Daily Mirror) - Former Minister, former Dean of the Faculty of Law and former Vice Chancellor of the University of Colombo, Prof. G.L. Peiris, today filed another Special Determination petition in the Supreme Court challenging the constitutionality of the Bill titled the “Twenty-Second Amendment to the Constitution.”

The petitioner contends that the proposed ad hoc amendment to the fundamental law of the country raises concerns that it could amount to an inducement under the guise of a constitutional amendment, particularly since the relevant clause, as drafted, would apply to incumbent Judges of the Supreme Court, including the incumbent Chief Justice, as well as Judges of the Court of Appeal.

He contends that this could be perceived by the public as an inducement offered to holders of judicial office.

The petitioner further contends that Clause 2 of the Bill is inconsistent with, inter alia, the Preamble and Articles 3, 4, 12(1), 28, 107, 108, 110, 111C and 83 of the Constitution.

He also states that Clause 2 of the Bill would alienate the inalienable sovereignty of the people, specifically the judicial power of the people, contrary to the provisions of the Constitution.

Prof. Peiris further argues that while the Bill seeks to extend the retirement age of Judges of the Supreme Court and Court of Appeal and make the extension applicable to sitting judges, including the incumbent Chief Justice, it seeks to unlawfully introduce, through the law-making process, a constitutional amendment with retrospective effect.

Prof. Peiris is also seeking a declaration that the Bill requires approval by the people at a referendum and a two-thirds majority in Parliament.

He states that if the Bill is enacted in its present form, it could mark the beginning of democratic backsliding rather than democratic progress.
