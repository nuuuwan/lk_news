A Budgetary Benefit That Barely Reaches the Poor

The Budget 2027 appears to have made a grand gesture towards helping consumers by reducing the tax on sprats by Rs. 90 per kilogramme and on Maldive fish by Rs. 290 per kilogramme. But a closer look at these concessions reveals how little thought has gone into the actual benefits reaching ordinary consumers.

Sprats, a popular and affordable source of protein among the poorer sections of society, currently sell at around Rs. 1,900 per kilogramme. A tax reduction of Rs. 90 translates into a saving of just Rs. 9 when a consumer purchases 100 grams. Is this what the Government considers meaningful relief for the poor?

The reduction in tax on Maldive fish is even more questionable. With prices ranging between Rs. 4,500 and Rs. 5,000 per kilogramme, this has long ceased to be an affordable food item for ordinary and low-income families. Those who can still afford to buy it are largely the more affluent sections of society, who will now be the principal beneficiaries of this concession.

One cannot help questioning whether these budgetary measures were designed to provide genuine relief to struggling households or merely to announce tax reductions that look impressive on paper. A tax concession is not necessarily a consumer benefit, particularly when its impact on the family budget is almost negligible.

At a time when millions are struggling to make ends meet, the Government must move beyond headline-grabbing tax reductions and introduce measures that deliver tangible relief to those who need it most. The poor need affordable food on their tables, not token concessions dressed up as budgetary generosity.
