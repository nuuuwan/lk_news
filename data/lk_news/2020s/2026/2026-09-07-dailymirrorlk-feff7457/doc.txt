Health reforms must move beyond projects and paperwork: Expert

Colombo, Sep. 7 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka needs to move away from a healthcare system dominated by bureaucracy, workshops and policy documents and focus more strongly on practical results for the public, health expert Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa has said.

He said successive governments had introduced numerous health-related projects, policies and programmes, but many had failed to produce the expected results at the grassroots level.

Dr. Sanjeewa said the country’s health system had been largely stagnant for around three decades while other countries had made significant advances through modern technology and innovative approaches.

He said healthcare reforms should therefore focus on strengthening implementation rather than simply introducing new projects.

Among the areas requiring urgent attention are health economics, modern hospital technology, human resource management, disease prevention, pharmaceutical management and the development of the health tourism sector, he said.

Dr. Sanjeewa also called for stronger coordination among the various institutions responsible for healthcare administration, including the Ministry of Health, State Pharmaceuticals Corporation, regulatory authorities, Medical Supplies Division and provincial health authorities.

He said the lack of effective coordination had resulted in different institutions pursuing separate approaches instead of working towards common national health goals.

He further questioned the effectiveness of programmes in which large amounts of public and donor funding were spent without producing clearly measurable benefits.

According to Dr. Sanjeewa, Sri Lanka also needs comprehensive national policies covering medicines, antibiotics, regional healthcare, recruitment, salaries, human resources and specialist medical training.

He said disease prevention should also be evaluated based on actual outcomes rather than expenditure, particularly as non-communicable diseases continue to place a heavy burden on the population.

Dr. Sanjeewa said the country must gradually transform its healthcare system by encouraging new ideas, improving accountability and ensuring that public funds are translated into tangible services.

He said that reforms such as the proposed family doctor system could only succeed if they were introduced as part of a broader effort to modernise the health sector.

“The health system must gradually move out of its administrative comfort zone and focus on delivering practical healthcare benefits to the people,” he said.
