Sri Lanka’s hospitality leaders to chart Vision 2030 roadmap at global conference

Sri Lanka’s hospitality industry is set to take a major step towards shaping its long-term future with more than 200 senior industry leaders expected to convene at the International Hospitality Leaders Conference (IHLC) 2026, scheduled for September 29 at the Cinnamon Grand Colombo.

Organised by the Sri Lanka Chapter of the Institute of Hospitality (IOH), the conference aims to move the industry beyond post-crisis recovery and towards a strategic Vision 2030 roadmap focused on global competitiveness, innovation and sustainable growth.

Backed by the Institute of Hospitality UK, a globally recognised professional body with more than 80 years of international standing, the conference will bring together hotel owners, developers, general managers, tourism policymakers, investors, academics and commercial leaders to formulate what organisers describe as the country’s first collective hospitality industry roadmap.

Conference Chairman Refhan Razeen, who is also General Manager of Shangri-La Hambantota, said the sector had successfully navigated one of the most difficult periods in its history and the focus must now shift towards sustained performance and future readiness.

“As we move beyond recovery, the conversation must shift from resilience to long-term performance. Vision 2030 is about ensuring that our industry remains globally competitive, commercially resilient and prepared for the future. The International Hospitality Leaders Conference has been created to bring together the leaders who will help shape that future,” he said.

Organisers said Sri Lanka’s tourism industry is now at a critical juncture as it seeks to respond to digital disruption, rapidly changing traveller expectations and increasingly stringent global sustainability requirements.

Unlike conventional industry conferences, IHLC 2026 has been designed as a strategic leadership platform intended to produce measurable commitments rather than simply facilitate networking.

The deliberations will culminate in the publication of a Vision 2030 Whitepaper, outlining key recommendations and priorities for the industry’s long-term development.

The conference agenda will focus on six strategic themes: future-ready leadership and governance; commercial strategy and business performance; innovation and digital transformation; sustainability and responsible tourism; talent development and workforce readiness; and destination competitiveness and global positioning.

Attendance is open through paid registration at Rs. 25,000 per delegate. Organisers are also inviting corporate partners to participate through sponsorship packages that will provide brand visibility, direct engagement with senior decision-makers and recognition in the Vision 2030 Whitepaper.

The Institute of Hospitality, headquartered in the United Kingdom, is an internationally recognised professional body serving the hospitality, tourism and leisure sectors.

Its Sri Lanka Chapter promotes professional standards, education, leadership development and industry collaboration while connecting local professionals with a global network committed to advancing excellence in hospitality.
