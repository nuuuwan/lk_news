Central Bank’s dollar buying hits 29-month high in August at US$ 579mn

Largest monthly net purchase since March 2024; Jan-Aug net buying close to US$ 1.5bn

Rupee ends August at Rs.328.01 as year-to-date depreciation eases to 5.5% from 7.8% at end-July

Reserves at US$ 6,600mn at end-July against the IMF’s US$ 8.6bn end-2026 projection as IMF mission arrives

The Central Bank bought a net US$ 579 million from the domestic foreign exchange market in August, its largest monthly purchase in 29 months, taking net buying for the year to close to US$ 1.5 billion as an International Monetary Fund (IMF) mission arriving this week, as per Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) data.

CBSL Senior Deputy Governor Dr. Chandranath Amarasekara highlighted that  the Central Bank purchased a further US$ 579 million on a net basis from the market in August, following net purchases of US$ 349 million in July, bringing total net foreign exchange purchases during January-August to close to US$ 1.5 billion.

He said gross official reserves had also strengthened, with official reserve statistics for end-August to be published shortly as per the advance release calendar.

The August figure is the highest monthly purchase since March 2024, when net intervention reached US$ 715 million on a trade date basis. The CBSL recorded no sales during the month. Its net purchases ran at US$ 200 million in January and US$ 461 million in February before easing to US$ 49 million in March, with the Central Bank turning net seller in April and May at US$ 13 million and US$ 211 million respectively, and buying resuming at US$ 71 million in June.

Dr. Amarasekara noted that  the rupee, which displayed some depreciation pressure with the escalation of tensions in the Middle East since March, appreciated notably during August. The currency ended the month at Rs.328.01 to the dollar against Rs.309.99 at end-2025 and Rs.292.58 at end-2024, with year-to-date depreciation at 5.5 percent at end-August, down from 7.8 percent at end-July.

However, the accumulation is still running behind the path the Fund has sketched for this year. The IMF’s latest country report projects gross official reserves at US$ 8,645 million by end-2026, against US$ 6,600 million as at end-July. Reaching it would require a build of US$ 409 million a month over five months, against a monthly average of US$ 186 million over the eight months to August, and comes as the merchandise trade deficit has widened to US$ 6,507 million in the seven months to July from US$ 3,850 million a year earlier.

An IMF staff team led by Mission Chief for Sri Lanka Evan Papageorgiou is scheduled to visit the island from September 10 to 23 for the combined Seventh Review of the Extended Fund Facility (EFF) and the 2026 Article IV Consultation.

Meanwhile, the buying carries a domestic cost. At Rs.328.01 to the dollar, a purchase of US$ 579 million equates to roughly Rs.190 billion released into the banking system, against a total outstanding market liquidity surplus of Rs.355.30 billion by September 4, up from Rs.318.13 billion a week earlier. Headline inflation, meanwhile, rose to 8.0 percent in August from 7.3 percent in July, against the CBSL’s 5 percent target, with the Overnight Policy Rate held at 8.75 percent.

The end-August reserve position, due shortly, will show how much of the month’s record buying survived external debt servicing.
