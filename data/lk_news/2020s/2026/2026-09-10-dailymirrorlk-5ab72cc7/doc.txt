Thennekumbura traders protest against govt., allege JVP supporters favored

Kandy, September 10 (Daily Mirror) - Traders in Thennekumbura, Kandy, expressed displeasure with the government following the removal of roadside stalls under a court order, alleging that supporters of the governing JVP were given preferential treatment during the clearance operation.

A tense situation was reported today (09) in the Thennekumbura area, within the Gangawata Korale Pradeshiya Sabha limits, as authorities moved to clear several commercial stalls located on the Mahaweli River reservation under the court order.

Court officials, Mahaweli Authority officials and police personnel arrived at the location to implement the clearance. The business owners were reportedly informed that they could face arrest if they obstructed officials in carrying out the court order.

Affected shop owners and local residents protested the clearance, alleging that the enforcement was selective and politically influenced. Protesters claimed that while several stalls had been identified for removal, one shop was left standing because of its alleged political connections to the governing Janatha Vimukthi Peramuna (JVP).

“If it was a court order, why was that shop spared? The court gave the order for that shop too, so why was only that shop spared?” the protesting vendors questioned.

The traders said they had operated their businesses at the location for between 25 and 30 years and had invested their own money in establishing and maintaining the stalls. They also claimed that they had operated under formal agreements and paid a monthly rent of Rs. 5,000 to the local Pradeshiya Sabha.

According to local representatives, the stalls had initially been established following arrangements involving the local Pradeshiya Sabha and the Mahaweli Authority. However, legal proceedings were later initiated against individual stall owners, eventually resulting in the court-authorized clearance.

The affected traders expressed deep concern over the loss of their livelihoods, saying their businesses provided the main source of income for their families and helped them meet the needs of their school-going children.

Some traders also said they had already suffered significant losses after Cyclone Ditwah and were now facing further hardship as a result of the removal of their businesses.

While stating that they respect the authority of the courts, the traders accused officials of presenting the matter in a politically biased manner when seeking the court order. They further alleged that political considerations may have influenced the implementation of the clearance.

The protests intensified as traders voiced their opposition to the government and confronted a National People's Power (NPP) member of the Gangawata Korale Pradeshiya Sabha who arrived at the scene.

They also warned that reduced commercial activity could leave the location less active and potentially contribute to security and public safety concerns, particularly during nighttime hours.

They have urged the authorities to intervene and seek a fair solution.
