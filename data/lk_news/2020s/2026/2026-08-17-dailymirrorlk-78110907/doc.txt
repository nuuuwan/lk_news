Hospital warns of dangerous kite-flying accidents

Colombo, Aug. 17 (Daily Mirror) - With the prevailing dry weather and school vacation period, kite flying has become a popular activity among children. However, following an increase in accidents linked to the activity, the Acting Director of Lady Ridgeway Hospital for Children, Specialist Dr. Pradeep Ratnasekera, has urged parents and the public to pay close attention to safety precautions.

In a special notice, Dr. Ratnasekera warned that kite flying without proper safety measures can expose children and others to serious and potentially life-threatening accidents.

According to Dr. Ratnasekera, accidents can occur when children fly kites in groups, particularly due to collisions and falls. There is also a risk of children falling from elevated locations while attempting to launch or retrieve kites.

One of the most serious dangers is kite strings becoming entangled with high-voltage power lines. Electricity can travel through the string and cause severe electrical burns to the person holding it, potentially requiring hospitalization and surgery.

Specialists have noted that electrical burns can cause extensive damage to body tissues, blood vessels and nerves, particularly in the arms and forearms, and in some cases the chest. Severe injuries may require the surgical removal of badly damaged tissue or parts of the affected limb.

Dr. Ratnasekera also warns that contact between conductive kite strings and high-voltage power lines can result in fatal electric shocks.

Parents and adults have therefore been urged to ensure that children do not fly kites near high-voltage power lines and to closely supervise them during the activity.

Dr. Ratnasekera said that while children's enjoyment is important, their safety must remain a priority. Parents, adults and the wider community are urged to act responsibly and take necessary precautions to prevent kite-flying accidents.
