Pushes for 50% local tea in duty-free re-export blends

Sri Lanka’s Planters’ Association has called for the minimum local tea content in blends made with duty-free imports for re-export to be raised to 50 percent from 30 percent, saying about a third of locally produced green and CTC tea remains unsold. The Association’s chief Samaradiwakara said in a guest column that the 30 percent requirement had been in place for roughly three decades. The association also wants imported tea held to testing standards equivalent to those applied to locally produced tea.

“About 30 percent of locally produced green and CTC tea remains unsold, while cheaper imports enter duty-free for re-export blended with just 30 percent local tea, a requirement unchanged for roughly three decades,” Samaradiwakara added.

The proposal comes amid investment by Sri Lankan producers in matcha, green and artisanal teas. Value-added teas already account for more than half of the country’s tea export volumes, according to Samaradiwakara.

Tea export earnings fell to US$ 701 million from US$ 743 million in the first seven months of 2026, while production declined 3 percent to 152.5 million kilogrammes, the column said.
