The Vihara Maha Devi Park drama  -- good sense prevailed

Opportunistic politicians joined in the march as it neared Colombo

The drama at the Vihara Maha Devi Park, demanding  President Dissanayake either resign or release from prison Gnanasara Thera whose pardon was revoked by a court of law,   is apparently over. The Reverend monks who were staging a satyagraha, threatening not to leave the protest site unless their demands were met, have packed up and left.

Satyagraha leaders announced that the Mahanayaka Theras (chief prelates) of the three Nikayas had informed them that they would take steps to resolve the issue through discussions. The Mahanayakas reminded the protesting monks of the importance of monastic discipline and spiritual restraint.

The Chief priests warned the protesters at Vihara Maha Devi Park that their demand for a presidential pardon for Galagoda Aththe Gnanasara Thera, was being exploited by external forces. This,  they said, could damage the reputation and standing of the clergy locally and internationally.

They urged monks across all Nikayas to maintain harmony, resolve disputes according to the Dhamma and Vinaya, and act in ways that build public faith.

The Mahanayakes showed a depth of understanding and keen mental perception of the evolving situation. They recognised how a misstep by government bureaucrats was beingportrayed by opportunistic political forces as a confrontation between the state (as represented by the president of the country) and the Buddhist clergy. They acted to defuse the situation and good sense has prevailed.

The Mahanayakes realised that,  if not nipped in the bud, the issue could  rouse ethno-religious tensions. The man at the centre of the storm  –  Rev. Gnanasara --  was convicted of Contempt of Court and for threatening the wife of journalist Eknaligoda.

Eknaligoda had been forcibly disappeared by rogue members of the military who faced trial for that crime. Rev. Gnanasara in fact brazenly interrupted court proceedings. He berated the trial judge and praised the accused as war heroes and threatened the wife of the missing journalist. He has a string of pending cases against him.

He was pardoned by a past president in 2018. That pardon was invalidated by the Supreme Court and the monk was re-arrested.

The protesting monks were  demanding that a wearer of the yellow robes was immune to judicial review,   whatever crime he/she may commit. They were also claiming religiosity (encompassing a community’s religious beliefs) was above the law and superior to even Executive Presidential authority.

Thanks to the sagacity of the Mahanayaka Theras, what threatened to be a standoff between the forces of law and order and the protesting monks was averted.  The satyagraha itself was a culmination of a protest march led by monks opposed to the disrobing of Gnanasara Thero by prison authorities without the permission of the head of his sect.

Yet, this issue was first raised by some political party leaders who claimed the authorities were not respecting Buddhist tradition and practice. The deliberate provoking of emotions, using distorted religious arguments and rhetoric,  saw a whipping up of hysteria among sections of the Buddhist clergy. This led to a protest march titled “Justice for the Robe Freedom March”.

Unfortunately, somewhere along the six-day march,  opportunistic politicians joined in the march as it neared Colombo and manipulated a change in the demand from “Justice for the Robe” to “Freedom for Gnanasara Thera” –- a person convicted of Contempt of Court, threatening the wife of a man forcibly disappeared in addition to rabble rousing and preaching race hatred among many other charges.

Crowds lined the streets as the protesting monks walked from the Dalada Maligawa in Kandy to President’s House in Colombo. However, unlike during the ‘Aragalaya’ of 2022 which drew large crowds of protestors to Colombo and led to the overthrow of then President Gotabhaya Rajapaksa, the onlookers did not join in the march.

Protestors made their last stand at the park, but were forced to disperse by the wisdom of the Mahanayaka Theras.

Our country has barely come out of a near three-decade-long ethnic conflict which nearly took the country back to the Stone Age. We should not permit corrupt,  defeated politicians  to whip up religious hatred as a means to regain political power. Government needs to crack down on  ethno-religious hatred.
