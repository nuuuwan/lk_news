Police to step up traffic operations ahead of festive season as road deaths reach 2,463

Colombo, Nov. 23 (Daily Mirror) - The Sri Lanka Police will intensify traffic enforcement operations across the country ahead of the December school holidays and Christmas festive season, following the deaths of 2,463 people in road accidents during the first 11 months of this year.

Deputy Inspector General of Police (DIG) in charge of Traffic Control and Road Safety W.P.J. Senadheera said traffic police personnel would be deployed island-wide to ensure strict enforcement of traffic laws during the busy festive period.

According to police statistics, a total of 2,330 fatal road accidents were reported between January 1 and November 21, 2026, while 2,463 people lost their lives.

During the same period, police recorded 5,868 serious accidents, 9,870 minor accidents and 4,488 accidents involving property damage.

Pedestrians and motorcyclists continued to account for a significant proportion of road fatalities, with 813 pedestrians killed during the period under review.

Motorcycle-related accidents claimed 1,044 lives, including 876 riders and 168 pillion passengers. In addition, 240 passengers, 190 drivers and 165 cyclists died in road accidents.

DIG Senadheera noted that most pedestrian fatalities involved people aged between 55 and 85, highlighting the vulnerability of elderly road users.

Comparing the figures with the corresponding period last year, he said the number of fatal accidents had increased by 11, although the overall death toll had declined by 16.

He warned that special operations targeting drivers under the influence of alcohol and drugs would be intensified ahead of the festive season.

Adequate breathalyser equipment has already been distributed to police stations island-wide, while additional equipment would be provided by Traffic Headquarters where necessary, he said.

Meanwhile, in collaboration with the National Transport Medical Institute (NTMI), Police will deploy mobile laboratory vehicles to conduct random roadside drug tests on drivers. Drug testing will also be carried out on drivers involved in road accidents.

DIG Senadheera urged motorists to exercise greater caution and comply with traffic regulations, warning that all traffic police units would remain on high alert to prevent road accidents and reduce fatalities during the year-end travel period.
