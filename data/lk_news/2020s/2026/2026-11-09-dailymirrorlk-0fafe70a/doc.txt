Cancer treatment machines worth billions remain idle

Colombo, Nov.9 (Daily Mirror) - Two cancer radiotherapy machines installed at the Badulla and Hambantota hospitals remain unused despite being ready to commence treatment leaving cancer patients facing further delays.

According to the Government Radiographers’ Association, the machines could begin treating patients, but services had been held up over the unavailability of dosimetry equipment.

The two machines are among five Linear Accelerator machines procured for government hospitals following years of delays, at a cost exceeding Rs. 600 million each.

Establishing a complete radiotherapy unit at each hospital is estimated to cost over Rs. 1 billion.

Installation of the Badulla machine was completed in April, while the Hambantota installation was completed during the first week of October.

However, neither hospital has commenced treatment, despite six months having passed since the Badulla installation and a month since the Hambantota installation was completed.

The association claimed that the required dosimetry equipment could be temporarily obtained from the National Cancer Institute in Maharagama (Apeksha Hospital), enabling treatment to begin without waiting for new equipment.

It said Deputy Health Minister Dr. Hansaka Wijemuni had also instructed officials during a recent visit to Badulla Hospital to obtain the equipment from Apeksha Hospital and commence services urgently.

However, the necessary arrangements have still not been made, it claimed, adding that similar arrangements had been used previously to activate LINAC machines at other hospitals.

The delay has forced patients to seek treatment at Apeksha Hospital and Kandy National Hospital.

The association warned that patients unable to access timely treatment at government hospitals could be forced to turn to private facilities, where radiotherapy treatment costs between Rs. 500,000 and Rs. 2 million.

Currently, LINAC radiotherapy services are available at Apeksha Hospital, Kandy National Hospital, Galle National Hospital, Jaffna Teaching Hospital and Batticaloa Teaching Hospital.

With waiting lists already extending over several months at Apeksha Hospital and Kandy National Hospital, the association warned that keeping newly installed machines idle was further delaying cancer treatment.
