Thriposha production hindered amid maize import delay and regulatory impasse

Production of Thriposha, Sri Lanka’s flagship nutritional supplement programme for combating child malnutrition and supporting pregnant and lactating mothers, has been heavily hindered due to delays in releasing a key imported maize consignment and shortages of compliant raw material, officials said.

While production has not been fully halted, sources confirmed that output has been significantly disrupted due to the unavailability of maize meeting strict safety standards—particularly aflatoxin levels below 5 parts per billion (PPB). Local supplies, officials said, often fall short of the required threshold due to gaps in post-harvest handling, storage, and testing capacity.

At the centre of the disruption is a shipment of around 2,290 metric tons of maize currently held at port, despite the importer having obtained the necessary licences and prior Cabinet approval in June 2025 under a national competitive bidding process.

However, trade authorities have so far withheld clearance for release, insisting that fresh Cabinet approval is required. This position appears to contradict earlier guidance attributed to the Cabinet Spokesperson in January 2026, which indicated that no additional approval was necessary and that implementing agencies were already authorised to proceed.

The continued delay has raised concerns over both supply chain stability and potential quality risks. Industry sources warned that prolonged storage of maize in sealed containers under high heat and humidity could increase the risk of aflatoxin contamination, potentially rendering the stock unsuitable for human consumption. There are also concerns about pest infestation as fumigation effectiveness diminishes over time.

Financial pressures are also mounting, with the supplier reported to have incurred about Rs. 214 million in procurement, taxes, and related costs. Demurrage and storage charges are estimated at between Rs. 700,000 and Rs. 1 million per day.

The consignment forms part of a 6,000-metric-ton contract awarded in 2024 to ensure continuity of Thriposha production for the 2024–2025 cycle. While 1,200 metric tons have already been delivered, the remaining stocks remain tied up in administrative delays, despite recommendations from the Ministry of Agriculture and the Food Policy and Security Committee for their release.

Health sector stakeholders have warned that continued disruption could affect nutrition programmes targeting vulnerable groups, where Thriposha remains a critical safety-net intervention.
