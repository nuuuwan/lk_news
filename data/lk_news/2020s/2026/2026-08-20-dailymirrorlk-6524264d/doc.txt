Former student leader and the General Secretary of Frontline Socialist Party Kumar Gunaratnam in an interview with Daily Mirror said that there are some contradictions within the students’ unions in the country, but he is hopeful that these unions will come back stronger in the future. He said that President Anura Kumara Dissanayake (AKD) is following the same path that was taken by former Presidents such as Ranil Wickremesinghe and has left is leftist ideologies. He said that AKD has used the JVP brand name to satisfy neoliberalists. Gunaratnam further said that the Party is not worried about the voter base as he firmly believes they will emerge when the suitable time and situation arise.

Q In a rally held early this year, Propaganda Secretary of Frontline Socialist Party Kumar (FSP) stated that it is essential to establish self-governing regions, bicameral legislature consisting of two houses and giving recognition to upcountry Tamils in order to solve the national issue. Don’t you think this itself could lead to many issues in the country?

The short answer is no, because at the last convention held in March, we introduced this programme. We, are going to introduce a bicameral legislature system, and two houses. One is similar to the present house, the house of representatives parallel to that is another house. It is basically equal representation of different nationalities, Such as Sinhala, Tamil and Muslim. The subject matter is different because the second house is planned for making decisions on national and cultural issues. So, even though having the first house, the majority can be a single majority government, but regardless of that, when they have to pass, a resolution, on the basis of national or cultural issues you have to through the second house and get confirmation.

It doesn’t mean that we can have complete solutions by introducing this, but up to some extent, you can make some decision; if the first does produce any decision, with the majority of the Sinhala parliamentarians, you can stop from the second one. When you talk about the upcountry Tamil people- who came here 200 years ago-they were forcefully brought by the British, imperialist. Their living conditions are poor compared to the other sections of the country’s society. That is a well-known factor. Throughout the years they have been used by different politicians, not only Sinhala politicians, but also the Tamil politicians there. And they have been used; by some of nationalists and chauvinist movements. And we believe that when we consider the national movement in the Northern and Eastern provinces, which had been making demands for decades, they didn’t include the up country Tamil people in their struggle. So we believe that we should find them as a national community. Who have been living here for decades?

Therefore, I don’t think that it’s going to be another problem because this is the reality, but it should have been solved in the past, but most of the politicians who ruled the country, the different parties, and the different names, different residents, use this for their sake. Therefore we, put forward this programme to solve the national issue.

Q  Years ago, you were a student leader and your political language has historically included revolution and revolutionary struggles. How do you see student movements at present? Do you think they represent the needs of the hour?

Yes, in the past, the student’s movement had a great influence in the political arena. But I think over the years, it has been declined up to some level, At the same time, as a revolutionary party, we believe in the revolution, and we believe in socialism, and we believe in a system change as well.

It is not the system change made by the JVP or NPP, but a real system change. We believe that the working class is the main force to do the system change. They are the strata, they are the class that are possessing power. But the student movement in Sri Lanka has been a kind of a radical movement. And they had a great influence on politics; especially when we considered free education for the last four to five decades. I think they had been fighting to secure education for the masses. So in that sense, I think the students’ movement, did a great the job in the past.

But at present, after introducing this neoliberal economy, that’s not only the economy, but it’s an ideology as well. This has been unfolding from 1977. Now we are facing the outcomes of the neoliberal economy and what’s happening to the society. Today’s society is thinking about themselves and they are not fighting for the others. We have a huge challenge in that regard. I think still they are a strong movement and have influence, but at the same time it has been declining for the last few years.

Q  AKD has moved from revolutionary leftist politics to leading a government that works within the existing state, market and international financial system. Do you believe he has betrayed the principles of the Left, or has he simply recognised that those principles cannot govern a modern state in their traditional form?

Yes, I think Anura Kumara Dissanayake, the president, long ago lost his revolutionary conscience. We were in the JVP and the split happened back in 2011. Prior to that there was a long internal argument since 2004, after forming the coalition government with Chandika Bandaranaike. Since 2011, there was a political debate unfolding. And finally, we had to form a political party. Since those days, we were very much clear about his thoughts. I think we call it as pragmatism. It means leaving your stance, leaving your policies and your theoretical basis, and you slowly, but gradually fall into their pragmatic way of thinking. We are witnessing that reality right now. I don’t think that before being elected as the president, he didn’t have any sort of leftist programme in his mind. Even though there was a programme called “pohosath ratak Lassana jeewithayak” (programme for new national renaissance) they had a number of policies, but none of them has been working and not being enacted. AKD is surrounded by people like Duminda Hulangamuwa, Hans Wijesuriya, Dhammika Perera, Ishara Nanayakkara and all other tycoons. He is following the same path which Mr. Ranil Wickremesinghe and others followed. We still believe in a system change and in a better society. Sri Lankan people, deserve a better society than this, and a better democratic solution for the problems.

The JVP is not only a name, but also a brand. What Mr. Gotabhaya Rajapakse failed, by using AKD the JVP and the brand, which was created by the sacrifice of a number of comrades in the past, it’s being invested into the neoliberal programme. So the United States of America, or India, or the IMF, the imperialist institutions are content with what is happening in Sri Lanka.

Q  You have historically opposed multinational corporations, global finance capital and institutions such as the IMF. But Sri Lanka’s current economic reality is very different from the period when you entered politics. If you were responsible for governing the country today, how would you finance the economy, manage our debts and attract foreign investment without relying on the very institutions and capital you have criticised?

I want to raise one question. What is the country which was successful by using this IMF programme or the IMF conditions? Look and countries like Greece and Argentina. Even the other countries where IMF programme was implemented it was a failure. In one hand, we have that reality, because this is not the first time, it’s the 17th time.

According to the Central Bank Governor we are short of dollars, and according to his explanation 8.2 billion US dollars is enough to pay debts. We are not going to gain that within a few months.

If we were to handle this situation, we should definitely, be supported by the masses. We are not going to separate the masses with the government. If we are going to gain economy wise, we are going to do that with the support of the masses. So if you are going to oppose IMF conditions, and if you are going to talk with the bilateral or other institutions, about the debt servicing, we are going to ask them to do audits. What were the exact amount we took and what for?

This is the way we put forward our programme; with the support of the masses. And we are going to impose new taxes, not for the masses, but for the some of the capitalists, because they have enough wealth and at the same time they have enough profit as well. We are going to introduce new taxes for the top capitalists and release the vat and the other taxes imposed on the masses. We will continue to fight against these IMF measures. That is one of the major components of our programme.

Q Does this mean you’re encouraging a participatory governance system. Is that what you’re saying?

Yes, we are very open to say that our ultimate goal is socialism. There’s no difference. We need to go through a transition period. But how do we go through this period? By getting support of the masses.

Even though we come to power, we should maintain some organization amongst the masses, with the workers trade unions, peasants and everybody. So they are members. In a participatory democracy, people have a voice on a regular basis, not after five years or after election by election,

Q Don’t you think that itself could create a monopoly where capitalists will get together and they will find a way to oppress the rest of the masses? When you try to impose tax on these people, don’t you think that the downtrodden masses who work under them will be affected?

Yes, you can argue on that basis. Before AKD  became the President the amount of unpaid taxes, was around 1000 billions, nearly a trillion. What happened to that? Now after two years in the office, do you have any information that they have paid back? Look at the tax burden on the masses. They haven’t done anything wrong. They pay whatever the taxes imposed on them. But the top capitalists, I’m not referring to small or medium scale entrepreneurs. Indeed, we must support them and strengthen them. To do so we must give loans with low interest rates. We have to use public banks and we have to restructure those banks. We also need regulations on some of the private banks, to support small scale and medium scale entrepreneurs. I’m talking about capitalists. While most of the people pay their taxes those are the people who didn’t pay their tax. So there’s a huge difference. That is unjust. That’s why we are saying this is an unjust society,

Once we try to accumulate, taxes from the top capitalists, they will attempt to topple the government. That’s what I’m saying, not only by the votes, but with the organizational power of the masses, we will come together. That is the way we are going to handle office. Whatever the actions we take and the attempts to implement, we should get the support from the masses, and they are active partners of this causes. That is the difference between us and other parties.

Q  The NPP has now occupied much of the political space traditionally associated with the Left, while the FSP remains outside Parliament. Has the NPP effectively made the FSP politically irrelevant or has the FSP failed to adapt to changing political realities?

Yes, I can give you one example. The JVP came in to power after 60 years. Until then, they maintained 3% of the votes. Therefore we don’t worry about our voter base because we know that when the time comes and the situation arises, there will be change. No one expected the aragalaya (Protest) and the birth of the NPP government. The traditional parties which are in the opposition criticise the NPP government with regard to the ongoing issue with judges. The people no longer believe in them due to their past conduct. Whenever they raise an argument against the NPP government regarding this Chief Justice issue they revert to question about the appointment of Justice Shirani Bandaranaike.

This is why our stand is stronger and it affects the government. Throughout the history we were in the JVP. While we were there we expected a socialist society, and now they have changed all their policies get into action in a pragmatic way.

We are living in socialism. It doesn’t mean that we are believing something that this is not a religion or that once we believe that we are not going to give up. This is the only scientific alternative. When I ask, do you like this society? Do you like this inequality? Do you like these IMF imposed conditions?  Do you like these taxes? Do you like the social environment? Do you like these ethnic issues? Now, you like these religious contradictions, and conflicts? We need to change it. So that is called socialism.

We have learnt a lot from the Soviet Union and other socialist countries, especially in Eastern European countries, and China as well. They have changed this society a lot. But still, we have learned a lot and we need to implement a new socialist programme.

Q  You emphasised on the phrase ‘being pragmatic’. Do you mean AKD is being pragmatic?

I said pragmatism. Being pragmatic is good. But pragmatism is not good because, then you fall into the circumstances. You don’t have a strategy. You don’t have a plan, long term plan. Whatever the situation arises, you fall into that. So that is called pragmatism. Then you don’t have a clear vision. The word Pragmatic is good because according to the situation, even though you have a plan and a vision and a strategic programme, still, you have to be practical. So that is a different thing. Pragmatism is totally different. You don’t have a vision.

Q Would you support an alliance formed by the opposition parties if the objective is to defeat the NPP led government? If not, why?

We are not going to form an alliance with the traditional parties, SJB or any other parties, because we have had enough experience about them in the past, and they haven’t changed anything in terms of economy or with regard to democracy. Now since NPP is in power they have an opportunity. They used to criticise the governments in the past. They criticised neoliberal economy and education reforms. The very people who criticised them are following the same .This means the former parties which were in power in the past can justify their actions in the past. They can justify their economic policy and prevention of terrorism and other anti-democratic laws. And they can justify how they intervene in the judiciary system. Who is giving oxygen to the parties who were defeated by the people, the same NPP and AKD’s Government.

But the reason why we, try to implement our programme by ourselves, doesn’t mean we don’t have any other. It is because there’s no party or type of a group that we can deal with. We have experience with the parties which were in power and now with the NPP. Even though they are criticising each other in terms of policy and democracy, they are following the same path. Therefore, we should find a different path. We should follow our own path, and we should organise people, mobilise people, towards a progressive society.
