14 Lamborghinis take new route through Sri Lanka due to strait of Hormuz closure

Colombo, Aug. 20 (Daily Mirror) - While the closure of the Strait of Hormuz amid the ongoing Middle East conflict has disrupted global shipping and trade, Sri Lanka appears to be emerging as an alternative transit point for high-value cargo, with a recent shipment of 14 Lamborghini vehicles routed through the country.

The 14 luxury vehicles, manufactured in Italy, were transported by sea to Hambantota Port. From there, they were loaded into seven 40-foot containers and transported to Bandaranaike International Airport, Katunayake, where they are scheduled to be transferred to a large cargo aircraft for onward transportation to Dubai and Doha, Qatar.

According to a representative of the Danish company handling the shipment, the new route could provide economic benefits to Sri Lanka by creating an alternative logistics corridor at a time when traditional routes have been affected by regional instability.

The shipment reportedly includes vehicles with prices ranging from around Rs. 100 million for certain sports cars to as much as Rs. 500 million for the most expensive models.

The vehicles arrived at Hambantota Port several days ago, while the aircraft carrying the consignment is scheduled to depart from Katunayake for Dubai and Doha on the morning of August 21.

However, what was presented as a potentially significant development for Sri Lanka's logistics sector was accompanied by an unusual media restriction.

Journalists who travelled to Katunayake Airport to cover the cargo operation said airport authorities initially provided the necessary assistance. However, officials of SriLankan Airlines working in the export section of the Cargo Village reportedly refused permission for media coverage of the operation and asked journalists to leave the area.

The incident has raised questions over why media personnel were prevented from reporting on an international cargo operation that could potentially demonstrate Sri Lanka's emerging role as an alternative logistics hub.

Adding to the controversy, people gathered around the operation were reportedly seen taking photographs and videos of the vehicles using their mobile phones, despite journalists being denied access for coverage.

The shipment comes at a time when disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz are forcing businesses to explore alternative routes, potentially creating new opportunities for ports and airports capable of handling high-value international cargo.
