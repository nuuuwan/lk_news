Indices on a downward track despite positive start to share trading

The CSE yesterday opened in a positive manner following US President Donald Trump’s ten-day ceasefire period announcement for the Middle East but the development proved short lived because investors became too concerned over how secure their investments were.

Amid those developments both indices moved downward. The All Share Price Index went down by 25.77 points, while the S and P SL20 declined by 26 points.

Those crossings were reported in DIMO where 138,320 shares crossed to the tune of Rs 207.5 million; its shares traded at Rs 1500, Galle Face Capital 1.2 million shares crossed for Rs 31.9 million; its shares traded at Rs 6, DFCC 209,000 shares crossed for Rs 28.9 million; its shares traded at Rs 138, CIC Holdings (Non Voting)1.2 million shares crossed for Rs 28.8 million; its shares sold at Rs 24 and Lanka IOC 169,000 shares crossed for Rs 22.7 million; its shares sold at Rs 140.50.

In the retail market top companies that mainly contributed to the turnover were; TJ Lanka Rs 558 million (20.6 million shares traded), Acme Printers Rs 228 million (34.2 million shares traded), JKH Rs 199 million (10.7 million shares traded), Colombo Dockyard Rs 164 million (1.3 million shares traded), Softlogic Capital Rs 87.3 million (six million shares traded), Sampath Bank Rs 86.6 million (553,000 shares traded) and HNB Rs 65.9 million (160,000 shares traded). During the day 145.5 million share volumes changed hands in 23455.

It is said that mixed market reactions were noted. Motor vehicle sector, especially DIMO, registered a significant performance, while the banking sector, especially Sampath Bank and HNB performed well. Portfolio management companies, like Galle Face Capital Partners, also performed well.

Ambeon Essentials announced that it was making a voluntary offer to buy Harishchandra Mills shares at Rs 3,400. The stock was trading up Rs 117.75 at 5,056.75.

Greentech Energy said that there was no undisclosed price sensitive information in relation to the unusual trading activity seen around the company’s stock. The share was trading up 20 cents at Rs 6.8.

Yesterday the rupee was quoted at Rs 314.50/80 to the US dollar in the spot market, weaker than Rs 314.30/70 the previous day, dealers said, while bond yields edged lower.

The telegraphic transfer rates for the American dollar were 310.9500 buying, 317.9500 selling; the British pound was 413.8674 buying, and 425.1708 selling, and the euro was 356.4410 buying, 367.8604 selling.
