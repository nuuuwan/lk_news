CTC’s pre-tax profit slips despite 7% revenue growth

Ceylon Tobacco Company PLC’s (CTC) third-quarter pre-tax profit slipped 0.5 percent to Rs.13.67 billion, despite the higher revenue, as a more than threefold rise in other operating expenses outweighed the savings on raw materials.

The cigarette maker said the cost-saving measures helped it manage the pressure from the volatile market conditions in the three months ended September 30.

The raw material costs fell 31 percent year-on- year to Rs.887 million but other operating expenses climbed to Rs.2.23 billion, from Rs.730 million and the employee costs rose 19 percent to Rs.929 million. CTC did not give a breakdown of the increase in other operating expenses.

The turnover rose 7.2 percent to Rs.56.62 billion, while the government levies increased at the same pace to Rs.39.08 billion. The revenue after levies grew 7.4 percent to Rs.17.55 billion. Pre-tax profit as a share of that revenue fell to 77.9 percent, from 84.1 percent a year earlier.

A lower income tax charge lifted the net profit by 10 percent to Rs.7.58 billion. The tax charge fell by Rs.751 million to Rs.6.10 billion, more than offsetting the decline in the pre-tax earnings. The earnings per share increased to Rs.40.44, from Rs.36.77.

The board recommended a third interim dividend of Rs.41 per share, payable on December 11. The proposed payout amounts to about Rs.7.68 billion based on the company’s issued shares.

For the first nine months of 2026, the revenue after levies rose 5.7 percent to Rs.50.18 billion, while pre-tax profit increased 2.7 percent to Rs.40.30 billion. The net profit rose 5 percent to Rs.21.97 billion, taking the earnings per share to Rs.117.28, from Rs.111.74 a year earlier.

The net cash from the operating activities fell 15.7 percent to Rs.17.69 billion over the nine months, weighed down by the higher income tax payments and a reduction in trade and other payables.

CTC paid Rs.20.08 billion in dividends during the period and ended September with Rs.28.77 billion in cash and cash equivalents, down from Rs.31.37 billion at end-December.
