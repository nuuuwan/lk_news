Saudi Prince  prevails in highprofile film dispute

A long-running legal battle involving a proposed international film project came to a close on 16 October 2026, when Sri Lanka’s Supreme Court ruled in favour of Prince Al Waleed Bin Talal Bin Abdul Aziz Al Saud, the Saudi billionaire investor and chairman of Kingdom Holding Company. The judgment brought an end to a claim pursued by M.Z.M. Rafick, a film writer, promoter and producer operating under the name Puma International.

The dispute stemmed from a film concept titled The Diamond Merchant, a story set against the backdrop of the Bosnian conflict and intended to promote understanding between Christian and Muslim communities. Rafick claimed he had met the Prince at a Colombo hotel in March 2005 and presented the storyline, and that the Prince had been sufficiently impressed by the concept to enter into an oral agreement to produce the film once the script had been developed further. When the project failed to materialise, Rafick commenced legal proceedings, alleging a breach of that agreement. However, the courts found that the evidence did not support the existence of the alleged agreement.

The Commercial High Court had dismissed the action in 2019, concluding that Rafick had failed to prove that any oral agreement had been reached and that there was no basis for the intellectual property claims that accompanied the case.

On appeal , the Supreme Cour t under took a detailed examination of the legal principles governing oral contracts. While reaffirming that oral agreements are recognised under both Roman-Dutch and English law, as well as by Sri Lankan courts, the Court emphasised that a party alleging the existence of an oral agreement bears the burden of proving it. In this case, it found that the evidence fell short of establishing, on a balance of probabilities, that such an agreement had ever been concluded.

The Court ultimately affirmed the Commercial High Court’s decision and held that no oral agreement existed between Rafick and the Prince. It also observed that, in light of concessions made during the proceedings, there was no need to issue separate declaratory relief in relation to the intellectual property rights claimed over The Diamond Merchant.

The ruling underscores the evidential hurdle facing parties who seek to establish contractual rights founded on conversations and understandings that were never reduced to writing.

Avindra Rodrigo, President’s Counsel, with Christina Hettiarachchi and Hashendra Jayasuriya, instructed by F J & G de Saram, appeared for the Prince, while Roshan Hettiarachchi, instructed by Kalindi Silva, appeared for Rafick.
