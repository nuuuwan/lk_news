Australian envoy highlights investment, development partnerships

Pledges open engagement with Sri Lankan media as new forum launched

Australian High Commissioner to Sri Lanka Matthew Duckworth yesterday briefed senior editors from leading Sri Lankan media houses on Australia’s diplomatic priorities and bilateral initiatives, ahead of the 80th anniversary of diplomatic relations between the two countries next year.

The briefing was organised by the OzLanka Journalists’ Consortium in partnership with Australia’s Market Development Facility (MDF). Discussions covered Australian investment, development partnerships and Australia’s contribution to Sri Lanka’s economic growth.

“Australia deeply values our longstanding partnership with Sri Lanka, and next year marks 80 years of our bilateral relations,” Duckworth said. “We take this very seriously and look forward to telling this story more broadly next year. As neighbours in this region, we recognise each other as long-term partners and friends, and we want to continue supporting and building upon these ties.”

The High Commissioner also reaffirmed Australia’s support for press freedom and regional media cooperation.

“We value independent and credible journalism as a public good. Therefore, we are committed to engaging with you regularly and operating as openly and transparently as possible,” he said.

He pointed to the recently launched “Digital Turtles” program and exchange visits for media representatives as evidence of Canberra’s commitment to skills development, information sharing and stronger ties between the two countries’ media sectors.

MDF Sri Lanka Country Director Maryam Piracha outlined the work of the Australian Government-funded program, which has operated in the country since 2015. MDF co-invests with private sector firms to promote sustainable, inclusive growth in high-value, export-oriented sectors, including tourism, specialty coffee and agriculture.

“MDF is committed to building partnerships that enhance competitiveness, drive economic diversification, and create inclusive opportunities, particularly for women and smallholder farmers across Sri Lanka’s key value chains, in both agriculture and tourism,” Piracha said.

The event also saw the launch of the OzLanka Media Forum, a platform created to open direct dialogue between the Australian High Commissioner and local journalists on developments in the bilateral relationship.

OzLanka Journalists’ Consortium President Claude Gunasekara welcomed the initiative and said he hoped the forum would help deepen bilateral understanding and engagement through Sri Lanka’s media.

Duckworth thanked the participating journalists and said he looked forward to further collaboration.
