Protest against PTA, proposed anti terrorism and organised crime Laws

The People’s Struggle Alliance has staged a protest against the Prevention of Terrorism Act (PTA), the proposed Protection of the State from Terrorism Act (PSTA) and proposed legislation to prevent organised crime, calling on the Government to withdraw laws that could threaten the people's fundamental rights.

Rajkumar Rajeevkanth, an executive committee member of the People’s Struggle Alliance, said the protest was held to protect human rights, democratic freedoms and the rule of law.

He said the group opposed laws that could give excessive powers to the police and security forces while weakening the role of the judiciary.

Such laws could lead to arbitrary arrests, long periods of detention and human rights violations, he said.

“Many people detained under the PTA have spent years in prison without being found guilty of any crime. Some were later released after losing valuable years of their lives, while their families and livelihoods were badly affected,” he said.

Rajeevkanth said protecting national security and preventing organised crime were important responsibilities of the Government. However, these goals should not be achieved by violating people's basic rights.

He also criticised the Government for attempting to introduce new laws that could give authorities similar powers to those provided under legislation it had previously opposed.

“Political positions may change when governments change, but our principles must remain consistent,” he said.

The alliance called on the Government to repeal the PTA without introducing another law with similar powers.

It also demanded the withdrawal of the proposed PSTA in its current form and called for public consultation and an independent human rights review before introducing any new anti-terrorism law.

The group further called for a review of the proposed organised crime legislation to remove provisions that could allow unfair arrests, excessive investigative powers and restrictions on people's freedoms.

It also demanded that every arrested person be brought before a judge without delay, informed of the charges and given access to a lawyer.

The alliance called for an urgent review of cases involving people held under the PTA for long periods, saying those who have no legal reason to remain in custody should be released.

It also urged the Government to conduct independent investigations into allegations of torture, enforced disappearances, unlawful detention and other human rights abuses.

The group said national security laws should be applied equally, without discrimination based on ethnicity, religion, language or political beliefs.

Rajeevkanth said the public should be given an opportunity to examine proposed laws before they are introduced.

“A law does not become democratic simply because it is introduced by a government that once opposed repression. Legislation must be judged by the powers it gives authorities and the safeguards it provides to the public,” he said.

He said the alliance would continue to oppose the misuse of State power, regardless of which political party was in Government.

“We are not demanding special privileges. We are demanding justice, accountability, fair legal procedures, judicial independence and equal protection under the law,” he said.
