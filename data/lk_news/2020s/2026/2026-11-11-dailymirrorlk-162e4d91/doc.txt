EY remains eligible for bank audits

Financial sector regulator, the Central Bank, clarified yesterday that Ernst & Young Sri Lanka remains eligible to audit licensed banks, after the regulator asked the firm to withhold bank audit assignments from two partners involved in auditing National Development Bank’s financial statements.

The Central Bank’s Bank Supervision Department said the restriction applies to the engagement partner and the quality assurance partner on the NDB audit.

It said EY retains its recognition as a qualified auditor under the Banking Act. The statement did not name the partners or specify the duration of the restriction.

The clarification followed media reports on testimony to Parliament’s Committee on Public Finance on 5 November.

NDB told the Colombo Stock Exchange on 29 September that Deloitte’s final forensic review put the internal fraud at Rs. 13.64 billion, Rs. 60 million above the interim finding disclosed in June. Parliament said the review confirmed that no customer accounts were affected and identified shortcomings in transaction monitoring, daily account reconciliation and internal audit.

The chair of the Sri Lanka Accounting and Auditing Standards Monitoring Board told the committee that findings from its separate inquiry into auditor conduct had been referred to the Attorney General on 15 October for an opinion on legal action.
