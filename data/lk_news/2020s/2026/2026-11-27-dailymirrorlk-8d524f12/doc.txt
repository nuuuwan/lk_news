Two years to complete reconstruction of Ditwah affected transport facilities

Colombo, Nov. 27 (Daily Mirror) - As the Leader of the House and Minister of Transport, Bimal Ratnayake informed Parliament that a two year programme to reconstruct the transport facilities affected by Cyclone Ditwah has got off the ground, Opposition Leader Sajith Premadasa stressed that Sri Lanka is still in need for an effective disaster resilience system to minimize effect of future disasters.

“It was proven that Sri Lanka’s disaster management system has failed. This was proven during the cyclone disaster of last year. What is needed for Sri Lanka is a shift from a disaster relief focus to a disaster resilience focus,” Premadasa said.

He also said the government is yet to complete the reconstruction work and provide compensation to those who were affected by Ditwah.

“This failure is disappointing as it has been recorded despite the allocation of Rs. 50 billion though a supplementary estimate last year.

“What Sri Lanka needs is a system which is aimed at minimising the effects of disasters,” he added.

Leader of the House and Minister of Transport Bimal Ratnayake said the government has already launched a two year programme to rebuild the roads, bridges and other transport facilities. He said Rs. 125 billion has been allocated for this programme.

“We assure that the Ditwah rehabilitation work will be expedited and completed as soon as possible,” he added.

He said Rs. 49,080 million has been allocated for the reconstruction of houses which were affected by Dilwah while a sum of Rs. 29,537 million has been allocated to provide relief and compensation to those who have been affected by Cyclone Ditwah.

Meanwhile, Parliament paid glowing tribute to those who died as a result of Cyclone Ditwah by observing two minutes silence as the first anniversary of the disaster dawned.
