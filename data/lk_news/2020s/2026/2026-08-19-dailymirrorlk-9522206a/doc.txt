GMOA calls for immediate revision of health sector cadre

Colombo, Aug. 19 (Daily Mirror) - The Government Medical Officers’ Association (GMOA) has called for an immediate revision of the approved cadre of doctors and other health workers, warning that delays could threaten the future stability of Sri Lanka’s free health service.

The GMOA said the health workforce is critical to ensuring continuous, equitable and internationally standardised patient care, and stressed the need to deploy adequate staff in all health institutions according to the services provided.

According to the association, a scientific and analytical revision of the approved health workforce covering the entire country has not been carried out for a long period. The last revision of the approved medical cadre was conducted in 2015, while subsequent amendments have been limited to selected hospitals.

The GMOA said the health service has expanded significantly over the past decade due to population growth, the establishment of new hospitals and hospital units, the introduction of new specialist fields and advanced treatment services, changes to the health system, evolving disease patterns, epidemics and natural disasters.

However, the approved cadre has not increased in line with these developments, resulting in health workers having to manage an unsustainable workload, it said.

The association also noted that Sri Lanka has lost more than 2,500 specialists and doctors since 2022 due to migration, while the number of approved doctors remains based on the 2015 cadre.

“However, that number is by no means sufficient to meet the current service needs of the Sri Lankan health system,” the GMOA said.

Although the GMOA has repeatedly highlighted the need to revise the approved medical cadre, the process has yet to be completed. The association said the failure to identify the actual workforce requirements has also hindered the recruitment of doctors needed for the future.

The GMOA said the Management Services Commission, which is responsible for making the final decision on cadre revisions, has yet to identify the actual human resource requirements of the health service.

It called for the revision to go beyond an administrative exercise and instead be based on a scientific and analytical assessment with a long-term outlook.

The association said the revision should consider patient numbers, service volumes, procedures, investigations, the complexity of services and the requirement for 24-hour services. It should also take into account the expansion of health services, newly established institutions and units, and new disciplines and specialist fields introduced over the past decade.

It further called for vacancies created by the migration of health professionals to be identified and filled, while future challenges, service expansion and structural changes should also be incorporated into the assessment.

The GMOA stressed that adequate human resources must also be ensured to improve service quality and patient safety.

It called for the approved cadre of the entire health workforce to be revised immediately through discussions and agreement among relevant stakeholders.

The association also proposed establishing a mechanism to regularly review approved cadres according to changing service requirements, preventing similar long-term delays in the future.

The GMOA warned that maintaining sufficient and quality health personnel is essential for the continued stability of Sri Lanka’s free health service, and that failure to revise the approved cadre immediately could pose a serious challenge to the stability of the country’s hospital system in the years ahead.
