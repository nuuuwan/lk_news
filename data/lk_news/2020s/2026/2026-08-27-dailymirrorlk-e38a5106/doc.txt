BASL calls for responsible scrutiny of Judiciary amid 22nd Amendment concerns

Colombo, August 27 (Daily Mirror) - The Bar Association of Sri Lanka (BASL) has called for a responsible scrutiny of the Judiciary while reiterating its opposition to the proposed Twenty-Second Amendment to the Constitution, citing concerns over judicial independence and the rights and interests of the people.

In a statement issued yesterday, the BASL said it had challenged the constitutionality of the proposed amendment before the Supreme Court, maintaining that the Bill raises serious concerns regarding the independence of the Judiciary.

The BASL observed that the Chief Justice would be the first Supreme Court judge to benefit from the proposed amendment if it becomes law. It also said there was a widespread belief that the amendment was being rushed to facilitate the extension of the Chief Justice’s tenure, which would otherwise end on December 1, 2026.

The statement comes amid allegations concerning the Judiciary, including the incumbent Chief Justice, made during Parliamentary proceedings and on social media. The BASL said it had itself been subjected to what it described as unfounded attacks on social media and other platforms.

The BASL stressed that the Judiciary, like every other institution exercising public power, must remain subject to legitimate scrutiny and accountability.

It said judicial judgments, judicial conduct and the administration of justice could properly be subjected to fair, informed and responsible criticism. Where credible concerns or allegations arise concerning any member of the Judiciary, including the Chief Justice, such matters should be examined objectively and addressed through the appropriate Constitutional and parliamentary processes, with due regard to fairness and due process.

At the same time, the BASL urged all parties to act responsibly when making allegations against individual judges, lawyers and others.

It warned that personal attacks based on unsubstantiated allegations could undermine public confidence in the administration of justice and adversely affect the independence and integrity of the judicial process and the justice system.

The BASL further emphasised that judicial independence should not be regarded as a shield against legitimate scrutiny or accountability. Rather, it said judicial independence is a safeguard for the people, ensuring that disputes are determined impartially and that individual rights, the Rule of Law and the Constitutional order are protected.

The BASL called on all organs of the State, relevant parties and the media to respect the role of the Judiciary and the system of administration of justice, while ensuring that genuine concerns are addressed fairly, responsibly and through procedures established by the Constitution and the law.

The statement was signed by BASL President Rajeev Amarasuriya and Secretary Nalin De Silva.
