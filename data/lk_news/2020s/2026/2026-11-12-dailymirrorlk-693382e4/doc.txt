Sri Lanka women dominate Emerging Nations opener as Pakistan hold test advantage

Gunaratne and Perera Power Sri Lanka Women to Dominant Victory

NOV.12 (Mirror Sports) - Sri Lanka made an impressive start to their ICC Women's Emerging Nations Trophy 2026 campaign, securing a convincing 10-wicket victory over Ireland in their opening Group A fixture in Ajman.

After winning the toss and bowling first, Sri Lanka restricted Ireland to 105 for nine in 20 overs, with Sathya Sandeepani and Kaveesha Dilhari leading the bowling effort with three wickets each.

Ireland had reached 84 for three before losing six wickets for just 18 runs, struggling to recover against Sri Lanka's disciplined attack.

In reply, Vishmi Gunaratne and Hasini Perera produced an outstanding batting display, scoring unbeaten half-centuries to guide Sri Lanka home without losing a wicket.

Both batters finished on 53 not out, sharing an unbroken 110-run opening partnership as Sri Lanka reached 106 without loss in just 13 overs, sealing victory with seven overs to spare.

Meanwhile, Pakistan strengthened their position in the first Test against Sri Lanka, reaching 57 for one in their second innings before rain and bad light brought an early end to the fourth day's play at Rawalpindi Cricket Stadium.

Pakistan now hold an overall lead of 45 runs, with Azan Awais unbeaten on 22 and Mohammad Taha not out on 24 at stumps.

Sri Lanka had earlier taken a 12-run first-innings lead after being dismissed for 446 in reply to Pakistan's 434 for nine declared.

Dhananjaya de Silva and captain Kusal Mendis were the standout performers for Sri Lanka, scoring 167 and 119 respectively while sharing a massive 249-run partnership that put the visitors in a strong position.

However, debutant Saqib Khan produced an outstanding bowling performance to bring Pakistan back into the contest, claiming seven wickets for 130 runs as Sri Lanka lost their final six wickets for just 42 runs on the fourth morning.

Asitha Fernando provided Sri Lanka with an early breakthrough in Pakistan's second innings, dismissing opener Tayyab Arif for 11. However, Azan and Taha steadied the innings before the weather intervened.

With Pakistan holding a 45-run advantage and nine second-innings wickets remaining, the opening Test remains finely poised heading into the final day's play.
