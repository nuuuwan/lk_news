2,000 MT of imported maize for Thriposha held up at Colombo Port, COPE told

Colombo, Nov. 25 (Daily Mirror) - A consignment of 2,000 metric tonnes of maize imported for the production of Thriposha has remained held up at the Colombo Port for nearly a year due to pending regulatory approvals, Sri Lanka Thriposha Limited Chairman Amal Attanayake informed the Parliamentary Committee on Public Enterprises (COPE).

Attanayake said the shipment, imported in 2024 by a private company following Cabinet approval, could not be released due to the failure to obtain the necessary facilities and approvals from the Controller General of Imports and Exports.

He said the shortage of maize had severely affected production, forcing the Thriposha factory to remain closed for two months in the recent past.

COPE was also informed that delays in obtaining approvals from the Ministry of Agriculture had contributed to the decline in production.

The Chairman said maintaining the factory's closure for a single day costs approximately Rs. 1.2 million.

Sri Lanka requires around 1.8 million 750-gram packets of Thriposha every month to distribute among pregnant and lactating mothers and children aged between three and five years.

Meeting this demand requires 1,337 metric tonnes of maize and rice. However, production currently meets only around 49.5 per cent of the total requirement due to shortages of raw materials.

The committee was further informed that only around 536,000 packets of Thriposha were produced in September because of the inability to meet the required maize supplies.

Speaking further, Attanayake said the imported maize had remained at the port for an extended period, raising concerns about its quality. He said fresh quality testing would be required once the consignment is released.

He added that Sri Lanka Thriposha Limited now has full authorisation to import maize directly for production, unlike the earlier arrangement under which the 2,000-metric-tonne consignment was imported by a private company.
