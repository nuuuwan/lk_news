Universities, media organisations silent on major media challenges, academic criticises

Colombo, November 24 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka's universities and media organisations have failed to adequately examine some of the most important challenges facing the country's media landscape, a senior academic said.

Addressing the opening ceremony of the Institute for Media Development and Research (IMDR) in Homagama, Professor Tudor Weerasinghe of the Sri Palee Campus, University of Colombo, said important issues affecting media freedom, digital communication and national security had not received sufficient academic attention.

He particularly questioned the limited public and institutional discussion surrounding proposed legislation aimed at protecting the State from terrorism.

Professor Weerasinghe said the proposed legislation could have far-reaching consequences for the existing media framework, yet media organisations had failed to properly examine its implications.

He also questioned why universities had not emerged as stronger voices in examining such developments.

He said Sri Lanka had hundreds of institutions offering media education, ranging from certificate courses to doctoral degrees, and around 6,000 to 7,000 media practitioners.

However, he questioned whether the country's existing media education and professional practices were making a meaningful contribution to national and human development.

He said a dedicated research institution was necessary to examine these shortcomings and generate knowledge that could guide the future of Sri Lanka's media industry.
