S&P upgrades Sri Lanka’s sovereign credit rating to B-

Colombo, Nov. 27 (Daily Mirror) - International credit rating agency S&P Global Ratings has upgraded Sri Lanka’s sovereign credit rating to B- from CCC+, assigning the country a stable outlook, citing the growing consolidation of improvements in its macroeconomic conditions.

In its latest assessment, S&P said the stable outlook reflects its expectation that Sri Lanka’s economy would remain resilient over the next six to 12 months despite prolonged disruptions to global energy markets.

The agency also noted that the Government was continuing to build fiscal and external buffers through policies aimed at strengthening macroeconomic stability.

According to S&P, improvements in the country’s macroeconomic conditions have contributed to a steady increase in fiscal and external buffers, strengthening the Government’s ability to manage external volatility and higher external debt repayments expected in the coming years.

“We believe Sri Lanka’s willingness and capacity to repay debt obligations is less vulnerable to unfavourable external financing conditions, and that a default scenario over the next two years is unlikely,” the agency said.

However, S&P cautioned that risks remained, particularly in the event of renewed funding and liquidity pressures.

It said a significant deterioration in the country’s external or fiscal performance could lead to renewed funding pressures and potentially result in a downgrade of the ratings.

The agency also outlined several factors that could support a further upgrade of Sri Lanka’s credit rating.

These include a substantial reduction in the Government’s interest burden as a share of revenue to levels more consistent with countries rated by S&P, continued improvement in the country’s external position, a sustained change in net general government debt of around 3% of GDP, and further strengthening of macroeconomic stability.

Despite the positive assessment, S&P warned that Sri Lanka remained vulnerable to global shortages of crude oil and petrochemicals.

It said a prolonged conflict in the Middle East could continue to place pressure on the country’s external position, particularly if remittance inflows weaken.
