India to approve 60 smart classrooms within 10 days

Colombo, Nov. 20 (Daily Mirror) - India is expected to grant final approval for a project to establish 60 smart classrooms in plantation schools within the next seven to 10 days, paving the way for the project to benefit schoolchildren before the end of this year, Minister of Plantations and Community Infrastructure Samantha Vidyaratne said.

The progress of the project was discussed during a meeting between Minister Vidyaratne, Head of the Development Cooperation Division of the Indian High Commission Roshni Thomson and an Indian delegation at the Ministry yesterday (19).

Thomson informed the Minister that the necessary approval from India's Ministry of External Affairs would be granted within a week to 10 days.

Minister Vidyaratne stressed the importance of expediting the approval process as the project had reached its final stage and was expected to expand educational opportunities for children in plantation communities.

The meeting also reviewed progress in India's housing projects in Sri Lanka.

Under the first phase, 670 of the planned 1,300 houses had been completed by October 31, while construction of another 306 houses had reached roof level. The Minister said the Government expected to complete nearly 1,000 houses by December 20.

Discussions were also held on expediting the construction of 2,026 houses under the second phase, for which title deeds have already been issued, and adopting more practical criteria for selecting beneficiaries in future phases.

The two sides also discussed India's contribution to the housing assistance programme for people whose houses were damaged by Cyclone Ditwah.

The Indian delegation expressed its readiness to provide the initial Rs. 2 million instalment for each of the 5,400 houses completely destroyed by the disaster, under Sri Lanka's Rs. 5 million housing assistance scheme. India also agreed to provide Rs. 500,000 for each partially damaged house.

Minister Vidyaratne thanked the Indian Government for extending assistance to disaster-affected families in addition to its ongoing housing programmes.

He also agreed to ensure that the assistance programme would be implemented transparently and systematically in coordination with the 'Rebuild Sri Lanka' programme supervised by the Presidential Secretariat.

Ministry Secretary Gunadasa Samarasinghe, Indian High Commission Second Secretary Ramya C.S. and Senior Accounts Officer Sanjeev Tiwari also attended the meeting.
