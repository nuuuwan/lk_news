Authorities plan climate adaptation measures; families obstructing drainage to be relocated

Colombo, November 11 (Daily Mirror) -  Colombo, a city built on wetlands, remains increasingly vulnerable to the effects of global warming, with intense rainfall and flash floods posing a growing threat. Municipal authorities, together with other stakeholders, are now planning the relocation of squatters obstructing drainage systems and climate adaptation measures for residents and daily commuters, an official said.

Colombo has expanded over wetland areas over the centuries, resulting in a reduction in its natural water absorption capacity, according to Municipal Commissioner A.D.S. Rohana.

He told the Daily Mirror yesterday that areas located below 2.5 metres above sea level are always prone to flooding.

The canal system built during the Dutch period can discharge a volume of 350 mm of water spread over a day. However, the drainage system designed during the British period to discharge rainfall of 30 mm per hour cannot cope with high-intensity rainfall, he said.

He said the clearance of drainage systems had begun, while people residing on lands obstructing them would be relocated.

“If there is vegetation, rainfall is absorbed. Today, the city is full of concrete structures. It has reduced the water retention capacity of the city,” he said.

Colombo receives an annual rainfall of around 2,500 mm, most of which occurs during the last three months of the year. Last Friday, the city received 68 mm of rainfall within 40 minutes.

“Climate adaptation is another aspect we look at. If we can get early forecasts for the day, the city's activities can be planned accordingly. Today, it is an indispensable option given the vagaries of climate change,” he said.
