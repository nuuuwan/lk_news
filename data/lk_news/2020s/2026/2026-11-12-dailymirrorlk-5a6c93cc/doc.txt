Sri Lanka to expand bunkering capacity, develop three ports as energy hubs – President

Colombo, Nov. 12 (Daily Mirror) - President Anura Kumara Dissanayake announced plans to expand Sri Lanka's bunkering capacity and develop the Ports of Colombo, Galle and Trincomalee to strengthen the country's position as a competitive regional energy hub.

Presenting the 2027 Budget in Parliament today, the President said the increasing number of vessels arriving at Sri Lankan ports had created significant opportunities to expand bunkering services.

"With the number of vessels arriving at Sri Lankan ports increasing day by day, there is great potential to expand and strengthen bunkering capacity and to establish Sri Lanka as a competitive regional bunkering hub," he said.

Accordingly, the Government expects to increase bunkering capacity at the Port of Colombo by 50,000 metric tonnes with the support of the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC) and private sector participation under a Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT) arrangement.

The President said the CPC and private sector would also work together to develop storage and pipeline systems required for bunkering services centred on the Port of Galle.

He further identified the Port of Trincomalee as having the potential to become a major fuel storage hub and a strategic link to the South Asian regional energy supply network.

"Steps will be taken to develop this jointly through State and international partnerships," he said.

The President said repair work on two of the five selected tanks at the Trincomalee Upper Tank Farm had already been completed.

A new pipeline system connecting the tank farm to the port is also planned, with the CPC expected to invest Rs. 7,375 million in the project.

The Government also plans to invite investment proposals in 2027 to develop 29 of the 61 tanks owned by Trinco Petroleum Terminal (Private) Limited at the Port of Trincomalee, which was jointly established by the CPC and Lanka IOC.

The development will be carried out under a public-private partnership.

The President expressed confidence that the proposed developments would significantly strengthen Sri Lanka's maritime competitiveness and establish the country as a key energy hub in the global shipping and maritime industry, including offshore bunkering.
