Public Health Inspectors left out of Budget 2027, union says

The Public Health Inspectors' Union of Sri Lanka has expressed dissatisfaction over the failure to address the long-standing issues faced by around 2,400 Public Health Inspectors in the 2027 Budget proposals.

The union said Public Health Inspectors serving under the Department of Health and provincial administrations continued to perform their duties islandwide despite facing several operational difficulties.

It pointed out that while office allowances for other field officers covering a single Grama Niladhari division had been increased in this year's Budget, no such increase had been proposed for Public Health Inspectors.

Public Health Inspectors are responsible for areas covering between 10 and 20 Grama Niladhari divisions and are required to maintain field offices. However, their current office allowances remain at Rs. 1,500 for urban areas and Rs. 750 for rural areas, the union said.

The union also raised concerns over the failure to revise travel allowances, noting that Public Health Inspectors travel around 100 kilometres daily while carrying out duties across their respective areas.

It further said the Budget had failed to introduce a programme to provide motorcycles to officers who were eligible to receive them but had not yet been provided with vehicles.

Expressing deep dissatisfaction over what it described as the neglect of their services, the union urged the authorities to take immediate steps to address these issues within the 2027 Budget.

The statement was issued by C.S. Muthukuda, General Secretary of the Public Health Inspectors' Union of Sri Lanka.
