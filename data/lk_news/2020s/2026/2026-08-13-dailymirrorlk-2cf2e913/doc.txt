Remembering Gunapala Malalasekera:  Great Intellectual who brought Sinhala and English together

Inspired by the Buddhist revival led by Anagarika Dharmapala, he began using the name ‘Gunapala Piyasena’ in place of his foreign names, ‘George’ and ‘Pieris’, and subsequently became known as G. P. Malalasekera

In today’s world, we are unlikely to encounter another academic, scholar, and diplomat who has left such an indelible mark—both internationally and within the nation’s own history—as Sri Lanka’s Gunapala Malalasekera.

indeed, he is a distinguished son of Sri Lanka who commands global renown. He left an indelible mark on the field of the English language through his English-Sinhala dictionary—a legacy destined to endure for centuries. He served as Sri Lanka’s first official representative in Russia, as well as High Commissioner to Canada and the United Kingdom.

Additionally, he served as Sri Lanka’s Permanent Representative to the United Nations in New York, distinguishing himself as a scholarly diplomat. A statue of this peerless Sri Lankan, who rendered incomparable service to the nation, is being unveiled on August 14, 2026, at 9:00 a.m. in the historic town of Panadura — the place where he lived. It is particularly significant that this initiative of the Professor Gunapala Malalasekera Foundation has come to fruition at this juncture.

He was born on November 9, 1899, in the Malamulla area of ​​Panadura, under the name Malalage George Peiris Malalasekera. His father was the renowned indigenous physician, Ayurvedic physician Malalage Siyadoris Peiris Malalasekera.

Mr. Gunapala Malalasekera received his education at St. John’s College, Panadura. Under the leadership of its principal, Mr. Cyril Jansz—a prominent educationist of the colonial era—the college was one of the leading English schools in Ceylon at the time. One day at school, another student noticed that his lunch packet was wrapped in a piece of a Sinhala newspaper; the student reported this to the school administration, resulting in him being punished.

The reason for this was that, as it was an English school, students were strictly prohibited from speaking or writing in Sinhala; this was a rule enforced in English schools during the era of British missionary administration. He responded to the criticism he once faced for upholding his Sinhala identity by creating a Sinhala-English dictionary that combined the language of the British with the Sinhala language.

After receiving his education at that school from 1907 to 1917, Mr. Gunapala enrolled at the Ceylon Medical College to qualify as a doctor, but his medical education was cut short due to his father’s death.

Subsequently, in 1919, he enrolled at the University of London as an external student and obtained a Bachelor of Arts (BA) degree with First Class Honours, specialising in Greek, Latin, and French.

He then enrolled at the University of London and, in 1925, demonstrated his academic excellence to the international community by simultaneously obtaining both a Master of Arts (MA) and a Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) in Oriental Languages ​​through the London School of Oriental Studies.

Inspired by the Buddhist revival led by Anagarika Dharmapala, he began using the name ‘Gunapala Piyasena’ in place of his foreign names, ‘George’ and ‘Pieris’, and subsequently became known as G. P. Malalasekera. Subsequently, he joined the staff of Ananda College, Colombo, and, through successive promotions, served as the college’s Deputy Principal and Acting Principal. Later, he was appointed as the first registered Principal of Nalanda College, Colombo, and elevated the institution to a prominent position within the country’s education system.

Following the establishment of the University of Ceylon in 1942, he became the Professor of Pali and Buddhist Studies there. He rendered significant service in the capacities of Head of that Department and Dean of the Faculty of Oriental Studies.

His research into Buddhism and Buddhist civilisation was extensive in scope; consequently, he became the Editor-in-Chief of the Encyclopaedia of Buddhism. Elected as Secretary of the All Ceylon Buddhist Congress in 1927, he subsequently served as its Vice-President and President, dedicating himself to an unceasing mission of service. Driven by a deep love for his country and nation, he further expanded his contributions by serving as an active member of the Royal Asiatic Society of Ceylon.

Malalasekera’s hallmark—or his most distinctive publication—was the ‘Malalasekera Sinhala-English Dictionary’ (1948). It was a magnificent contribution to the linguistic and scholarly heritage of the nation. This work garnered attention and acclaim not only within the country but across the globe. He also represented Sri Lanka at various international conferences, including those held in England, Hawaii, India, Pakistan, and the United States. Furthermore, he held the presidency of the World Fellowship of Buddhists and the Ceylon Arts Society with great distinction.

Professor Malalasekera served with distinguished diplomatic skill as Ceylon’s High Commissioner to Canada, as Ceylon’s Permanent Representative to the United Nations in New York, and as Ceylon’s High Commissioner to the United Kingdom.

Professor Gunapala Malalasekera, who played a distinguished role in the fields of language, literature, national culture, and international service, passed away on April 23, 1973. This brilliant luminary of learning, gifted to both the nation and the world, continues to bring honor to our country to this day. Although fifty-three years have elapsed since his passing, the radiance of his scholarship endures undimmed.

(This writer, too, shares the ancestral heritage of the ‘Malalage’ family lineage to which the Professor belonged.)
