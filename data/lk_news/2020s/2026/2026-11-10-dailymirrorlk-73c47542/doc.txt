Up to 1.49 million Sri Lankans may need food assistance until May 2027

Colombo, Nov. 10 (Daily Mirror) - Between 1 million and 1.49 million people in Sri Lanka may require humanitarian food assistance until May 2027, as rising food prices, increasing living costs and weather-related disruptions continue to put pressure on vulnerable households, according to the latest outlook by the Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS NET).

The report projects widespread Stressed (IPC Phase 2) food security conditions between October 2026 and January 2027, as household food stocks from the 2026 Yala harvest gradually decline and families become increasingly dependent on market purchases.

The greatest pressure is expected between October and January, particularly among low-income plantation workers, poor coastal fishing communities and the urban poor. Food assistance needs are forecast to peak between October and December. However, no large-scale humanitarian food assistance is anticipated during the October 2026 to May 2027 projection period.

Seasonal rainfall associated with the second inter-monsoon and northeast monsoon could bring floods, strong winds, landslides and rough seas, disrupting agricultural and fishing activities. Combined with high production, fuel and transportation costs, these conditions are expected to affect household incomes and reduce purchasing power.

However, food security conditions are expected to improve from February 2027, particularly in the Dry Zone, as the 2026/27 Maha harvest replenishes household food stocks, increases market supplies and creates additional agricultural employment opportunities.

The Maha harvest, expected between January and March 2027, is forecast to be broadly favourable and comparable to the recent five-year average. Nevertheless, rice prices are expected to remain above their five-year averages despite improved market supplies.

In September, retail prices of Nadu and Samba rice in Colombo were 11% and 5% above their respective five-year averages. FEWS NET expects prices to remain elevated as household stocks decline before easing slightly with increased supplies from the Maha harvest in February and March.

Low-income plantation workers, particularly those dependent on irregular agricultural wages, are among the groups expected to face continued difficulties in meeting both food and essential non-food expenses.

Poor coastal fishing households are also likely to experience difficulties as rough seas and seasonal weather conditions reduce fishing days and catches, while high fuel and operating costs further affect their earnings.

Meanwhile, despite continued economic recovery, renewed inflationary pressure and rising essential costs are affecting household purchasing power.

Headline inflation increased from 2.3% in January to 8.3% in October 2026, while the rupee depreciated by 6.2% against the US dollar between January and September, according to the report.

Fuel costs have also increased, with diesel and gas prices rising by approximately 41% between January and October 2026, partly due to higher global energy and shipping costs. These increases have added to the costs of food transportation, agricultural production, fishing and other essential goods and services.

The report also warns that below-average rainfall expected during the March-April 2027 first inter-monsoon could restrict water availability and affect agricultural and livestock activities. This could delay land preparation and planting for the next Yala season, particularly in rainfed areas and locations with limited irrigation.

Although staple food availability is expected to remain broadly adequate until May 2027, continued pressure from food prices, fuel costs, agricultural inputs and other essential expenses is likely to limit vulnerable households' ability to meet their basic needs.
