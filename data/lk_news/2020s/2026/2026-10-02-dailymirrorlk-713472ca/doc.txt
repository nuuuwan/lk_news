Fuel distribution faces crucial decision today

Colombo, Oct. 2 (Daliy Mirror) - The country's fuel distribution network could face a major disruption from tomorrow as the Ceylon Petroleum Private Tanker Owners Association (CPPTOA) warned that private fuel bowsers may halt deliveries unless a long-pending revision of fuel transportation commission charges is resolved today.

CPPTOA Chairman A.M.H. Adhikari told the Daily Mirror that today's discussions with Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC) Chairman, two professors from the University of Moratuwa and association representatives would be decisive in determining whether private tanker operators continue fuel distribution.

He said the Association was seeking a revised fuel transport formula that would provide tanker owners with a commission of at least 20%.

Adhikari said the CPPTOA had requested a revision of transportation charges in 2025, with discussions held to introduce the revised rates from January 2026. However, he said no revision had been implemented so far despite several rounds of discussions with relevant authorities.

“The Association has held several rounds of discussions with the relevant authorities but has failed to receive a favourable response. We need a commission of at least 20% under the fuel transport formula,” he said.

He said the CPC Chairman had assured the Association that a solution would be provided following today's discussions.

Adhikari also claimed that the recent increase in fuel prices had sharply increased the operating costs of private fuel bowsers, while the prices of spare parts, tyres, tubes and vehicle servicing had risen by more than 50%.

“These increased costs have made it extremely difficult for the Association to continue fuel distribution services,” he said.

The CPPTOA Chairman stressed that fuel distribution was an essential service and said tanker owners had continued operating despite mounting costs in an effort to prevent disruptions to the country's fuel supply.

“However, we have now reached a point where we can no longer bear these costs. That is why we have decided to take this decision,” he said.

He warned that if the dispute was not resolved, fuel deliveries to filling stations could be halted across the country from tomorrow.

According to Adhikari, several private fuel bowsers had already stopped transporting fuel, which he said had contributed to queues at a number of filling stations.

The Association is expected to decide on further action depending on the outcome of today's(2) discussions with the CPC Chairman.

Meanwhile, Adhikari also referred to the recent fuel supply situation, claiming that concerns over a possible fuel price increase had resulted in delays in placing fuel orders and contributed to shortages experienced in some areas.
