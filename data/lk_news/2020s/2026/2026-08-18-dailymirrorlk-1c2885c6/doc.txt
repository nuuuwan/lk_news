Cancer patients at risk amid reported shortage of G-CSF drugs: Specialist

Colombo, Aug. 18 (Daily Mirror) - Cancer patients are being placed at risk due to an alleged shortage of essential drugs used to boost immunity and increase white blood cell levels during cancer treatment, Dr. Sanjeewa, of the Doctors' Trade Union Alliance for Medical and Civil Rights Chairman Specialist Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa said.

He said drugs including Neup, Neupogen, GCadF and Filgrastim are reportedly in short supply, leaving patients who depend on them facing a serious threat to their health.

He said these medicines, commonly known as G-CSF drugs, are administered to cancer patients undergoing treatment to stimulate the production of white blood cells and strengthen the body's ability to fight infections.

“These drugs directly affect the white blood cell count and are essential in protecting cancer patients from infections while they undergo treatment,” Dr. Sanjeewa said.

He claimed that the Medical Supplies Division has been unable to accept stocks of the medicines due to an issue that arose during the importation process.

Dr. Sanjeewa also criticised the Health Ministry, saying it was regrettable that a continuous supply of essential and life-saving cancer medicines had not been ensured for nearly two years.

He further claimed that the shortage is being experienced not only in government hospitals but also in the private sector, warning that the situation could have serious consequences for cancer patients who require the drugs as part of their treatment. The claims could not be independently verified.
