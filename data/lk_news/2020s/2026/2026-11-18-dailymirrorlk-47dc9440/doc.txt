75 King Coconut villages established under national programme

Colombo, Nov. 18 (Daily Mirror) - Preparations for the establishment of 75 King Coconut villages across 18 districts under the Government's “Ceylon King Coconut Village 250” national programme have been completed, as Sri Lanka moves to expand cultivation and boost export earnings.

The programme, implemented by the Coconut Cultivation Board under the Ministry of Plantation and Community Infrastructure, aims to increase King Coconut production and productivity while expanding international markets for the fruit and related products.

Launched on September 2, 2026, to coincide with World Coconut Day, the initiative aims to increase the country's annual King Coconut harvest by 25 million nuts by 2030 and raise export revenue from King Coconut and related products by more than US$12 million.

Under the three-year programme, 250 King Coconut villages are expected to be established islandwide, with 75 villages in 2026, 80 in 2027 and 95 in 2028.

So far, 31,128 King Coconut saplings have been planted, while 6,163 existing trees have been rehabilitated.

Farmers are being provided with a subsidy of Rs.700 for the rehabilitation of each existing tree. New saplings, which have a market value of Rs.750 each, are being supplied to farmers at a subsidised price of Rs.150.

Meanwhile, 45 training and awareness programmes have been conducted to improve farmers' technical knowledge and skills in cultivation and crop management.

A database covering 2,544 land plots belonging to King Coconut growers is also being developed.

The 75 villages cover Kurunegala, Kegalle, Gampaha, Kalutara, Puttalam, Matara, Ratnapura, Hambantota, Anuradhapura, Trincomalee, Polonnaruwa, Ampara, Matale, Galle, Batticaloa, Badulla, Monaragala and Kandy districts.

The programme is also expected to generate new income sources and employment opportunities across the entire supply chain, including harvesting, transportation, processing and exports.

It further aims to improve the livelihoods of those engaged in the King Coconut industry, strengthen rural economies and make rural communities active partners in the country's economic development.
