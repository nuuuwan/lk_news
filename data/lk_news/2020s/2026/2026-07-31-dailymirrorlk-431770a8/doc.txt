Sajith to host ’Sri Lankan Leopard Day’ tomorrow

Colombo, July 31 (Daily Mirror) - Leader of the Opposition, Sajith Premadasa, will host the 'Sri Lankan Leopard Day 2026' tomorrow (1 Aigist) from 2.00 p.m. to 5.00 p.m. at the Opposition Leader's office in Colombo 7, bringing together leading experts, policymakers, conservationists, researchers, government institutions, civil society organisations, the private sector and young people to strengthen national efforts to conserve the Sri Lankan leopard.

The Sri Lankan leopard (Panthera pardus kotiya) is one of the country's most treasured wildlife species and an apex predator that plays a vital role in maintaining the ecological balance of Sri Lanka's forests and natural ecosystems. As an endemic subspecies found nowhere else on Earth, its conservation is a matter of national and global importance.

Despite its significance, the Sri Lankan leopard continues to face growing threats from habitat fragmentation, snaring, poisoning, road fatalities, human-induced mortality and other forms of wildlife crime. Addressing these challenges requires stronger collaboration among all sectors of society, supported by sound science, effective policy, public awareness and community participation.

The Sri Lankan Leopard Day 2026 has been organised to provide a national platform for dialogue and action on leopard conservation. The programme will highlight the latest scientific knowledge, discuss practical conservation solutions and encourage stronger partnerships among all stakeholders committed to protecting Sri Lanka's unique natural heritage.

A key feature of the event will be the active participation of students and young conservation leaders. By engaging directly with scientists and wildlife experts, the programme seeks to inspire a new generation of environmental stewards who will champion wildlife conservation and biodiversity protection in the years ahead.

Premadasa stated that protecting the Sri Lankan leopard is not solely a wildlife conservation issue but a national responsibility. Conserving this iconic species safeguards the health of Sri Lanka's ecosystems, strengthens the country's natural heritage and reflects a broader commitment to sustainable development and responsible environmental stewardship.

The event is expected to attract representatives from government agencies, universities, research institutions, conservation organisations, environmental groups, the corporate sector, diplomatic missions, the media and youth organisations, reaffirming a shared commitment to ensuring the long-term survival of the Sri Lankan leopard.
