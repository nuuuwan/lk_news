Premature babies need lifelong follow-up care, Deputy Health Minister says

Colombo, Nov. 17 (Daily Mirror) - The responsibility of healthcare services should not end with saving the life of a premature baby, as continuous medical care and follow-up are essential to ensure the child's healthy growth and development, Deputy Health Minister Dr. Hansaka Wijemuni said.

He made these remarks at a special awareness programme organised by the Perinatal Society of Sri Lanka to mark World Prematurity Day, which falls on November 15.

The event was held yesterday at the Courtyard by Marriott hotel at Colombo City Centre, under the patronage of Dr. Wijemuni.

The Deputy Minister stressed that premature babies require continued medical attention even after being discharged from Neonatal Intensive Care Units (NICUs).

He warned that the lack of proper follow-up care could lead to complications affecting children's overall development, including their eyesight and respiratory health.

He said strengthening maternal and neonatal healthcare services remained a key priority of the Ministry of Health and commended healthcare professionals for their contribution to managing high-risk pregnancies and saving the lives of premature babies.

He also stressed that caring for premature babies should not be considered the sole responsibility of the state healthcare system, adding that professional associations, the private sector and other stakeholders must work together to ensure these children receive the care they need.

"Every premature infant deserves the care and opportunities required to reach their full potential in life. All stakeholders must act with a sense of collective responsibility," he said.

A special expert discussion involving the Deputy Minister and medical specialists was also held during the programme. A set of special guidelines aimed at improving the health and well-being of premature babies and their long-term health outcomes was unveiled.

According to available data, around 10 per cent of births in Sri Lanka are premature.

Although advances in medical science have improved survival rates among premature babies, proper medical treatment and long-term follow-up remain essential to minimise complications associated with premature birth.

Such interventions are also important in reducing the long-term social and economic impact of premature births on families and society.

The Perinatal Society of Sri Lanka is a multidisciplinary professional organisation comprising specialists in obstetrics and gynaecology, paediatrics and neonatal care. It works to improve maternal, fetal and newborn healthcare through research, professional training and the promotion of scientific knowledge.

The event was attended by Society President Professor Ruwanthi Perera, Secretary Consultant Physician Dr. Jithma Fonseka, Treasurer Consultant Physician Dr. Ruwan Silva, President-elect Consultant Physician Dr. Sri Tharan and Immediate Past President Consultant Physician Dr. Harendra Dissanayake, along with other medical specialists and representatives.
