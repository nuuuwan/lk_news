Energy Ministry seeks legal advice on private fuel operators’ pricing agreements

The Energy Ministry is seeking legal advice on long-term agreements with private fuel operators, which allow them to revise certain fuel prices to avoid losses and could require the Government to compensate them if the prescribed selling prices result in losses, Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC) Chairman D. J. Rajakaruna said.

He said the CPC had secured sufficient fuel supplies and stocks to ensure uninterrupted distribution well into the middle of next year, assuring the public that there was no fuel shortage or supply issue in the country.

Rajakaruna said the CPC had no difficulties maintaining a steady fuel supply, while recent queues reported at certain filling stations were mainly due to distribution constraints faced by some private petroleum companies.

According to him, private operators, including Sinopec and RM Parks, had reduced their fuel distribution volumes due to financial losses under the existing pricing structure. This had resulted in temporary supply disruptions in certain areas, particularly where private filling stations were the main fuel providers.

He said discussions had been held between the Ministry, the CPC and the private operators to address the situation, with the companies instructed to prioritise fuel distribution in areas where supply constraints had been reported, despite lower profit margins.

The CPC also plans to establish 75 additional filling stations to improve fuel distribution and accessibility across the country, he said.

Explaining the difficulties faced by private fuel operators, Rajakaruna said the issue was linked to long-term agreements signed for a period of 20 years, which included provisions relating to a ceiling price and the applicable pricing formula.

Under these agreements, the Government or the relevant Ministry could be required to compensate private operators if the prescribed selling prices resulted in losses, he said.

He added that private operators were also permitted under the agreements to revise certain fuel prices to avoid losses, which had led to adjustments in the prices of Octane 95 petrol and Super Diesel sold by private operators.

Commenting on international oil prices, Rajakaruna said global crude oil and refined petroleum product prices had increased significantly compared with February, with some increases estimated at between 80 per cent and 90 per cent.

However, local fuel prices had increased by only around 40 to 43 per cent, as the Government had absorbed a considerable portion of the additional cost to minimise the impact on consumers, he said.

He added that international market conditions had shown some signs of stabilisation amid developments in conflict-affected regions.

Any benefits arising from a reduction in international fuel prices would be passed on to consumers at the earliest opportunity, Rajakaruna assured.
