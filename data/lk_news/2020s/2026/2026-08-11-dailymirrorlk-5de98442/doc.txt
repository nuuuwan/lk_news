Farmer’s fight over water supply ends in Supreme Court after 20 years

Colombo, Aug. 11 (Daily Mirror) - The Supreme Court has dismissed a nearly 20-year-old legal battle instituted by a farmer in Aralaganwila, Polonnaruwa against a local Govi Sanvidanaya after the organisation temporarily cut off the water supply to his paddy field over a Rs. 15,000 loan default.

The Supreme Court judgement dated August 7, 2026 brings to an end a legal dispute that began with the farmer’s District Court action in July 2006 and continued through the Polonnaruwa District Court, Provincial High Court of Anuradhapura and Supreme Court for approximately two decades.

A three-judge bench comprising Justices Sampath B. Abeykoon, Mahinda Samayawardhena and M. Sampath K. B. Wijeratne, held that there was no basis to interfere with the decision of the Provincial High Court, which had dismissed the farmer’s compensation claim.

The case was instituted by B.G. Dingiri Banda before the Polonnaruwa District Court on July 20, 2006, against the Maduruthenna Bedum Ela Govi Sanvidanaya, its President, Secretary and Treasurer and several other office bearers.

The Plaintiff farmer had sought Rs. 200,000 in damages, claiming that the defendants had unlawfully obstructed the water supply to his paddy field, preventing him from cultivating during the 2006 Yala season and causing him financial loss.

The defendants, however, maintained that the temporary closure of the water access was prompted by the farmer’s failure to repay a Rs. 15,000 loan obtained from the Govi Sanvidanaya.

Evidence revealed that the farmer had failed to attend a general meeting of the Govi Sanvidanaya held on April 7, 2006, despite sending an excuse letter. The general membership rejected his explanation. By that time, he was in arrears on the loan obtained from the organisation.

The Govi Sanvidanaya subsequently decided to close the water access to his paddy field on May 19, 2006, when the farmer had already ploughed the field and was preparing it for planting seed paddy.

The Supreme Court observed that the Govi Sanvidanaya was a voluntary organisation functioning under the patronage of the Mahaweli Development Authority to manage water distribution and other agricultural activities in the area. The farmer and the other defendants were members of the organisation.

Justice Abeykoon observed that the water supply was an essential facility for farmers and that members of the Sanvidanaya were expected to abide by its rules, regulations and established traditions.

The Court found that the closure of the water access was intended as a temporary measure to persuade the farmer to honour his loan obligations or negotiate a settlement with the organisation. The Court further observed that the farmer could have sought a settlement instead of taking a confrontational approach.

The Supreme Court also held that the farmer had failed to take reasonable steps to mitigate his alleged loss and had not produced sufficient evidence to establish the Rs. 200,000 damages claimed.

The District Court of Polonnaruwa had initially ruled in the farmer’s favour on September 9, 2016 and awarded him Rs. 200,000 in damages. However, the Provincial High Court of the North Central Province later set aside that judgement on March 20, 2018 and dismissed his action.

Counsel Chathura Galhena with Naduni Hemantha instructed by Manoja Gunawardana appeared for the plaintiff farmer. Counsel Nuwan Bopage with Chathura Weththasinghe instructed by Achala Wannigama appeared for the defendant.
