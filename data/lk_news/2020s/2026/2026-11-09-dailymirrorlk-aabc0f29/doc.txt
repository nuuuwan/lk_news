Dengue cases surpass 107,000 as Western Province accounts for more than half of infections

Colombo, Nov. 9 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka has reported more than 107,000 dengue cases this year, with the Western Province alone accounting for over half of all reported infections, raising fresh questions about the effectiveness of dengue prevention and control measures.

According to the latest National Dengue Surveillance System figures, a total of 107,651 dengue cases had been reported by midnight on November 7, while 81 deaths had been recorded during the year.

The Western Province has emerged as the country's biggest dengue hotspot, accounting for 55,734 cases, or 51.77 per cent of the national total.

Gampaha District recorded the highest number of infections with 22,465 cases, followed closely by Colombo with 20,771 cases. The Colombo Municipal Council area alone reported 4,413 cases.

The figures raise concerns over why the country's most densely populated and administratively developed province continues to carry such a heavy dengue burden.

The Southern Province recorded the second-highest number of cases at 15,228, accounting for 14.15 per cent of the total, followed by the Central Province with 11,062 cases (10.28 per cent).

Kandy reported 8,765 cases, while Kalutara recorded 6,691 and Matara 6,487.

Meanwhile, 37 Medical Officer of Health (MOH) areas have been identified as high-risk areas during the 44th week of 2026. The average midnight dengue case count for the week stood at 472.

Although the number of reported cases has declined from the July peak, the overall burden remains substantial.

July recorded the highest monthly figure with 29,964 cases, followed by June with 21,533. August saw 10,155 cases, while September and October recorded 5,230 and 5,532 cases respectively.

A further 1,399 cases were reported during the first seven days of November.

The latest figures also show that dengue infections are spread across all nine provinces, with the Northern Province recording the lowest provincial total at 1,718 cases.

Despite the decline from the mid-year peak, the reporting of more than 107,000 infections and 81 deaths raises questions about whether existing mosquito-control campaigns and public awareness programmes are sufficiently effective.

With the Western Province accounting for more than half of all infections, authorities face growing pressure to address the underlying reasons for the concentration of cases and strengthen preventive measures.
