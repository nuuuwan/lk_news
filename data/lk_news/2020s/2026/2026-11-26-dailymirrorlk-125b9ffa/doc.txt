HIV infections rise 48% in Sri Lanka as youth face growing risk

Colombo, Nov. 26 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka has recorded a 48% increase in new HIV infections, despite a significant decline in infections worldwide, raising concerns over the growing number of young people at risk and the country's efforts to contain the spread of the virus.

Since the first HIV case was reported in Sri Lanka in 1987, a total of around 8,111 cases had been identified by the end of the third quarter of 2026. An estimated 8,700 people are currently living with HIV in the country.

Men account for nearly 87% of reported cases, while 87% of HIV-positive men are men who have sex with men, according to the latest figures.

The increasing number of infections among young people aged 15 to 24 is another major concern. HIV transmission through injecting drug use is highest in this age group, highlighting the need for greater awareness and prevention measures.

In contrast, new HIV infections worldwide declined by 42% in 2025 compared with 2010. Sri Lanka, however, has recorded a 48% increase during the same period.

“The increase in HIV infections among young people aged 15 to 24 is particularly worrying. We are also seeing drug use associated with sexual activity, which can increase the risk of HIV transmission. It is important for people to understand these risks and take necessary precautions,” said Dr. Madhavi Gunathilake, Specialist in Community Medicine.

She stressed that early testing and awareness were essential to prevent further transmission of the virus.

People can obtain HIV tests through government sexual health clinics or by visiting **knowforsure.lk**. HIV self-testing kits are also available through the website and can be delivered to people's homes, allowing them to check their HIV status privately.

“People should not hesitate to get tested. They can visit a government sexual health clinic or use knowforsure.lk to access testing services. Self-test kits can also be delivered to their homes, making testing more accessible while protecting their privacy,” Dr. Gunathilake said.

She also highlighted the importance of practising safer sex and avoiding behaviours that increase the risk of infection, including drug use associated with sexual activity.

People diagnosed with HIV must take their medication every day, at the prescribed time, and continue treatment throughout their lives. Interrupting treatment or failing to take medication properly can weaken the immune system and increase the risk of HIV progressing to AIDS.

“Some people do not take their medication regularly or at the correct time. This can weaken their immune system and allow the disease to progress to AIDS. However, with proper treatment and regular medication, people living with HIV can maintain their health and lead normal lives, just like those who are not infected with the virus,” she said.

Dr. Gunathilake said that HIV testing and treatment services are provided with strict confidentiality and urged people who may have been exposed to the virus to seek testing without fear or embarrassment.

The rising number of infections in Sri Lanka, particularly among young people, underlines the urgent need for greater public awareness, regular testing and proper treatment to prevent the spread of HIV.
