Mahanayaka Theras question decision to produce Gnanasara Thera in lay attire

Colombo, October 7 (Daily Mirror) - The Mahanayaka Theras of the four main Nikayas have expressed concern over the decision to produce Ven. Galagoda Aththe Gnanasara Thera in court wearing lay clothes after he was imprisoned following a court verdict.

In a letter to the Commissioner General of Prisons, the Mahanayaka Theras said they were concerned and surprised by the decision to remove the monk’s robes.

The letter was signed by Most Ven. Thibbotuwawe Sri Siddhartha Sumangala Thera of the Malwatu Chapter, Most Ven. Warakagoda Sri Gnanarathana Thera of the Asgiri Chapter, Most Ven. Karagoda-Uyangoda Maithri Murthi Thera of the Amarapura Maha Nikaya, and Most Ven. Makulawe Sri Wimala Thera of the Ramanna Maha Nikaya.

They said they respected the Constitution and the judicial process, but maintained that removing a monk’s robes or ending his status as a monk was a religious matter that should be decided by the Maha Sangha, not by prison authorities alone.

They said the relevant Sangha Council had not decided that Ven. Gnanasara Thera had committed a Parajika offence or that he should be removed from the Buddhist Order.

The Mahanayaka Theras also referred to Regulation 550 of the Prison Standing Orders, which says convicted prisoners should not be taken to court in prison uniforms and should be allowed to wear the type of clothing they normally wore before their conviction.

They said that, in the case of a Buddhist monk, this would mean his monastic robes.

They also referred to the United Nations Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners, which allow prisoners to wear their own ordinary clothing when taken outside prison.

The Mahanayaka Theras therefore requested the Commissioner General of Prisons to allow Ven. Gnanasara Thera to wear his robes when appearing in court and to maintain his status as a monk while in prison custody.
