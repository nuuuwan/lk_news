Seylan Bank 2Q earnings climb 16%

Seylan Bank PLC reported a 16.4 percent increase in profit after tax to Rs.3.17 billion for the second quarter of 2026, supported by higher fee income and trading gains, despite slower growth in core interest earnings and a sharp rise in impairment charges.

Profit before income tax rose 11.9 percent year-on-year to Rs.4.75 billion, while earnings per share increased to Rs.4.99 from Rs.4.29.

Quarterly operating income grew 14.1 percent to Rs.13.17 billion. Interest income increased 21.1 percent, but interest expenses rose faster at 33.1 percent, limiting net interest income growth to 7.1 percent at Rs.9.83 billion.

Net fee and commission income rose 17.9 percent to Rs.2.32 billion, while trading gains surged to Rs.1.34 billion from Rs.76 million a year earlier. However, other operating income swung to a Rs.339 million loss from income of Rs.251 million.

Impairment charges more than tripled to Rs.632 million from Rs.193 million, accounting for most of the Rs.733 million charge recognised in the first half. Operating expenses rose 9.5 percent to Rs.6.15 billion.

The second-quarter performance lifted first-half profit after tax by 10.8 percent to Rs.6.08 billion, while profit before income tax rose 10.1 percent to Rs.9.30 billion. Earnings per share increased to Rs.9.57 from Rs.8.63.

For the six months, net interest income grew 10.1 percent to Rs.19.56 billion. However, interest expenses increased 26.4 percent, outpacing the 19.1 percent growth in interest income and narrowing the net interest margin to 4.16 percent from 4.50 percent at end-2025.

Net fee and commission income rose 20.9 percent to Rs.4.65 billion, while trading gains increased nearly sixfold to Rs.1.12 billion. Total operating income grew 13.3 percent to Rs.25.54 billion.

Operating expenses rose slightly faster at 14.2 percent to Rs.12.28 billion, pushing the cost-to-income ratio to 48.09 percent from 44.52 percent.

Total assets expanded 6.0 percent from December to Rs.976 billion. Net loans and advances grew 8.4 percent to Rs.650.37 billion, outpacing the 5.2 percent increase in deposits to Rs.771.07 billion. The CASA ratio stood at 27 percent.

Asset quality improved on a gross basis, with the Stage 3 impaired-loan ratio declining to 6.97 percent from 7.54 percent. The net Stage 3 ratio remained at 1.03 percent, while provision coverage eased to 85.26 percent from 86.33 percent.

Despite higher earnings, return on equity declined to 14.74 percent from 15.89 percent and pre-tax return on assets fell to 1.98 percent from 2.31 percent.

Capital buffers also narrowed. The common equity Tier I ratio declined to 10.91 percent from 12.39 percent, while the total capital ratio fell to 15.56 percent from 17.89 percent. Both remained above regulatory minimums. The bank redeemed Rs.6 billion in Tier II debentures in April.

Fair-value losses on debt investments reduced total comprehensive income by 29.4 percent to Rs.5.09 billion, even as reported profit increased.

Shareholders’ equity rose 3.1 percent from December to Rs.84.46 billion, while net asset value per share increased to Rs.132.87 from Rs.128.87.
