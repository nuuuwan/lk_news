Where did your 100rs go? Budget 2026 explained

Where did Sri Lanka’s Budget 2026 money go? The biggest share went on interest. According to the Ministry of Finance’s Citizens Budget 2026, Rs. 37 of every Rs. 100 the government set aside for spending went on interest on past loans.

That is Rs. 2,617 billion, or about Rs. 120,100 for every person in Sri Lanka. Measured against what the government expected to collect, interest took Rs. 49 of every Rs. 100.

Budget 2026, presented in November 2025 and passed in December, set aside Rs. 7.10 trillion for government spending, not counting loan repayments. That number is too big to picture, so we shrank it to Rs. 100. We show it two ways: out of every Rs. 100 the government spent, and out of every Rs. 100 it collected. Each square is one rupee.

Budget 2026 was planned to spend Rs. 7.10 trillion against expected revenue of Rs. 5.37 trillion, leaving about Rs. 1.74 trillion to be borrowed. Interest alone costs more than health, education and national security combined.

Budget 2027 will be presented in Parliament on Thursday, 12 November 2026. The second reading debate runs from 13 to 20 November, followed by the committee stage, and the final vote is scheduled for 14 December.

Three questions will show whether anything has changed. Does interest still take more than a third of every Rs. 100 spent? Does revenue close the gap, so the government needs to borrow less than Rs. 32 of every Rs. 132 it spends? And do health, education and capital projects get a bigger share?
