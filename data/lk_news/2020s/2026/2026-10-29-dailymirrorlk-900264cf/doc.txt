Active investors on CSE drop to 60,000 as exchange targets up to 150,000

The number of active investors on the Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) has fallen to around 60,000, from a peak of about 78,000 last year, as the market conditions weakened, prompting the exchange to target 100,000 to 150,000 active investors through a wider branch network and an agent scheme, CSE Chairman Dimuthu Abeyesekera said.

Speaking on Tuesday at the Institutional Investor Forum, jointly organised by the Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka (SEC) and CSE, Abeyesekera pointed out that the market had only about 20,000 to 30,000 active clients in 2023 and 2024. The figure climbed to around 78,000 last year, driven by the initiatives of the CSE and SEC and a rigorous marketing campaign by the stockbroking community.

“Now it has come down to 60,000 because of the market situation,” he told the gathering.

The drop of about 23 percent comes as the All Share Price Index has shed about 13 percent since the start of the year, after gaining nearly 42 percent in 2025. Although the number of Central Depository Systems (CDS) accounts crossed one million last year, the active investors make up only about 6 percent of those accounts.

Noting that only around 0.2 percent of the population participates actively in the stock market, Abeyesekera stressed that the target should be to lift the active investor base to between 100,000 and 150,000, which would require it to grow up to two and a half times from the current levels.

The CSE opened three new branches this year, in Kegalle, Galle and Gampaha, taking its network to 13 branches, with at least one in every province. The exchange now aims to have a branch in every district, which would mean adding 12 more to cover all 25 districts.

Abeyesekera likened the approach to that of the banks, which continue to open branches despite offering online services, because a physical presence helps showcase what they offer. He added that seven or eight stockbrokers have started operations at each new branch.

The exchange is also relying on an agent scheme, under which the SEC has already granted approvals, along with the awareness campaigns outside Colombo.

In his welcome address, CSE Chief Executive Officer Rajeeva Bandaranaike revealed that the CSE, together with the SEC and stockbrokers, had conducted a record number of investor education programmes this year, directly reaching 49,000 people by September. A broader branding campaign is scheduled to commence in January 2027.

He also urged the stockbroking firms to speed up client onboarding, reminding them that CDS had decentralised account opening a few years ago, enabling the brokers to onboard new clients instantly.

SEC Chairman Senior Professor D.B.P.H. Dissabandara noted that the seminars alone could reach at most 50,000 to 100,000 people and that technology, including artificial intelligence, could help carry the capital market message to the wider public.

“The campaigns can create interest; the educational programmes can develop understanding and the digital platforms can facilitate access. But it is the stockbroker who ultimately converts that initial interest into a long-term investment relationship,” Bandaranaike remarked. (NF)
