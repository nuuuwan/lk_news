Patients’ lives at risk as private hospitals face medicine shortage

Colombo, Nov. 2 (Daily Mirror) - Patients seeking treatment at private hospitals and medical centres are at risk due to a shortage of essential and life-saving medicines, Consultant Physician Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa has warned.

Dr. Sanjeewa, President of the Medical Professionals' Alliance for Medical and Civil Rights, said several medicines needed to treat patients during emergencies were currently unavailable in the private sector.

He said the shortage had affected medicines that private hospitals, nursing homes and medical centres were required to keep in stock.

The missing medicines include IV Adrenaline, Atropine, IV Piriton, IV Ondansetron, IV Maxolon, IV Phenergan and IV Hydrocortisone.

He blamed the shortage partly on suppliers and manufacturers failing to maintain regular supplies and accused the National Medicines Regulatory Authority (NMRA) of failing to properly register the suppliers needed to bring medicines into the country.

Dr. Sanjeewa said the shortage had made it difficult for private medical institutions to maintain stocks of emergency medicines.

He warned that doctors could face serious difficulties in saving patients who suddenly develop severe allergic reactions or cardiac problems if these medicines are not available.

He urged the Ministry of Health and the NMRA to take immediate action to ensure a regular supply of essential medicines to private hospitals and medical centres.
