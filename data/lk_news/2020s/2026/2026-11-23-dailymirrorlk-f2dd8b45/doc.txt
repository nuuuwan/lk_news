CA Sri Lanka brings together leading experts to analyse National Budget 2027

The Institute of Chartered Accountants of Sri Lanka (CA Sri Lanka) hosted a comprehensive post-budget seminar last week to provide members, business leaders, tax professionals and policymakers with an in-depth analysis of the key economic, fiscal and taxation proposals contained in National Budget 2027.

Held at CA Sri Lanka, the seminar attracted over 700 participants, both in person and virtually, and featured leading experts from the public and private sectors who shared insights on the Budget’s potential impact on economic growth, investment, taxation, exports, public finances, and tax administration.

Speaking at the event, Tishan Subasinghe, President of CASri Lanka, noted that Budget 2027 marks a significant phase in Sri Lanka’s economic journey as  the  country  transitionsfrom a period of stabilisation towards sustainable growth and development. He also spoke on the performance of the country’s revenue-generating agencies and highlighted the importance of maintaining fiscal discipline while strengthening foreign exchange earnings, investment and economic resilience.

Saman Srilal, Chairman of the CA Sri Lanka Faculty of Taxation, observed that the Budget places considerable emphasis on productive sectors of  the  economy  throughinvestments in export-oriented manufacturing,  tourism,agriculture, technology, digitalisation and SMEs. He welcomed several tax measures including personal income tax relief, investment incentives to encourage export-oriented projects, and initiatives aimed at strengthening tax administration and improving compliance processes.

Delivering the budget highlights, Sulaiman Nishtar, Partner and Head of Tax at EY Sri Lanka, provided a detailed overview  of  the  Budget proposals and the legislative process.

Offering an independent economic perspective, Dr. Nishan de Mel, Executive Director and Head of Research at Verité Research, discussed the broader fiscal and economic implications of the Budget.

Representing    the Government’s  policy perspective, Tanuja Perera, Tax Policy Advisor at the Ministry of Finance, provided insights into several tax proposals announced in the reforms relating to digital and cross-border taxation.

Addressing developments in tax administration, P. Nandan Kumara, Commissioner of the Inland Revenue Department, outlined ongoing efforts to modernise the tax system through   digitalisation, improved taxpayer services and greater inter-agency integration.

The panel discussion further featured contributions from Nisreen Rehmanjee, Vice President and Head of Corporate Finance, Group Tax and Social Entrepreneurship at John Keells Holdings PLC, who shared practical perspectives from the corporate sector on tax compliance, taxpayer facilitation and broadening the tax base.
