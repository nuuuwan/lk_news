ESSENTIAL ENFORCEMENT TO STOP NOISE POLLUTERS

The news that the police have been instructed to take a strict approach to stop noise pollution deserves applause.  However, the enforcement of the strict measures will require a huge concerted effort.  As someone who has lived and suffered in a neighbourhood with thrice weekly booming, all night techno, rave parties for 7 months of the year, I believe some of the following will need to happen:

Police who refuse to stop noisy parties and other noise pollution and destroy the sound equipment must be brought before the Bribery Commission.    If the police are ineffective, Special Forces,  army personnel (similar to the drug squads) must be brought in to deal  with the noise polluters.  The “party zones” which were set up in certain  areas such as Weligama must be cancelled.  They were set up as a  misguided, desperate effort to bring in any kind of tourist after Covid  with no consideration for the wellbeing of the local residents and the  knock-on ill effects to the economy.  Tuk tuk drivers who go around playing booming,  low vibe music must also be stopped.

Frustration has been rising in neighbourhoods where regular noise polluters including party organisers have deeply disturbed community harmony and wellbeing.  As soon as the noisy party places go, the area can attract significant investment in regenerative tourism projects which can bring positive economic and wellbeing benefits for the area. One further point; regenerative tourism can include fun parties and music events.  However, regenerative parties and events are run in a considerate way so they do not disturb the neighbourhood, including the wildlife.
