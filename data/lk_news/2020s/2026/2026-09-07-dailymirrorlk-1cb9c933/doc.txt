Tax charge trims Ceylon Guardian buyback as Carsons investment twins return Rs. 1bn

Ceylon Investment PLC and Ceylon Guardian Investment Trust PLC will return a combined Rs. 1.02 billion to shareholders through pro-rata share repurchases opening on 22 September, with Guardian disclosing that the size of its offer was fixed by the cash remaining after a Rs. 33.42 million tax charge, as per offer documents filed with the Colombo Stock Exchange.

Ceylon Guardian is funding the exercise from Rs. 467.46 million of cumulative undistributed dividend income and Rs. 222.80 million of operational profits, with only the second portion attracting 15 percent withholding tax.

The number of shares to be bought back, its offer document states, was “determined based on the net funds available after tax”. At the Rs. 406.43 offer price, that Rs. 33.42 million is the equivalent of roughly 82,000 shares, or about 5 percent on top of the 1,616,138 ordinary and deferred shares the company will retire.

Ceylon Investment carries no such cost. Its Rs. 365.99 million repurchase is funded wholly from dividend income already received and, because of that, its offer document says the consideration “will be exempt from income tax”, with no advance income tax deducted.

Ceylon Investment will repurchase up to 1,800,014 ordinary shares at Rs. 203.33, on the basis of one for every 54 held, while Guardian will take up to 1,510,529 ordinary shares and 105,609 unlisted deferred shares at Rs. 406.43, one for every 53 held, worth Rs. 613.92 million and Rs. 42.92 million respectively after tax. Both prices are pegged to audited net asset value as at 31 March 2026 and sit just below the 30 June NAV of Rs. 205.57 and Rs. 412.10 a share.

Both, Ceylon Gurdian and Ceylon Investment are closed-end investment vehicles within the Carson Cumberbatch group rather than operating businesses, carry no employees of their own and have their portfolios run by Guardian Fund Management Limited. Ceylon Investment, incorporated in 1919, held Rs. 20.83 billion in assets at end-June, of which Rs. 13.55 billion sat in equity-accounted strategic holdings. Guardian, formed in 1951, heads a group with Rs. 43.78 billion in assets, though about 45 percent of that is parked in cash and fixed deposits rather than equities.

Both boards note in the offer documents that their shares are “trading at a significant discount to the NAV of a Share”, and their own tables show that discount widening rather than closing. Ceylon Investment’s moved from 41 percent at end-September 2025 to 49 percent at end-June 2026, and Guardian’s from 34 percent to 44 percent over the same period. At Rs. 203.33 and Rs. 406.43, the two companies are paying 94 percent and 77 percent above their respective 30 June closing prices.

However, the offers are small against that gap, covering 1.85 percent of Ceylon Investment’s shares in issue and 1.89 percent of Guardian’s ordinary shares. Both controlling shareholders have indicated to their boards that they will accept in full — Carson Cumberbatch PLC, which holds 68.27 percent of Guardian, and Guardian itself, which holds 66.58 percent of Ceylon Investment — leaving only the 33.42 percent and 31.60 percent public floats able to free up shares for those tendering beyond their entitlement.
