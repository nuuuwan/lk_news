Bikini photoshoots in national parks spark controversy; Environment Ministry launches probe

Colombo, Nov. 12 (Daily Mirror) - Questions have been raised over alleged commercial bikini photoshoots inside Sri Lanka’s national parks, with a former wildlife official calling for an investigation while the Environment Ministry says a probe has been launched.

An accommodation provider in Thanamalwila has also attracted attention after promoting bikini and fashion photoshoots in a wilderness setting near Yala National Park through a social media post.

Several photographs featuring foreign women wearing bikinis have since been widely circulated on social media, triggering debate over the commercial use of Sri Lanka's natural environments and the activities taking place in and around protected wildlife areas.

However, no evidence has so far established a direct connection between the promotional activities of Anilka Rooms & Eco Estate and the separate allegations of unauthorised commercial photoshoots inside national parks.

Deputy Environment Minister Anton Jayakody told the Daily Mirror that he had not received any information about the alleged activities from the Yala National Park security counter.

He said there was no legal provision prohibiting visitors from entering a national park wearing bikinis or similar clothing. However, he confirmed that an investigation had been launched into the incident.

Meanwhile, former Director General of the Department of National Zoological Gardens Sharmila Rajapaksa has called for an immediate investigation into alleged commercial bikini photoshoots taking place inside Lunugamvehera, Yala and Kumana National Parks.

In a Facebook post titled "Our National Parks Are Sacred Safe Spaces for Wildlife and Visiting Families: Stop This Illegal Bikini Shoot Operation Now!", Rajapaksa questioned the activities of a private commercial outfit reportedly advertising photography packages featuring foreign models wearing revealing swimwear.

According to her allegations, the photoshoots involve models posing inside national parks, with open safari jeeps allegedly being used as part of the arrangements.

She questioned how such activities could take place without the knowledge of wildlife officials and safari trackers assigned to accompany visitors.

Rajapaksa has urged the Department of Wildlife Conservation (DWC), Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA), Tourist Police and other relevant authorities to investigate whether the operators had obtained the necessary permits and approvals.

She also questioned whether the foreign nationals involved were legally permitted to engage in commercial modelling and photography activities while visiting Sri Lanka.

According to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, filming within wildlife-protected areas requires prior approval from the Department of Wildlife Conservation. Applications must be submitted at least two weeks before the proposed filming.

The allegations have raised broader questions about the monitoring of commercial activities inside protected areas, the enforcement of permit requirements and the responsibilities of wildlife officials and safari operators.

However, the allegations concerning commercial photoshoots inside the national parks have yet to be independently verified. No official response from the DWC or SLTDA was available at the time of publication.

The promotional activities of Anilka Rooms & Eco Estate and the separate allegations involving photoshoots inside national parks remain distinct matters, with no confirmed evidence linking the two.
