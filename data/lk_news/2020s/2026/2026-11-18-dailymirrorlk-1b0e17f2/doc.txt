Kazakhstan accelerates artificial intelligence drive with third supercomputer planned

Kazakhstan is stepping up its artificial intelligence and digital transformation programme, with plans to introduce a third supercomputer before the end of 2026.

President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev outlined the country's digital development priorities in his State of the Nation Address.

He said two supercomputers had already entered service under the country's digital development programme, while a third was expected to be introduced before the end of this year.

Kazakhstan has also completed a fibre-optic communication link across the Caspian seabed and is developing new data centres to strengthen its digital infrastructure.

The President stressed that digital transformation should go beyond introducing automation into existing systems and focus on building a fully developed digital economy.

The strategy includes the commercial application of artificial intelligence, blockchain technology and asset tokenisation to create new investment opportunities and expand participation in asset ownership.

Artificial intelligence is also expected to play a greater role in public administration by reducing bureaucracy, identifying inefficient public expenditure and supporting better decision-making.

President Tokayev further highlighted the need to ensure that digital development benefits rural communities and does not widen the gap between urban and rural populations.

He said technological advancement would be a major factor in strengthening Kazakhstan's economic competitiveness.
