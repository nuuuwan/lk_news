Supreme Court stresses best interests of child in adoption cases

Colombo, Oct. 5 (Daily Mirror) - The Supreme Court has emphasized that the best interests of a child must be a paramount consideration in determining matters concerning children, as recognized under international law and Section 5(2) of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR) Act No. 56 of 2007.

The Court made this observation while permitting the withdrawal of an application challenging the refusal of an adoption order for a child who had already completed 14 years and 20 days, and directing that a fresh adoption application be considered by the District Court of Balapitiya without being bound by the previous judgments.

A three-judge Bench comprising Justices Yasantha Kodagoda, PC, Arjuna Obeyesekere and Menaka Wijesundera delivered this order in an application challenging decisions of the District Court and the Civil Appellate High Court, which had rejected the adoption application on the ground that the child had exceeded the statutory age limit.

This relates to the circumstances in which the petitioner had been caring for the child since birth after the child was placed in the petitioner’s care by the biological mother. The identity of the biological father was not known.

According to the facts placed before Court, the petitioner had continued to care for the child and protect the child’s wellbeing. However, owing to a lack of knowledge of the applicable legal procedures, an adoption application had not been filed before the child reached the age of 14.

The District Court had dismissed the application in view of Section 30 of the Adoption of Children Ordinance No. 24 of 1941, as amended, which defines a child for the purposes of the Ordinance as a person under the age of 14 years. The Civil Appellate High Court subsequently affirmed that decision.

However, Section 10 of the ICCPR Act No. 56 of 2007 defines a child as a person under the age of 18 years. Section 5(2) of the same Act provides that, in all matters concerning children, the best interests of the child shall be of paramount importance.

During the proceedings, the Supreme Court invited the Attorney General to assist Court as amicus curiae and directed that steps be taken to facilitate the expeditious issuance of a birth certificate to the child.

Following the Court’s interim orders, the relevant authorities issued a birth certificate and a National Identity Card to the child. These documents also enabled the child to register for the 2026 GCE Ordinary Level Examination.

The Supreme Court noted that these developments had taken place as a result of its previous orders.

Counsel for the petitioner informed Court that the petitioner remained interested in obtaining a formal adoption order, as the petitioner continued to care for the child and safeguard the child’s wellbeing.

It was submitted that, without a formal arrangement, the child could face destitution, particularly as the biological parents had disowned the child.

In view of the developments and observations made by the Supreme Court, counsel sought permission to withdraw the application to enable the petitioner to file a fresh adoption application before the District Court of Balapitiya.

The Supreme Court permitted the withdrawal, while expressly clarifying that it should not prevent the petitioner from filing a fresh adoption application.

The Court directed that, if a fresh application is filed, the District Court should entertain and process it in accordance with the Adoption of Children Ordinance, while treating the protection of the child’s best interests under Section 5(2) of the ICCPR Act as the governing principle in determining the matter.

It further directed that the fresh application be considered afresh in light of the developments that had taken place since the earlier proceedings. The District Court was specifically directed not to be bound by the previous judgments of the District Court or the Civil Appellate High Court.

Counsel also informed Court that the petitioner intended to submit supporting documents with the fresh application, including the Probation Report filed in the previous proceedings and an affidavit made by the biological mother dated March 25, 2024.

Counsel Manoja Gunawardana instructed by Achini Liyanadura appeared for the 2nd Petitioner-Petitioner-Petitioner.

Counsel Diana Stephnie Rodrigo instructed by Dilini Chamindika Pathiranage appeared for the 1st Petitioner-Petitioner-Respondent.
