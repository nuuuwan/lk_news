Businesses press govt. for faster action on operating constraints

Sri Lanka’s medium and large-scale businesses in the Southern province have pressed the government to address the constraints affecting their operations, taking a series of concerns and proposed solutions directly to the Finance Ministry, as the private sector seeks quicker resolution of the obstacles facing the businesses.

The representatives of several business associations affiliated with the Southern Province Chambers Forum met Finance and Planning Deputy Minister Dr. Anil Jayantha Fernando and Economic Development Deputy Minister Nishantha Jayaweera, at the Finance Ministry yesterday, following a request from the regional business grouping. During the discussions, the business representatives outlined the challenges confronting their operations and presented proposals on how they could be addressed, according to a Finance Ministry statement.

The ministry, however, did not disclose the specific constraints raised by the businesses or the measures proposed by them.

The talks nevertheless appear to have moved beyond an initial airing of grievances, with Jayaweera indicating that the government had reached policy-level agreement on addressing many of the issues brought forward by the business community. He said further discussions could be held on the timeframes required to implement the proposed solutions, signalling that the focus could now shift towards translating the policy commitments into action.

Fernando also told the meeting that various measures had already been initiated to address the concerns raised by the businesses, although the Finance Ministry statement did not provide the details on those measures or their implementation timelines.

The participation of officials from Sri Lanka Customs and the Inland Revenue Department, alongside the Finance Ministry officials, also gave the meeting a broader economic and regulatory representation.

The businesses continue to look for greater predictability and speed in the implementation of economic and regulatory reforms, with the effectiveness of policy changes increasingly dependent on how quickly they translate into improvements at operational level.

The Southern Province Chambers Forum had sought the meeting with the government and the business representatives welcomed the opportunity to engage with the two deputy ministers within a short period, following their request.
