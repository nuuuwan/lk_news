Budget 2027 fails to address people’s nutritional needs: Harsha

Colombo, Nov. 16 (Daily Mirror) - Samagi Jana Balawegaya (SJB) MP Dr. Harsha de Silva told Parliament today that Budget 2027 has failed to address pressing nutritional deficiencies in the country, pointing out that high food costs prevent Sri Lankans from consuming adequate amounts of protein.

Referring to recent remarks by Minister K. D. Lalkantha that Sri Lankans consume excessive amounts of rice, Dr. de Silva agreed with the observation, noting that citizens are unable to afford protein-rich foods.

"A person requires 60 grams of protein daily, yet the high cost of essential food items prevents people from meeting this requirement. No tax concessions have been granted for essential goods," the MP said.

Highlighting the cost barriers to protein sources, he pointed out that chicken is currently subject to an 18 percent Value Added Tax (VAT), while the import duty on corn—a primary input for poultry feed—has surged from Rs. 25 per kilogram a few years ago to Rs. 1,000 today. With Sri Lanka producing only 50 percent of its corn requirement locally, the remaining half must be imported at steep costs.

Dr. de Silva also noted that a person would need to consume around 60 sprats a day to meet their daily protein requirement, adding that roughly 3.6 million Sri Lankans currently cannot afford even one proper meal a day. "The nutritional crisis highlighted by Minister Lalkantha has been completely overlooked in this budget," he charged.

Turning to public sector remuneration, the SJB MP criticized the targeted salary hikes proposed in Budget 2027, claiming that only 16 percent of state employees stand to benefit, leaving 84 percent of the public workforce without relief.

He further faulted the government for neglecting small and medium-scale (SME) rice millers, noting that the budget lacked initiatives to introduce a dedicated rice brand to support them—similar to the ‘Shakthi’ brand launched by previous administrations.

Pointing to stalled infrastructure, Dr. de Silva noted that the budget failed to allocate funds to complete the Prabhaswara cold storage facility, despite its ceremonial opening by President Anura Kumara Dissanayake. He further revealed that he had received reports today of thieves breaking into the facility and stealing equipment.

Concluding his remarks, Dr. de Silva emphasized that Sri Lanka requires genuine economic transformation, accusing the administration of stalling the Economic Transformation Act purely out of political reluctance because it was introduced by a previous government.
