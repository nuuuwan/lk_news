Six-month trial planned for passenger drone operations

Colombo, Nov. 10 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka is set to conduct the maiden test flight of an electric, autonomous passenger drone tomorrow (11) at the Colombo Port City premises, marking an initial step towards introducing passenger drone services in the country.

The initiative follows discussions held in August between representatives of China's passenger drone manufacturer EHang and senior officials of the Ministry of Ports and Civil Aviation.

The meeting was attended by Minister Anura Karunathilaka, Deputy Minister Janitha Ruwan Kodithuwakku, Ministry Secretary W. W. S. Mangala and senior officials of the Civil Aviation Authority of Sri Lanka (CAASL).

Discussions focused on the potential use of electric Vertical Take-Off and Landing (eVTOL) aircraft, which do not require runways, for tourism, passenger transportation and emergency services.

The proposed project will require coordination among several government agencies, including the Civil Aviation Authority, Ministry of Defence, Sri Lanka Air Force, Telecommunications Regulatory Commission of Sri Lanka, Port City Commission, Sri Lanka Tourism Promotion Bureau, Board of Investment and airport authorities.

The initiative will initially operate as a trial project rather than a commercial passenger service, with testing scheduled to continue at the Port City premises for approximately six months.

An EH216 two-seater passenger drone has already been imported to Sri Lanka for the planned flight tests.

The aircraft is equipped with 16 propellers, 12 battery sets, three flight-control systems and an integrated computer management system. It can be operated remotely from a centralised control station and is designed to automatically activate safety protocols in the event of a system malfunction or loss of communication.

Authorities expect the technology to create new opportunities for aerial tourism, including transporting visitors to destinations such as Sigiriya, Pidurangala, hill-country locations and coastal islands.
