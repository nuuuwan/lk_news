Patients face days of delays as MRI services restricted at National Hospital

Colombo, Nov. 9 (Daily Mirror) - Patients admitted to the National Hospital of Sri Lanka in Colombo are being forced to wait for two to three days for MRI scans, despite the hospital having several MRI machines worth around Rs. 400 million each, a medical association has alleged.

Specialist Dr. Chamal Sanjeeva said the number of MRI scans carried out daily at certain units of the National Hospitals in Colombo and Karapitiya had been restricted to between 10 and 12, causing severe difficulties for patients.

He said patients admitted to Wards 81, 9 and 10, as well as the Neurotrauma and Neurosurgical units, were among those affected by the limited services.

According to him, patients sometimes remain in hospital wards for several days awaiting MRI scans, resulting in delays to planned surgeries.

He also questioned why expensive MRI equipment was not being used to its maximum capacity, particularly when official circulars had instructed hospitals to ensure the maximum possible utilisation of medical equipment.

Dr. Sanjeeva said four MRI machines at the Colombo National Hospital, each valued at approximately Rs. 400 million, were not being utilised effectively.

As a result, patients who cannot obtain scans within the hospital are compelled to seek private services, where an MRI scan can cost around Rs. 100,000.

He said a similar situation was reported at the National Hospital in Galle.

"A patient is admitted to a ward and stays for three days, but the surgery cannot be performed because an MRI scan is unavailable. Ultimately, an impoverished patient is compelled to pay Rs. 100,000 to get the scan done elsewhere. This is unacceptable when there is a Rs. 400-million machine available," he said.

Dr. Sanjeeva called for an immediate investigation into the matter and urged the authorities to take steps to ensure the available MRI facilities are used effectively to reduce delays and ease the burden on patients.
