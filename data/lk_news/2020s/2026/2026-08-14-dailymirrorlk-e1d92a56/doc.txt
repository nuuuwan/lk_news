President calls for stronger Co-operative network to regulate prices

Colombo, August 14 (Daily Mirror) - President Anura Kumara Dissanayake has stressed the need to strengthen Sri Lanka’s co-operative movement and state-owned retail institutions to play a more active role in the market and help regulate prices.

The President said that, given Sri Lanka’s relatively small market, prices of goods and services cannot be expected to be regulated solely through market competition.

He called for a strong price regulation mechanism, with Lanka Sathosa and the co-operative movement playing a key role.

The President made these remarks today (14) during a meeting at the Presidential Secretariat to review the progress of projects implemented under the 2026 Budget allocations of the Ministry of Trade, Commerce, Food Security and Co-operative Development and discuss the Ministry’s proposals for the 2027 Budget.

The meeting reviewed programmes carried out by 16 departments, self-financing institutions and institutions funded through the Treasury, with particular attention to food security and consumer protection.

It was noted that 45 mechanical dryers are being imported under a concessionary programme to support farmers in drying paddy and other grains. The dryers will be provided to 45 institutions selected from among 65 applicants.

Progress was also reviewed on the establishment of nine wholesale distribution centres covering all nine provinces. Construction of centres in Kurunegala and Matara has already been completed.

The President also reviewed the Rs. 1 billion programme to establish rural-level rice processing centres, as well as the construction of a potato purchasing centre in Nuwara Eliya and a grain-legume processing centre in Monaragala. He called for new programmes to support big onion and maize farmers.

Meanwhile, 34 new Production Co-operative Societies have been registered under a Department of Co-operative Development programme aimed at encouraging greater participation by young people in the co-operative sector.

The meeting also reviewed programmes of the Food Commissioner’s Department, including the establishment of new warehouse complexes and the installation of solar power systems.

Discussions focused on strengthening the Co-operative Wholesale Establishment, Lanka Sathosa and the wider co-operative movement to increase competition, improve services and provide consumers with better-quality goods.

There are approximately 2,100 co-operative outlets across the country, including Co-op Mega City and Co-op City outlets. The President stressed the need to increase their market share while addressing challenges affecting the sector.

It was also revealed that legislation is being drafted to address management deficiencies identified within the co-operative sector.
