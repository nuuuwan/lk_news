Colombo lights up in orange to stand against gender-based violence

Netherlands Ambassador to Sri Lanka Wiebe de Boer lighting up the Colombo Municipal Council building

Women In Need, the Netherlands Embassy and the Colombo Municipal Council unite to champion justice and protect survivors during the 16 Days of Activism

From left: Women In Need Executive Director Savithri Wijesekara, Women and Child Affairs Ministry Secretary Tharangani Wickremasinghe and Netherlands Ambassador to Sri Lanka Wiebe de Boer

Colombo took on a symbolic shade of orange from 25 to 27 November, as two of the city’s prominent landmarks were illuminated in support of the global campaign to end gender-based violence against women and girls.

Led by Women In Need (WIN), in collaboration with the Embassy of the Kingdom of the Netherlands and the Colombo Municipal Council (CMC), the initiative saw the Colombo Municipal Council building and the Lotus Tower (Nelum Kuluna) illuminated in orange for three consecutive evenings, from 6 p.m. to 11 p.m. The initiative marked the commencement of the 16 Days of Activism Against Gender-Based Violence 2026, an international campaign dedicated to raising awareness, strengthening collective action and addressing violence against women and girls.

Lighting the landmarks was intended to visibly express solidarity and demonstrate Colombo’s collective commitment to creating a safer, more inclusive society where women and girls can live free from violence, discrimination and fear. By bringing together public institutions, diplomatic partners, civil society organisations and the wider community, the initiative seeks to transform a symbolic gesture into a broader call for accountability, justice and meaningful action.

Netherlands Ambassador to Sri Lanka Wiebe de Boer emphasised that preventing gender-based violence requires collective responsibility.

“Preventing gender-based violence is everyone’s responsibility. The 16 Days of Activism aims to raise awareness, foster support and build networks to end violence against women and girls. Boys and men play an important role in this effort. The Embassy has partnered in a series of activities this year to work towards a violence-free society for our mothers, sisters, wives and daughters,” he said.

Colombo Mayor Vraîe Cally Balthazaar emphasised the city’s role in the campaign, highlighting local leadership in addressing gender-based violence and building safer communities.

“Colombo is a city for everyone, and ensuring the safety, dignity and rights of women and girls must remain a shared responsibility. By illuminating our landmarks in orange, we are sending a clear message that our city stands in solidarity with survivors and against all forms of gender-based violence. This initiative reflects our commitment to building a city where women and girls can live, work and move freely without fear, and where their voices are heard, respected and protected,” she said.

For Women In Need, the initiative represents an opportunity to bring greater public attention to an issue that continues to affect women and girls across all sections of society. As an organisation working to prevent violence against women and provide support to those affected, WIN emphasises the importance of creating spaces where survivors can seek assistance, access justice and rebuild their lives with dignity.

Women In Need Program and Legal Manager Mariam Mohamed and Netherlands Embassy Operations Manager Helene de Bruijn at the Lotus Tower

Women In Need Executive Director Savithri Wijesekara noted that meaningful progress requires more than awareness, calling for continued conversations, stronger institutional responses and greater support for survivors.

“Gender-based violence is not an issue that can be addressed by one organisation or institution alone. It requires a collective commitment from the Government, law enforcement, healthcare professionals, communities and individuals. Through our work at Women In Need, we see first-hand the challenges women face when seeking help and navigating systems of support and justice. We believe it is important to keep these conversations alive, challenge the silence surrounding violence and ensure that every survivor knows that support is available and that they are not alone. We are pleased to work alongside the Colombo Municipal Council and the Embassy of the Kingdom of the Netherlands in bringing this message to the city,” she said.

The campaign officially commenced on 25 November, with the illumination of the Colombo Municipal Council building taking place during a United Nations event at the CMC. The Lotus Tower was also illuminated in orange on the same evening, with the landmarks continuing to display the campaign’s signature colour until 27 November.

As part of the program, Women In Need will also convene a panel discussion on 30 November under the theme ‘From Silence to Justice: Everyone’s Role in Ending Violence Against Women and Girls’. Bringing together representatives from law enforcement, healthcare, legal advocacy and frontline survivor support, the discussion will examine the barriers women face when reporting violence, accessing essential services and pursuing justice.

The Embassy of the Kingdom of the Netherlands is also supporting the initiative, reaffirming the importance of international partnerships in advancing gender equality, human rights and the protection of women and girls. Through this collaboration, the campaign seeks to strengthen public awareness and encourage continued engagement beyond the three evenings of landmark illumination.

Ultimately, Colombo Lights Orange is intended to be more than a symbolic campaign. It is a call for individuals, institutions and communities to recognise their responsibility in addressing gender-based violence, challenge the culture of silence surrounding abuse and support survivors in accessing the protection and justice they deserve.

As Colombo’s landmarks glowed in orange, the message was clear: ending violence against women and girls requires collective action, sustained commitment and a society that refuses to remain silent.
