Audit exposes Rs. 350.7mn bill for rebranded SOE unit with limited progress

The Auditor General’s Department has exposed a staggering Rs. 350.7 million expenditure over three years on a specialised state-owned enterprise restructuring unit and foreign consultants, with severely limited tangible outcomes.

According to the National Audit Office’s summary report for the Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development for the year ending December 31, 2025, the unit failed to complete its core mandate by the specified deadline and merely underwent a name change.

The State-Owned Enterprise Restructuring Unit was initially established in December 2022 to spearhead the reform of government businesses. A Cabinet decision dated May 15, 2023, outlined an ambitious goal to establish a holding company by May 20, 2026, to manage 72 listed entities as subsidiaries for restructuring.

However, the audit reveals this holding company was never formed. Highlighting the stalled progress, only Canwill Holdings, the state-owned parent company of the unfinished Grand Hyatt Colombo property, managed to advance to the Request for Proposal stage for its divestiture.

The financial burden of this administrative bottleneck has been substantial. The audit report details that while Cabinet approval was granted in 2023 to appoint transaction advisors, a sum of Rs. 193 million was paid in 2024 to two foreign firms, utilising the Ministry of Finance’s general deposit account 18/62P. Despite this heavy expenditure on external expertise, the Auditor General explicitly states that the objectives of establishing the restructuring unit had not been achieved by the end of 2024.

Following political changes and a shift in government policy regarding the management of state assets, the new administration simply rebranded the existing entity rather than overhauling the stalled process. Through a Cabinet decision on November 4, 2025, a new unit named the State-Owned Enterprise Reform Unit was set up under the Ministry of Finance.

Between 2023 and the end of 2025, an additional Rs. 157.7 million was incurred merely to maintain the administrative unit. The audit report firmly concludes that despite the Rs. 350.7 million total financial outlay, the intended restructuring mandate remained unfulfilled, casting significant doubt on the execution and financial accountability of the divestiture programme.
