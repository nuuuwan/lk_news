Zambia’s President Hakainde Hichilema wins second term

ALJAZEERA - Zambia’s President Hakainde Hichilema ⁠has won re-election for ⁠a second five-year term, according to the electoral authorities, after a race marred by allegations of fraud.

Results released by the election commission in the early hours of Tuesday morning showed that Hichilema received roughly 61.4 percent of valid votes, compared with ⁠38 percent for his main challenger, Brian Mundubile.

“I therefore declare Hichilema Hakainde S to be president-elect of the Republic of Zambia this 18th day of August, 2026,” commission chairperson Mwangala Zaloumis said.

It was Hichilema’s seventh run for the presidency, and he was widely expected to win due to the advantages of incumbency, while it was Mundubile’s first bid to become head of state.

After his victory was announced, hundreds of Hichilema’s supporters gathered in the capital, Lusaka, wearing the red colours of his United Party for National Development (UPND), singing, dancing and setting off firecrackers in celebration.

Hichilema, 64, issued a statement after his victory, saying he was “truly humbled by the trust you have placed in our hands”. He also told those who had backed his opponents, “We hear you, and we will continue to work for you.”

There was no immediate reaction from Mundubile, the opposition leader.

Tensions had gripped the country in recent days after a relatively quiet run-up to the August 13 vote.

On the night of the election, ⁠authorities arrested 11 people, including leading opposition figures, in a raid that involved an exchange of gunfire, while Mundubile was present.

The government said the raid targeted “militia activity”, with those arrested allegedly caught in possession of military-grade weapons meant for an armed insurrection.

Mundubile’s National Reconciliation Party for Unity and Prosperity (NRPUP) rejected that account, saying the raid took place at their leader’s home and left a member of parliament wounded by gunfire.

The next day, the electoral commission suspended vote-counting for hours due to ⁠reports of violence against polling staff and the theft of ballot papers.

Mundubile said he had received information ⁠about the possible alteration of result forms from ⁠polling stations and called for an urgent independent investigation.

International observers, including the European Union, said the election was largely peaceful, but that the “broader electoral process took place in an environment that limited fundamental freedoms”.

“Late legal changes, legal uncertainty, and unequal campaign conditions distorted the playing field,” said Michael McNamara, chief of the EU observation mission.

Zambia, home to 22 million people and Africa’s second-largest copper producer, has a long record of peaceful transfers of power through the ballot box. Every change of president since the return to multiparty politics in 1991 has been achieved through elections.
