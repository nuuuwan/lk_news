SL kicks off long-awaited Rs.1.5bn interim campaign

Immediate Rs.1.5bn interim promotional drive paves way for a broader Rs.5bn global branding campaign in March 2027

Authorities revise 2026 projections upward, targeting 2.7mn tourist arrivals and US $ 4.2bn in earnings

Interim campaign will be launched systematically across 6 primary source markets with market-specific strategies

Private sector leaders urge strict accountability, monthly progress tracking and expanded joint mechanisms with international travel distributors

The Sri Lanka Tourism Promotion Bureau (SLTPB) yesterday officially launched a Rs.1.5 billion interim digital and public relations campaign, marking the country’s first major structured international promotional push in over a decade.

The interim campaign will run from August 2026 through April 2027, specifically targeting six high-yielding source markets.

The launch is designed to capitalise on the upcoming winter booking season and restore Sri Lanka’s destination visibility ahead of a broader Rs.5 billion global branding campaign scheduled to roll out in March 2027.

The authorities announced an upward revision of the country’s tourism targets for the year, largely buoyed by the anticipated boost from this campaign. While the government previously downgraded its 2026 expectations to 2.5 million arrivals and US $ 3.5 billion in earnings, amidst the Middle East crisis and aviation disruptions, the targets have now been elevated.

SLTPB Chairman Buddhika Hewawasam announced in Colombo yesterday that aided by the interim campaign and increased airline connectivity, the tourism authority now projects over 2.7 million arrivals and US $ 4.2 billion in earnings for the current year.

Hewawasam expressed confidence that the subsequent global campaign will drive the arrivals past the three million mark next year, generating over US $ 5 billion, while setting a long-term trajectory toward a US $ 10 billion revenue target by 2030. “The waiting is over. Sri Lanka’s next tourism chapter and nation branding journey are officially underway,” he said.

The interim campaign will be launched systematically across six primary source markets, with market-specific strategies. In Australia, the campaign commences this August for a six-month period, featuring digital activations across platforms such as Meta, TikTok and YouTube, supplemented by roadshows in Melbourne, Sydney, Perth and Adelaide as well as Auckland and Christchurch in New Zealand.

The United Kingdom and Germany campaigns will kick off in September for an eight-month duration, strategically timed alongside the major industry events such as the World Travel Market in London and ITB Berlin.

Furthermore, a focused winter seasonal campaign will run in Russia through March 2027.

Tourism Deputy Minister Prof. Ruwan Ranasinghe emphasised that the promotional drive aims to attract the high-spending tourists and shift the focus toward specialised market segments such as wellness, diving and golf.

He noted that the global campaign, which has been delayed for a considerable period, will run through February 2029 and will be supported by an AI-powered travel discovery platform to optimise Sri Lanka’s presence in global search engines.

“This has been a day that all of us were waiting for years,” Prof. Ranasinghe noted.

“Whenever we sit for a press conference, the question that is asked is, ‘Where is the global campaign?’ So, it is right here now and we are launching it here today.”

He underscored the importance of remaining realistic in the marketing narrative, stating that the destination must promote what is genuinely available while continuously developing new product portfolios across the uncharted regions like the Northern and Eastern provinces.

The industry stakeholders warmly welcomed the long-awaited campaign but stressed the need for strict accountability and collaborative execution.

Sri Lanka Association of Inbound Tour Operators President Nalin Jayasundera urged the authorities to establish joint promotional mechanisms with international tour operators and airlines, pointing out that stakeholder synergy is vital for achieving the ambitious five million arrival target by 2030.

“Starting with six important source markets is a strong step but I believe we should expand this to at least 10 countries without waiting for next year’s global campaign, ensuring our top 10 source markets are actively addressed,” Jayasundera said.

Meanwhile, The Hotels Association of Sri Lanka President Ashoka Hettigoda highlighted that the hotel sector has invested over 15 billion dollars over the decades and requires sustained returns through high-spending, long-staying visitors.

Hettigoda proposed implementing monthly review briefings to track the campaign’s empirical progress and ensure that every rupee spent yields the highest possible return on investment.

“Excellence is not a static award; it is a daily commitment to improve. Now that the campaign has been launched, we cannot afford complacency,” Hettigoda said.
