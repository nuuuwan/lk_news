’’Only an inhuman person could dismiss his mother’s condition as non-exceptional’’ - Defence Counsel

Colombo, Nov. 14 (Daily Mirror) - President’s Counsel Ali Sabry, appearing for Parliamentarian Namal Rajapaksa, told the Colombo High Court that while some people might be able to act like robots without considering their parents’ health, his client could not do so, particularly when his mother was facing a life-threatening medical condition with 95% blockage in her heart. He argued that such circumstances clearly constituted an exceptional situation and maintained that the Bribery Commission’s application seeking to suspend the bail order had no legal basis.

The Colombo High Court yesterday announced that it would deliver its order on November 18 on whether to issue an interim injunction suspending the bail order issued by the Colombo Magistrate’s Court releasing Parliamentarian Namal Rajapaksa, who had been remanded in connection with the Krrish transaction.

The order was announced when the revision application filed by the Commission to Investigate Allegations of Bribery or Corruption, challenging the bail order granted to Namal Rajapaksa on November 3 on the grounds that it was legally flawed, was taken up before Colombo High Court Judge Lal Bandara.

Assistant Director of Legal Affairs of the Bribery Commission, Thanuja Bandara, appearing for the Commission, submitted that the bail order issued by the Colombo Chief Magistrate was contrary to the law.

She submitted that under Section 149(1) of the Anti-Corruption Act, a suspect produced before court with a certificate issued by the Director General of the Bribery Commission must be remanded until the conclusion of the trial.

She further stated that although the law provides an exception allowing bail in exceptional circumstances, the Magistrate had erred in invoking that exception to release the suspect in this case.

The Assistant Director also informed court that following Namal Rajapaksa’s remand, two witnesses who had previously been reluctant to testify due to fear had come forward before the Bribery Commission and provided evidence.

She submitted that a witness in the case, Nimal Perera, was currently residing in Australia due to alleged threats to his life.

Arguing that there was a risk of witnesses being influenced following the suspect’s release on bail, she requested court to issue an interim injunction suspending the bail order issued by the Colombo Chief Magistrate on November 3.

However, President’s Counsel Ali Sabry, appearing for Namal Rajapaksa, strongly objected to the application.

He questioned the urgency of the Bribery Commission’s application, pointing out that his client was still in remand custody.

He further argued that the interim relief sought by the Commission was identical to the final relief sought in the revision application.

Referring to Supreme Court judgements, he submitted that a final order sought through a petition could not be granted as an interim injunction before the conclusion of the proceedings.

He maintained that the Chief Magistrate had issued the bail order within the framework of the law and questioned the legal basis for the Commission’s application, arguing that the serious heart condition of the suspect’s mother had been reasonably considered by the Magistrate as an exceptional circumstance.

President’s Counsel Ali Sabry stated that Namal Rajapaksa’s mother had 95% blockage in her heart and questioned how such circumstances could be considered ordinary.

“We did not plan these circumstances or make them up. Some people may be able to act like robots without thinking about their parents, but my client cannot do that. His mother was in a life-threatening condition. No human being could say that this was not an exceptional circumstance. Only an inhuman person could say so. There is no legal basis whatsoever for the application made by the Bribery Commission,” he submitted.

President’s Counsel Ali Sabry therefore requested court to dismiss the Bribery Commission’s application for an interim injunction.

Having considered the submissions, the Colombo High Court Judge announced that the order on whether to issue the requested interim injunction would be delivered on November 18.
