Forensic Science: The silent guardian of justice in Sri Lanka

When a serious crime occurs, the public often sees police officers, detectives, and court proceedings. However, behind every successful criminal investigation lies another group of professionals who rarely appear in the spotlight—forensic scientists. Working quietly in laboratories, they use science to uncover the truth and provide evidence that helps ensure justice is served.

Forensic science is the application of scientific knowledge to legal investigations. It involves examining physical evidence collected from crime scenes, victims, suspects, and other relevant sources. Unlike eyewitness testimony, which can sometimes be inaccurate or influenced by memory, forensic evidence is objective and based on scientific principles. It, therefore, plays a crucial role in both convicting the guilty and protecting innocent individuals from wrongful accusations.

Sri Lanka has a proud history of applying science to the administration of justice. The Government Analyst’s Department has served the nation for more than a century as the country’s principal forensic science institution. Today, its highly trained forensic scientists provide scientific services to the Sri Lanka Police, the Criminal Investigation Department (CID), the judiciary, and other law enforcement agencies. Through the use of modern technology and internationally accepted scientific methods, they assist in solving thousands of criminal and civil cases each year.

Modern forensic science covers a wide range of specialised disciplines. One of the most significant is forensic DNA analysis. DNA technology has transformed criminal investigations by enabling the identification of individuals from even tiny amounts of biological evidence, such as blood, saliva, semen, hair roots, bones, and teeth. DNA testing is widely used in homicide investigations, sexual assault cases, paternity disputes, missing person investigations, disaster victim identification, and the identification of highly decomposed or skeletal remains. Even decades after death, DNA recovered from bones or teeth can help restore a person’s identity and bring closure to grieving families. Another important field is forensic toxicology, where scientists detect poisons, drugs, alcohol, pesticides, and other toxic substances in biological samples. These examinations are essential in cases involving suspicious deaths, poisonings, road traffic accidents, and drug-related offences. Forensic scientists also examine firearms and ammunition to determine whether a particular weapon was used in a crime. Fingerprint experts compare fingerprints recovered from crime scenes with known samples to identify suspects. Specialists in questioned documents investigate forged signatures, counterfeit currency, altered documents, and fake certificates, while trace evidence experts analyse microscopic materials, such as fibres, glass fragments, paint, soil, and hair that can establish connections between suspects, victims, and crime scenes. With the rapid growth of technology, digital forensic experts now play an increasingly important role by recovering evidence from computers, mobile phones, surveillance systems, and other electronic devices.

The work of a forensic scientist extends far beyond laboratory testing. They carefully examine evidence using validated scientific methods, maintain strict quality assurance standards, prepare detailed scientific reports, and present expert testimony before courts. Their responsibility is not to support the prosecution or the defence but to present impartial scientific findings. Their ultimate duty is to assist the court in discovering the truth.

Advances in scientific technology have significantly strengthened forensic investigations in Sri Lanka. Sophisticated instruments, such as DNA genetic analyzers, real-time PCR systems, gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS), high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), comparison microscopes, and advanced digital imaging systems, enable scientists to analyse evidence with remarkable sensitivity and accuracy. These technologies allow investigators to detect evidence that may be invisible to the naked eye.

Despite these advances, forensic science in Sri Lanka continues to face several challenges. Increasing numbers of criminal investigations, emerging synthetic drugs, cybercrime, and thegrowing demand for sophisticated forensic services require continuous investment in laboratory infrastructure, modern equipment, research, and professional training. Strengthening forensic capabilities will enable scientists to meet future challenges more effectively while maintaining the highest standards of scientific excellence.

Forensic science benefits society in many ways. It helps identify unknown victims, solve crimes, establish family relationships through DNA testing, prevent wrongful convictions, and strengthen public confidence in the justice system. Every scientific report prepared by a forensic scientist represents countless hours of careful examination and objective analysis aimed at revealing the truth.

Although forensic scientists often work behind laboratory doors and remain largely unknown to the public, their contribution to society is invaluable. They combine science, integrity, and impartiality to ensure that justice is based on reliable evidence rather than speculation. As crime becomes increasingly sophisticated, the importance of forensic science will continue to grow, making forensic scientists indispensable guardians of justice in Sri Lanka.
