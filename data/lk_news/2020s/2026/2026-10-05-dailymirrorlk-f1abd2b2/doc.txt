U.S. mum on concerns over Iranian vessels stranded off Sri Lanka

Colombo, Oct. 5 (Daily Mirror) - In response to a query whether concerns had been raised regarding Iranian vessels stranded off Sri Lankan waters, the U.S. Embassy in Sri Lanka, while asserting that substance on the private diplomatic communications would not be commented on, said the United States is working with allies and partners to close off avenues for 'illicit Iranian revenue', and raise global awareness of the risks of doing business with the Iranian regime.

An Embassy spokesman said that information regarding U.S. sanctions, including designated vessels, is publicly available through the U.S. Department of the Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control.

Responding to the question on sanctions in the event of extension of any assistance, the spokesman referred to the the September 25 press release on maritime sanctions-evasion training, which stated: “The United States has previously designated entities—including brokers, fuel suppliers, and cargo certifiers—for facilitating the operations of sanctioned vessels, underscoring that providing services such as fuel, food, or provisions to a sanctioned vessel can expose companies and individuals to U.S. sanctions risk, including potential civil or criminal penalties.

Meanwhile, a Foreign Ministry spokesman said the US had issued secondary sanctions as their own common policy perspective which is applying globally and do not target only Sri Lanka or any specific country.

These secondary sanctions were announced by the US. on August 24 and it says that if any individual or entity provides services to US sanctioned vessels risks losing access to the US financial system, according to him.

He said that these kind of vessels are not only in Indian Ocean and loitering in some other oceans as well due to the current conflict situation and these secondary US sanctions are commonly apply across the world.
