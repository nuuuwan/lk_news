Sunshine September-quarter profit rises 82.4% to Rs.2.3bn

Sunshine Holdings PLC’s consolidated profit after tax rose 82.4 percent year-on-year to Rs.2.3 billion in the quarter ended September 30, 2026, as the revenue growth accelerated, although its six-month results showed continued pressure on the operating margins.

The group revenue increased 32 percent to Rs.21.6 billion in the second quarter of the financial year ending March 2027. The healthcare revenue grew 20.5 percent, while the tea export revenue rose 60.9 percent, supported by additional business from key customers and a higher contribution from the value-added products.

The stronger quarter lifted the consolidated profit after tax for the six months ended September to Rs.3.7 billion, up 44.3 percent from a year earlier. The first-half revenue increased 24.4 percent to Rs.40.2 billion. However, the profit growth outpaced the improvement in the operating earnings. The higher net finance income and a lower effective tax rate supported the six-month bottom line, while the earnings before interest and taxes rose 10.5 percent to Rs.6.1 billion.

The group’s operating profit margin narrowed to 15.1 percent, from 17 percent. Gross profit increased 11.7 percent to Rs.11.5 billion but the gross margin fell to 28.7 percent, from 31.9 percent, reflecting the regulated pharmaceutical price reductions and the impact of a weaker rupee on the healthcare operations. The net profit margin nevertheless improved to 9.2 percent, from 8 percent.

Healthcare remained the largest revenue contributor, accounting for 49 percent of the first-half turnover, followed by consumer operations at 37 percent and agribusiness at 14 percent.

The healthcare revenue increased 13.3 percent to Rs.19.6 billion over the six months, although its operating margin declined to 11.2 percent, from 13.1 percent as the pricing restrictions and higher import costs weighed on the earnings.

Within the sector, the pharmaceutical agency revenue contracted 24 percent, which the company attributed to a change in its operating model, lower shipments from certain principals and regulatory-related stock shortages.

Lina Manufacturing’s revenue rose 35.1 percent, supported by the deliveries against the government orders. The medical devices revenue increased 19.9 percent, while Healthguard Distribution grew 4.1 percent. the Healthguard Pharmacy revenue remained broadly stable.

The consumer revenue rose 53.5 percent to Rs.15 billion, with the consolidation of spice exporter JAPC accounting for a substantial part of the increase. Excluding JAPC, the sector revenue grew 13 percent.

The consumer operating margin edged up to 8 percent, from 7.8 percent. The tea export revenue increased 25.4 percent over the six months, while the branded tea and confectionery revenue grew 5.6 percent and 2.2 percent, respectively. JAPC’s spice export revenue rose 28.6 percent.

The agribusiness revenue increased 7.2 percent to Rs.5.6 billion, driven by the oil palm operations. The oil palm revenue rose 9.1 percent to Rs.5 billion on higher own-crop production, although the second-quarter growth moderated as the bought-crop availability and oil extraction rates declined.

The dairy revenue fell 2.9 percent to Rs.561.1 million as the higher selling prices only partly offset the lower milk sales volumes. The agribusiness operating margin narrowed to 47.8 percent, from 49.1 percent, reflecting the higher oil palm operating costs. During the first half, Sunshine finalised a joint venture agreement with India’s Zydus Lifesciences, to develop an oral solid dosage pharmaceutical manufacturing facility at the Horana Export Processing Zone.

After the reporting period, Sunshine Healthcare Lanka acquired a 21.92 percent stake in Digital Health, the operator of the Doc990 platform, in October. The group also increased its holding in Watawala Plantations to 77.02 percent through Sunshine Wilmar, while Watawala Plantations acquired the remaining non-controlling interest in Lonach Dairy, making it a wholly-owned subsidiary.
