Namal Rajapaksa’s Airbus scandal:  SRI LANKAN  POLITICAL SCRIPT  OF MAKING  A HERO  OUT OF A  CROOK

The government, however, has a duty to defend the integrity of the judicial process and the credibility of the CIABOC and other investigating apparatus

Namal Rajapaksa, an SLPP lawmaker and the heir apparent to the Rajapaksa throne, was remanded on Sept. 04 by the Colombo Chief Magistrate’s Court for allegedly receiving 100 million rupees as a bribe in connection with a controversial Airbus procurement deal in 2013.

The probe, launched by the Commission to Investigate Allegations of Bribery and Corruption (CIABOC), is a rare case in which the state apparatus has decisively pursued a high-profile allegation of corruption linked to the former regime heavyweights.

The case is important in many respects. First is the extent of corruption itself and its wider implications – not simply the two million US dollars in bribes allegedly received by local politicians and Sri Lankan airlines officials. Sri Lanka later paid $ 115million as a cancellation penalty for the same corrupt procurement contract, and overpaid US$ 50 million for two old aircraft procured under the same deal.

Second is the impunity with which how the old regime actors had long protected their own, behind a brief court appearance scripted for public consumption. The allegations first came to light in 2020 as part of a French, US and British investigation into alleged practices of bribery and corruption in Airbus, which later agreed to pay a $ 4billion fine in settlement.

Third is the regular Sri Lankan political script of how to make a hero —or a political victim— out of a crook. That melodrama is currently in play.

Fourth is the integrity of the ongoing investigations, which are undertaken in collaboration with the Airbus and British, French and US law enforcement authorities. However, none of that is an antidote for political slander at home. Perception matters as much as substance. Therefore, deliberate political slander and disinformation should be viewed as an attack against the integrity of the independent institutions, in this case CIABOC. Ideally, they should be dealt with under strong Libel laws similar to Singapore.

First, about the corruption itself. In 2013, Sri Lankan Airlines had reached an agreement to purchase and lease a total of 16 aircraft from France’s Airbus. The CIABOC alleged there had been an agreement between the two parties to pay a USD 1 million bribe for each aircraft. Subsequently, after the purchase of two aircraft, Airbus transferred nearly 1.45 million euros (then equivalent to USD 2 million) to an account of BIZ Solutions Inc maintained at the Standard Chartered Bank in Singapore. The investigators found the parent company of BIZ Solutions Inc was registered in Brunei, with Priyanka Niyomali Wijenayake as the sole director, the wife of former Sri Lankan Airlines Chief Executive Officer Kapila Chandrasena.

According to the submissions made before the court, 400,000 euro and AU$ 200,000 had been transferred from the BIZ Solution Inc account to a personal account of Kapila Chandrasena maintained at the Commonwealth Bank of Australia. Another 168,400 euros from the same account had been transferred to a Singaporean company named ‘SL Agro,’ which then had transferred Rs. 9 million, Rs. 7 million and Rs. 2 million on three occasions to a company called “Sasisi Media” owned by Shamendra Rajapaksa, the youngest son of former Speaker of Parliament, Chamal Rajapaksa.

Shamendra Rajapaksa, who was then a director at SriLankan Airlines, had allegedly received another direct payment of Rs. 6 million. Investigators alleged he had altogether received Rs 25 million in bribery payments.

Investigators also discovered that US$ 800,000 in two separate payments had been transferred from the BIZ Solutions account to an account maintained at OCBC Bank of Singapore for a company named Sabre Vision Holdings Ltd, owned by businessman Nimal Perera.

Investigators had visited Perera, who currently resides in Australia. Perera has refused to return to Sri Lanka, and instead has given an affidavit in which he claimed that he had personally handed over RS 100 million to Namal Rajapaksa in cash.

Airbus has cooperated with the Sri Lankan investigation, and its officials have visited Sri Lanka on three occasions, given four statements, and handed over all official documents, emails and bank account records pertaining to local transactions.

Priyanka Niyomali Wijenayake had jumped bail and is currently absconding from the court and living in Australia. Shamendra Rajapaksa is living in the US, and Nimal Perera is in Australia. The court was informed that steps had been underway to bring Wijenayake and Rajapaksa to Sri Lanka.

However, much of the weight of Namal Rajapaksa’s indictment lies in the credibility of the affidavit of Nimal Perera, who was a close Rajapaksa associate. The investigators have curiously failed to hold Nimal Perera to account for receiving $ 800,000 into a bank account owned by him.

The high-profile corruption in the Airbus deal came to light only after the European aircraft manufacturer admitted in 2020 to paying multimillion dollar bribes to politicians and airline officials, including Sri Lanka, Malaysia, Indonesia, Taiwan and Ghana to secure aircraft deals. Airbus agreed to pay $ 4 billion in fine after a three-year investigation by France, the UK and the US into Airbus’s illegal business practices from 2006 to 2016.

However, the Sri Lankan investigations during the Gotabaya Rajapaksa regime in 2020 led nowhere. Kapila Chandrasena and his wife were briefly arrested and, upon released bail, Ms. Wijenayake fled to Australia.

The Airbus deal negotiated in 2013 by the Rajapaksa administration was subsequently terminated by its successor, Yahapalanaya, in 2015. Sri Lanka paid a cancellation penalty of around $115 million to Airbus and aircraft lessors to terminate the contract. It was also alleged that Sri Lankan also overpaid by roughly US$ 50 million above the market price when it acquired two older A330S under the same contract— which is now also embroiled in the bribery payment.

The regime protection and impunity extended to wheeler-dealers and corrupt regime operators were a way of life in Sri Lankan politics; it has, for the first time, faced a decisive assault. The de facto bipartisan consensus defending regime holders, save minor political sideshows, had been dutifully followed by both the UNP and the Rajapaksa regime. While the JVP/NPP administration’s sincerity in combating corruption, or for that matter corruption under its predecessors, is yet to be determined, in this particular incident it carries no political baggage. Stakes may be different if the government chose to pursue the VFS global Visa scam.( The government, neither otherwise activist CIABOC had chosen not to, nor has the opposition SJB, true to the old bipartisan consensus.)

The opposition outcry against Namal Rajkapaksa’s arrest— unfortunately, the UNP, among others, being the loudest and most desperate — is yet another episode of the old guard defending their own crooks. The UNP, in a statement, claimed “no modern, civilised society would approve of depriving an individual of such democratic rights,” adding that “Gen Z generation viewed such action with strong disapproval.”

There is also a time-tested Sri Lankan political script that transforms a crook into a political victim and a hero. Namal Rajapaksa quotes Nelson Mandela from his remand prison. The government, however, has a duty to defend the integrity of the judicial process and the credibility of the CIABOC and other investigating apparatus. Political slander against the judicial process and CIABOC should be tried under Libel laws. The differentiation between mala fide and legitimate criticism, at times, might be hard to establish, and the onus of that should be placed on the courts themselves, rather than letting vested interests run roughshod over the independent state institutions. Those were the same sins the JVP was culpable of in the past, but that dubious practice has to be held accountable before the court if Sri Lanka is to build its institutions.
