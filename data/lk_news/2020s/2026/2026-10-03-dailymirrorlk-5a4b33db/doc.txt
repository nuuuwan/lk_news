Govt. urges public to cut fuel use, switch to EVs

The Government has urged the public to cut fuel consumption, avoid peak energy-demand periods and switch to more fuel-efficient vehicles, including electric vehicles, as Sri Lanka moves towards cleaner energy sources.

Minister of Energy Anura Karunathilaka said controlling fuel prices alone would not be enough to strengthen the country's energy security, stressing that reducing consumption was equally important.

"As a country, we must focus not only on controlling fuel prices but also on controlling fuel consumption. The public also has a major responsibility in this regard," he said.

Speaking at the commencement of construction of new petroleum infrastructure at Muthurajawela yesterday, the Minister said Sri Lanka was undergoing an energy transition in which electric vehicles and solar power would play a key role.

He urged consumers to change their energy-use patterns, avoid periods of high demand and make greater use of electricity while moving towards fuel-efficient and electric vehicles.

The Government expects to add 1,200 megawatts of solar power capacity to the national grid by 2029 as part of its renewable energy expansion programme.

The remarks came as construction began on a new Jet A-1 aviation fuel pipeline and storage complex and three new petroleum storage tanks at Muthurajawela.

The Jet A-1 project includes five storage tanks with a combined capacity of 92,000 cubic metres and a 21-kilometre underground pipeline linking the Muthurajawela complex with Bandaranaike International Airport in Katunayake.

Meanwhile, the Ceylon Petroleum Storage Terminal Limited is constructing three additional tanks at Muthurajawela, increasing the country's petroleum storage capacity by a further 40,000 cubic metres.

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, who attended the event, said the Government aimed to maintain a strong state presence in the energy market while ensuring an efficient service to the public.
