Court orders three-month suspension of Rakhitha Rajapaksa’s bank accounts, vehicles

Colombo, Nov. 5 (Daily Mirror) - Colombo High Court Judge Ruwan Dissanayake today ordered the suspension of bank accounts and three vehicles belonging to lawyer Rakhitha Rajapaksa, which had been frozen by the Commission to Investigate Allegations of Bribery or Corruption, for a period of three months.

The order was issued by the Colombo High Court after considering a request by the Bribery Commission.

Lawyer Rakhitha Rajapaksa, the suspect in the case, was also present before the court when the matter was taken up.

Making submissions on behalf of the Bribery Commission, an Assistant Director General of the Commission informed court that an investigation had been initiated into an alleged incident in which a bribe of Rs. 120 million had been obtained from the wife of detained alleged underworld drug kingpin Harak Kata, alias Nadun Chinthaka Wickramaratne, in Dubai.

The alleged bribe was reportedly obtained in exchange for ensuring his safety and facilitating his transfer to a secure prison.

She further stated that investigations had revealed information regarding bank accounts and three vehicles belonging to Rakhitha Rajapaksa, and that further inquiries had been conducted in this regard.

Based on the findings, sufficient grounds had been established to conduct an investigation against the suspect under the Proceeds of Crime Act No. 5 of 2025, she said.

Accordingly, the Bribery Commission had taken steps on October 29 to freeze the relevant bank accounts and vehicles under the provisions of the Act, she informed court.

The Assistant Director General requested the court to issue an order suspending the relevant bank accounts and vehicles for a period of three months in terms of the provisions of the Anti-Corruption Act and the Proceeds of Crime Act.

Appearing on behalf of Rakhitha Rajapaksa, Counsel Chaminda Athukorala submitted that evidence establishing that the bank accounts and vehicles constituted proceeds of crime must be presented before court before an order to retain them could be issued.

However, no such evidence had been produced before court at this stage, he argued. He also pointed out that the bank accounts in question had been opened before the alleged offence took place.

In response, the Assistant Director General of the Bribery Commission informed court that Section 75 of the Proceeds of Crime Act empowered the court to issue an order suspending such assets.

Having considered the submissions made by both parties, the Judge observed that there was no legal impediment to issuing such an order under the provisions of the Proceeds of Crime Act.

Accordingly, the Judge ordered the suspension of the relevant bank accounts and vehicles for a period of three months.

The Judge further directed that preservation orders be communicated to the banks where the relevant accounts are maintained and to the Commissioner General of Motor Traffic in respect of the vehicles and related assets.
