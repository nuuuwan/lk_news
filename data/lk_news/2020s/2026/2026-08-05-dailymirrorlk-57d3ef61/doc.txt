Greater vigilance by CB would have prevented US$2.5 million theft : Deputy Minister

Colombo, Aug. 5 (Daily Mirror) - The US$2.5 million theft could have been avoided if the Central Bank (CB) had been more vigilant, a Deputy Minister told Parliament today.

"CB could have been more vigilant when suspicions arose on the theft. The theft would have been avoided if CB was more vigilant," Deputy Minister Kaushalya Ariyaratne said.

"The theft had taken place during the transitional period before system changes were implemented after the enactment of the Public Debt Management Act. Those attached to the Public Debt Management Office were trained for 18 months. The theft had taken place during this period," she said.

She said responsibility for handling the Public Debt Management Office has now been fully transferred to the Public Debt Management Office. "These developments during the post-theft period is important," the Deputy Minister said.

While claiming that the US$2.5 million theft was a cybercrime, the Deputy Minister said such crimes have now become a global phenomenon.

Ms. Ariyaratne, who is also a member of the Committee on Public Finance (COPF), said a report by the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) revealed that the USA has lost US$16 billion as a result of cybercrimes. Australia has lost around US$2 billion.

She said the opposition's allegation that the interdiction of four middle-level officers was aimed at placing the blame on them while protecting senior officials was far from the truth.
