Bus driver, passenger face legal action for feeding wild elephant in Yala

Colombo, Nov. 19 (Daily Mirror) - Legal action is being taken against a bus driver, conductor, owner and a passenger after a wild elephant was fed from a passenger bus travelling through the Yala National Park, wildlife officials said.

The incident took place along the Buttala-Kataragama road, which passes through the highly sensitive ecological zone of the national park.

According to wildlife officials, the bus driver had stopped the vehicle, allowing a passenger to feed the wild elephant. Both the driver and the passenger had violated regulations prohibiting the feeding of wild animals inside national parks.

Park Warden, who oversees the Galge entrance to Yala Block 5 (Veheragala), and his staff have initiated legal proceedings against those involved.

The bus has been identified, and legal action is being pursued against the driver, conductor, bus owner and the passenger who fed the elephant. Court cases are expected to be filed in the coming days.

Wildlife officials warned that feeding wild elephants could make them dependent on food provided by people and encourage them to approach passing vehicles in search of food.

They said such behaviour could create dangerous situations for both elephants and motorists.

Although feeding wild animals may be seen as an act of kindness, officials stressed that it was dangerous and a punishable offence within national parks.

The public has been urged to refrain from feeding wild elephants and to follow wildlife regulations when travelling through protected areas.
