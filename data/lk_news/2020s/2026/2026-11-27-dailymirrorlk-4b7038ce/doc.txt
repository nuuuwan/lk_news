Sri Lanka CERT warns of global website security attack

Colombo, Nov. 27 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka CERT has urged organisations to strengthen website security following a global software supply chain attack that affected 178 organisations worldwide and could expose website visitors to information theft.

The attack, identified on November 16, involved malicious JavaScript being injected into legitimate website scripts, potentially allowing unauthorised browser activity and the theft of sensitive information.

Sri Lanka CERT warned organisations using Optimize Web Experimentation and other third-party JavaScript services to take immediate steps to check whether their websites and web applications had been compromised.

Organisations have been advised to identify websites using externally hosted JavaScript services and contact Optimize to verify whether their accounts or projects were affected.

Sri Lanka CERT also recommended inspecting JavaScript files, website configurations and historical changes for unauthorised modifications, while reviewing security logs for suspicious network connections and unusual browser activity.

Websites handling online payments, financial transactions and user login information have been urged to pay particular attention to possible exposure.

Organisations have also been advised to strengthen website security by implementing restrictive Content Security Policy (CSP) controls, regularly monitoring third-party scripts and investigating suspicious login activity.

Where exposure is confirmed, affected organisations should reset credentials, invalidate compromised sessions and preserve security logs and suspicious files for investigations.

Sri Lanka CERT urged organisations to report suspected security incidents immediately and remain vigilant about third-party services connected to their digital platforms.
