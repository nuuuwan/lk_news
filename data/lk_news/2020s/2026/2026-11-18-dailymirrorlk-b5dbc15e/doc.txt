Tax credit lifts DFCC’s third-quarter shareholder profit by 23%

DFCC Bank PLC’s third-quarter profit attributable to shareholders rose 23 percent to Rs. 3.38 billion from a year earlier, as an income tax credit offset a drop in pre-tax earnings, its unaudited interim financial statements showed.

Group profit before income tax fell 23 percent to Rs. 3.33 billion in the three months ended September 30, 2026. The group booked a Rs. 98.7 million income tax credit, against a Rs. 1.52 billion charge a year earlier. Group profit after tax rose 23 percent to Rs. 3.43 billion, while earnings per share increased to Rs. 7.55 from Rs. 6.28.

Net interest income rose 11 percent to Rs. 8.66 billion and net fee and commission income climbed 14 percent to Rs. 2.32 billion. The group recorded a trading loss of Rs. 591 million, against a gain of Rs. 1.17 billion a year earlier, pulling total operating income down 8 percent to Rs. 10.72 billion.

Impairment charges fell 80 percent to Rs. 177 million during the quarter, cushioning the decline in operating income. Operating expenses rose 18 percent to Rs. 6.02 billion, led by a 23 percent increase in personnel costs. Operating profit before taxes on financial services fell 19 percent to Rs. 4.52 billion.

DFCC completed its acquisition of Standard Chartered’s Sri Lankan wealth and retail banking business on August 1 for Rs. 4.42 billion in cash, inclusive of taxes. The transaction brought about 50,000 customer accounts and 260 employees into DFCC, Chief Executive Thimal Perera said. The bank cited staff joining through the acquisition and higher card-related costs among the factors behind its increase in expenses over the nine months.

For the nine months through September, group profit attributable to shareholders fell 13 percent to Rs. 7.43 billion and earnings per share declined to Rs. 16.60 from Rs. 19.51. Group profit before income tax fell 27 percent to Rs. 9.13 billion, while profit after tax from continuing operations fell 11 percent to Rs. 7.57 billion.

At bank level, loans grew 18 percent from end-December to Rs. 610 billion and customer deposits rose 23 percent to Rs. 694 billion. The net Stage 3 impaired loan ratio eased to 3.44 percent from 4.55 percent at year-end. The group’s total capital adequacy ratio fell to 14.11 percent from 15.99 percent, above the regulatory minimum of 12.5 percent.
