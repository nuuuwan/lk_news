Short-term loans take bigger share as private credit hits Rs.2.1tn

Private-sector credit rose to Rs.2.1 trillion in the first nine months of 2026, as short-term loans took a larger share, prompting the lawmakers to ask whether enough lending was going towards new investment and expanding production capacity.

The credit extended during the period increased from Rs.1.9 trillion a year earlier, with the licensed commercial banks accounting for about 74 percent of the total, the Central Bank officials told Parliament’s Committee on Public Finance.

The share of short-term loans climbed to 47 percent, from 42 percent, while that of long-term loans fell to 29 percent, from 35 percent.  The committee, chaired by MP Harsha de Silva, sought the details on growth in mortgage and vehicle loans and discussed whether lending was adequately supporting capital formation.

That discussion fed into a wider review of Sri Lanka’s long-term growth prospects.

The briefing, attended by Central Bank Governor Dr. Nandalal Weerasinghe, covered trade, labour and land reforms alongside monetary policy.

The participants also identified skills development, higher productivity and foreign direct investment as priorities for sustaining growth.

On monetary policy, the Central Bank officials said the headline inflation target, measured by the Colombo Consumer Price Index, would remain at 5 percent over the next three years. They said seven special reports had been submitted to Parliament on occasions when inflation stayed below the 3 percent lower bound of the target range.

The Central Bank Act requires the bank to report to Parliament when it misses its inflation target for two consecutive quarters.

The briefing also covered the health of bank balance sheets. The officials said the banking sector’s Stage 3 nonperforming loan ratio had fallen to 8.8 percent, from 9.7 percent, while capital and liquidity remained above the regulatory requirements.

On NDB Bank, the officials said the forensic audit and a report by the Sri Lanka Accounting and Auditing Standards Monitoring Board had been referred to the Attorney General on October 15.

The Central Bank also imposed restrictions on dividend distributions, branch expansion and directors’ remuneration at the bank.

The committee reviewed the illegal deposit-taking and financial scams that use AI-generated images and voices of public figures, including the Governor and Prime Minister. The Central Bank said it was coordinating with the telecommunications regulator, Sri Lanka CERT, police and Meta to counter such scams.

The lawmakers also discussed the regulation of virtual assets and cryptocurrencies, supervision of banking activities in Colombo Port City and the proposed consolidation of state-owned banks.
