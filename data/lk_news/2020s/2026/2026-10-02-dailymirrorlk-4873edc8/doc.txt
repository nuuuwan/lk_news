CPC still incurs losses on diesel, petrol despite price increase and subsidy

Colombo, Oct. 2 (Daliy Mirror) - The Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC) continues to incur a loss of Rs. 66 per litre on diesel and Rs. 52 per litre on petrol despite the recent fuel price increase and government subsidy, CPC Chairman D.J. Rajakaruna said yesterday.

Speaking to the Media, Rajakaruna said the global market price of a litre of diesel was equivalent to Rs. 528, while it is sold in Sri Lanka at Rs. 392, resulting in a Rs. 66 loss per litre even after a government subsidy of Rs. 70.

He said a litre of petrol costs Rs. 446 at global market prices but is sold locally at Rs. 414, resulting in a loss of Rs. 52 per litre.

“Super Diesel costs Rs. 581 per litre, but it is sold at Rs. 528, resulting in a loss of Rs. 53. This is a global issue and is beyond our control,” he said.

Rajakaruna urged the public to limit fuel consumption as much as possible, stressing that the country must face the situation collectively.

He said the monthly fuel import bill, which had remained below USD 200 million, increased to as much as USD 520 million in May due to the surge in global fuel prices.

However, Rajakaruna said the CPC was capable of managing the current losses as it generates profits from other products and operations, including jet fuel, bunkering and the refinery.
