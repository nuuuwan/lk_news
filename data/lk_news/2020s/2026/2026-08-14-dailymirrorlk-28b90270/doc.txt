25,000 patients await surgery as heart list extends to 2029

Colombo, August 14 (Daily Mirror) - More than 25,000 patients across Sri Lanka are currently awaiting heart surgeries, major surgical procedures and essential medical tests, with the waiting list for some heart operations reportedly extending as far as 2029, raising fresh questions over the Government’s response to the country’s growing healthcare backlog.

Doctors' Trade Union Alliance for Medical and Civil Rights President Specialist, Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa said it was regrettable that despite the Government announcing that funds from the President’s Fund would be used to reduce waiting lists, the programme had not yet been actively implemented.

He said the situation was particularly concerning for poor and needy patients who could not afford to seek treatment at private hospitals while remaining on lengthy waiting lists at government hospitals.

Dr. Sanjeewa proposed increasing allocations from the President’s Fund to enable patients awaiting surgery in government hospitals to obtain treatment at private hospitals at prescribed rates.

He said another option would be to increase the number of surgeries carried out daily at government hospitals by providing doctors, nurses and other healthcare workers with the prescribed payments for additional services.

According to him, such measures could help reduce the immediate backlog while patients continue to face long delays for potentially critical procedures.

However, Dr. Sanjeewa highlighted that short-term measures alone would not resolve the problem, calling for a broader overhaul of the country’s healthcare capacity.

He said the Government should increase the number of doctors trained in specialised fields, expand hospital facilities and improve the availability of surgical and diagnostic equipment to prevent waiting lists from continuing to grow.

The reported extension of the heart surgery waiting list to nearly 2029 highlights the scale of the challenge facing Sri Lanka’s public healthcare system, with patients potentially having to wait years for procedures that may be medically urgent.

The situation also raises concerns over whether existing Government commitments and funding mechanisms are being translated into timely treatment for patients who depend on the State health system.
