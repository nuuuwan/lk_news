BASL president addresses high court of Bhutan during historic visit

Colombo, Aug. 19 (Daily Mirror) - Bar Association of Sri Lanka (BASL) President Rajeev Amarasuriya addressed the High Court of Bhutan during the first-ever visit by a BASL delegation to the Kingdom of Bhutan, as the two countries’ legal professions sought to strengthen cooperation and exchange experiences in the administration of justice.

Amarasuriya addressed the Superior Bench of the High Court, comprising eight Justices and presided over by Chief Justice Justice Duba Drukpa.

Welcoming the BASL delegation, the Chief Justice said the Court was honoured by their presence and described the visit as an opportunity to strengthen ties between the legal communities of Bhutan and Sri Lanka.

He stressed that the Bench and the Bar are two branches of the same profession, united by the common pursuit of justice, and emphasised the importance of mutual respect, professional integrity and a shared commitment to the Rule of Law.

Addressing the Court, Amarasuriya expressed appreciation for the warmth and hospitality extended to the BASL delegation by Bhutan’s judiciary and legal profession.

He said the visit marked an important new chapter in relations between the two legal professions, noting that while Bhutan and Sri Lanka had followed different historical and constitutional paths, they remained connected as South Asian nations, members of the SAARC community and countries of the Global South with a shared spiritual and cultural heritage.

Referring to Bhutan’s constitutional development and its philosophy of Gross National Happiness, Amarasuriya said the country had demonstrated that development must ultimately focus on people.

He said the same principle should guide the administration of justice, noting that courts and lawyers have a broader responsibility to protect human dignity, resolve disputes peacefully, restrain arbitrary exercises of power and safeguard rights.

On the relationship between the Bench and the Bar, the BASL President said judges and lawyers, despite having different functions, are partners in the common endeavour of administering justice.

He emphasised that judicial independence and the independence of the legal profession are not privileges belonging to judges or lawyers, but safeguards that ultimately belong to the people.

Amarasuriya identified legal education, continuing professional development, opportunities for young lawyers, constitutional and public law, technology, court administration and institutional exchanges as areas where the two countries could cooperate and learn from each other.

He said that although the Himalayas and the Indian Ocean geographically separate Bhutan and Sri Lanka, the values that sustain justice recognise no such boundaries.

The engagement at the High Court followed the first bilateral meeting between the BASL and the Bar Council of Bhutan, where the two institutions affirmed their commitment to strengthening cooperation between their respective legal professions.

The commitment was reflected in a Joint Bar Statement focusing on justice, the Rule of Law, professional integrity, the administration of justice, and the independence of the judiciary and legal profession.

During the visit, the BASL delegation also met Chief Justice of the Supreme Court of Bhutan Lyonpo Norbu Tshering, Attorney General Sonam Tashi and other dignitaries.

The visit marks a significant step in strengthening professional and institutional ties between the legal communities of Sri Lanka and Bhutan.
