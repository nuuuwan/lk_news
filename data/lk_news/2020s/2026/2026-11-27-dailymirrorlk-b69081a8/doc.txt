Everyone wants ‘my share’ despite pay hike, says Labour Minister

Says sections of society demand their share regardless of what govt. delivers

Ties govt.’s success to revenue collection, credits closed doors to corruption

A three-stage public service pay hike has done little to quell demands from sections of society who want ‘my share’ regardless of what the government delivers, Labour Minister and Deputy Minister of Finance and Planning Dr. Anil Jayantha Fernando said on Wednesday.

Speaking as chief guest at InsureChamp 2026, the Insurance Regulatory Commission of Sri Lanka’s (IRCSL) inter-university insurance quiz competition, Dr. Fernando pointed to a tendency within society to put individual claims first. “There is a little tendency within society that no matter what the government does, ‘we need my share’. Everyone claims this,” he remarked.

“Let’s look at what we have done over the last two years. We have stabilised, and we have offered a huge public service salary increase in three stages, with the last increase to be given on January 1, 2027. But still, people are not happy,” he added.

His remarks came as trade unions representing engineers, university non-academic employees, teachers, principals and pre-school teachers intensified protests against Budget 2027. The Association of Public Service Engineers staged a token strike on November 13 before moving to island-wide action. Twenty trade unions representing executive service categories also announced strike action, while teacher and principal unions called a protest over the non-allocation of a proposed Rs.25,000 interim allowance.

Although he did not name any union, Dr. Fernando took aim at professionals who rank their own contribution above everyone else’s. “It could be a doctor, an engineer or an accountant. Yes, everyone’s contribution to society is very, very important, but you can’t say that ‘my contribution is the biggest one’. Everyone’s contribution is critical,” he insisted.

He urged the public to rally behind a common programme, warning that a fixation on individual benefits would only end in debate. “If everyone tries to be selfish and maximise his or her own benefits, what would happen? As a society, you can’t move forward,” he cautioned.

However, Dr. Fernando linked the state’s ability to deliver directly to its tax take, arguing that a government stands or falls on its ability to collect the revenue it has planned for. With few natural resources to fall back on, the government relies mainly on tax income to fund roads, healthcare, education and law and order, he explained.

He contrasted this with past experience, when governments collected too little and wasted much of what they did collect, opening the door to corruption. “We have closed all doors for corruption from the political sphere. That is the reason why we have been able to collect the revenue,” he asserted.

That revenue, he noted, helped the government cushion two external shocks, namely natural disasters and the fallout from the Middle East war, through welfare benefits and concessions. Dr. Fernando, however, conceded that the state had absorbed the impact only “to some extent”. He warned that no one knows how long the geopolitical tensions will last or how intense they will become, a risk he described as unique to a Sri Lanka that is recovering from collapse and looking for “the big jump”.

Meanwhile, turning to insurance, Dr. Fernando described the industry at its core as a means of sharing risk, where everyone contributes but only those who suffer losses are indemnified. He observed that the profit motive could sometimes override those fundamentals. “It doesn’t mean that profit is something bad. Profit is important, but it should be tied and connected to your value addition. Otherwise, there is no end to profit maximisation,” he stressed.

He then put the question to the university students in the audience, asking whether there was any limit to maximisation. “No, there is no limit. It never ends,” he answered, calling for the concept to be redefined through public discussion, particularly within the insurance industry, so that social security is ensured for everyone.
