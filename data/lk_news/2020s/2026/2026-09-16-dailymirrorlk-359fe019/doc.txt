Why is system change  so tricky and elusive?

Corruption in the public sector is coupled with the lethargy from  top to bottom of the rank and file

As if justifying the delay in the implementation of government’s promises, Deputy Minister of Transport and Highways Dr. Prasanna Gunasena has stated that although his party, the National People’s Power (NPP) has gained power of government, it has not gained the state power in its entirety.

He apparently means that state machinery, mainly the officialdom,  is not under the full control of the party and hence, things are not moving at the pace the party and the government desire.

This is the standpoint of the NPP leaders since they took the reins two years ago. We remember that two ministers of the NPP government in December, 2024  expressed their frustration over the lack of cooperation on the part of a section of the officialdom in their efforts to make changes in the existing system.

Agriculture Minister K.D. Lalkantha had accused bureaucrats for obstructing the Government from taking the country forward. A controversial figure, Lal Kantha warned: “The next tsunami will happen against the corrupt bureaucracy of this country.” Minister of Industries Sunil Handunnetti also during an event said that the “state officials, who have got used to work according to the corrupt policies and practices of former governments,  are not prepared yet to get rid of those old practices.”

During a discussion with the heads of the Department of Immigration and Emigration, Sri Lanka Customs, and Airport and Aviation Services, President Anura Kumara Dissanayake also highlighted early last year the need to eliminate negative perceptions surrounding Sri Lanka Customs and underscored the urgency of implementing strict disciplinary and legal action to restore public confidence.

Does Deputy Minister Gunasena’s statement indicate that the government has failed to  tame the black sheep withing the state machinery even after two years? Not totally, as we see some progress in some areas such as transport, customs and inland revenue.  DR. Gunasena further says that had the Janatha Vimukthi Peramuna captured power through any of its two insurrections in 1971 and 1988, the state machinery would have come under the total control of the party.

Gunasena cannot lay claim to this statement either. Nandana Gunathilaka, a former JVP stalwart who later turned to be its strong advesary, said in  reply to the laments by Lalkantha and Handunnetti that if the JVP’s assumption of power had been a revolutionary capture of power, it would have brought the state machinery under total control of the party.

“Following a non-revolutionary capture of power nothing more could be done, except for working within the existing system. Yet, revolutionary changes have to be brought in, in order to keep the promises given to the people, ” Gunathilake observed.

A democratic transfer of power through elections means that the state machinery which is largely corrupt and lethargic would be intact and unchanged, even after the election. Former President Maithripala Sirisena, addressing the 45th National Conference of the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Sri Lanka (CA Sri Lanka) in October 2024,  vividly explained how corruption is rampant within the state machinery,

He said that Ministers cannot steal without the knowledge of their secretaries and chief accountants. He also noted that after witnessing the minister’s behaviour, these high-level officials often begin to follow the minister’s example, a corrupt practice that eventually trickles down to lower-level employees within the ministry.

Corruption in the public sector is coupled with the lethargy from  top to bottom of the rank and file. Dr. Sarath Amunugama, when he was serving as  Special Assignment Minister,  had stated that a large majority of public servants do not work at least four hours of their eight-hour duty. Such a system would not make a positive change overnight as a powerful section of the society is making use of this corrupt and lethargic system for their financial gains. The irony is that every citizen in the country prefers his/her children to benefit from  system change, but does not prefer the change to approach themselves.

A system change is a gigantic task in view of such attitudes.
