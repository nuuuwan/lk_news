Professionals seek fairer tax policy ahead of Budget

Colombo, Nov. 9 (Daily Mirror) - Professionals, including doctors and salaried employees, have called on the Government to introduce a fairer tax structure, arguing that the existing system places an excessive burden on people earning a monthly salary.

GMOA Media Spokesman Dr. Chamil Wijesinghe said revisions made to the tax policy last year were insufficient and that further measures were necessary to address the concerns of professionals.

Welcoming President Anura Kumara Dissanayake's decision to disclose certain Budget proposals ahead of the presentation, he said the proposed tax revisions were a positive development.

He said both the President and the National People's Power (NPP) had acknowledged the need to revise the existing tax policy.

One of the main concerns was the difference in the way salaried employees and business owners are taxed.

While professionals and employees in the public, semi-government and private sectors are taxed on their gross monthly income, business owners are taxed on their profits, he pointed out.

"This is highly unfair. Imagine what would happen to a business if it were taxed on its total revenue instead of its profits. That is effectively what is happening to professionals," Dr. Wijesinghe said.

He said that professionals were not seeking exemption from taxation but wanted a reasonable and affordable tax structure.

He said professional groups had proposed introducing specific tax relief measures for professionals and other salaried employees, citing the Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE) tax system introduced in 2018 as an example of a previous approach to taxing employees.

Dr. Wijesinghe said a series of analytical proposals had been prepared following an in-depth study to reduce the tax burden on professionals while maintaining the existing number of tax files and strengthening the tax net.

The proposals were developed with the involvement of groups representing professionals, including the United Professionals' Alliance and the Government Medical Officers' Association.

He said a request had also been submitted to the Presidential Secretariat seeking discussions with the Government on the proposed changes.

However, he expressed disappointment over the absence of any meaningful engagement with the authorities.

Dr. Wijesinghe said the professional groups hoped the forthcoming Budget would introduce meaningful relief for salaried employees and address inequalities in the existing tax system.
