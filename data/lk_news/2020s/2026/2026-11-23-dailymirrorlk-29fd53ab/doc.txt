U.S. scrutiny of Sri Lanka likely if GSP renewal is taken up – Blake

Colombo, Nov. 23 (Daily Mirror) - Former U.S. Ambassador to Sri Lanka Robert O. Blake Jr. said that Sri Lanka will be under renewed scrutiny by the Democrats -controlled U.S. Congress if it decides to take up GSP renewal, as many Democrats would be likely to push stronger worker rights provisions and potentially add eligibility criteria covering issues like environmental issues and human rights. However, he said it would be lower down the priority list, in his opinion.

Also, he proposed the appointment of a new ambassador toe the U.S. as soon as possible since engagement is needed with new members of the Congress who know nothing about Sri Lanka.

Delivering remarks online during a webinar organized by Pathfinder Foundation on the recently conducted U.S. midterm elections titled ‘America Votes, South Asia Adjusts’, the former ambassador said he visited Sri Lanka last month as part of an Asia Foundation board delegation and met Prime Minister Harini Amarasuriya , Foreign Minister Vijitha Herath and a wide range of other people.

He said that some Sri Lankan friends asked him about prospects for the next Congress to renew GSP, which has lapsed since 2020.

“As many of your listeners may know, GSP provides duty-free treatment for thousands of products imported from designated developing countries, including Sri Lanka. And the answer to the question is, it's not clear how much of a priority this will really be in the next Congress.

My guess is it'll be lower down the list of priorities. One argument in favour is it would help lower costs for consumers by bringing in cheaper imports and therefore it would help the affordability agenda of the Democrats. However, if the Democrats do decide to take up GSP renewal, many Democrats would be likely to push stronger worker rights provisions and potentially add eligibility criteria covering issues like environmental issues and human rights.

The latter in particular could bring renewed scrutiny on Sri Lanka,” he said.

He said as Sri Lanka now prepares for the new Congress, he would recommend several steps to improve prospects for garnering support from the new Congress.

First, it's really important for the government to appoint a new ambassador. I think Mahinda Samarasinghe did a great job representing his country and was very active both in the executive branch and on the Hill. And so we'll need somebody who is prepared to act with similar vigour to represent Sri Lanka's interests both on the Hill and in the executive branch. And of course, it'll be very, very important to engage a lot of these new members, many of whom know absolutely nothing about Sri Lanka.

Second, while President Dissanayake has made important progress on addressing corruption, I think the new Democratic Congress is likely to scrutinise the human rights records of trading partners much more closely than the last Congress did. And so I think there are steps that Sri Lanka could take to sort of improve its standing and improve its prospects for eligibility for GSP. And these are steps that I think are politically feasible, given the strong support that President Dissanayake continues to enjoy,” he said.

He said that the number one on the list should be the repeal of the Prevention of Terrorism Act.

“We've been talking about this for 20 years since I was an ambassador there. And it's long outlived its usefulness. It's a real thorn in the side of government right now. And I will add that it would improve chances for renewal of the EU's GSP Plus programme. So I know various efforts are underway now to craft a new version of that.

And I hope that that will help to reduce a lot of the extraordinary powers that those security services now enjoy. I do think it will be important to take some steps on reconciliation. And so that would include possibly things like demilitarising the North and East, returning some of the seized lands, and then work towards devolution of power.

Again, these are all longstanding issues that have been promised and shouldn't be that difficult to do. And again, President Dissanayake has quite important support still in the parliament. Yeah, let me stop right there,” he said.
