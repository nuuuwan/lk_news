Meatless Monday brings plant-based cuisine into focus this November

‘A Taste of Plant-Based Cuisine’, an evening of food and conversation was held at Belle Colombo on 2 recently.

Organised by Meatless Monday Sri Lanka (MMSL), an initiative of SLYCAN Trust, in collaboration with EthicalX: Climate & Innovation Hub, the event brought together media representatives, content creators, communicators, entrepreneurs and other invited guests to experience a specially curated plant-based menu.

The gathering also provided a platform to showcase local ingredients and businesses contributing to more sustainable food choices.

The event was part of MMSL’s annual activities marking World Vegan Day on November 1. For more than a decade, MMSL has promoted awareness of plant-based food and its connection with climate change, nutrition and sustainable food systems through culinary experiences, partnerships with restaurants and community engagement.

This November, MMSL is continuing these efforts through a series of activities in Colombo and Galle, including dining experiences, public engagement and the documentation of local culinary traditions, ingredients and plant-based food offerings. These activities aim to celebrate Sri Lanka’s diverse food heritage while encouraging greater visibility of restaurants, chefs and entrepreneurs offering plant-based choices.

The evening featured a fully plant-based menu developed by Belle Colombo’s culinary team, led by Executive Head Chef and Co-owner Wishmalak Perera.

The specially curated menu was presented across six food stations, drawing inspiration from the destinations explored through MMSL’s Plant-Based Gastronomy Tours over the years. Bringing together flavours, local ingredients and culinary traditions from Jaffna, the hill country, the southern coast and other parts of Sri Lanka, the menu highlighted the diversity of the country’s plant-based cuisine, alongside creative interpretations of familiar dishes.

The presentation demonstrated how traditional ingredients and regional food knowledge can be brought into contemporary dining experiences.

“Sri Lanka has a rich variety of plant-based foods, many of which are already part of our everyday diets. Through our activities this November, we hope to bring greater attention to these foods, the people and businesses behind them, and the role of food choices in addressing climate change and building more sustainable food systems,” said Anura Sathurusinghe, Chief Operating Officer of SLYCAN Trust.

An important element of the evening was the inclusion of ingredients and products sourced from entrepreneurs associated with EthicalX.

Following the opening event in Colombo, MMSL will take its November activities to the southern coast with Taste of South, a public dining experience scheduled for tomorrow (29).

The event will bring together three restaurants in Hiriketiya, Unawatuna and Galle Fort, giving selected participants an opportunity to explore the plant-based options on their menus.
