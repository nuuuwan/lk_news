After midterm rout, Vance tries to build his own 2028 brand

Reuters - Trump has called Americans’ affordability concerns a Democratic “hoax.” He ​launched a war against Iran following sustained lobbying from Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Trump went on to endorse Netanyahu in the Israeli election.

The post for the first time put Vance openly on the side of ending the war.

Three people familiar with the White House and Vance's communications told Reuters that the vice president was not breaking with Trump and said White House chief of staff Susie Wiles knew in advance about Vance's post.

On Friday, Trump reshuffled senior White House leadership, replacing ​Wiles with Stephen Miller in a move that suggests the White House plans to double down on some of Trump’s more hardline policies.

Trump has acknowledged the Iran war played a role in the Republican rout because of the ​resulting high gas prices but has continued to defend it. "I would have done nothing different," he told reporters on Wednesday.

Reuters could not determine whether Trump was aware of the post in advance.

Donald Trump Jr., the president's eldest son, endorsed Vance's remarks ‌on social media, ⁠along with some conservative allies.

The dialogue further elevated Vance as a potential post-Trump conservative standard-bearer, said Michael Ricci, a Republican strategist and former adviser to House Speakers John Boehner and Paul Ryan.

He said Vance’s message forced conservatives to debate the merits of the war, and more broadly, what core tenets Republican presidential candidates should embrace in 2028.

"It’s simplistic to say this about him looking for ways to differentiate himself from Trump," Ricci said. "This is about him using his megaphone of the vice presidency to set the terms of debate."

Representatives for Vance declined to comment. White House representatives referred Reuters to Trump's comments on Wednesday, when he told reporters he understood ​Vance's perspective on the election results.

Democrats are poised to ​tie Vance – or any Republican nominee – to Trump ⁠in 2028, especially if Trump plays a large role in vetting conservative hopefuls.

"There’s a real challenge of how you position yourself to be viable in a general election while winning a primary that still requires you to bow down and kiss the ring,” said Igor Volsky, a Democratic strategist.

Vance’s brand did not emerge from the midterms unscathed. ​Republicans lost in states where he campaigned. Some of the strategies he endorsed – including an ad by Ohio gubernatorial candidate Vivek Ramaswamy that cast doubt on the ​sexual abuse suffered by the ⁠victorious Democratic candidate, Amy Acton – may have swung races away from Republicans.

Other possible 2028 presidential contenders have offered their diagnoses of the midterm defeat. US Senator Ted Cruz of Texas said Republicans hadn't done enough on affordability. Florida Governor Ron DeSantis said losing candidates had focused too heavily on transgender issues and neglected voter concerns about data centers and artificial intelligence. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has stayed silent.

Vance's effort to shape the debate was visible during the closing days of ⁠the campaign.

In stump ​speeches, he told voters to support Republicans because they would investigate fraud in federal health care and antipoverty programs, tying that to the ​White House's immigration agenda and cost of living. He almost entirely ignored the Iran war and gas prices, and seldom mentioned Trump by name.

“I want my kids to raise a family in a country that is vibrant and thriving and free and prosperous. I want the ​simple things,” Vance told one of his final rallies in North Carolina. “And because I want that for me, I want it for you too.”
