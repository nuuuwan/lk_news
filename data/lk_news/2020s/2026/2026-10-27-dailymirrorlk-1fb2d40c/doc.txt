Fitch assigns Asia Asset Finance’s proposed subordinated debt ‘A-(EXP)(lka)’

Fitch Ratings has assigned Asia Asset Finance PLC’s (AAF, A+(lka)/Stable) proposed Sri Lankan rupee-denominated subordinated listed debentures of up to Rs.3 billion an expected National Long-Term Rating of ‘A-(EXP)(lka)’.

The proposed debentures are likely to have a five-year maturity and will be listed on the Colombo Stock Exchange.

The company plans to use the proceeds to strengthen its Tier 2 capital base and support loan-book expansion.

The final rating is subject to the receipt of final documentation conforming to the information already received.

The proposed debentures are rated two notches below AAF’s National Long-Term Rating. Fitch applied the Bank Rating Criteria in rating this instrument, as it believes the prudential capital framework for Sri Lankan finance companies is closer to that for banks.

The expected rating reflects Fitch’s baseline notching for loss severity for this debt class and expectation of poor recoveries in the event of default. There is no additional notching for non-performance risk, as the proposed debentures do not contain going-concern loss-absorption features.

AAF’s National Long-Term Rating reflects Fitch’s expectation that its majority shareholder, India-based Muthoot Finance Ltd (MFL, BB+/Stable), would provide extraordinary support to AAF if needed. This view is based on MFL’s 78.4 percent shareholding, record of ordinary equity support and the strategic and operational alignment between the parent and subsidiary in their core product - gold-backed loans.

Even so, Fitch assesses AAF’s strategic importance to MFL as limited, given the subsidiary’s small size, modest role and contribution, limited brand sharing and operations in a different jurisdiction.

AAF’s standalone credit profile does not drive the rating but Fitch assesses it as significantly weaker than its support-driven rating.
