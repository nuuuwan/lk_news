Households left with just Rs.4,000 a month after spending, down from Rs.13,000 in 2019

Average household’s monthly margin after spending narrows to Rs.4,075, from Rs.13,284

Median spending overtakes median income by Rs.18,175, reversing a 2019 margin of Rs.5,789

Estate households spend Rs.16,133 a month, more than they earn

The average Sri Lankan household is left with only about Rs.4,000 a month, after meeting its expenses, down from more than Rs.13,000 in 2019, as spending has outpaced incomes, the first official household income and expenditure survey since the economic crisis shows.

The preliminary data from the Department of Census and Statistics (DCS) put the average monthly household income at Rs.120,266 in the first quarter of 2025, against the average monthly expenditure of Rs.116,191, leaving a margin of just Rs.4,075 or 3.4 percent of income.

In 2019, the average household earned Rs.76,414 and spent Rs.63,130, retaining Rs.13,284 or 17.4 percent, meaning the margin has narrowed by about 69 percent as spending rose 84 percent, against a 57 percent increase in income.

The median household income of Rs.53,333 exceeded the median spending of Rs.47,544 by Rs.5,789 in 2019 but the position has since reversed, with the median spending of Rs.94,057 overtaking the median income of Rs.75,882 by Rs.18,175 or about 24 percent, in 2025. Although the two medians need not describe the same household, the shift points to a larger share of families spending more than they report earning.

At the constant 2016 prices, the median household income fell 11.1 percent, from 2019 to Rs.41,623, a steeper decline than the 1.7 percent slip in average income to Rs.65,969, which the DCS said suggests that the erosion of purchasing power was “more pronounced for the household at the midpoint of the income distribution”.

However, the real spending moved the other way, with the average expenditure rising 15 percent to Rs.63,734 and the median expenditure 23.6 percent to Rs.51,593.

The urban households alone retain a meaningful margin, earning Rs.178,455 a month on average, against spending of Rs.158,023, which leaves them Rs.20,432 or 11.4 percent of income.

In the rural sector, home to 2.93 million of the 3.42 million people below the poverty line, the households earned Rs.110,613 and spent Rs.108,918, leaving only Rs.1,695 or 1.5 percent.

Meanwhile, the estate households spent Rs.88,691 a month against an average income of Rs.72,558, a shortfall of Rs.16,133 or more than a fifth of their income. The estate sector also recorded the highest incidence of poverty, at 23 percent, compared with 17.3 percent in rural areas and 7.8 percent in urban areas.

The preliminary results do not show how the households are covering these gaps, although the DCS counts withdrawals from savings, bank deposits and provident funds as income, meaning the gaps persist even after such drawdowns.

The figures on household debt, also collected in the 2025 survey, are not part of the preliminary release.

The DCS put the poverty headcount index, the share of the population living below the official poverty line, at 16 percent in the first quarter of 2025, up 1.7 percentage points, from 14.3 percent in 2019, a rise the DCS said “suggests a reversal of the previous declining trend in poverty”.

The poverty line used in the survey was Rs.16,417 per person a month, almost 2.4 times Rs.6,966 of 2019.

The official poverty line has since risen to Rs.17,742 per person a month in September 2026, as per the DCS data, 8.1 percent above the line used in the survey. Almost all of that increase has come in the past year, as the line stood at Rs.16,413 in September 2025.

For an average household of 3.4 persons, this translates into about Rs.60,300 a month to meet the basic needs, compared with about Rs.55,800 at the survey’s poverty line.

Colombo recorded the highest district line at Rs.19,136 and Monaragala the lowest at Rs.16,965.
