Prison system under severe strain

Colombo, Aug. 8 (Daily Mirror) - A wave of violent unrest across Sri Lanka’s prison system since July 5 has raised serious concerns over overcrowding, security failures, and the management of high-risk inmates, prompting calls for urgent government intervention to prevent further incidents and protect both prison officers and inmates.

In response to the escalating national security threat and to prevent copycat prison riots, the government has placed major correctional facilities under heightened security, with Police, the tri-forces, the Police Special Task Force (STF), riot squads and aerial surveillance units kept on standby to respond to any further unrest, while prison authorities continue to maintain security inside the prison premises, the Daily Mirror learns.

The crisis escalated following a deadly clash at Negombo Prison on July 5, which resulted in 32 deaths, including 10 prison officers. Further unrest was later reported at Mahara and New Magazine prisons, while a major confrontation at Kuruwita Prison on August 7 left two inmates dead and several others injured. A tense situation also unfolded at the Pallansena Open Prison yesterday, where authorities deployed security personnel to restore order.

The unrest at New Magazine Prison reportedly erupted after officials intercepted a suspicious parcel allegedly thrown into the prison premises from outside. The incident led to a clash involving around 40 inmates, with one inmate later dying after being hospitalised among 11 injured prisoners.

At Kuruwita Prison, the unrest reportedly began following an attempted escape by an inmate and later escalated into a confrontation involving other prisoners. It is learnt that inmates damaged prison property to obtain bricks, which were allegedly used in clashes with prison officers. Authorities responded by firing shots into the air and using tear gas to regain control.

Investigations have uncovered evidence suggesting that the escape attempt may have been planned, including makeshift ropes allegedly made from bed sheets to scale prison walls.

A few hours after the Kuruwita incident, security has been tightened around the Pallansena Open Prison Camp in Dalupotha, Negombo, after a group of inmates climbed onto the roof and launched a protest.

Police, the Police Special Task Force (STF), the riot control unit, and the Air Force have been deployed to the area as part of security measures. It was reported that unrest broke out at the facility last morning (7) after a group of inmates began a protest on the rooftop.

Meanwhile, intelligence reports and high-level security reviews revealed that an organised plot linked to drug-related syndicates and underworld networks is driving the wave of prison riots across Sri Lanka.

Accordingly, military and STF personnel have set up outer perimeters at major penitentiaries to guarantee facility security.

Following specific intelligence warnings of imminent copycat riots, Southern Province Governor Prof. Susiripala Manawadu mobilised emergency reviews across major regional facilities, including Boossa, Galle, Matara, Agunukolapelessa, and Weerawila prisons.

In the north as well, security has been heightened in the Jaffna prison.

Authorities have initiated the removal of items that could potentially be used as weapons or to damage prison infrastructure as part of ongoing efforts to strengthen security within correctional facilities. The measure is intended to reduce the availability of materials that could be dismantled or repurposed during incidents of unrest.

Meanwhile, it was reported that notorious underworld figures and organised crime elements have been selectively moved to high-security prisons, such as the newly established high-security facility at the Welisara Navy Camp and the Boossa High Security Prison, to prevent them from coordinating syndicate operations from inside.

The Opposition blamed the recent prison unrest on poor management and long-standing issues within the prison system, urging the government to introduce reforms instead of relying solely on tighter security.

They said the unrest reflected broader systemic problems and criticised the government for failing to appoint a permanent Commissioner General of Prisons, questioning why the post remains vacant.

Public Security Minister Ananda Wijepala said recent unrest in prisons may be connected to ongoing efforts to curb narcotics and organised criminal activities, while stating that the coordinated nature of the incidents has raised concerns over possible organised attempts to disrupt the prison system.

Responding to criticism from the Opposition, the Minister rejected claims that the unrest represents a failure of national security, while stressing that the government is treating the situation with the highest priority.

He said both immediate security measures and long-term administrative reforms are being implemented. The Minister added that President Anura Kumara Dissanayake has also convened an urgent meeting with key stakeholders to accelerate measures aimed at restoring stability and strengthening security across correctional facilities.

Justice and National Integration Minister Harshana Nanayakkara said the repeated prison disturbances were not isolated incidents but appeared to be organised attempts by criminal elements to destabilise the prison system.

He said that the prison population has increased from around 29,000 to nearly 40,000 inmates since the current administration assumed office, describing overcrowding as a long-standing issue caused by decades of insufficient investment in prison infrastructure.

According to the latest official statistics, the total prison population stood at 39,400 inmates as of August 5, 2026. Of the total number of inmates, 28,702 are classified as unconvicted prisoners, while the convicted prisoner population stands at 10,698, comprising 10,434 males and 264 females.

The Minister said that previous major incidents had caused significant casualties, including the deaths of 10 prison officers and injuries to 21 officers during the Negombo unrest, while the Mahara prison clashes resulted in 22 inmate deaths and 106 injuries.

He said investigations into previous incidents remain ongoing, with arrests being made based on findings.

To support prison officers affected by the violence, he said the government has appointed a committee to develop special compensation measures covering issues such as financial assistance, pensions, disability benefits, and family welfare.

Steps are being taken to strengthen prison infrastructure and improve operational capacity, with new officer barracks planned at several prison facilities including Welikada, Vavuniya, Anuradhapura, Trincomalee, Monaragala, Pallansena, Boossa, Mahara, and Negombo.

Meanwhile, Rs. 46 million has been allocated for capacity-building programmes and specialised weapons training. Procurement processes have also commenced for body cameras and riot control equipment worth Rs. 13.7 million, including 150 riot helmets, 300 riot batons, and 300 riot shields.

A high-level ministerial committee chaired by Justice Minister Harshana Nanayakkara has also been appointed to examine the root causes of prison congestion and recommend legal, administrative, and policy reforms. The minister said that the committee will study measures to reduce delays in court proceedings, review bail procedures and alternative sentencing options, promote rehabilitation programmes, and propose reforms to improve prison capacity and management.
