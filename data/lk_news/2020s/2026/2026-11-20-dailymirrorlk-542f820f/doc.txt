A little girl on a bed, four lives lost; Firefighters recount Kaduwela tragedy

Colombo, Nov. 20 (Daily Mirror) - “We tried to break a window on the upper floor to get inside. The entire floor was filled with thick smoke. Then, through the smoke, we spotted a small child lying on a bed. Our officers were heartbroken,” recalled D.S. Jayalal, team leader of the Sri Jayewardenepura Kotte Municipal Council Fire Brigade.

What began as an early morning fire at an electrical appliance showroom turned into a devastating tragedy, claiming the lives of four members of a family who were living on the upper floor of the building.

The victims were identified as Narada Sandaruwan (35), his wife Nilakshika Madhushani (28), and their two daughters, Dineli Hasanya (5) and Oneli Hasanya (2), residents of Dutugemunu Mawatha, Thalangama North.

The bodies of the four victims were found on two beds on the upper floor by firefighters, who managed to reach them only after bringing the flames under control.

The tragedy came to light at around 5.30 a.m. yesterday (19), when an employee at the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation filling station opposite the building noticed smoke rising from the upper floor.

“I was working at the filling station when I noticed smoke coming from the upper floor of the building opposite. When I went closer, I could see what appeared to be a fire inside. The smoke kept getting heavier, so we immediately informed the officers at the nearby Kaduwela Police Post,” the employee said.

The Kotte Municipal Council Fire Brigade received the alert at around 6.20 a.m. and rushed to the scene.

“We reached the location within about half an hour. By then, the ground floor was already engulfed in flames, and the fire was spreading,” Jayalal said.

Two fire engines were initially dispatched, but another was called in after firefighters learnt that four people could be trapped inside.

The team kept in touch with its control room while rushing to the scene, gathering information to prepare for the rescue operation.

However, what awaited them was far worse than they had anticipated.

The ground floor had become a mass of flames, while thick smoke from the burning business premises had risen to the upper floor. The building had no windows on the ground floor to provide adequate ventilation, making the situation even more difficult.

“We were initially told that only one person was living in the building. But when we arrived, we learnt that an entire family of four were upstairs. We wanted to get inside immediately, but the flames were so intense that we could not even approach the building. Even with our protective clothing, it was impossible to get close,” he recalled.

After battling the fire, the firefighters finally managed to make their way upstairs. The upper floor was covered in thick black smoke.

They broke a window in an attempt to enter the premises. It was then that they spotted the little girl lying on the bed.

The discovery of the child, followed by the sight of the other family members, left the rescuers devastated.

“Our officers had to crawl along the floor to reach the child. We managed to get the little one out first. We had already arranged for 1990 Suwa Seriya ambulances to be on standby. The mother and father were lying on the floor. We then brought them down along with the other child,” Jayalal said.

The parents had been working at the electrical appliance showroom and had moved into the upper floor of the building about three months earlier, police said.

But the building's layout and lack of emergency exits had made the rescue operation extremely difficult.

Jayalal said there was a small staircase towards the side of the upper floor, but it had been blocked with a metal grill. Goods had also been piled up in several places, leaving very little room to move.

“Even our officers found it extremely difficult to get inside, despite wearing protective clothing. Their equipment kept getting caught in the obstructions. It appeared that little attention had been paid to basic safety arrangements,” he said.

The building had only one staircase providing access, while another entrance had been locked with padlocks. There was no alternative exit at the rear.

“The lack of basic safety arrangements may have cost these four people their lives. Every building where people live should have an alternative exit for emergencies. This building had no such safety provisions and was in an extremely unsafe condition,” Jayalal said.
