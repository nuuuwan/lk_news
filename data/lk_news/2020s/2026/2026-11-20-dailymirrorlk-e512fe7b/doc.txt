West Asia crisis hinders expressway construction in Sri Lanka

Colombo, Nov. 21 (Daily Mirror) - The West Asian crisis has hampered road construction, including the development of proposed expressways, due to difficulties in importing bitumen, a petroleum by-product, an official said.

Sri Lanka usually requires between 15,000 and 25,000 tonnes of bitumen, commonly known as tar, for road construction and repairs.

Iran has been a key supplier of bitumen to Sri Lanka, with the country previously sourcing Iranian-manufactured material through established market channels in Dubai.

Asked about the situation, Director General of the Road Development Authority (RDA) Anuradha Hettiarachchi said Sri Lanka's current bitumen requirement for constructing new highways and expressways, as well as repairing the existing road network, amounted to between 15,000 and 25,000 tonnes.

He said limited stocks were now arriving from countries such as India, China, Thailand and Malaysia.

"We need more and more bitumen when we start work on the proposed expressways," he said.

In Budget 2027, the government proposed the construction of three expressways: the Pothuhera-Galagedara section of the Central Expressway, the Kurunegala-Dambulla Expressway and the Ingiriya-Ratnapura Expressway.

The government has decided to undertake these projects using local financing.

However, Mr. Hettiarachchi said that 15-20 per cent of the project expenditure would have to be allocated for importing construction materials.

Bitumen or tar is a petroleum by-product, mainly manufactured in Iran.
