Hambantota Port, Al-Futtaim discuss expanding vehicle shipment operations to Gulf markets

Hambantota International Port Group (HIPG) and Al-Futtaim Logistics have discussed expanding their existing business relationship, as shipping disruptions in the Middle East highlight Hambantota's growing role as an alternative gateway for vehicle shipments to Gulf markets.

The discussions took place during a courtesy visit to Hambantota International Port (HIP) by a delegation led by the Vice Chairman of Al-Futtaim Group.

Talks focused on strengthening business ties, including the role of the CASCADIA RoRo Feeder Service in improving Hambantota's connectivity with Gulf markets.

The discussions also covered potential investment opportunities at the port after the visiting delegation expressed interest in investing in HIP. Port officials briefed the delegation on opportunities across port operations and its industrial park.

The visit follows the publication of Al-Futtaim's white paper, titled "Resilience in Motion", which identifies Hambantota as one of three alternative gateways used to maintain regional automotive supply chains following disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz in March 2026.

The other two gateways were Port Sultan Qaboos in Muscat and Port Khor Fakkan in the Gulf of Oman.

According to the white paper, the three routes collectively transported more than 25,000 finished vehicles to the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait and Saudi Arabia, helping maintain vehicle supplies to dealers and supporting automobile manufacturers in restoring production.

The figure represents the combined contribution of all three routes and does not reflect the volume handled by Hambantota Port alone.

Hambantota's inclusion in the alternative shipping network highlights the importance of ports outside areas directly affected by disruptions in maintaining regional trade.

The CASCADIA RoRo Feeder Service, launched on June 9, 2026, is part of this connectivity, linking Hambantota with vehicle supply chains serving regional markets.

The service is operated by CASCADIA, a Canada-headquartered maritime logistics specialist with operations in major trade hubs, including Dubai.

Al-Futtaim's white paper also highlights the importance of contingency planning, shipment visibility and coordination between different customs and regulatory systems in responding to disruptions in global supply chains.

Al-Futtaim Logistics, part of the Dubai-headquartered Al-Futtaim Group, provides integrated logistics services across the automotive, aerospace, retail, and food and beverage sectors. Its global network extends to more than 150 countries through strategic partners.
