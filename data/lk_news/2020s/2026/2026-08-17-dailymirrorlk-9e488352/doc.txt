No anchor, no accountability as economists blast Central Bank over two-year inflation miss

Real interest rates in SL became among the highest and most volatile in the region during the two-year inflation undershoot, economists say

Despite a legal requirement to explain sustained target misses to parliament, that oversight has “completely stalled,” notes Verité Research’s Executive Director

The Central Bank of Sri Lanka’s handling of monetary policy came under sustained fire at a panel hosted by the Sri Lanka-Korea Business Council, with economists questioning the institution’s competence, the near-total absence of parliamentary accountability, and the damage inflicted on debt sustainability and private sector growth by two years of missed inflation targets.

Legally bound to keep headline inflation within a 3-7 percent band around a 5 percent target, the Central Bank instead let it undershoot for eight consecutive quarters, often hovering at 1-2 percent and slipping as low as -4.2 percent in February 2025, before the target was finally breached from the other direction this year.

Speaking at the panel discussion “Sri Lanka’s Future - Forecast, Scenarios and Challenges,” organised by the Sri Lanka-Korea Business Council of the Ceylon Chamber of Commerce in Colombo last week, Verité Research Executive Director Dr. Nishan de Mel said, “Once you get into a gazetted agreement with the government to deliver a certain level of inflation, you have to meet that. There is no management discretion here. These are simple facts of accountability and competence.” He likened the lapse to a statutory check that exists but has gone unused: “In the Bank of England, as in Sri Lanka, we’ve set up a device that says if you miss it by a certain amount, you must go to Parliament and explain why you did it. We haven’t been able to get Parliament to ask good enough questions to rectify the problem, so it keeps getting missed for eight quarters in a row.”

De Mel argued the scale of the failure was especially telling given the institution’s standing. “The Central Bank failing for eight quarters in a row on a single target that it is legally mandated to do tells you how seriously we must look at this problem of competence, because it is one of the most competent institutions we have, and yet there we have it,” he said. He tied the miss directly to broken inflation anchoring - the point at which market expectations stop tracking a central bank’s guidance closely enough for the economy to stabilise on its own - and to distorted debt dynamics.

“When the government pays high interest and there is no inflation, what you call the real interest rates in Sri Lanka became amongst the highest in the region and also became extremely volatile,” de Mel said, arguing the state ended up paying massive real interest costs on its domestic debt as a result. He was equally critical of how the Central Bank read the shocks driving the miss in both directions.

“We got it wrong on both ends. We had a sustained negative supply-side shock that we didn’t respond to by reducing interest rates, which we should have, to stimulate demand. But when we had a negative supply shock that’s basically transient, we intervened by jacking up interest rates 100 basis points,” he said - a critique that lands squarely on the Central Bank’s May 2026 decision to raise its Overnight Policy Rate by exactly 100 basis points, to 8.75 percent, in response to the current energy-driven inflation spike.

Advocata Institute Chairman Murtaza Jafferjee tied the same dynamics to debt sustainability, invoking the standard relationship between real interest rates and growth that determines whether debt stays manageable. He argued that prolonged high real rates, driven by depressed inflation, produced a large wealth transfer to capital holders and complicated the recovery, and that restoring real GDP growth to at least 5 percent - the pace needed to rebuild fiscal space through tax buoyancy - requires trade liberalisation and the dismantling of protectionist barriers rather than further monetary intervention.  (NF)
