Generic vs. brand name prescriptions: Who benefits?

The government has ordered doctors to write only the GENERIC name, not the brand name. On paper, it sounds pro-people. Cheap drugs for all. But on the ground, it is dangerous if implemented without safeguards.

Let me be honest and show both sides. THE PLUS SIDE - Why the Government Did It: It’s cheaper for the patient: A generic is always cheaper than a branded product. It stops the Pharma Mafia: We  know some doctors prescribe only one expensive brand because they get foreign trips, commissions and gifts from that company. Generic prescribing breaks that unholy nexus. In the UK, USA and Australia, doctors write the generic name. It is the global system and we should follow it.

But here’s the minus side – the real danger no one talks about.

When a doctor writes “Atorvastatin 20mg”, he is NOT deciding which company makes it. He is leaving that decision to the PHARMACY. Now, who controls the pharmacy? The Medical Rep. Today, the rep has to convince ONE doctor. Tomorrow, he only has to convince ONE pharmacy owner. Give him 30% extra commission, give him a fridge, give him a foreign tour. The pharmacy will push THAT brand.

And all generics are NOT the same quality. A good company’s Atorvastatin and a cheap, unregulated company’s Atorvastatin have a huge difference in quality, bioavailability and side effects. The doctor knows which company’s drug works well. He has seen it for 10+ years with his own patients. The pharmacy counter girl does not.

What will actually happen?  Patient goes with a generic chit.  Pharmacy gives the brand which gives them the highest profit, not the best quality.  If the drug fails, if the patient gets a reaction, who is responsible? Doctor will say “I didn’t write that brand”. Pharmacy will say “I only gave what was written”. The doctor’s accountability is GONE.

My Solution -- Don’t ban brand names. Make it MANDATORY to write BOTH. Or: Doctor writes generic + can RECOMMEND a quality brand, but the patient is allowed to choose.
