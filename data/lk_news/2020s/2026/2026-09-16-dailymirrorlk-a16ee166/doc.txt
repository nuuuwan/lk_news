CSE selling deepens as ASPI breaks below 21,200

The Colombo Stock Exchange extended its decline for a third consecutive session on 16 September 2026, with broad-based selling intensifying as elevated global oil prices continued to weigh on investor sentiment.

The ASPI fell 128.72 points, or 0.61%, to close near the session low at 21,133.41, while the S&P SL20 declined 29.38 points, or 0.49%, to 5,947.29. Selling accelerated during the afternoon, pushing the ASPI below the key 21,200 level. Brent crude was indicated at USD 108.75 per barrel, keeping concerns over higher energy costs and their potential economic impact firmly in focus.

Market turnover stood at LKR 1.63 billion, with 69.38 million shares traded. The Food, Beverage and Tobacco sector led activity with LKR 782.27 million, largely driven by Sunshine Holdings, which generated LKR 616.14 million in turnover.

Crossings accounted for around 23% of total turnover. Sunshine Holdings led block activity with approximately LKR 159.50 million, followed by John Keells Holdings with LKR 110.17 million and Ceylon Cold Stores with LKR 61.35 million.

A key feature of the session was the sharp deterioration in market breadth, with only 46 gainers against 172 decliners, producing an advance-decline ratio of just 0.27. Foreign activity was comparatively neutral, with a marginal net outflow of LKR 8.01 million.

John Keells Holdings was the largest negative contributor to the ASPI, followed by Ceylinco Insurance, Lion Brewery, Melstacorp and RIL Property. Richard Pieris provided the strongest positive contribution.

The third consecutive decline, the ASPI closing near its session low and the heavily negative market breadth indicate that selling pressure has become increasingly broad. With oil prices remaining elevated, near-term sentiment is likely to remain cautious unless stronger buying support emerges around current market levels.
