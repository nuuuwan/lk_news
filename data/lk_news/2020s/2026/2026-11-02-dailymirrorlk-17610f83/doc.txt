World’s energy supply buffers have been eroded, ADNOC CEO says

Reuters - The world has eroded its supply buffers by draining nearly 1 billion oil barrels from global ​inventories since the US-Israeli war on Iran disrupted shipping ‌through the Strait of Hormuz, the CEO of Abu Dhabi's state-owned oil company ADNOC said.

Diesel stockpiles in some areas are at 35-year lows ​for this time of the season, while others face an LNG supply gap of ​600 billion cubic feet, Sultan Al Jaber added on Monday at the ⁠UAE's ADIPEC conference.

Prices of diesel in many markets have surged ​to record highs, boosted by wartime supply disruptions and pose inflation and cost-of-living ​threats to governments and consumers.

"We need expanded strategic storage, larger stockpiles of products, and flexible refining capacity closer to consumer markets," Al Jaber said. "Infrastructure ​must be built before it is needed and enabled ​by advanced logistics."

The cost of shipping and logistics has risen to 30% of the ‌cost ⁠of a barrel, from 5% before the Iran war, Al Jaber said.

The international benchmark Brent is trading around $110 a barrel, up about 50% from February just before the war began.

Last month, ​the International Energy ​Agency agreed ⁠to release more than 20 million barrels of diesel to the market and any oil remaining from a ​record emergency reserves release pledged in March ​to boost ⁠supply and cool soaring prices.

Amin Nasser, CEO of Saudi Arabia's state oil company Saudi Aramco (2222.SE) said last month that fewer than 6 billion barrels ⁠of commercial ​inventories remained, with the vast majority not ​practically available.
