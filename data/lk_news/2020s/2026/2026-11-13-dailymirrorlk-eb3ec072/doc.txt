Budget 2027 banks on goods and services taxes to lift revenue to Rs. 6.15 trn

The government is counting on taxes on goods and services to deliver nearly 70 percent of next year’s increase in government revenue, under a budget that targets Rs. 6.15 trillion in collections while proposing relief for personal income taxpayers and cuts to selected import levies.

The budget presented to parliament yesterday by President and Finance Minister Anura Kumara Dissanayake projected revenue to rise by Rs. 199 billion, or 3.3 percent, from the revised 2026 estimate of Rs. 5.95 trillion. Including Rs. 30 billion in grants, total revenue and grants are forecast at Rs. 6.18 trillion.

Taxes on goods and services are expected to bring in Rs. 3.59 trillion, up Rs. 138 billion, and account for nearly six in every 10 rupees of government revenue. Taxes on external trade are projected to rise by Rs. 41 billion to Rs. 754 billion, while income tax receipts are expected to increase by Rs. 30 billion to Rs. 1.34 trillion.

The three categories are forecast to lift total tax revenue by Rs. 209 billion to Rs. 5.69 trillion. A projected Rs. 10 billion decline in non-tax revenue to Rs. 463 billion will absorb part of that increase.

Even with higher collections in rupee terms, government revenue is projected to fall to 15.8 percent of gross domestic product from 16.5 percent in the revised 2026 estimates. Tax revenue is forecast to ease to 14.6 percent of GDP from 15.2 percent.

The Treasury estimates that the measures listed in the budget’s revenue-impact table will reduce net collections by Rs. 21.15 billion in 2027. Their annual cost is put at Rs. 28.42 billion, with income tax revisions and the next phase of import CESS cuts accounting for the bulk of the reduction.

“Accordingly, a person receiving a monthly salary and allowances of up to Rs. 162,500 will not be required to pay tax on his or her salary,” Dissanayake said in his budget speech.

The proposed threshold is up from Rs. 150,000 a month. Wider tax bands would also move the point at which the top rate of 36 percent applies to about Rs. 433,000 a month from Rs. 358,000. The income tax revisions carry an estimated annual revenue cost of Rs. 16 billion.

The government also proposed cutting existing CESS rates by 25 percent on 800 selected tariff lines, at an estimated annual cost of Rs. 10 billion, and removing the 10 percent Ports and Airports Development Levy on specified newsprint imports from January. The revision of the Special Commodity Levy was the only measure in the table projected to yield additional revenue on an annual basis, at Rs. 950 million.

Dissanayake also proposed changes to tax laws to reflect new forms of transactions and a review of procedural requirements that can prevent taxpayers from receiving refunds or input tax credits.

“These measures aim to establish a fair, transparent, and efficient tax administration system that encourages voluntary tax compliance, safeguards the taxpayers’ legitimate entitlements, and protects government revenue,” he said.

To encourage investment in industries that earn foreign exchange, the budget proposes a capital allowance equivalent to 200 percent of qualifying expenditure for eligible Board of Investment-approved export manufacturing and tourism projects with a minimum qualifying investment of US$ 3 million. It also proposes allowing newly VAT-registered exporters to qualify earlier for expedited refunds. The speech did not quantify the additional export or tourism earnings expected from those measures.
