Fire at Anawilundawa Ramsar Wetland brought under control; Probe underway

Colombo, August 22 (Daily Mirror) - A sudden fire that broke out at the Anawilundawa Ramsar Wetland Sanctuary has been brought fully under control, with the damage limited to approximately eight acres, the Ministry of Environment said.

Environment Deputy Minister Anton Jayakody inspected the affected area today (22) and held discussions on the damage caused and measures required to strengthen the conservation of the wetland.

The fire had spread for nearly two days amid dry weather and strong winds. Following alerts raised by several parties, including the Chairman of the Arachchikattuwa Pradeshiya Sabha, the Government launched immediate measures to contain the blaze.

With the assistance of Deputy Minister of Defence Aruna Jayasekara, Sri Lanka Air Force helicopters equipped with Bambi Buckets were deployed to drop seawater over the affected areas.

The Wildlife Conservation Department, Sri Lanka Police, the Divisional Secretariat and local residents also joined the firefighting efforts, preventing the fire from causing widespread damage to the wetland, which covers more than 3,000 hectares.

A special investigation is underway to determine whether the fire was deliberately set or involved any act of sabotage.

“If this was intentional or an act of sabotage, maximum legal action will be taken against those responsible,” Deputy Minister Jayakody said, stressing that strict action would be taken against anyone found responsible for unlawful destruction of the environmentally sensitive area.

The Deputy Minister also said steps would be taken to urgently reconstruct the bund of the Anawilundawa reservoir, which was breached following Cyclone Ditwah.

With technical assistance from national expert institutions and engineers, the Government plans to restore the reservoir before the upcoming monsoon season.

He said the objective was to restore a suitable ecological environment for wildlife, including migratory birds expected during the December-January period.

Deputy Minister Jayakody further said the Government would continue coordinated and timely interventions across ministries to protect critical ecosystems from both climate-related and human-induced threats.
