Services lead Sri Lanka’s private sector expansion in July

Sri Lanka’s services sector expanded at a faster pace in July, supported by growth in transportation, financial and professional services, while manufacturing activity also strengthened during the month, according to the Central Bank.

The Services Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) rose to 61.4 in July from 56.5 in June, signalling a sharp acceleration in business activity.

The expansion was broad-based, led by transportation, financial and professional services, while the other personal services segment also recorded notable growth.

New business continued to increase, driven mainly by transportation and professional services, with financial and other personal services also contributing.

Employment rose during the month, supported by fresh recruitment and a slowdown in staff resignations. Backlogs of work increased, with the relevant sub-index rising to 51.2 from a neutral 50.0 in June.

Businesses became more upbeat about the near-term outlook, with the expectations for activity sub-index climbing to 75.0 from 72.7. The Central Bank attributed the stronger sentiment to anticipated economic improvements and expectations of higher tourist arrivals linked to the Kandy Esala Perahera.

The Manufacturing PMI, meanwhile, increased to 55.0 in July from 53.0 in June, indicating a continued and faster expansion in factory activity. A reading above 50 signals expansion, while one below 50 indicates contraction.

Growth in new orders and production was driven primarily by the food and beverage manufacturing sector. The new orders sub-index rose to 55.9 from 50.0, while production increased to 54.5 from 52.0.

Employment edged up to 51.0 from 50.5, reflecting manufacturers’ positive expectations. However, the stock of purchases sub-index slowed to 51.5 from 56.6.

Suppliers’ delivery times remained lengthened, pointing to continuing supply-chain pressures.

Manufacturers remained optimistic about activity over the next three months, although both sectors identified broader global economic uncertainties as a downside risk to the outlook.
