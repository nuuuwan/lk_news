Govt. moves to consolidate labour laws under new reform framework

Proposed reforms to cover employment contracts, working hours, leave, maternity and paternity protection

Four pillars include labour standards, workplace safety, industrial relations and trade union rights

Public consultation opens as four drafted chapters of Labour Standards Bill are published online

The Government is moving to overhaul and consolidate Sri Lanka’s existing labour law framework through a proposed new legislative package covering employment conditions, workplace protections, social security and trade union rights.

The proposed reforms, being formulated by an expert committee appointed with Cabinet approval, aim to bring the country’s labour laws in line with the changing and future demands of the world of work, Labour Ministry officials told Members of Parliament at a special briefing held on 18 November.

The initiative seeks to establish a new legal framework by reviewing and consolidating the major labour laws currently in force, with the proposed reforms structured around four key areas; labour standards, occupational health and safety, industrial relations and disputes, and trade union rights.

The proposed framework is expected to address a broad range of employment-related provisions, including formalisation of employment contracts, working hours, leave entitlements, overtime, maternity protection, paternity leave and the prevention of violence and harassment in the workplace.

It will also seek to strengthen provisions governing part-time employment, social security benefits and the right of employees to a safe working environment, signalling a broader review of employment protections and workplace rights.

The Ministry has already published the four chapters of the proposed Labour Standards Bill drafted so far on its official website, inviting members of the public and relevant stakeholders to submit comments and proposals.

Officials said the views of trade unions, employers and employee representatives would also be considered in developing the proposed legislation, indicating that stakeholder consultation will form part of the law-making process.

The briefing was held under the patronage of Labour Minister Dr. Anil Jayantha who is also the Ministerial Consultative Committee on Labour Affairs Chairman. Labour Deputy Minister Mahinda Jayasinghe, MPs and Ministry officials also participated.
