Leopard cub rescued after wandering into house in Bulathkohupitiya

Colombo, September 17 (Daily Mirror) - A well-grown leopard cub that had wandered into a house in Dedugala, Bulathkohupitiya, was safely rescued by wildlife officials following a coordinated operation.

The rescue operation was launched on September 15 after the Bulathkohupitiya Wildlife Office received information that a leopard (Panthera pardus) had entered a residence in the area.

Wildlife officers, acting on veterinary advice, rescued the animal and subsequently attempted to reunite it with its mother. However, the attempt was unsuccessful.

The leopard was therefore transported to the Randenigala Wildlife Health Management Unit on September 16 for further treatment and observation.

Wildlife veterinary officers and other personnel are currently attending to the animal and making arrangements to release it into a suitable habitat after it receives the necessary medical treatment.

The leopard is a strictly protected species under the Fauna and Flora Protection Ordinance and is considered threatened with extinction.

Wildlife authorities urged the public to avoid approaching or attempting to rescue leopards found trapped in snares or confined to a particular location.

Members of the public who encounter such an animal are advised to immediately move away from the area and inform the nearest wildlife office or contact the Department of Wildlife Conservation emergency hotline on 1992.

Wildlife officials also commended the homeowner and residents in the area for following instructions and extending their cooperation to ensure the safe rescue of the animal.

The operation was conducted under the guidance of Saman Padmasiri, Wildlife Range Officer of the Ma-imbul Kanda Range Office.

Wildlife Range Assistants W.A. Udith Viduranga and K.P. Udaya Karunanayake, and Field Assistants K.A.N. Kaluarachchi and D.W. Anura Jayatissa of the Bulathkohupitiya Wildlife Office participated in the operation.

Wildlife Veterinarian Dr. Akalanka Pinidiya of Randenigala and his staff also assisted in the rescue and subsequent treatment of the leopard.

Police personnel led by Bulathkohupitiya Police OIC Kosala Buddhadasa provided assistance during the operation.
