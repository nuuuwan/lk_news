What’s  brewing at the Bar?

Today a large section of the people in our country live in dire poverty

Government is proposing bringing in an Amendment to the Constitution extending the retirement age of Supreme Court and Appeal Court Judges. A large number of lawyers affiliated to the Bar Association of Sri Lanka oppose the move. Members of the parliamentary Opposition stringently voice their opposition to the government’s proposal.

The Bar Association and members of the Opposition claim the proposed Amendment to the Constitution would undermine public confidence in the impartiality of the Superior Courts. Neither body however, has let on, as to how by extending the age of retirement of Superior Court Judges the public will lose confidence in the impartiality of the Courts of law.

Government on its part claims the need to extend the retirement age of Judges of the Superior Courts is to help clear the backlog of pending cases awaiting conclusion. Today there is a backlog of approximately 1.1 million pending cases. Government claims extending age of retirement of judges is part of an effort to improve the efficiency of Sri Lanka’s judicial system and address a backlog.

The parliamentary Opposition on the other hand claims the government wants to extend the age of retirement of superior court judges merely to extend the tenure of a particular Justice. But, by making such claims,  are not members of the Bar Association and Members of Parliament (MPs) themselves bringing into disrepute a senior member of the judiciary by making unsubstantiated charges against him? Is it not like cutting off one’s nose to spite the face?

At a Bar Association meeting held two days ago, -- and given wide media coverage- - the association unanimously opposed government’s move to extend the age of retirement of superior court judges. The Bar Association has a membership of 26,000 members, of whom around 3,700 were said to have participated at the meeting and voted to oppose government’s proposal.

This means only around one-seventh of its members voted to oppose the proposal to extend the retirement age of Superior Court Judges. Again,  the idea of extending the age of retirement of Judges is not a new one. As columnist M.S.M. Ayub points out, a Parliamentary Subcommittee consisting of members of all parties in the House, including the current Opposition parties, way back in 2016 themselves recommended the extension of the retirement age of judges!

Today a large section of the people in our country live in dire poverty. Nearly 25% of our children, according to UNICEF,  are malnourished and families are coping with rising costs by cutting down on meals. Small and Medium Enterprises (SME) which form the backbone of our industrial production – producing 51% and employing over 45% of the workforce -- are facing closure due to price fluctuations caused by the US war on Iran.

For a majority of these people whether a particular Justice retires on or before he reaches the age of 60 is of no consequence. Their problem is one of keeping the wolf from the door. It is a majority of these people who are often brought to courts on trivial charges of minor crime  who are part of the over million pending cases.

Many of these helpless souls awaiting ‘justice’, languish in our over-crowded prisons, either unable to make bail payment or afford to pay lawyers’ fees when cases are postponed for one reason or another. A number of them were probably among those who died during the recent riot at the Negombo prison.

A large section of our people are unable to have two square meals a day. Parents of infants are unable to afford to  infant food. The Household Survey on the Impact of the Economic Crisis – 2023, conducted by the Department of Census and Statistics, found that the schooling of 54.9 percent of those aged 3 - 21 was impacted by the economic crisis. Premier Amarasuriya admitted that over 20,000 students drop out of school annually due to poverty.

It’s high time our politicians, lawyers and the elite in our society saw the gravity of the problems around us. Let us even for a little while put the country before our own private interests.
