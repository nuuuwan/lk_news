Sarath Manamendra files petition in support of 22nd Amendment

Colombo, Aug. 28 (Daily Mirror) - Leader of the Nava Sihala Urumaya political party Sarath Manamendra today filed an intervenient petition before the Supreme Court, seeking a determination that the proposed Twenty-Second Amendment to the Constitution, in its entirety, is not inconsistent with the provisions of the Constitution and therefore does not require the approval of the people at a referendum.

Manamendra is further seeking an order rejecting the relief sought in the Special Determination petition filed by former Minister Prof. G.L. Peiris against the proposed Twenty-Second Amendment to the Constitution.

In a separate Special Determination petition, Prof. G.L. Peiris had sought a declaration that the Bill requires approval by the people at a referendum in addition to being passed by a two-thirds majority in Parliament.

However, Manamendra stated that extending judicial tenure does not infringe sovereignty under Article 3 so as to mandate a referendum.

The intervenient-petitioner further said the proposed amendment to Article 137 expanding the Court of Appeal judges from 19 to 24 is a critical and long-overdue reform. He further stated that increasing the capacity of the Court of Appeal directly addresses chronic systemic backlogs and accelerates the disposition of long-pending appeals.

He maintained that the prior judicial observations in the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Bill (SC/SD/64/2022) must be interpreted against their specific factual context and cannot serve as an absolute bar to all future general adjustments to judicial tenure.
