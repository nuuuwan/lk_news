Sri Lanka to revamp SOEs via divestments and holding company

ECONOMYNEXT — Sri Lanka is preparing to divest and restructure state-owned enterprises under a planned legal framework to eliminate commercial distortions, Deputy Minister of Finance and Planning Anil Jayantha said.

Speaking at an industry forum hosted by the Securities and Exchange Commission and the Colombo Stock Exchange, Jayantha said a dedicated committee has been appointed to review public enterprises and formulate restructuring strategies.

Of 527 entities tagged as state enterprises, only a select few are genuine commercial businesses, he said, while many others perform administrative functions under specific statutory criteria.

“Our main task is to facilitate. But as of now, there are many state-owned enterprises… Some of these SOEs really we can divest, sometimes we may have to merge,” Jayantha said.

Jayantha emphasized that the state has no interest in engaging in basic business operations such as trading or running hotels, preferring to focus purely on regulatory and economic facilitation.

However, the government will retain ownership in strategic utility sectors like energy to prevent private market manipulation. Commercial state enterprises and state banks will also be shifted away from traditional bureaucratic controls toward competitive corporate models.

“New SOE Act would facilitate, would allow separate management so they can be reorganized under one umbrella. So, those organizations would be incorporated as limited liability companies,” he said.

Jayantha said the proposed holding structure would grant state companies managerial autonomy to respond to market conditions, with reformed entities potentially listed on the stock exchange to attract private and foreign investment.

The timeline to present the bill to parliament remains uncertain due to backlogs at the Legal Draftsman’s Department. While authorities initially targeted parliamentary passage by the end of this year, Jayantha noted that the schedule could slip.

“First, we need to get it cleared from the Legal Draftsman’s Department; if that is the case, at least we can submit to the Parliament, maybe that could be next year,” he said.

He noted that while administrative reforms take time, moving steadily will establish sound institutional foundations to support Sri Lanka’s economic transformation. (Colombo/Oct28/2026)
