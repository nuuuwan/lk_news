Sri Lanka celebrate double cricket success as both teams reach new heights

Nov.27 (Mirror Sports) - Sri Lanka A stormed into the Asia Cup Rising Stars final after producing a remarkable batting display to defeat India A by eight wickets in the first semi-final in Doha.

Chasing a challenging target of 318, Sri Lanka A reached 318 for two in just 41.3 overs, with Lasith Croospulle and Sineth Jayawardena leading the charge with outstanding centuries.

India A had earlier posted 317 for eight after being sent in to bat. Abhishek Sharma top-scored with 78 off 63 deliveries, while captain Tilak Varma made 53 off 66 balls. Shahbaz Ahmed added an unbeaten 54 from 48 deliveries to strengthen India's total.

Vaibhav Sooryavanshi also contributed 40, while Janith Liyanage, Nipun Dhananjaya and Ravindu Fernando claimed two wickets each for Sri Lanka A.

Sri Lanka A made an aggressive start to the chase, reaching 85 without loss in the first 10 overs as the opening pair put the Indian bowlers under immediate pressure.

Jayawardena smashed 105 off just 70 balls, including 10 fours and six sixes, while Croospulle produced an equally impressive 118 from 107 deliveries, with 12 fours and three sixes.

Their opening stand of 174 runs effectively put Sri Lanka A in control of the chase.

Following Jayawardena's dismissal, Nishan Madushka maintained the momentum with an unbeaten 58 off 56 balls.

Croospulle departed in the 36th over after taking Sri Lanka A beyond the 250-run mark, but captain Pavan Rathnayake ensured a comfortable finish with an unbeaten 31 off just 16 deliveries, including three sixes and a four.

Madushka and Rathnayake added an unbeaten 53-run partnership to complete a dominant victory and book Sri Lanka A's place in the final.

Sri Lanka's women's team crowned a remarkable unbeaten campaign by defeating Ireland by six wickets in the final to win the ICC Women's Emerging Nations Trophy 2026 in Ajman, United Arab Emirates.

The triumph completed a perfect tournament for Sri Lanka, who secured eight victories from eight matches to claim the title in impressive fashion.

Stand-in captain Harshitha Samarawickrama praised the collective effort of her players, highlighting the importance of every squad member understanding and fulfilling their role throughout the competition.

Vishmi Gunaratne was one of Sri Lanka's standout performers, finishing as the tournament's leading run-scorer with 324 runs.

Spinner Sathya Sandeepani also enjoyed an outstanding campaign, claiming 17 wickets to share the tournament's leading wicket-taker award with Ireland's Cara Murray.

Sandeepani played a decisive role in the final, taking four wickets for just 13 runs to earn the Player of the Match award and help Sri Lanka secure the trophy.

"I'm very happy about our team and I think this is a complete team effort," Samarawickrama said after the victory.

She also praised the players for their confidence and consistency, noting how well they had performed after understanding their individual responsibilities.

For Sandeepani, the tournament represented a significant personal achievement. Having previously played just one international match in 2020, she made the most of her opportunity after earning a call-up following impressive performances for Sri Lanka A.

Reflecting on her breakthrough campaign, Sandeepani credited her family and coaches for their support.

Despite the defeat, Ireland captain Gaby Lewis acknowledged that her side had not performed to its potential in the final but remained positive about the lessons gained from the tournament.

Sri Lanka's title-winning campaign marks an impressive achievement for the women's team, who maintained their unbeaten record throughout the competition.
