Government Dental Service must focus more on prevention: PM

Colombo, Nov. 22 (Daily Mirror) - Prime Minister Dr. Harini Amarasuriya has called for a shift towards preventive dental care, saying the Government Dental Service must focus not only on treatment but also on prevention, early detection and equal access to services.

She made these remarks at a ceremony held at the Bandaranaike Memorial International Conference Hall (BMICH) yesterday (22) to mark the centenary of the Government Dental Service in Sri Lanka.

The Prime Minister said the service, which began on a small scale at the Colombo General Hospital in 1926, had expanded into a nationwide network of 600 treatment centres, employing around 1,350 dental surgeons, including 106 specialists.

She identified the establishment of the Dental College in 1943 and the graduation of the first batch of locally trained dentists in 1947 as major milestones in the development of dental services.

She also highlighted the contribution made by the Faculties of Dentistry at the Universities of Peradeniya and Sri Jayewardenepura towards strengthening dental education in the country.

Expressing her appreciation to dental surgeons, specialists, school dental therapists, laboratory technicians, dental assistants and other staff, she said their dedication had helped build the service over the past 100 years.

The Prime Minister said that oral health went beyond treating toothaches and extracting teeth, as it directly affected nutrition, speech, self-confidence and overall quality of life.

She pointed out that the prevalence of tooth decay among 12-year-old children had declined from around 67 per cent in earlier national surveys to 30.4 per cent in the 2015-2016 survey, demonstrating the impact of school dental services and preventive programmes.

However, she said greater public awareness was needed about oral cancer and the risks associated with tobacco, betel, areca nut and harmful alcohol consumption.

"Many people remember dental advice only after a toothache develops. We need to change this attitude. Healthcare should begin before a disease occurs, not after it," she said.

She also said the need to bring basic dental services closer to communities and ensure that access to quality treatment was not determined by income, geographical location or social status.

Special attention, she said, should be given to economically disadvantaged communities, persons with disabilities and older people.

The Prime Minister further highlighted that modern developments such as digital dentistry should be made equally accessible to all sections of society.

A commemorative stamp and first-day cover marking the centenary of the Government Dental Service were also launched at the ceremony with the participation of the Prime Minister and Minister of Health and Mass Media Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa.

Deputy Director General of Health Services (Dental Services) Dr. Chandana Gajanayake and several medical and dental professionals also attended the event.
