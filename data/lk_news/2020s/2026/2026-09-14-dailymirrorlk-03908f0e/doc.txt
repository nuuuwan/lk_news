Railway unions maintain Sept. 17-18 strike

Colombo, Sep. 14 (Daily Mirror) - The Joint Railway Trade Unions have decided to proceed with their planned two-day token strike on September 17 and 18, as no acceptable decision has been reached regarding the removal of the General Manager of Railways.

The decision was announced following a special discussion held today (14) between Transport Ministry Secretary and representatives of the Joint Railway Trade Unions.

During the meeting, the trade unions highlighted a range of administrative and operational issues within the Railway Department, employee-related concerns and disruptions affecting passengers.

The unions said their main demand was the removal of the current General Manager of Railways, claiming that they had lost confidence in his administration.

They stressed that the proposed trade union action was not aimed at opposing the Government, but at rebuilding the Railway Department, establishing an efficient and reliable service and improving services for passengers.

The Transport Ministry Secretary reportedly acknowledged that the unions' objective was to improve the Railway Department.

He also said that issuing a letter seeking an explanation from the Secretary of the Station Masters' Association was inappropriate and that such action should not have been taken.

However, the Secretary informed the unions that he needed additional time to make a final decision on the removal of the General Manager of Railways.

The unions rejected the request, saying sufficient time had already been given to address the issue, which they claimed had continued for nearly two years.

They reiterated that they no longer had confidence in the current administration of the Railway General Manager.

Accordingly, the unions informed the Ministry Secretary that unless an acceptable decision is reached on the removal of the General Manager, the planned token strike would go ahead on September 17 and 18.

The discussion ended without an agreement to suspend or cancel the planned trade union action.

Therefore, the Joint Railway Trade Unions' decision to launch the two-day strike remains unchanged for the time being.
