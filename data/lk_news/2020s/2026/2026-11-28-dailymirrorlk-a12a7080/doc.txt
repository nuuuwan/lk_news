International Day for the Elimination of Violence Against Women

November 25 marked the commencement of 16-days of international activism against gender-based violence. Our Prime Minister addressing Parliament, drew attention to the barriers and risks women face when travelling on roads, at workplaces, in politics, in cyberspace and when attempting to leave abusive relationships.

She recalled the memory of the Mirabal sisters, who were assassinated in the Dominican Republic on  November 25 1960, which led to the United Nations General Assembly designating November 25 as the International Day for the Elimination of Violence Against Women in 1999.

We Lankans can relate to the premier’s call,  especially her reference to the fate of the Mirabel sisters. We recall a similar horrific, brutal rape and killing of 18-year-old school-girl Krishanthi Kumaraswamy of Jaffna on September 7, 1996.

Krishanthi was abducted while returning home after her GCE ‘A’ level exam. She was abducted and gang-raped by nine Sri Lankan soldiers. After the soldiers satisfied their lust, they strangled the child and buried her in a shallow pit according to first accused in the case Lance Corporal Somaratne Rajapaksa.

Subsequently the accused killed  three members of her family who came in search of her. Five days later, the bodies of all four victims were discovered in  shallow graves in the Chemmani area of Jaffna.

At the end of the trial in 1998, when the death sentence was passed on him, Lance Corporal Somaratne charged that hundreds of corpses of slain Tamils were secretly buried by the Army in the Chemmani area.  Excavations revealed bodies including children’s school bags among other items. The report on the investigation into the mass grave site at Chemmani in October 2026, however did not definitively establish when or how people died.

The Minister of Justice however claims a separate medico-legal reports - rather than the archaeological report alone - will address cause and manner of death. We sincerely hope the Minister’s words will lead to swift action and help bring closure not only to the issue of mass graves but prove the government’s commitment to actively pushing back against violence against women and girls.

As our Prime Minister emphasised the fight against violence cannot be limited to words. It must lead to quick follow-up action. Let us face it  -- UNFPA studies reveal that 90% of Sri Lankan women and girls continue to face sexual harassment in public transport. Our own Department of Census and Statistics records show one in four women report having experienced physical and/or sexual violence since age 15 (Department of Census & Statistics, 2019)

We are home to the four great religions in the world and a fifth – Judaism -  now entering our shores due to Israeli presence. Building Chabad houses in touristic areas  are attempting to turn our country into a rest and recreation destination  for members of the Israeli defence Force (IDF) who are targetting woman as part of the genocide they are committing in Palestine’s Gaza.

Yet, despite our premier’s clarion call that laws must be strictly enforced to end violence against women, the government she leads is issuing free 30-day tourist visas for these perpetrators of violence against women to enter our country.

Amnesty International reports since 7 October 2023 the IDF have killed over   22,331 women and girls among the 74,237 Palestinians killed between October 7, 2023 and October 1, 2026, making up 30.1 percent of all fatalities.

A big question arises. Can our prime minister expect Lankans to believe she is serious when exhorting our people against committing violence against women, while at the same time the government she leads is extending 30-day free tourist visas to Israeli visitors who may include troops who have served in Gaza? Come, come, madam Prime Minister,  actions more than words are needed to end violence against women.
