Access Engineering exits unravelled Colombo port logistics hub with capital largely intact

Access Engineering PLC expects to recover about Rs.1.45 billion, or over 82 percent of its investment, after shareholders of the US$ 392 million South Asia Commercial and Logistics Hub at the Port of Colombo mutually agreed to abandon the project, a stock filing showed.

The company, which holds 15 percent of the venture alongside China Merchants Port Holdings and the Sri Lanka Ports Authority (SLPA), has invested approximately Rs.1.762 billion. It is expected to receive US$ 3.286 million, or about Rs.1.087 billion, as its share of the settlement refund, plus Rs.365 million in distributable funds, at an indicative Rs.331 to the dollar.

Having recognised its Rs.387 million share of losses in 2025/26, which cut the investment’s carrying value to about Rs.1.38 billion, the Group anticipates no further material loss. However, as the stake is carried at cost in the company’s own books, a company-level loss of approximately Rs.310 million is expected, roughly 10 percent of 2025/26 profit after tax, with the estimates subject to payment-date exchange rates and final settlement arrangements.

The returning cash should ease pressure as funding costs climb, exceeding the Group’s Rs.1.40 billion finance cost for the June quarter, up 167 percent when compared to the same period last year, and lifting its cash holdings of Rs.4.83 billion by about 30 percent.

The outcome nonetheless sets back the country’s logistics ambitions, as the hub, billed in 2023 as South Asia’s largest commercial and logistics complex, had yet to take off three years on. The partners cited “changes in external circumstances” and their respective commercial considerations.

Belt and Road Initiative Sri Lanka Director Maya Majueran attributed the exit to compounding risks, including Indian and US concerns over Chinese involvement in the port, a shorter tax break period linked to IMF fiscal consolidation and slow approvals amid land disputes.

US interest in the port has meanwhile grown, reflecting Colombo’s strategic weight in the Indian Ocean from both a commercial and a security standpoint. Last week, US Ambassador Eric Stern Meyer unveiled a US$ 17 million grant to upgrade the port’s Maritime Single Window and Port Community System, weeks after a US Federal Maritime Commission delegation toured the port. On the security side, the US Navy’s USS Canberra called at Colombo in June, the same month Washington handed the Sri Lanka Navy a US$ 4 million satellite communications system.

However, WIDGE think tank Geopolitical and Diplomatic Analyst HMS Bernard Shaw viewed the exit as a commercially driven realignment, arguing that targeted digital grants spare Sri Lanka from locking port land into 50-year leases without adding to public debt.

Access Engineering’s own port portfolio straddles both camps. Through Access-CHEC JV, its 51 percent-owned partnership with China Harbour Engineering Company, it has worked on the East Container Terminal since 2022, besides completing phase one works at the Adani-led terminal and since has move on on to phase two. Its mechanical engineering arm commissioned equipment at both.

The SLPA announced on Monday that it would call competitive bids for a new logistics hub under a public-private partnership.
