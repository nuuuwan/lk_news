Govt. moves to introduce child-safety clearance for public service

Colombo, Sep. 15 (Daily Mirror) - The Ministry of Women and Child Affairs has taken steps to introduce a mandatory clearance certificate for public-sector employees whose duties involve direct contact with children, Women and Child Affairs Minister Saroja Savithri Paulraj said.

Speaking at a media briefing held at the Department of Government Information, Minister Paulraj said the Cabinet paper proposing the new requirement has already been submitted for approval.

Under the proposed system, individuals seeking public-sector positions involving direct contact with children would be required to obtain a formal clearance certificate from the police or another relevant authority confirming that they have no record of child-related offences or allegations.

The proposed requirement would also apply to existing public-sector employees. According to the Minister, such employees would be required to renew their clearance certificates at relevant stages of their service, including performance reviews, efficiency bar examinations and salary-increment evaluations.

The measure is intended to strengthen safeguards for children by ensuring that individuals entrusted with responsibilities involving them are subject to regular background checks throughout their public service careers.

Minister Paulraj said that preventing individuals with child-related criminal histories from occupying positions that provide direct access to children is an important step toward strengthening child protection mechanisms in Sri Lanka.
