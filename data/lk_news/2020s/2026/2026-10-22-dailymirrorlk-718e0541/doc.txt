ADB approves US$ 100mn in loans for Sri Lanka’s social protection reforms

The Asian Development Bank (ADB) has approved US$ 100 million in loans to strengthen Sri Lanka’s social protection system, support livelihoods for 30,000 low-income households and expand eldercare services.

The financing, announced on October 21, comprises a US$ 50 million policy-based loan and a US$50 million project loan. It will support reforms and investments aimed at helping vulnerable households benefit from the country’s economic recovery.

ADB said poverty and vulnerability to shocks remained high despite the recovery, particularly among women, young people, the elderly, persons with disabilities and communities in rural and disadvantaged regions.

Under the programme, 30,000 households identified through the Aswesuma social registry across six districts will receive support to improve their livelihoods and access employment. Assistance will include livelihood grants, employment and business support, skills development, financial literacy training and access to reliable markets.

The initiative will support the government’s National Empowerment Programme, with women and youth among its beneficiaries.

“Sri Lanka’s economic recovery must translate into meaningful improvements in the lives and livelihoods of vulnerable people,” ADB Country Director for Sri Lanka Shannon Cowlin said.

The financing will also support improvements to the policy and regulatory framework for eldercare and establish five model elderly day care centres in poorer districts.

The centres will provide integrated care and support services while testing approaches that could be expanded nationwide as Sri Lanka responds to the needs of an ageing population. A further component will strengthen the delivery of social protection through improvements to digital platforms, monitoring and evaluation, case management and data governance.

ADB said the programme would also improve institutional arrangements and coordination to provide timely assistance during climate-related shocks and other emergencies.
