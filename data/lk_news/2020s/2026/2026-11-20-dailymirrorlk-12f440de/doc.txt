Parliamentary Committee approves new Immigration Bill

Colombo, Nov. 20 (Daily Mirror) - The Sectoral Oversight Committee on Governance, Justice, and Civil Protection has approved the new Immigration Bill, which was recently presented to Parliament for its First Reading.

Chaired by MP Najith Indika, the committee granted its approval subject to specific Committee Stage amendments mandated by the Supreme Court’s determination on the proposed legislation.

During the committee session, officials explained that the new Bill will replace two outdated pieces of legislation: the Immigrants and Emigrants Act (Chapter 351) and the Passports and Exit Permits Act, No. 53 of 1971. The overarching goal is to modernize Sri Lanka’s immigration framework to align with current international standards and technological advancements.

They further stated that all the directions and amendments required by the Supreme Court following the legal challenge to the Bill had been incorporated into it. In particular, legal safeguards have been included to ensure that the collection and storage of biometric data comply with the Personal Data Protection Act.

Officials also explained that several designations used under the existing law have been updated. Accordingly, titles such as Controller, Deputy Controller and Authorised Officer have been replaced with Controller General, Additional Controller General and Immigration Officers.

The Bill also provides for limited powers to be granted to members of the three Armed Forces and the Coast Guard to prevent illegal entry into the country, where necessary. Such powers may be assigned by the President through a Gazette notification, upon the request of the Minister.

Furthermore, the proposed legislation provides for legal action to be taken in Sri Lanka against human trafficking and organised crimes committed in other countries using forged travel documents, even where the individuals concerned had legally departed from Sri Lanka.

It also provides for cases relating to such organised illegal activities to be heard by the High Court.

In addition, officials pointed out that the Bill establishes the legal framework required to introduce modern facilities, including automated electronic gates (E-Gates), which will allow passengers to pass through immigration without having their passports stamped.
