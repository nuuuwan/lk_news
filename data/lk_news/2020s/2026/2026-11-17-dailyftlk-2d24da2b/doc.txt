Manufacturing, services PMI expand at faster pace in October

Activity in the country’s manufacturing and services sectors expanded at a faster pace in October, according to the Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) released yesterday by its compiler, the Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL).

The PMI is based on a monthly survey of businesses. A reading above the neutral threshold of 50 shows expansion compared with the previous month, and a reading below 50 shows contraction.

The Manufacturing PMI rose to 55.3 in October from 53 in September. The CBSL said the expansion was broad-based, with all sub-indices above the neutral threshold. However, the index was still below the 61 recorded in October 2025.

The New Orders sub-index rose to 55 from 51. The CBSL said the increase was driven by the textiles and wearing apparel and the food and beverages sectors, reflecting seasonal demand. Production rose to 52.2 from 51, also driven by seasonal demand in the textiles and wearing apparel sector.

Employment continued to expand, although at a slower pace, with the sub-index easing to 51.1 from 52.6. Stock of Purchases rose to 56.1 from the neutral 50 recorded in September, in line with the growth in New Orders and Production. Meanwhile, Suppliers’ Delivery Time lengthened further, with the sub-index rising to 66.1 from 63, reflecting increased demand for inputs.

The CBSL said the outlook for manufacturing activities over the next three months is favourable, underpinned by anticipated demand during the upcoming festive season.

The Services PMI, measured by the Business Activity Index, rose to 62.5 in October from 60.5 in September, indicating that services activities expanded at a higher rate. The index was below the 66 recorded in October 2025.

Business Activity was driven primarily by improvements in other personal services, with notable contributions from financial services and goods transportation.

New Businesses continued to grow, although at a slower pace, with the sub-index easing to 59.8 from 60.9. According to the CBSL, the growth mainly reflected expansion in financial services and other personal services, alongside positive contributions from accommodation, food and beverage services, and transportation of goods.

Services Employment rose to 56.1 from 54, supported by recruitment across several sub-sectors to meet rising operational requirements. Meanwhile, Backlogs of Work declined at a faster rate, with the sub-index falling to 48.1 from 49.4.

Expectations for Business Activity over the next three months stayed positive, although the sub-index eased to 74.7 from 76.2. The CBSL said expectations were supported by favourable macroeconomic conditions, anticipated increases in tourist arrivals and the upcoming festive season. However, it warned that prevailing global uncertainties continue to pose downside risks to the outlook.

Sri Lanka’s expansion comes as PMIs in major economies continue to rise. The CBSL release cites S&P Global data as of 16 November, 2026. On that data, manufacturing activity was rising at a slower rate globally and in India, China, the US and the Eurozone, and at a higher rate in the UK. Services activity was rising at a slower rate globally and in China, the US and the Eurozone, and at a higher rate in India and the UK.
