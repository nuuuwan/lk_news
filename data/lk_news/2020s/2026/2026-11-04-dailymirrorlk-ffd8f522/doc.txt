Sri Lanka calls for global action to minimise nitrogen losses

Colombo, Nov. 4 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka has called for global action to minimise nitrogen losses from agricultural fertilisers, stressing that improving their efficient use should take priority over simply reducing fertiliser application.

Deputy Environment Minister Anton Jayakody made this call while addressing a high-level panel discussion at the N2026 International Conference and the Nitrogen Policy Dialogue held in Kyoto, Japan, from November 2 to 6.

Representing Sri Lanka at the conference, organised with the involvement of the United Nations Environment Programme’s Global Partnership on Nutrient Management (GPNM) and the South Asian Nitrogen Hub (SANH), he said that excessive nitrogen losses contribute to global warming and environmental degradation.

He pointed out that nearly 60 per cent of nitrogen fertilisers, including urea used in agriculture worldwide, are lost without being absorbed by crops. These losses enter water bodies or are released into the atmosphere.

Drawing on Sri Lanka’s past experiences, Jayakody said abruptly restricting or banning chemical fertilisers was not a practical solution.

"The priority should not be reducing nitrogen use outright, but minimising the losses of applied nitrogen," he said.

He stressed that countries should adopt science-based technologies and better management practices to improve Nitrogen Use Efficiency (NUE) and reduce environmental damage.

Sri Lanka's contribution to international efforts on sustainable nitrogen management, including the Colombo Declaration in 2019 and the Sustainable Nitrogen Management Resolution (UNEA 5/2), adopted at the UN Environment Assembly in 2022, was also recognised at the conference.

The Deputy Minister said the Government would consult relevant ministries and stakeholders to formally adopt Sri Lanka's National Nitrogen Action Plan and its accompanying roadmap.
