SLPA plans new logistics hub at Colombo Port under PPP model

New project to be carried out through a competitive bidding process, under PPP model, subject to necessary approvals

The Sri Lanka Ports Authority (SLPA) has decided to develop a new logistics hub at the Port of Colombo, under a public-private partnership (PPP) model, after the shareholders decided not to proceed with the previously planned US $ 280 million joint venture.

The earlier project involved China Merchants Port Holdings Company Limited and Access Engineering PLC, together with the SLPA. It was planned through a joint venture company called South Asia Commercial and Logistics Hub (SACLH), established in 2023.

China Merchants Port Holdings Company Limited, through its wholly-owned subsidiary, held a 70 percent stake in the company, while the SLPA and Access Engineering PLC each held a 15 percent stake.

The proposed project was expected to provide around 150,000 square metres of floor space for logistics operations.

However, after considering the external conditions, commercial factors and the interests of the shareholders, a decision was made not to proceed with the project, the SLPA said.

Under the agreed arrangements, the three shareholders will receive refunds of their investments, subject to the regulatory requirements and operational expenses.

The total amount to be refunded by SACLH is approximately US $ 29.8 million, with each shareholder receiving an amount based on its shareholding.

The SLPA said it would continue with plans to develop modern logistics facilities at the Port of Colombo, despite the cancellation of the earlier joint venture.

The new project will be carried out through a competitive bidding process, under the PPP model, subject to the necessary approvals.

The planned facility will cover a large area within the port and support a range of port-related and other logistics services.

The project aims to expand the role of the Port of Colombo beyond container transshipment and develop it into a major maritime and logistics centre. It is expected to provide better services to the shipping lines, cargo owners, logistics companies and other businesses involved in international trade.

The SLPA expects the new approach to attract private investment, make better use of port land and infrastructure and generate long-term revenue.

The authority said the project would also help improve the port’s efficiency and strengthen its position in regional and international trade.

The SLPA said that the decision to stop the earlier joint venture did not mean it had abandoned its plans to develop the logistics facilities. Instead, it said the new approach would better support its long-term plans and commercial goals.

The authority said it remained committed to developing the Port of Colombo into a leading maritime, transshipment and logistics hub in South Asia, while ensuring that future projects are financially sustainable and beneficial to Sri Lanka’s economy. (CPS)
