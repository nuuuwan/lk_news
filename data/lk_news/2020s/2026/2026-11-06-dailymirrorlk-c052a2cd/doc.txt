Democrats’ sweep cools oil, opening window for rupee and rate relief:  FCR

Brent falls about 20% to US$ 93/bbl from late-October peak of US$ 117

Lower fuel import bill seen narrowing trade deficit, easing fuel and power prices

Outlook hinges on progress in US-Iran talks over Strait of Hormuz

The slide in oil prices after the Democrats’ sweep of the US Congress and a hung parliament in Israel is set to trim Sri Lanka’s fuel import bill and ease pressure on the rupee and interest rates, provided progress towards reopening the Strait of Hormuz holds, First Capital Research (FCR) said yesterday.

Following the escalation of US-Israel operations against Iran earlier this year, “2026 has become a year in which geopolitics has driven the pricing of risk,” the research arm of Janashakthi Group-owned First Capital Holdings PLC noted in a flash note.

Brent crude has fallen by about US$ 24 a barrel, or roughly 20.5 percent, from its late-October peak of nearly US$ 117 to US$ 93, a move the research house attributed to markets pricing in a lower probability of further escalation in the Middle East. On Wednesday alone, Brent futures shed 8.7 percent to settle at US$ 94.62 a barrel. Even so, the benchmark remains around 40 percent above the mid-US$ 60s level at which it began the year, before strikes on Iran in late February and the temporary closure of the Strait of Hormuz drove it past US$ 100.

For Sri Lanka, a sustained decline in Brent would reduce fuel import costs, narrow the trade deficit and ease pressure on domestic fuel and electricity prices, while lending support to the rupee, which the research house pointed out has weakened 6.8 percent this year, largely on account of higher energy import costs.

As per CBSL data, the fuel import bill rose 51.5 percent to US$ 4.49 billion in the first nine months of 2026 when compared to the same period last year. That increase accounts for about 57 percent of the US$ 2.7 billion widening in the merchandise trade deficit, which reached US$ 7.9 billion. Relief is yet to reach the pump, however, with the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC) raising Petrol 92 Octane by Rs.10 to Rs.424 a litre and Auto Diesel by Rs.5 to Rs.397 from 2 November.

Lower energy prices would also ease inflation risks and “strengthen the case for reduced pressure on interest rates,” the research house added. The Central Bank raised its Overnight Policy Rate by 100 basis points to 8.75 percent in May, while inflation reached a three-year high of 8 percent in August. The note flagged the Monetary Policy Review on 20 November as the next domestic development to watch.

First Capital Research is maintaining its “stay short, add the belly” fixed-income strategy, which favours short-dated securities while adding exposure to the middle of the yield curve, and continues to prefer sectors most leveraged to lower interest rates and energy costs.

The Colombo bourse rallied yesterday amid optimism over the US election outcome. The All Share Price Index gained 589.18 points, or 2.87 percent, to close above the 21,000 mark at 21,130.30, while the S&P SL20 rose 3.34 percent to 6,029.88, as per CSE data. Turnover reached Rs.5.43 billion, nearly six times the Rs.921 million recorded on Monday. The rupee, by contrast, closed marginally weaker at Rs.332.75/80 against the US dollar on Wednesday, according to dealers.

Meanwhile, the research house expects Democratic control of Congress to reduce the likelihood of further US fiscal expansion, pulling Treasury yields lower and softening the dollar. Closer to home, it argued that the recently implemented 10 percent Section 301 tariff leaves Sri Lanka’s US export market, worth approximately US$ 3 billion, on a stable footing relative to regional peers. With improved sovereign credit metrics, foreign reserves and GDP growth of 4.7 percent in the first half of 2026 adding to these tailwinds, First Capital Research believes “conditions remain favourable for continued foreign participation in government securities and equities.” CBSL data showed gross official reserves at US$ 6.9 billion by end-September.

In the US, the Democrats’ capture of both the House and the Senate at the 3 November midterms enhances congressional oversight of Middle East policy, which, the note observed, could make it “more difficult for the administration to escalate tensions with Iran without congressional scrutiny or support.”

Israel’s 27 October election, meanwhile, left Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc with 53 seats, down 11 from 64 in 2022 and eight short of the 61 required to govern. First Capital Research anticipates protracted coalition formation, reinforcing expectations of a cautious approach to Iran.

However, the note cautioned that “the de-escalation narrative remains contingent on progress in Hormuz negotiations,” with the Qatar- and Pakistan-mediated US-Iran talks seen as the key determinant of Brent’s trajectory.

A prolonged Israeli coalition impasse, renewed friction between the White House and Congress or delays to a Hormuz agreement could keep geopolitical risk premia elevated, it warned.

“A reopening of the Strait would remove a significant geopolitical risk premium from oil markets,” First Capital Research stated. It projected that this could push Brent towards US$ 90 a barrel or below over the medium term, a level only around 3 percent beneath current prices. (NF)
