Namal questions spending of rural development funds

Colombo, Nov. 27 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka Podujana Peramuna (SLPP) National Organiser and MP Namal Rajapaksa has questioned the Government over the slow utilisation of funds allocated for rural development, claiming that only around 3% of the Rs. 35 billion allocation had been spent.

Speaking in Parliament today, Rajapaksa said the failure to utilise allocated funds was preventing development projects from reaching rural communities, despite large sums being announced in the Budget.

He said the Rs. 35 billion allocation was intended for projects including village roads, culverts, self-employment opportunities and entrepreneurship development.

However, only around Rs. 1 billion had been spent during the period covered by the reports, representing approximately 3% of the total allocation, he said.

Referring to reports submitted to the Committee on Public Finance, chaired by MP Harsha de Silva, Rajapaksa said several Government expenditure programmes had recorded utilisation rates of just 7%, 8% and 10% over several months.

He questioned the purpose of allocating billions of rupees in the Budget if the money was not being spent on projects that directly benefited the public.

Rajapaksa also challenged criticism levelled at previous administrations over capital expenditure, referring to remarks by Transport, Highways and Urban Development Minister Bimal Ratnayake regarding capital expenditure performance in 2020 and 2022.

He pointed out that capital expenditure execution had reached 88% in both years, despite the country facing severe economic and political challenges, particularly during 2022 when the economic crisis triggered widespread protests and led to the removal of former President Gotabaya Rajapaksa.

He argued that the current Government should not continue blaming previous administrations or external circumstances for delays in implementing development projects.

Rajapaksa said that strengthening the rural economy was essential to building a strong national economy and urged the Government to ensure that budget allocations were translated into actual development work.

He said the Government should prioritise the effective utilisation of public funds rather than making promises that failed to deliver tangible results to rural communities.
