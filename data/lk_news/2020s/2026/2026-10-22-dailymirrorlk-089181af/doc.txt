CSE tests 19,700 as ASPI reaches new YTD low

The Colombo Stock Exchange closed lower for a fourth consecutive session on 22 October 2026, with both benchmark indices weakening as selling pressure remained broad across the market.

The ASPI declined 209.38 points, or 1.05%, to close at a new YTD low of 19,717.92, while the S&P SL20 fell 38.44 points, or 0.68%, to 5,618.48. The ASPI remained under pressure throughout the session, falling below 19,900 during the morning and 19,800 during the afternoon before closing at the day’s low.

Market turnover remained subdued at LKR 1.10 billion, significantly below the YTD daily average of LKR 3.49 billion, with 64.28 million shares traded. The Banks sector led activity with LKR 249.91 million in turnover, followed closely by Capital Goods at LKR 240.14 million. John Keells Holdings recorded the highest individual counter turnover at LKR 157.39 million.

Market breadth remained heavily negative, with only 40 gainers against 162 decliners, resulting in an advance-decline ratio of 0.25 and confirming that selling pressure remained broad-based.

Foreign investors provided some support during the session, recording a net inflow of approximately LKR 39.22 million, with foreign purchases exceeding sales.

Distilleries was the largest negative contributor to the ASPI, followed by Hatton National Bank, Dialog Axiata, SINS.N and Carson Cumberbatch. SMB Finance provided the strongest positive contribution, followed by C T Holdings, Ceylon Tobacco, MAL.N and KHC.N.

The fourth consecutive decline, new YTD low and weak market breadth indicate that short-term momentum remains under pressure. However, the return of net foreign buying provides a modest positive signal, with the 19,700 region now becoming an important near-term level to watch for signs of market stabilisation.
