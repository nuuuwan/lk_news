Cyclonic storm threat looms over Sri Lanka as heavy rains forecast

Sri Lanka could face severe weather conditions in the coming days, with a developing low-pressure system over the Bay of Bengal threatening to intensify into a cyclonic storm, raising fears of heavy rainfall, flash floods and landslides across several parts of the country, the Asia Pacific Alliance for Disaster Management Sri Lanka (A-PAD SL) has warned.

In a weather advisory issued on Friday (13), A-PAD SL said a low-pressure area had formed over the southeastern Bay of Bengal and was expected to develop into a depression around November 15.

The system could further intensify into a cyclonic storm over the central Bay of Bengal by November 16, according to the advisory, which was based on updates issued by the Department of Meteorology.

The organisation warned that Sri Lanka could experience increased rainfall from November 14 and 15, with heavy showers expected to affect several parts of the country in the following days.

The Northern, North Central, North Western, Western and Eastern provinces are among the areas likely to experience significant rainfall.

A-PAD SL cautioned that the country could face severe weather impacts if the depression moves northward along Sri Lanka’s eastern coastline. However, it noted that the system’s exact trajectory and intensity remained uncertain.

The risk of flash floods and landslides is considered particularly high as the ground across much of the country is already saturated following heavy rainfall experienced over the past week.

The advisory further noted that approximately 17 major reservoirs had reached spill levels last week, while landslide early warnings had been issued for seven districts from November 12.

A-PAD SL warned that intense rainfall within short periods could trigger sudden flash floods, potentially with little or no advance warning. Low-lying urban areas, locations near rivers and canals, reservoir spillway areas and hilly regions were identified as particularly vulnerable.

The developing weather system could also bring thundershowers accompanied by strong gusty winds reaching 60 to 70 kilometres per hour, along with lightning.

Meanwhile, sea conditions off the northern and eastern coasts could become rough, prompting further caution for coastal communities and those engaged in maritime activities.

Urging the public and businesses to take precautionary measures, A-PAD SL advised people to clear drainage systems, move vehicles and valuable equipment to higher ground and avoid travelling through flooded roads.

The organisation also stressed the importance of closely monitoring official weather updates and following evacuation instructions issued by the Disaster Management Centre to minimise the risk to lives and property.
