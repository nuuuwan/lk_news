Search for Sri Lanka’s ‘Coolest Projects’ is on, with ComBank empowering young tech talent

Some of the contestants from last year’s Coolest Projects competition

Advances its commitment to STEM education with a nationwide competition that empowers youth to build tech-driven solutions with global exposure

The search for Sri Lanka’s ‘Coolest Projects’ is in full swing, with Commercial Bank of Ceylon PLC once again reaffirming, its strategic commitment to uplifting national education.

Having established over 450 IT Labs and 200 STEM classrooms island-wide to build the technical capabilities of school children, the Bank is championing this nationwide initiative as the definitive platform for youth to showcase their digital proficiency. As registrations draw to a close, the next stage of mentoring and innovation beckons.

Powered by Commercial Bank in partnership with the STEMUp Educational Foundation, the official local partner of the UK-based Raspberry Pi Foundation, Coolest Projects Sri Lanka 2026 is inviting young creators under 18 years of age to demonstrate how imagination, curiosity and technology can “achieve greater things.” The competition offers participants the opportunity to showcase their digital projects, receive one-to-one guidance from industry professionals and gain exposure on a platform built around international standards of innovation.

With submissions accepted from individuals and teams of up to three members, the competition aims to attract 500 applications from school students across all provinces, opening the door to a diverse range of ideas, experiences and approaches to solving real-world problems through technology.

The focus will soon shift from submissions to selection, as an independent evaluation panel prepares to identify the 100 best-performing entries for the next stage, the Bank said. Entries will be assessed on their creativity, complexity, presentation and usability, before the selected entrants embark on a structured mentoring journey to refine their software and hardware prototypes ahead of the national showcase and awards ceremony, tentatively scheduled for December at Trace Expert City, Colombo.

The possibilities are as varied as the young minds behind the projects. Categories include hardware, Scratch programming, web development, advanced programming and app development, creating space for students with different interests and levels of technical experience to explore their potential. The emphasis is not simply on what participants can build, but on how effectively they can apply technology, solve problems and communicate their ideas.

A defining feature of Coolest Projects is its commitment to making opportunity accessible beyond the country’s main urban centres. More than 60% of participants have come from rural schools outside Colombo, highlighting the potential of the initiative to connect young people with opportunities for learning, mentorship and recognition that might otherwise be beyond their reach.

The impact of that approach was evident at last year’s competition, when two students from Pallebowala National School secured a remarkable victory with a project that impressed the judges through its creativity, problem-solving approach and potential impact, despite the students’ limited access to technology and facilities. Their achievement captured the spirit of Coolest Projects: innovation is not the preserve of those with the greatest resources, but can emerge wherever young people are given the opportunity to explore, experiment and create.

Coolest Projects provides an opportunity for young minds to go beyond consuming technology and begin creating solutions of their own. The Bank said it is particularly encouraged by the program’s reach beyond Colombo, recognising that talent and ingenuity exist in every part of the country and deserve the opportunity to flourish. Through its support, the Bank demonstrates its commitment to advancing digital literacy, education and youth empowerment through meaningful initiatives.

Coolest Projects is a globally recognised program delivered in countries including the United Kingdom, Ireland, Belgium, South Africa and Malaysia. Its international framework gives young Sri Lankan innovators an opportunity to present their work in an environment that celebrates creativity, technical achievement and practical problem-solving, while connecting their aspirations with a wider culture of technology-driven innovation.

STEMUp Educational Foundation Co-Founder Prabhath Mannapperuma said: “Coolest Projects is about giving young people the space and encouragement to turn their curiosity into something tangible. An idea that begins as a simple experiment can develop into a meaningful technology project when students are challenged to think creatively, solve problems and learn from others. The mentoring journey is particularly important because it helps participants refine their ideas and strengthen their projects before presenting them to a wider audience. With the continued support of Commercial Bank, we are working to ensure that young innovators from across Sri Lanka have the opportunity to be seen, heard and encouraged to pursue their potential.”

Commercial Bank’s support for Coolest Projects Sri Lanka forms part of its broader ‘ComBank Akshara’ corporate social responsibility initiative, which seeks to advance STEM inclusion, digital literacy and youth empowerment. By supporting access to professional mentorship and opportunities for practical technology learning, the Bank aims to help bridge the digital divide and expand the horizons of students from both urban and rural communities.

The partnership also builds on a wider history of collaboration between Commercial Bank and the STEMUp Educational Foundation, including joint Day of Code initiatives conducted in 2021, 2022, 2024 and 2025, and previous Coolest Projects showcases in Colombo in 2024 and 2025. Beyond financial support, the Bank is participating in the execution of the 2026 program, including event organisation, the evaluation process and support on the showcase day.

As the registration phase approaches its close, the spotlight now turns to the ideas that will emerge from classrooms, homes and communities across Sri Lanka. For the young people taking part, Coolest Projects offers more than a competition: it is an opportunity to test their ingenuity, learn from industry professionals and demonstrate how technology can turn a promising idea into a practical solution. In December, the country will discover which projects stand out, but the journey of discovery has already begun.
