Drought tests humanity as wildlife struggles for survival

Colombo, Sep. 13 (Daily Mirror) - As dry conditions intensify across the country amid the effects of El Niño, the Ministry of Environment has warned of the growing challenges faced by both people and wildlife, highlighted that compassion and responsible human behaviour are essential to protect animals during the difficult period.

The Ministry said the arid conditions were a shared natural challenge affecting both humans and wildlife, prompting the Department of Wildlife Conservation (DWC), together with institutions affiliated with the Ministry, to implement emergency measures to support animals affected by the drought.

“Thirst and hunger are burdens shared by both humans and beasts. During these harsh dry spells, ensuring that wildlife is kept out of harm’s way is a responsibility shared by all,” the Ministry said.

According to the Ministry, water is being transported by bowsers to reservoirs inside national parks and protected areas, while artificial waterholes are being maintained and fodder provided to wildlife facing food shortages.

The Ministry also said that, with the support of the tri-forces, continuous efforts were being made to rescue stranded wildlife and provide urgent veterinary care to wild elephants and other animals injured as a result of human activities.

It stressed that wildlife was facing additional dangers from gunshots, explosive devices such as ‘hakka patas’, illegal electric fences and poisoning.

“While the drought is a natural challenge, the suffering caused by human cruelty is preventable. Shooting animals, setting up illegal electric fences or laying poison can turn an already difficult situation into a tragedy,” the Ministry said.

The Ministry urged the public, particularly communities living close to wildlife habitats, to refrain from harming animals and instead allow them to move safely in search of water and food.

It said the current situation was also a test of society’s humanity and its willingness to protect species that share the island.

“Far harsher than the drought in nature is the drought of empathy in human hearts. The true denizens of the wild also have a rightful place on this Earth,” the Ministry said.

The Ministry called on the public to support efforts to protect wildlife during the dry spell, stressing that safeguarding animals was not merely a conservation responsibility but also a reflection of the country’s compassion and respect for life.
