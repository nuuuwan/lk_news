Are elephants truly essential to preserve the Perahera tradition?

The Kandy Esala Perahera has deep roots extending over 1,700 years, while the present-day procession associated with the Sacred Tooth Relic developed several centuries ago. Elephants have long been an important and magnificent feature of this tradition.

As children, many of us counted the elephants in the procession and believed that their number reflected the success of the Perahera. However, times have changed. Society is now more conscious of animal welfare, and the principle that change is inevitable must also be accepted.

Elephants are often chained during the Perahera for safety and control, but this restricts their freedom of movement and may cause injury. The organisers naturally fear that stricter laws and penalties could affect the future use of elephants in the procession.

The Diyawadana Nilame has stated that at least 70 elephants are required to stage a successful Perahera. But this raises an important question: Are elephants truly essential to preserve the tradition, or have we become attached to a feature that can no longer be justified under modern standards of animal welfare?

Traditions must be respected, but they must also adapt. The Perahera could continue with fewer elephants, or even without them. Robotic elephants, already developed in India, may eventually provide a practical and humane alternative.

The true greatness of the Kandy Perahera lies in its religious and cultural significance—not simply in the number of elephants that parade through its streets. Perhaps the time has come to accept change while preserving the dignity and spirit of this ancient tradition.
