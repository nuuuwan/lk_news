Hello Kitty fronts Sri Lanka campaign against plastic waste

Sri Lanka’s battle against plastic pollution received an unusual boost this week with the arrival of Hello Kitty, as Japanese and Sri Lankan authorities teamed up to target environmental awareness among schoolchildren.

More than 150 students gathered at a special programme organised by the Central Environmental Authority (CEA), where the globally recognised character helped launch a trilingual environmental campaign centred on reducing plastic waste and encouraging sustainable habits among younger generations.

The initiative is being carried out through a partnership between the Japan International Cooperation Agency and the CEA under a wider project aimed at strengthening Sri Lanka’s plastic waste management capacity.

At the heart of the campaign is the “Plastic Smart Song,” produced in Sinhala, Tamil and English with the support of Sanrio Co., Ltd., the company behind Hello Kitty. Organisers hope the song and related school outreach activities will help turn environmental messaging into something more accessible and memorable for children.

Rather than relying solely on traditional awareness campaigns, authorities appear to be adopting pop culture and entertainment-based engagement to influence behavioural change, especially among younger audiences increasingly exposed to consumer-driven lifestyles and rising plastic use.

Officials attending the launch stressed that plastic pollution remains one of Sri Lanka’s growing environmental concerns, with mounting pressure on waste management systems and ecosystems.

The programme also aligns with the Government’s broader “Clean Sri Lanka Initiative,” which seeks to improve environmental protection and public participation in sustainability efforts.

CEA Chairman Prof. Tilak Hewawasam, Director General Kapila Rajapaksha, senior representatives from JICA, the Ministry of Education and environmental officials attended the event.

Hello Kitty is expected to visit several schools during the week as part of the awareness drive, with organisers hoping the campaign will encourage students to rethink everyday plastic consumption and influence practices within their homes and communities.
