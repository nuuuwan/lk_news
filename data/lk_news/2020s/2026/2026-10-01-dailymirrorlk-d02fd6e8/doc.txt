Africa’s industrial future takes shape in Kenya with US$16 Billion oil refinery investment

Oct. 1 (Daily Mirror) - A significant chapter in East Africa’s industrial transformation opened yesterday with the groundbreaking of the US$16 billion Dangote East Africa Petroleum Refinery and Petrochemicals Special Economic Zone at Mokowe in Lamu County, Kenya.

The landmark ceremony brought together Kenyan President William Ruto, Ugandan President Yoweri Kaguta Museveni, Benin President Romuald Wadagni, Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed, Togo President Jean-Lucien Savi de Tové and former Nigerian President Olusegun Obasanjo, alongside Africa’s richest person and leading Nigerian industrialist Aliko Dangote and other senior dignitaries.

The proposed refinery by Dangote with a planned processing capacity of 700,000 barrels of crude oil per day, represents one of the most ambitious industrial investments undertaken in the region. The project is designed to serve not only Kenya but the wider East African market, reducing dependence on imported refined petroleum products, strengthening regional energy security and promoting greater value addition within Africa.

Dangote Petroleum Refinery in Lagos, Nigeria—Africa’s largest oil refinery, with a processing capacity of about 650,000 barrels per day—demonstrating the scale of industrial investment that African private enterprise is increasingly capable of delivering.

President Ruto described the project as a generational undertaking and placed it within a much broader vision for African industrialisation. He argued that Africa’s vast natural resources should become the foundation of African industry rather than simply being exported for processing elsewhere.

Ruto said the project would turn Lamu into a major industrial and energy hub and emphasised that large African projects must move beyond announcements to actual delivery. He also held Dangote to his commitment that the refinery would be completed within 40 months, stressing the importance of firm timelines in delivering major investments.

Dangote, meanwhile, described the refinery as another demonstration that Africa has the capacity to conceive, finance and execute industrial projects of global scale. Drawing on the experience of his refinery in Nigeria, he said the Lamu project should help reduce East Africa’s dependence on imported petroleum products while creating employment, transferring skills and building industrial capacity. He pledged to deliver the refinery within 40 months.

Uganda”s President Museveni welcomed the investment but stressed that East Africa would require several refineries as its economies and populations expand. He said the Dangote refinery should complement planned refinery developments elsewhere in the region, including Uganda and Tanzania, rather than compete with them.

Museveni’s broader message was equally significant: Africa must increasingly process its own natural resources instead of continuing the traditional economic model of exporting raw materials and importing finished products. Regional integration, industrialisation and value addition, he argued, are essential if Africa is to retain more of the wealth generated from its resources.

The scale of the gathering itself carried an important message. The presence of leaders from East and West Africa demonstrated that major investments of this magnitude are increasingly being viewed not merely as national projects, but as part of a wider African economic transformation.

Former Sri Lankan Ambassador and businessman Kananathan, who attended the groundbreaking ceremony, said the development should also carry an important message for Sri Lanka.

“For Sri Lanka, the lesson is equally clear. As Africa industrialises, we should not remain spectators. Sri Lankan enterprises should position themselves early and become partners in Africa’s next phase of growth,” Kananathan said.

He noted that Africa is increasingly shifting towards value addition, manufacturing, energy development, infrastructure and regional supply chains, creating opportunities that extend far beyond conventional trade.

For Sri Lankan businesses looking for the next frontier of international growth, Africa deserves to be firmly on the radar. The opportunities are emerging now, and Sri Lankan companies should position themselves before these markets become considerably more competitive,” Kananathan said.
