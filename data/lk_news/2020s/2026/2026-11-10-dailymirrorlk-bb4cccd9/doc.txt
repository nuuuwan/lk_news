Ravi K urges HSBC to reconsider closure of Sri Lanka operations

Colombo, Nov. 10 (Daily Mirror) - Former Finance Minister and MP Ravi Karunanayake has appealed to HSBC’s global leadership to reconsider its decision to close its Global Service Centre in Sri Lanka warning that the move could affect thousands of livelihoods and limit employment opportunities for young people.

In a letter addressed to the Group Chairman and Group Chief Executive of HSBC Holdings plc, Karunanayake called for an urgent dialogue to explore ways of retaining the bank’s operations in Sri Lanka before the planned relocation becomes irreversible.

Karunanayake recalled that he had initiated discussions to bring the investment to Sri Lanka while serving as Minister of Trade and Commerce between 2001 and 2004.

He said HSBC’s decision to establish its operations in Colombo had created significant opportunities for Sri Lankan employees, helping thousands develop internationally recognised skills and build professional careers over the past two decades.

The operations initially began at the World Trade Centre in Colombo in 2004 before expanding to facilities at Union Place and Mireka Tower.

According to Karunanayake, the centre had employed around 4,500 Sri Lankans at its peak. However, HSBC’s notification dated October 7, 2026, identified a workforce of 1,659 employees as of September 30, 2026.

He noted that HSBC had stated that the proposed relocation followed a global operating-model review and was not a reflection of the performance of its Sri Lankan company.

Karunanayake said this provided a reason to explore alternatives rather than proceed with the closure.

He also highlighted the potential impact on employees and their families, pointing out that the loss of employment could affect their children’s education, housing commitments and responsibilities towards elderly parents.

While acknowledging HSBC’s responsibilities towards its shareholders, customers and employees, he said concerns relating to operating costs, workforce availability and competitiveness should be discussed to identify possible solutions.

Moreover, Karunanayake, who has also served as Minister of Foreign Affairs and Minister of Finance, said he was prepared to work with the Government, relevant authorities and industry partners to help address challenges and ensure that HSBC could continue its operations in Sri Lanka.

He stressed that retaining the investment was a national responsibility that went beyond political differences.

Emphasising the importance of employment opportunities for the younger generation, he said HSBC had provided young Sri Lankans with valuable exposure to international business practices, professional development and opportunities to work across cultures.

He urged HSBC’s global leadership to reopen the decision and hold an urgent, time-bound discussion on possible changes to costs, recruitment, training, infrastructure and policy that could help retain its Sri Lankan operations.

As such, Karunanayake said a renewed commitment by HSBC would not only protect jobs but also send a positive message about Sri Lanka’s efforts to rebuild its economy and attract investment.
