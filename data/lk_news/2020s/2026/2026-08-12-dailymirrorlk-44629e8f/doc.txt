Singer Sri Lanka 1Q profit rises 44% as sales surge

Singer (Sri Lanka) PLC reported a 44 percent year-on-year increase in group net profit for the June quarter, supported by strong revenue growth across its main business segments, although higher borrowing and direct interest costs weighed on the gains.

Group profit after tax rose to Rs. 2.24 billion for the three months ended June 30, 2026, from Rs. 1.55 billion a year earlier, while revenue climbed 41 percent to Rs. 38.72 billion from Rs. 27.55 billion.

Operating profit increased at a faster 59 percent to Rs. 4.80 billion, with gross profit rising 42 percent to Rs. 12.42 billion.

The stronger operating performance, however, came alongside a sharp increase in financing-related expenses. Direct interest costs rose 77 percent to Rs. 2.37 billion, while finance costs increased 54 percent to Rs. 1.21 billion. With finance income falling 33 percent, net finance costs more than doubled to Rs. 971.8 million from Rs. 437.3 million.

Selling and administrative expenses also increased 32 percent to Rs. 7.59 billion, while impairment losses on trade and other receivables rose 56 percent to Rs. 187.3 million.

Despite the higher costs, group profit before tax advanced 49 percent to Rs. 3.47 billion. Income tax expenses increased 58 percent to Rs. 1.23 billion.

Profit attributable to Singer shareholders rose to Rs. 2.15 billion from Rs. 1.49 billion, while earnings per share increased to Rs. 1.84 from Rs. 1.28.

Singer’s core retail businesses recorded broad-based sales growth during the quarter. Home appliances remained the group’s largest revenue contributor, with turnover rising 39 percent to Rs. 13.60 billion, while financial services revenue increased nearly 50 percent to Rs. 7.18 billion. Digital products revenue grew 20 percent to Rs. 6.93 billion and consumer electronics increased 24 percent to Rs. 3.64 billion.

The strongest percentage increase came from the automobile and other segment, where turnover more than doubled to Rs. 4.72 billion from Rs. 1.89 billion. Its profit before tax increased to Rs. 293.2 million from Rs. 76.0 million.

Financial services generated the largest segment profit before tax at Rs. 1.93 billion, up from Rs. 1.23 billion, followed by home appliances at Rs. 1.57 billion compared with Rs. 977.6 million a year earlier.

Singer’s balance sheet also expanded considerably. Group assets stood at Rs. 188.80 billion at end-June, up from Rs. 131.49 billion a year earlier and Rs. 181.79 billion at end-March. Trade and other receivables due within one year rose to Rs. 94.23 billion from Rs. 56.68 billion a year earlier, while inventories increased to Rs. 33.12 billion from Rs. 23.34 billion.

Interest-bearing loans and borrowings due within one year increased to Rs. 58.05 billion from Rs. 36.30 billion a year earlier.

Total group liabilities reached Rs. 165.33 billion, compared with Rs. 114.34 billion at end-June 2025. The expansion in working capital continued to pressure cash generation. The group recorded a net operating cash outflow of Rs. 4.10 billion during the quarter, compared with an outflow of Rs. 5.18 billion a year earlier, largely reflecting increases in receivables. Financing activities generated Rs. 3.82 billion in net cash, including a Rs. 4.57 billion increase in customer deposit liabilities. Singer declared a first interim dividend of Rs. 0.50 per share, amounting to Rs. 583.8 million, for 2026/27. The company also completed the Rs. 630 million acquisition of premises in Piliyandala in July to expand its local manufacturing business.
