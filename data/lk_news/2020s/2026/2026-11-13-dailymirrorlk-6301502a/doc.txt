Budget 2027 may drive doctors overseas, warns medical alliance chief

Consultant Physician Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa has warned that the failure of Budget 2027 to provide meaningful relief to professionals, including doctors, could accelerate the exodus of medical professionals from the country.

Dr. Sanjeewa, President of the Medical Professionals' Alliance for Medical and Civil Rights, said the budget had failed to make substantial allocations for the development of the education and healthcare sectors, while the relief offered to professionals remained minimal.

He said professionals, particularly doctors, had not received the expected salary increases, improved allowances, vehicle permits or better hardship and transport allowances.

He warned that the situation could encourage more young doctors and consultant physicians to seek employment overseas, particularly as salaries continued to fall behind the rising cost of living.

Dr. Sanjeewa also criticised the budget for lacking a clear strategy to attract domestic and foreign investment or develop an innovation-driven economy, saying the country's deteriorating economic situation could further discourage professionals from remaining in Sri Lanka.

He further pointed out that allocations for education and healthcare had not increased substantially, while the Government had failed to complete several programmes initiated by the Ministry of Health since 2024.

He said the budget had also failed to introduce practical new proposals to address the existing challenges in these sectors.

Dr. Sanjeewa warned that 2027 and 2028 could be particularly difficult years for professionals, with the country facing the risk of losing more of its skilled workforce.
