Hong Kong-based Transwell submits zero-treasury turnaround proposal with strict KPIs for SriLankan Airlines

Colombo, Aug. 8 (Daily Mirror) - Hong Kong-based private equity firm Transwell Corporation Limited has submitted a comprehensive private investor-led turnaround and restructuring proposal for loss-making SriLankan Airlines, outlining a strict milestone-based roadmap to achieve sustainable profitability within 24 to 36 months.

The strategy aims to completely shift all financial liabilities away from the state treasury while steering the national carrier toward commercial viability.

The proposal was formally submitted by Transwell Chief Executive Officer Christopher Radhakrishan through corporate advisory channels for review by Dr Hans Wijayasuriya and the government’s restructuring committee.

Under the proposed framework, incoming investors would assume full responsibility for capital requirements from day one, covering operational expenses, fleet expansion, and growth initiatives without relying on treasury support or state-backed debt. The financial structure suggests the government would initially retain full ownership of the airline by placing its shares into an escrow trust, with a convertible note instrument subsequently governing the transfer of a minimum 49 per cent equity stake to the investors over a 10-year operational term.

Addressing the carrier’s structural challenges, Radhakrishan emphasised that the airline retains core strategic value despite severe headwinds. He noted that while global travel demand remains strong, the airline's gap between revenue per available seat mile and cost per available seat mile has widened significantly due to high fuel prices and legacy inefficiencies.

"Sri Lankan is not an asset in decline, but rather a company with significant unlocked potential," Radhakrishan stated, adding that his analysis indicates the carrier is "underperforming on ancillary revenue" and "suffering from low asset utilisation".

He stressed that the turnaround plan relies on specific, measurable actions to generate positive cash flow quickly, rather than a market miracle.

The operational roadmap utilises a milestone-based funding mechanism where investor capital is held in escrow and released only when the airline meets defined key performance indicators across a phased recovery timeline. During the initial year of operational takeover, the management team must secure an on-time performance rating of at least 85 per cent over a rolling 90-day period, while reducing its cost per available seat kilometre by 5 per cent through aggressive renegotiations of supplier contracts.

To rectify low asset utilisation, the second phase of the turnaround focuses heavily on fleet lease restructuring. The incoming team must push active narrow-body aircraft utilisation to an average flying time of at least 12 hours per day.

Furthermore, the airline will be required to retire legacy sub-fleets to create a unified aircraft mix and slash unscheduled aircraft on-ground maintenance delays to below 1.5 per cent of total scheduled flights.

By the third year, the strategic focus transitions to optimising the Colombo hub, targeting a 15 per cent year-over-year increase in connecting passenger traffic and a system-wide passenger load factor of at least 80 per cent across all core routes. The final phase outlines a calculated approach to route expansion, dictating that at least 75 per cent of all newly launched routes must reach a positive operating margin within 180 days of launch. The airline is also expected to boost workforce productivity by 10 per cent and double its incoming connecting revenue generated through international airline alliances.

Founded in May 2002, Transwell Corporation Limited invests across global private equity markets with a focus on smart cities, digitalisation, and aviation infrastructure. The firm’s current aviation portfolio includes master planning and funding development for a Southeast Asian airport and an adjacent "AeroCity" project with an initial phase quantum exceeding 2 billion euros.
