International Finance Corporation taps HSBC for rupee funding in Asia first

HSBC Sri Lanka provides six-year, fixed-rate rupee facility equivalent to US$ 5mn

Facility helps fund IFC’s 10-year rupee loan to a Sri Lankan renewable energy company

The International Finance Corporation (IFC) has secured its first rupee borrowing line in Sri Lanka from HSBC, helping fund a 10-year loan equivalent to US$10 million for a local renewable energy company and allowing the borrower to repay in rupees.

The World Bank Group yesterday said its Treasury had arranged the six-year, fixed-rate facility, equivalent to US$5 million, between IFC and HSBC Sri Lanka. It described the transaction as the first of its kind in Asia.

The HSBC facility, together with additional rupee funding arranged by the Treasury, supports IFC’s loan to what the World Bank Group described as one of Sri Lanka’s largest renewable energy companies. The group did not name the borrower or disclose the interest rates on either facility.

The renewable energy loan is IFC’s longest-tenor Sri Lankan rupee loan to date and its first rupee-denominated infrastructure loan. It also marks IFC’s first infrastructure investment in Sri Lanka since the country’s economic crisis.

For businesses that earn in rupees, borrowing in the same currency removes the risk that a weaker rupee will drive up the local cost of servicing a foreign currency loan. The World Bank Group said it would build on the HSBC transaction to expand access to longer-term rupee financing for private investment in Sri Lanka. “Access to long-term local currency financing can help businesses invest, grow and create jobs without taking on foreign exchange risk they may not be able to manage,” said World Bank Group Director of Treasury Client Solutions and Structuring Keshav Gaur.

“This first borrowing line in Asia expands the financing solutions we can bring to emerging markets, helping channel private investment to sectors that can drive growth and economic opportunity.”

HSBC Sri Lanka Chief Executive Mark Surgenor said the bank would explore further ways to expand access to longer-term rupee financing through its partnership with IFC.

The Sri Lankan transaction follows local currency borrowing lines IFC established with Citi in Kenya in 2024 and South Africa in 2026. IFC has committed more than US$ 33 billion in local currency financing across 71 currencies over the past decade, the World Bank Group said.
