Cashback at Till: Convenience at What Cost?

With reference to the proposal by Mr. Sumith Silva, a regular contributor to the Daily Mirror, to introduce a “cashback at till” system in our supermarkets, similar to the system in the UK (Letter to the Editor, August 24, 2026), I wish to highlight some potential drawbacks that merit consideration.

While the system may offer customers the convenience of obtaining cash while making a purchase, its implementation could present several challenges for supermarkets:

Card-processing charges could potentially apply to the total transaction, including the cash withdrawn.

2. Cash-flow constraints: Frequent cashback transactions could reduce the cash available with the cashier for providing change.

3. Security concerns: Holding larger amounts of cash with the cashier could increase the risk of theft and other security incidents.

4. Longer queues: Cashback transactions may take additional time, particularly during peak hours, potentially causing delays for other customers.

5. Accounting and reconciliation: Supermarkets would need to separately account for the value of goods sold and cash dispensed, adding to administrative and reconciliation requirements.

6. Risk of fraud and misuse: The facility could potentially be misused through stolen cards

7. Tax and reporting considerations: Appropriate systems would be required to distinguish retail sales from cash-dispensing transactions for accounting, auditing and regulatory purposes.

Therefore, although “cashback at till” may appear attractive from a customer-convenience perspective, its wider implications should be carefully evaluated. The additional costs, cash-management difficulties, security risks and administrative burdens on supermarkets should be considered before such a system is introduced.
