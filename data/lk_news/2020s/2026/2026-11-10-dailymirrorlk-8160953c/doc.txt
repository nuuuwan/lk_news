ADB approves $300 million for Sri Lanka’s water and sanitation sector

Colombo, Nov. 10 (Daily Mirror) - The Asian Development Bank (ADB) has approved two projects worth a total of US$300 million to improve water supply and sanitation services across Sri Lanka, with more than 1.3 million people expected to benefit from improved access to safe and reliable drinking water.

The funding includes a US$200 million Climate-Resilient Sustainable Water Supply and Sanitation Project and a US$100 million second subprogram of the Water Supply and Sanitation Reform Program.

The US$200 million project will improve access to safe water in the Western and Southern provinces while upgrading sewerage and sanitation services for nearly 250,000 people in Jaffna in the Northern Province.

Under the project, new climate-resilient water treatment plants, transmission and distribution networks, and storage reservoirs will be constructed in Colombo and Lunugamwehera.

It will also fund wastewater treatment facilities, sewerage networks and pumping stations in Jaffna.

The initiative will further focus on reducing water losses, increasing the use of renewable energy and strengthening the institutional capacity of the National Water Supply and Drainage Board (NWSDB), while encouraging greater private sector participation in the water sector.

Meanwhile, the US$100 million second subprogram will build on reforms introduced under the first phase of the Water Supply and Sanitation Reform Program, strengthening governance, improving service delivery and attracting private sector financing for future investments.

The reforms will focus on improving water resource management, water safety, sanitation management and preparedness for natural hazards, while enhancing the operational and financial performance of institutions in the sector.

Key measures include reforms to the National Water Resources Policy, water tariff revisions, the adoption of a resilience roadmap for the water supply and sanitation sector, and improvements to the financial sustainability and corporate performance of the NWSDB.

ADB Country Director for Sri Lanka Shannon Cowlin said access to safe, reliable and sustainable water and sanitation services was essential for public health and economic growth.

She said the projects would support the Government's efforts to strengthen water sector governance, improve service delivery and enhance the resilience and financial sustainability of sector institutions.

The investments are also expected to support Sri Lanka's broader economic reform agenda, promote green economic growth and strengthen the country's capacity to withstand natural disasters and extreme weather events.
