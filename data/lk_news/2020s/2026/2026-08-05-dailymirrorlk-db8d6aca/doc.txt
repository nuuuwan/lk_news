Customer-friendly hotlines a must!

Before automation, company phones were answered by human beings within 3 rings to build trust, show respect for time, and to stop callers from hanging up.

However, the digital age has reshaped this notion entirely with designated hotlines provided by many organisations includ­ing banks and  gov­ern­ment offices where the automated voice calls answered  by lengthy recor­ded mes­sages -- “this call will be recorded to ensure quality service”,  “ your call will be answered by one of our customer service representatives  or “your call is important to us, currently all our customer service representatives  are busy ,  the next available  agent  will assist you” .

But this next available agent never answers and keeps the customer on line for unreasonably long time whilst the companies promote their products.

In many instances cus­tom­ers are effect­ively pay­ing for the privilege of listen­ing to the company’s lengthy product and services because they con­tinue to incur mobile or tele­phone charges while they keep on hold,  sac­ri­ficing their valu­able time.

Blindly following these automated response trends typically setup without consideration can lead to inefficiency, overwhelm organisations, aban­doned calls due to cus­tomer frus­tra­tion, losing a vital business deal and unne­ces­sary con­ges­tion in call centres.

Ironically,  if the calls are recorded to ensure quality service don’t these companies analyse the waiting time exper­i­enced by cus­tom­ers, how many calls have been aban­doned due to the long wait­ing time and if the calls are important to these very com­pan­ies. Why don’t they ser­i­ously consider intro­du­cing more cus­tomer-friendly sys­tems that place greater value on the time of the very people who keep their busi­nesses alive and those who have genu­ine griev­ances to be sorted?
