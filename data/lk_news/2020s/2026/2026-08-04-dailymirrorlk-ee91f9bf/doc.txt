Public urged to beware of social media extortion scams

Colombo, August 4 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka Computer Emergency Readiness Team (Sri Lanka CERT) has warned the public of a fresh surge in online extortion scams carried out through Facebook Messenger, Instagram and WhatsApp, with several victims already reporting incidents in recent weeks.

According to Sri Lanka CERT, preliminary investigations suggest that a group of fraudsters believed to be operating from India may be behind many of the scams, although authorities also suspect that some Sri Lankans could now be involved.

The scammers typically initiate contact through social media platforms such as Instagram, Facebook Messenger or Telegram, often using fake profiles posing as attractive young women. Victims are first sent a simple greeting before the conversation gradually develops over several days to gain their trust.

Investigators said the fraudsters often add the victim's siblings, relatives or friends to their own social media contacts before making direct contact, enabling them to exert additional pressure later.

Once the victim becomes emotionally engaged, the scammers send provocative or explicit images before persuading them to continue the conversation through a WhatsApp video call. During the call, victims are encouraged to switch on their camera and remove their clothes, while screenshots or recordings are secretly captured.

In some reported cases, victims were blackmailed even without appearing naked, as scammers used screenshots of suggestive conversations and video calls to threaten them.

The fraudsters then send the screenshots or recordings to the victim's relatives or friends through social media and demand money to prevent wider distribution. Initial demands typically range from Rs. 5,000 to Rs. 10,000 but quickly escalate, with some victims reportedly losing hundreds of thousands of rupees after making repeated payments.

Sri Lanka CERT said earlier cases involved money being transferred to Indian bank accounts. However, recent complaints indicate victims are now being instructed to deposit money into personal bank accounts in Sri Lanka, raising suspicions that local accomplices may also be involved.

Many victims reportedly sought help from Sri Lanka CERT only after the extortion became continuous and financially overwhelming.

Authorities have urged social media users to exercise extreme caution when responding to messages, friend requests or video calls from unknown individuals. The public is advised to verify identities before engaging in private conversations, avoid sharing intimate images or participating in compromising video calls, and immediately report any attempted extortion to Sri Lanka CERT or the Sri Lanka Police.
