Sri Lankan Scholar Teaching Hindi at University of Michigan

In a historic appointment for Sri Lankan academia, a Sri Lankan educator is teaching Hindi at a foreign university for the first time. Last October Ridma Nishadinee Lansakkara, of the National Institute of Language Education and Training (NILET), received an appointment to the faculty of the University of Michigan, a first-class overseas institution as a Hindi instructor.

Ridma’s journey from a curious child in Kurunegala to a faculty member at a prestigious American university is a testament to dedication and innovative scholarship. It is doubly remarkable, as Hindi academia is a highly competitive arena, even for native speakers.

Ridma’s passion for Hindi began not in a classroom, but through the vibrant world of Indian cinema and music. Growing up in a multilingual Sinhala and Tamil-speaking environment, she was drawn to the language’s expressiveness. “That early curiosity became the foundation of my academic journey,” she recalls.

Her formal training began at Maliyadewa Girls’ College in Kurunegala, continuing at the Hindi Sansthan Sri Lanka. Her exceptional abilities earned her a scholarship from the Indian government in 2009 to study at the Central Institute of Hindi in Agra. She later graduated top of her class with a Special Degree in Hindi from the University of Kelaniya in 2013. Subsequently, in 2017 she obtained an MPhil in Hindi from Kelaniya, at which institution she is, currently, completing her PhD thesis.

Prior to her international appointment, Ridma built a robust career at home. She served as a Temporary and Visiting Lecturer at the University of Kelaniya from 2013 to 2018. In 2018, she joined NILET as an Academic and Research Officer, simultaneously serving as a Visiting Lecturer in Hindi at the Universities of Colombo, Ruhuna, and Sabaragamuwa.

“I got to know her as a subordinate when I served as Chair of NILET,” noted Vinod Moonesinghe, a former colleague. “Her commitment to language pedagogy was always evident.”

Faced with limited opportunities within the local university system, Ridma began applying internationally. Her persistent scholarship, publications, and a student-centred teaching philosophy which emphasises cultural context, role-play, and real-world application, ultimately secured her the position at the University of Michigan.

The transition presented challenges, from adapting to a new academic culture to being far from family. “Professionally, I had to adapt my teaching to students with diverse backgrounds and learning styles,” she states. “Over time, I learned to navigate these differences and transform them into strengths.”

Ridma views her role as both a privilege and a responsibility. “I want to show that Sri Lanka contributes meaningfully to global scholarship and that our multilingual heritage enriches conversations about South Asia,” she says. She considers the appointment a milestone for Hindi studies in Sri Lanka, particularly as a non-native speaker.

She remains a passionate advocate for foreign language education for Sri Lankan students, stressing that languages create pathways to international education, careers, and cross-cultural empathy. Her advice to students is to “start early, be consistent, and use media you genuinely enjoy.”

Ridma extends her gratitude to her family, her sole mentor in Sri Lanka, Senior Professor Upul Ranjith Hewawitanagamage, and her husband for their unwavering support. She also thanks the Indian Council for Cultural Relations (ICCR) for funding her pivotal studies in India.

Looking ahead, she envisions stronger institutional support and more international exchanges for language education in Sri Lanka. “After serving at the University of Michigan for some time, I hope to return to my home country and contribute to uplifting the field of language education,” she affirms. “I love my country deeply, and I want to give back to the system that shaped me.”

Her story underscores a powerful message: that curiosity and persistence, rooted in Sri Lanka’s rich linguistic landscape, can indeed open doors across continents.

Ridma would be happy to help and share her experiences with anyone interested in learning Hindi or other foreign languages. She may be contacted at Rnishadinee8822@gmail.com
