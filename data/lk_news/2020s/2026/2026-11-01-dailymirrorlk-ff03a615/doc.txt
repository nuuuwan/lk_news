CAA detects 98 suspected violations in islandwide lubricant raids

Colombo, Nov. 1 (Daily Mirror) - The Consumer Affairs Authority (CAA) has detected 98 suspected violations of regulations governing the importation, manufacture and sale of lubricants and grease during islandwide raids conducted from October 27 to 30.

The raids were carried out across several parts of the country to check whether traders were complying with Direction No. 88 issued by the CAA, which sets out legal requirements for the importation, manufacture and sale of lubricants and grease used in motor vehicles.

Retail outlets selling engine oil, gear oil, other lubricants and grease were inspected, while several products suspected of being linked to violations were taken into custody as case productions.

Under Direction No. 88, published in Extraordinary Gazette No. 2327/36 dated April 12, 2023, under Section 10(1) of the Consumer Affairs Authority Act No. 9 of 2003, a valid agreement with the Government of Sri Lanka is required to import or manufacture lubricants and grease.

The direction also requires the relevant government agreement number to be printed on the packaging or displayed on a permanent label on products stored or offered for sale.

The CAA said the requirement was intended to help establish the legal status of imported and locally manufactured lubricants and identify their supply sources.

However, the presence of a government agreement number alone does not guarantee the quality of a product.

The authority is conducting further investigations into the suspected violations and will institute legal action in the relevant Magistrate's Courts against traders and other responsible parties once sufficient evidence is established.

The CAA has advised traders to ensure that products offered for sale carry the required government agreement number and urged consumers to check product labels and obtain receipts when making purchases.

Consumers can report suspected violations by calling the CAA hotline 1977 during office hours.
