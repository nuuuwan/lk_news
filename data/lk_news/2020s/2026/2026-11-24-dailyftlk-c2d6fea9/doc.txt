Shanakiyan questions Govt.’s rights record as HRCSL warnings on Bills go unheeded

Says Govt. cannot claim domestic accountability works while ignoring its own Human Rights Commission

HRCSL found PTA-like provisions in Organised Crime Bill, called for its withdrawal

Says ITAK will pursue justice for Chemmani victims through Courts, not political theatrics

Urges Election Commission to hold Provincial Council polls, postponed for years, at least in 2027

Seeks beach-cleaning machinery for Batticaloa and a raise in the Rs. 750 parliamentary staff allowance, unchanged for a decade

Ilankai Tamil Arasu Katchi (ITAK) Parliamentary Group Leader MP Shanakiyan Rasamanickam has questioned the Government’s commitment to human rights and accountability. He said it was going ahead with two security-related Bills despite serious objections from the Human Rights Commission of Sri Lanka (HRCSL).

Speaking in Parliament, Rasamanickam asked how the Government could keep telling the international community that domestic mechanisms were enough to deliver justice and accountability while it disregarded the recommendations of its own independent Commission.

He said the HRCSL had written to the Minister of Justice and National Integration about the Prevention of Organised Crime Bill. The Commission identified provisions resembling those in the Prevention of Terrorism Act (PTA), warned that the proposed law threatened fundamental rights and freedoms, and called for the Bill’s withdrawal.

It had raised similar concerns about the Protection of the State from Terrorism Bill, warning that provisions introduced under the pretext of protecting the State could seriously undermine citizens’ fundamental rights and freedoms.

“How can the Government proceed with these Bills without addressing the concerns raised by its own Human Rights Commission? This is a serious issue that raises fundamental questions about the Government’s commitment to human rights and accountability,” Rasamanickam said.

He pointed out that whenever Sri Lanka’s human rights record was discussed at the United Nations Human Rights Council (UNHRC), successive governments had maintained that accountability could be achieved through domestic mechanisms. He argued that the Government’s apparent unwillingness to act on the HRCSL’s recommendations called the credibility of those assurances into question.

“If the Government is unwilling to listen to its own independent Human Rights Commission, how can the Tamil people or the international community be expected to place confidence in domestic accountability mechanisms?” he asked.

Turning to the Northern and Eastern Provinces, Rasamanickam said long-standing problems of land occupation, restrictions on fundamental freedoms, intimidation and harassment remained unresolved. The continued failure to return land to its rightful owners was depriving affected communities of their fundamental rights and preventing them from rebuilding their lives, he said.

Civilians, journalists and people advocating for justice and accountability also continued to face intimidation and harassment. He urged the HRCSL to investigate these matters thoroughly and said he was concerned that such allegations kept emerging under the present Government despite its promises of democratic reform and respect for fundamental rights.

On the Chemmani mass grave investigation, Rasamanickam responded to remarks the Minister of Justice made in Parliament about a report submitted by archaeologist Prof. Somadeva. The Minister had described the document as merely a report and indicated that the Court had taken a similar position. Rasamanickam said ITAK remained committed to pursuing justice through the judicial process.

He noted that ITAK General Secretary President’s Counsel M. A. Sumanthiran had appeared before the Court in person and had submitted affidavits on behalf of individuals willing to undergo DNA testing, to help further investigations.

“We are pursuing justice for the victims of Chemmani through the Courts. We have no intention of engaging in cheap political theatrics,” he said.

He also criticised individuals who, he alleged, had collected money in connection with demonstrations organised during Maaveerar Day commemorations, and said his party would not engage in such practices. He also criticised a Minister representing the Northern Province for what he described as a lack of understanding of these matters.

Rasamanickam noted that the HRCSL’s report on the Northern and Eastern Provinces had also addressed the Chemmani issue. He said justice for the victims and their families required credible investigations, forensic evidence and meaningful judicial action rather than political statements.

Rasamanickam also called on the Judicial Service Commission (JSC) to resolve an administrative problem in the Batticaloa District without further delay. Fines collected by the Police within the jurisdiction of the Valaichchenai Pradeshiya Sabha were being credited to another local authority, he said.

The matter had been brought to the JSC’s attention and raised repeatedly with the Minister of Justice, but it had remained unresolved for about a year. The Minister had indicated that it required the JSC’s intervention. Rasamanickam also highlighted a shortage of staff in the Court Registrars’ Service, which he said was contributing to delays in judicial proceedings.

He urged the Election Commission to ensure Provincial Council elections are held at least in 2027. He noted that while the Commission had most recently conducted the Local Government elections, the provincial polls had been postponed for several years. The absence of elected Provincial Councils deprived people of their democratic right to representation at the provincial level, he said, and he urged the authorities to use the funds allocated for elections to hold the polls next year.

Rasamanickam welcomed the environmental work carried out under the Government’s Clean Sri Lanka program in several parts of the country, including the renovation of the Bambalapitiya Railway Station and the cleaning of its surroundings. However, he said coastal communities in the Batticaloa District faced serious environmental problems.

Large quantities of beer bottles, medical vials and plastic waste were frequently washing ashore along the district’s coastline, much of it believed to come from vessels at sea rather than from coastal residents. He asked the Government to provide specialised beach-cleaning machinery to the district, noting that the Kaluwanchikudy Pradeshiya Sabha had also submitted a proposal for such machinery. He also called for adequate public toilet facilities in public places throughout the district.

He also called for the additional allowance paid to parliamentary employees to be reviewed and increased. At Rs. 750, it had remained unchanged for about 10 years despite the rising cost of living and the responsibilities these employees carry.

Concluding, Rasamanickam said the Government must show its commitment to human rights, democratic governance and accountability through concrete action rather than assurances given in Parliament or before international institutions.

“Respect for fundamental rights, accountability and the rule of law must begin with the Government itself,” he said.
