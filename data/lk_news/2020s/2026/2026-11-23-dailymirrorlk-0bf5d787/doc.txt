Schools to be reorganised with transport facilities, says PM

Prime Minister Dr. Harini Amarasuriya has assured that transport facilities will be provided as a mandatory requirement when schools are consolidated under the school reorganisation programme.

She said there were no obstacles to introducing measures based on the specific needs and challenges of individual areas. The current Budget also includes provisions to provide transport facilities for students with disabilities.

The Prime Minister made these remarks at a meeting of the Colombo District Education Sub-Committee held at the Parliament complex on November 23.

During the meeting, officials revealed that student attendance had increased significantly in certain areas following the introduction of school nutrition programmes.

The need to establish a mechanism to recruit security personnel to protect school property was also discussed. Such personnel could be recruited either at school level or from the local community, subject to the necessary approval from the Provincial Council.

The meeting also discussed providing funds for security services through daily payment schemes based on the requirements of individual schools or through budgetary allocations. The importance of following proper criteria when obtaining external services was also highlighted.

The shortage of Tamil-medium schools in the Colombo District was identified as a key reason for school dropouts and poor attendance in certain areas. Accordingly, it was decided to introduce Tamil-medium classes in schools in affected areas and work towards establishing a trilingual school in the future.

The Prime Minister stressed that every child has the right to receive an education in their mother tongue and that ensuring this right is the Government's responsibility.

The meeting also discussed difficulties faced by students who enter schools without birth certificates and later encounter problems obtaining the necessary documents.

It was proposed to introduce mobile services and coordination programmes involving schools, Divisional Secretariats and other relevant institutions to address the issue.

Meanwhile, officials noted that a formal policy on the transfer of principals within the Western Province had been formulated and approved by the Public Service Commission, ending the previous absence of a clear policy.

As a result, the transfer process is expected to be implemented systematically from next year.

The importance of principals' commitment and leadership, particularly in managing schools with limited facilities and those located in difficult areas, was also emphasised. The meeting noted that decisions concerning schools should take into account the specific conditions and requirements of each institution.

Discussions were also held on improving the training of preschool teachers. It was proposed to provide them with practical training kits in addition to their formal training to help them carry out classroom activities more effectively.

The Prime Minister further said next year's teacher training programmes should place greater emphasis on the meaning of a student-centred approach and how it could be effectively implemented in classrooms.

Deputy Minister of Mass Media Kaushalya Ariyaratne, Deputy Minister of Urban Development Eranga Gunasekara, Deputy Minister of Industries Chathuranga Abesinghe, Colombo District Coordinating Committee Chairman Lakshman Nipunaarachchi, MP Samanmalee Gunasinghe and Colombo Additional District Secretary Kanchana Gunawardena attended the meeting, along with officials from the Ministry of Education.
