Cargills 2Q profit up 8.4% as group lines up new leadership

Cargills (Ceylon) PLC’s profit rose by 8.4 percent to Rs.3.0 billion in the September quarter on revenue of Rs.76.3 billion, as the Group appointed senior executives from within to lead its Retail, Dairy and F&B sectors and Group Finance from January 2027, according to the company.

Group revenue increased by 16.2 percent year-on-year, supported by strong growth across its Retail, FMCG and Restaurants businesses, while EBITDA grew by 13.9 percent to Rs.7.6 billion despite pressure on operating costs from higher energy and input prices. Earnings per share improved to Rs.11.80 from Rs.10.83 a year earlier. Over the six months to 30 September, revenue grew by 15.2 percent to Rs.152.4 billion and profit by 10.4 percent to Rs.5.6 billion.

The FMCG sector was the Group’s largest contributor to operating profit in the quarter, accounting for nearly half the total while bringing in about 23 percent of revenue. Sector revenue grew by 20.4 percent, driven primarily by volume expansion across the general trade, modern trade and food service channels. Operating profit rose by 27.4 percent to Rs.2.6 billion, aided by a favourable product mix and ongoing cost optimisation. For the six months, FMCG also overtook Retail in operating profit, reversing the position of a year earlier.

The Retail sector’s revenue increased by 14.7 percent, supported by same-store sales growth of 13.2 percent on the back of higher customer footfall, while operating profit rose by 8.5 percent to Rs.2.2 billion. Cargills Food City added four stores during the quarter, taking its nationwide footprint to 564 stores. Selected large-format stores will also be repositioned to offer a wider assortment and more services.

The Restaurants sector, comprising KFC Sri Lanka, grew revenue by 19.0 percent, with same-store sales increasing by 17.4 percent. Selective menu price revisions partly offset rising costs. KFC added six restaurants to reach 97 outlets, all of which remain EBITDA positive, and volumes are expected to strengthen during the December festive period.

Cargills attributed the performance to resilient consumer demand and its focus on growing volumes, strengthening operational capabilities and delivering value to consumers amid a challenging cost environment.

The company described the appointments as part of its long-term succession planning as it positions itself for its next phase of growth. It noted that the executives taking on the new roles have built substantial careers within the Group and bring extensive operating experience, institutional knowledge and a deep understanding of its businesses, values and purpose.

Looking ahead, Cargills remains optimistic about Sri Lanka’s medium-term economic prospects while staying mindful of global volatility. The Group has committed to investing in modernising its retail and food service networks, developing agricultural and dairy sourcing, expanding manufacturing capacity and strengthening its distribution, cold chain and logistics infrastructure, alongside digital transformation and talent development. Capital expenditure on property, plant and equipment rose by 83 percent to Rs.4.7 billion in the first half, from Rs.2.6 billion a year earlier.
