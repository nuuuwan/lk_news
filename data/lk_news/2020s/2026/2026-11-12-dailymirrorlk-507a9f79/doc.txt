Government moves to tap mineral resources for industrial development

The Government has announced plans to strengthen the use of Sri Lanka’s mineral resources to develop industries and increase their contribution to the domestic economy.

Despite the country having significant mineral resources, their potential has not been adequately integrated into the national economy, according to the Government.

Under the National Policy on Mineral Resources, the Geological Survey and Mines Bureau (GSMB) will conduct preliminary feasibility studies to assess the potential of mineral deposits and explore ways to maximise their economic benefits.

The Government will also seek specialised technical expertise from overseas to improve mineral exploration activities.

As part of these efforts, the Government plans to assess the graphite reserves at the Kahatagaha Graphite Mine, which covers an area of 102 acres, in collaboration with the GSMB.

Once the quantity of graphite reserves is established, steps will be taken to identify a suitable investor for a value-addition project covering graphite mining and processing.

Meanwhile, Cabinet approval has been granted to allocate 58 mineral-sand grids along the eastern coastal belt to Lanka Mineral Sands Limited.

A Cabinet-appointed Negotiating Committee and a Project Committee have also been established to select an investor for a mineral-sand value-addition industry based on the output of Lanka Mineral Sands Limited.

Exploration work is currently under way to assess a silica sand deposit covering 40 acres in the Nattandiya area. The Government plans to identify a suitable investor in 2027 after assessing the potential of the deposit.

In addition, 28 metric grid units have been identified in connection with the Kotigambokka quartz deposit in the Wellawaya area. Steps will be taken in 2027 to find an investor for a project involving the mining and value addition of the quartz deposit.
