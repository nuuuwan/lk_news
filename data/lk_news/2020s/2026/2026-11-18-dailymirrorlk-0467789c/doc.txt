Labour officials receive specialised training to identify forced labour

Colombo, Nov. 18 (Daily Mirror) - Public officials from the Western and Southern Provinces are receiving specialised training on identifying forced labour and related crimes under a three-day residential programme launched at the National Institute of Plantation Management in Athurugiriya.

The programme, which commenced on November 16, brings together officials from the Department of Labour, Sri Lanka Police and the Foreign Employment Division of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Foreign Employment and Tourism.

The training focuses on identifying forced labour, understanding relevant legal provisions, providing legal remedies to victims and taking appropriate action when such offences are detected.

It also covers related issues such as child labour and human trafficking, with discussions based on real-life cases and practical situations.

The initiative was launched by the Ministry of Labour in 2025 with the technical support of the International Labour Organization (ILO).

Eight workshops have been conducted so far, including two in 2025 and six in 2026. These included a programme in Kandy for Central Province officials and a Tamil-medium workshop in Colombo in October for officials from the Northern and Eastern Provinces.

The programme has so far trained 305 officials, comprising 140 from the Department of Labour, 125 from the Police and 40 from the foreign employment sector.

In addition, 30 officials have received specialised Training of Trainers (ToT) to serve as resource persons in future programmes.

The three-day workshop goes beyond classroom lectures, incorporating group activities, interactive discussions and practical problem-solving exercises to improve cooperation among the participating institutions.

The Ministry of Labour plans to continue and expand the initiative throughout 2027.
