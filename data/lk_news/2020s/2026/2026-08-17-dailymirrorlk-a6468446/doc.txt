Cargills Bank 2Q profit slips despite stronger core income

Cargills Bank PLC saw its second-quarter profit edge lower despite a sharp improvement in core banking income, as higher operating costs, impairment charges and taxes absorbed gains from stronger lending and fee-based business.

Profit for the three months ended June 30, 2026 fell 3 percent year-on-year to Rs. 75.8 million from Rs. 78.0 million, even as profit before tax rose 23 percent to Rs. 177.0 million from Rs. 144.2 million. Earnings per share declined to Rs. 0.06 from Rs. 0.09.

Taxes on financial services jumped 54 percent to Rs. 161.4 million, while income tax expense rose 53 percent to Rs. 101.2 million.

Net interest income rose 20 percent to Rs. 1.17 billion, while net fee and commission income surged 45 percent to Rs. 268.2 million. That helped lift total operating income 21 percent to Rs. 1.47 billion despite a 39 percent decline in other income.

However, impairment charges increased 40 percent to Rs. 75.6 million, reversing part of the benefit from income growth. Total operating expenses climbed 15 percent to Rs. 1.06 billion, with personnel expenses up 18 percent and depreciation and amortisation rising 34 percent.

The quarter therefore points to improving earnings from Cargills Bank’s core intermediation business, although cost growth, credit charges and taxation continued to constrain the conversion of that income growth into bottom-line profit.

For the first half, profit after tax fell 25 percent year-on-year to Rs. 180.7 million, while profit before tax declined 22 percent to Rs. 362.4 million. The weaker headline result, however, masks a substantial shift in the composition of earnings away from treasury-related gains and towards banking operations.

Net interest income increased 20 percent to Rs. 2.21 billion and net fee and commission income rose 17 percent to Rs. 515.6 million. Cargills Bank said the increase in net interest income was “primarily driven by loan growth” alongside the repricing of deposits and advances to reflect market conditions. Net interest margin improved to 4.64 percent from 4.38 percent at end-2025.

The improvement was largely offset by the collapse in other income, which fell 89 percent to Rs. 54.7 million from Rs. 477.0 million. The bank attributed this to the high base in the previous year, when earnings benefited from substantially higher realised capital gains on financial assets and gains on assets measured at fair value through profit or loss.

The Banking segment swung to a pre-tax profit of Rs. 306.0 million from a Rs. 27.3 million loss in the corresponding period, as segment net interest income rose 42 percent to Rs. 2.05 billion. Treasury and Investments pre-tax profit, meanwhile, dropped to Rs. 56.5 million from Rs. 491.3 million.

Balance-sheet growth remained strong, with net loans expanding 15 percent from December to Rs. 72.3 billion and customer deposits rising 18 percent to Rs. 77.9 billion. Total assets increased 14 percent to Rs. 104.7 billion. On a year-on-year basis, loans grew 28 percent and deposits 32 percent.

Deposit growth was heavily concentrated in time deposits. Domestic-currency time deposits increased to Rs. 60.14 billion from Rs. 49.48 billion at end-2025, while savings deposits rose only modestly to Rs. 9.85 billion from Rs. 9.64 billion. That funding mix is relevant as the bank seeks to protect margins while expanding its loan book.

Asset quality improved on a gross basis, with the gross Stage 3 ratio falling to 11.30 percent from 12.00 percent at end-2025. The net Stage 3 ratio, however, was virtually unchanged at 6.53 percent against 6.52 percent, while Stage 3 provision coverage declined to 42.26 percent from 45.65 percent.

The bank said the improvement followed “rigorous scrutiny and recovery actions”. First-half impairment charges fell to Rs. 100 million from Rs. 180 million a year earlier.

Costs remain one of the key constraints on profitability. First-half operating expenses increased 11 percent to Rs. 2.04 billion, pushing the cost-to-income ratio to 73.43 percent from 70.68 percent at end-2025. Personnel expenses rose 17 percent, while depreciation and amortisation increased 35 percent, which the bank attributed primarily to investment in information technology and infrastructure.
