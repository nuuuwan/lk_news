Senior parliament staff summoned to CIABOC

Colombo, Sep. 4 (Daily Mirror) - Several senior Parliament officials have given statements to the Commission to Investigate Allegations of Bribery or Corruption (CIABOC) in connection with a complaint filed against Speaker Jagath Wickramaratne by former Secretary General of Parliament Chaminda Kularatne.

A senior Parliament official confirmed to the Daily Mirror that a number of staff members had been summoned before CIABOC yesterday and had provided statements regarding the allegations contained in the complaint.

The officials who appeared before the Commission were the Deputy Director (Administration), Assistant Director (Finance), and a Transport Officer of Parliament.

CIABOC had recently informed the Secretary-General of Parliament, Attorney-at-Law Kushani Rohanadeera, to direct the relevant parliamentary officials to appear before the Commission to provide information concerning the corruption allegation against the Speaker.

The Commission had also instructed the Secretary-General to immediately submit a comprehensive report containing details of the facilities provided to the Speaker.

The requested documents include details of the official residence used by the Speaker, vehicles allocated to him, fuel obtained for those vehicles, food and beverages provided by Parliament, and information relating to his personal staff.

Kularatne lodged the complaint with CIABOC in February 2026, raising six allegations against the Speaker and calling for an investigation.

One of the allegations relates to the use of official vehicles. Kularatne claimed that the Speaker had been using three vehicles despite a June 7, 2025 circular stipulating that the Speaker was entitled to two vehicles.

He also alleged that the Speaker had been using a van bearing registration number WP NC 4923, which had been allocated for general use by Parliament.

Another allegation concerns fuel allowances. According to the complaint, the Speaker had allegedly claimed two fuel allowances, one in his capacity as Speaker and another as an MP, with the latter allegedly being claimed from December 17, 2024 to November 2025.

Kularatne claimed that 568 litres of fuel had been used during this period and alleged that approximately Rs. 2 million had been drawn in connection with the fuel allowances.

The complaint also raises questions over meal allowances. Kularatne alleged that the Speaker had been having meals with his personal secretary and that, although Rs. 900 had been approved for an additional person, only Rs. 150 per day had been paid.

He further alleged that the Speaker’s personal secretary had been provided transport facilities, including fuel, outside established procedures.

Another allegation concerns the use of equipment and personnel attached to Parliament’s media unit. Kularatne alleged that the Speaker had allocated a media team for his personal use despite there being no provision for such an arrangement under existing procedures.

The complaint also questioned the Speaker’s decision to rent an official residence at Lauries Lane in Bambalapitiya, despite the availability of an official bungalow near the Parliamentary complex.

Kularatne further alleged that the Speaker had appointed an officer to conduct an investigation against him outside established procedures, claiming that the decision was linked to a personal grievance against him.

In another allegation, Kularatne claimed that Parliament’s Right to Information Unit had been instructed not to provide information he had requested concerning the Speaker under the Right to Information Act.

Kularatne has called on CIABOC to investigate all the allegations and has also requested whistleblower protection in connection with the complaint.

The statements obtained from Parliament officials are expected to form part of CIABOC’s inquiry into the allegations. No finding of wrongdoing has been established against the Speaker at this stage.
