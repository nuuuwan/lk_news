Deploying security forces to the rescue of elephants

At least now Sri Lanka is taking the much needed efforts to protect wild elephants, a species protected by the law. The demise of tusker Kavantissa is a reminder about the brutal ground reality. Solutions to resolve the human-elephant conflict should go beyond boardroom discussions. News of a gunshot injury on Agbo, another tusker that had sustained multiple gunshot injuries, made authorities realise the gravity of the situation. Opposition factions have been scrutinising the government for delays in responding to these incidents on the ground.

While cases of known elephants are being reported, incidents of many other elephants often go unreported. Special army teams were deployed to trace the whereabouts of tusker Agbo and it was safely found near the Nachchaduwa Tank forest reserve in the Anuradhapura District.  But there’s only so much that the security forces could do in terms of rescuing elephants in distress as they are not properly trained for the job. However the urgency of the situation demands some intervention to begin with. It is not clear as to whether the authorities have understood the need for precise interventions to treat injured elephants in the wild.  Any interventions to protect elephants are welcome in this harrowing backdrop of increasing elephant deaths.
