No revenue for water board for one - fourth of supplies

Colombo, Nov. 9 (Daily Mirror) - The National Water Supply and Drainage Board (NWSDB), commonly known as the Water Board, failed to generate revenue from nearly one-fourth of the water it supplied during the first half of 2026.

The mid-year fiscal report submitted by the Finance Ministry says that water categorised as Non-Revenue Water (NRW) stood at 24.4 percent.

NRW is a result of water leakages, illegal connections, metering inaccuracies and authorised but unbilled connections.

The latest figure is marginally higher than the 24.2 percent reported during the corresponding period last year.

During the first six months of 2026, the NWSDB added nearly 60,000 water connections to its system, increasing the total number of connections to 3.31 million as at the end of June 2026, the report seen by the Daily Mirror says.

However, at the end of June 2026, piped-water supply coverage and safe drinking water coverage decreased to 62.8 percent and 94.5 percent, respectively, compared to 63.7 percent and 99.1 percent last year.

This was mainly due to differences in reporting methodologies, as coverage indicators were reported on a household basis in 2025 and on a population basis in 2026.

Meanwhile, piped sewerage coverage increased marginally from 2.08 percent as at the end of June 2025 to 2.11 percent as at the end of June 2026.

The revenue of the NWSDB for the first six months of 2026 increased by 5.1 percent to Rs. 43.6 billion, compared to Rs. 41.5 billion in the same period of 2025.

However, the cost of sales increased by approximately 10 percent to Rs. 16.5 billion from Rs. 15 billion, mainly due to higher pumping costs, rent, rates and other taxes.

As a result, the Board recorded a gross profit of Rs. 27.1 billion in the first six months of 2026, compared to Rs. 26.5 billion in the same period of 2025.

Nevertheless, net profit decreased by 8.5 percent to Rs. 16.2 billion, compared to Rs. 17.7 billion in the same period of 2025.

Meanwhile, during the period under review, the average unit cost stood at Rs. 88, an increase of Rs. 6 compared to Rs. 82 during the same period of 2025.

During this period, water production and water sales each increased by approximately 5 percent, reaching 440.8 million cubic metres and 333.5 million cubic metres, respectively, compared to 419.1 million cubic metres and 317.6 million cubic metres, respectively, in the same period of 2025.

During the first six months of 2026, the Board completed two projects. In addition, the General Treasury provided equity capital of Rs. 5.9 billion to facilitate loan repayments and the completion of major water supply projects.
