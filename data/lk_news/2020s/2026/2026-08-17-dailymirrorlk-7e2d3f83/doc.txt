Did Navy craft hit sunken wreck of Cyprus-flagged vessel off Angulana?

Colombo, Aug. 17 (Daily Mirror) - Salvage preparations are underway to recover the Sri Lanka Navy Fast Attack Craft that sank off Angulana, as a special board of experts appointed by the Navy Commander begins a formal investigation into the incident, the Navy said.

The Navy has located the capsized vessel and launched a major salvage operation to bring the craft to the surface before it is towed to the Colombo Port. However, rough sea conditions pose challenges to the recovery operation.

Meanwhile, search operations were continuing until yesterday afternoon for a missing Sri Lanka Navy sailor following the incident, which occurred at around 11:40 a.m. on Saturday when the Fast Attack Craft (FAC) encountered severe difficulties while returning to Colombo after a routine patrol.

Eyewitnesses reported seeing the vessel apparently collide with a submerged object, after which smoke was seen coming from the craft before it sank within minutes. However, the exact cause of the incident has not yet been officially confirmed.

It was reported that, authorities and maritime experts are examining whether the Sri Lanka Navy Fast Attack Craft may have collided with the long-abandoned, Cyprus-flagged merchant vessel 'MV Thermopylae Sierra'. The 155-meter ship had been stranded in local waters off Panadura since 2009 due to pending court litigation before it ultimately sank following years of severe neglect, heavy corrosion, and structural deterioration. Given that the wreckage rested submerged and largely unlit close to the coastline—the exact operational zone where the naval craft met with tragedy—investigators are probing whether the FAC struck the hazardous, ghost-like wreck during its routine patrol, leading to the sudden and catastrophic impact.

The Merchant Shipping Secretariat (MSS) of Sri Lanka is legally empowered under the Merchant Shipping Act No. 52 of 1971 to oversee all national matters regarding maritime wrecks and the safety of navigation.

Operating as the primary maritime administration, the Director General acts as the Principal Receiver of Wrecks, commanding investigations, managing salvage operations, and enforcing pollution mitigation under international MARPOL standards when accidents occur in local waters.

A joint response involving the Sri Lanka Navy, the Sri Lanka Air Force and local fishermen led to the rescue of 11 of the 12 naval personnel who were on board.

Navy Spokesman Commander Chaminda Walakuluge said the rescued sailors are currently receiving treatment at the Welisala Navy Hospital and that none of them are in critical condition.

The search for the missing naval officer is continuing alongside the salvage preparations, with authorities closely monitoring the rough sea conditions.

The special board of experts appointed by the Commander of the Navy will investigate the circumstances surrounding the incident and determine the exact cause of the vessel’s sinking.
