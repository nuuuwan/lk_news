Seat belt law to be strictly enforced on expressways from September 19

Colombo, Aug. 19 (Daily Mirror) - Police have warned that the requirement for all passengers travelling in vehicles on Sri Lanka’s expressways to wear seat belts will be strictly enforced from September 19.

Addressing a media briefing, Senior Superintendent of Police Manoj Ranagala, Director of Traffic Control and Road Safety, said the one-year grace period given to owners of vehicles that were not factory-fitted with seat belts to install them will end on September 19.

Accordingly, both drivers and passengers travelling on expressways will be legally required to wear seat belts. Those found travelling without seat belts could face a fine of Rs. 1,000.

SSP Ranagala said there is also a legal provision to impose fines on passengers travelling without seat belts on express buses.

He stressed the importance of wearing seat belts, noting that injuries and fatalities resulting from passengers not wearing seat belts in road accidents have increased significantly.

“Hereafter, when entering an expressway, the police will strictly monitor whether the driver and passengers are wearing seat belts properly. Vehicles will not be allowed to enter expressways without the required seat belts being worn,” he said.

Meanwhile, Chairman of the National Road Safety Council Manjula Kularatne said the grace period given to owners of vehicles without seat belts to install them would also end on September 19.

According to Gazette Notification No. 2455/29, issued on September 25, 2025, the seat belt requirement is to be enforced for all vehicles travelling on expressways.

SSP Ranagala said the measure is also based on the legal provisions introduced through Gazette Notification No. 1718/12, issued on August 9, 2011.

Authorities have urged motorists and passengers to comply with the requirement and ensure that seat belts are properly worn before entering an expressway.
