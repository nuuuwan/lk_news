Compact crossovers lead auto recovery as Chinese brands gain ground

The SUV and crossover category emerged as the strongest-performing passenger vehicle segment in June 2026, driving the broader market recovery as consumer preferences shift toward practical urban crossovers and Chinese automakers capture market share.

According to the latest vehicle registration commentary by JB Securities CEO/MD Murtaza Jafferjee, total SUV and crossover registrations rose to 5,933 units in June from 5,511 units in May, propelled entirely by a surge in brand-new unit registrations.

Countering the widespread perception that the growth of SUVs increases overall fuel consumption across the country, detailed technical classifications reveal that small engine capacities dominate the market segment. Crossovers with engine displacement below 1,500cc accounted for an overwhelming 96 percent of all SUV and crossover registrations during the month.

“Crossovers below 1,500cc continued to dominate the SUV market accounting for 96 percent of total registrations, far from the popular perception that SUVs are fuel guzzling vehicles which is clearly not the case in Sri Lanka. Most of these vehicles are really crossovers - more like hatchbacks rather than full size SUVs,” Jafferjee stated in his analysis.

Toyota maintained its overall segment leadership with 1,965 registrations in June, led heavily by compact utility offerings such as the Raize with 1,448 units, the Urban Cruiser with 309 units, and the Yaris Cross with 97 units.

Honda followed with 1,209 registrations, almost exclusively represented by the Vezel with 1,175 units, while Suzuki recorded 599 units, driven almost entirely by its Fronx model.

However, the passenger vehicle landscape is undergoing a rapid diversification, driven by Chinese vehicle manufacturers expanding their footprint across both battery electric and hybrid lineups. Overall Chinese imports reached 8,165 units in June, up from 6,774 units in May. In the brand-new passenger vehicle market, BYD solidified its position as a primary contender by registering 1,035 passenger vehicles, including 516 SUVs and 519 motor cars.

Jafferjee highlighted that Chinese marques are gaining significant traction among Sri Lankan buyers through competitive technology and price positioning. Beyond BYD—which saw strong demand for its Sealion SUV with 351 units and Atto variants with 165 units—several other Chinese manufacturers maintained a meaningful market presence, including Jetour with 148 units, BAIC with 110 units, Jaecoo with 96 units, GAC with 91 units, Changan with 36 units, and SAIC with 31 units.

The surge in Chinese imports is equally prominent in the two-wheeler sector, which remains the country’s largest vehicle category with 40,007 total registrations in June. Electric two-wheeler registrations witnessed a sharp rebound, jumping to 6,426 units from 4,625 units in May. This expansion was driven almost entirely by Chinese electric motorbike brands, with Yadea leading the segment with 3,287 units, followed by Aima with 592 units.
