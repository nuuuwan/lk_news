Apparel’s September rebound leaves US$ 210mn gap to close

Sri Lanka’s apparel exports returned to growth in September, helped by stronger shipments to the United States and European Union. However, a US$ 210 million shortfall over the first nine months leaves the industry heading into the final quarter with considerable ground to recover.

September export earnings rose 3.47 percent year-on-year to US$ 416.99 million from US$ 403.01 million, according to figures released by the Joint Apparel Association Forum (JAAF). However, January–September earnings fell 5.52 percent to US$ 3.59 billion from US$ 3.80 billion a year earlier, with every major market recording a cumulative decline.

The September improvement was concentrated in the industry’s two largest markets. Shipments to the US increased 7.17 percent to US$ 172.03 million, while exports to the EU, excluding the UK, rose 6.5 percent to US$ 132.46 million.

Together, the two markets generated about 73 percent of September’s apparel export earnings. Their combined year-on-year increase of roughly US$ 19 million offset falling shipments to the UK and other destinations, underscoring the sector’s reliance on its principal buyers.

Exports to the UK declined 5.92 percent to US$ 47.33 million, while shipments to other markets fell 3.89 percent to US$ 65.17 million. September therefore marked an improvement in the headline figure, but not a recovery across markets.

September’s year-on-year gain amounted to just under US$ 14 million, against a nine-month earnings gap of US$ 209.79 million.

To finish 2026 level with the previous year, fourth-quarter exports would have to exceed their year-earlier value by that entire shortfall, equivalent to nearly US$ 70 million in additional earnings per month. A continuation of September’s dollar increase alone would leave much of the gap unfilled.

The cumulative market figures also show that the weakness extends beyond a single destination. Exports to the UK recorded the steepest nine-month decline, at 10.85 percent. Shipments to the EU fell 5.44 percent, other markets declined 5.43 percent and the US contracted 3.69 percent.

The US has consequently provided the greatest relative resilience among the major markets, although its September growth has yet to translate into higher cumulative earnings.

Europe presents a more complicated competitive challenge. JAAF pointed to changing trade flows as Chinese and Indian suppliers turn greater attention to European buyers following higher US tariffs.

More suppliers competing for European business could put further pressure on prices at a time when, according to JAAF, overall demand remains soft. Growth in export earnings alone also does not establish whether manufacturers are securing better margins: the release provides no shipment-volume, unit-price or profitability data.

JAAF Secretary General Yohan Lawrence linked the durability of the recovery to domestic operating conditions.

“September’s growth in the US and EU is encouraging, and it shows our manufacturers can compete when demand is there. We’re still more than five percent behind where we were this time last year. What the industry needs now is steady, competitively priced energy and a supportive policy environment, so that this momentum lasts beyond a single month,” he said.

Competitive energy costs could help manufacturers defend orders and margins, but September’s figures do not establish how much of the year-to-date decline stems from domestic costs, weaker demand or changes in sourcing.
