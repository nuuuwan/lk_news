Buddika steps down from SLTDA

Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA) Chairman Buddika Hewawasam has stepped down from his position at the regulatory authority, citing personal reasons and professional commitments, firmly denying the speculation that his departure is prompted by political interference.

Speaking to the media on the sidelines of The Hotels Association of Sri Lanka (THASL) event yesterday, Hewawasam clarified that while relinquishing his role at the SLTDA, he would continue to serve as Chairman of the Sri Lanka Tourism Promotion Bureau (SLTPB).

Hewawasam dismissed the reports alleging outside pressure, asserting that he did not tolerate being dictated to in his official capacity. He explained that his decision was primarily driven by the need to attend to his primary profession and personal commitments, noting that he had initially agreed to hold office for a limited tenure of one year.

Hewawasam is an experienced aviation and travel industry professional, who also serves as Sri Lanka Manager for the International Air Transport Association (IATA).

Addressing the operational aspects of his tenure, the industry sources confirmed that he had been working in his capacity at the tourism bodies without drawing a salary, underscoring his professional independence.

However, he acknowledged that having a single chairperson head both the SLTDA and SLTPB would be a better choice to maintain synergy across the institutions.

He further noted that the legislative reforms to the Tourism Act are currently in progress, which in particular aims to address the existing issues in the administration.

Addressing the key industry priorities, Hewawasam stated that the tourism promotion authority is moving forward with its plans, with the interim campaign set to be launched today and a press conference scheduled for Wednesday to formally outline the rollout schedule.

His clarification follows widespread industry speculation, suggesting that the resignation is linked to political friction and corporate lobbying, with the sources pointing to interference from political associates and prominent business figures, including a former tourism chairperson.
