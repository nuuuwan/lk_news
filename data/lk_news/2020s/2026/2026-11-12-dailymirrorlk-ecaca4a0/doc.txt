Rat fever cases near 7,000; health alert issued

Colombo, Nov. 12 (Daily Mirror) - Health authorities have warned of an increased risk of Leptospirosis, commonly known as rat fever, across the country with the onset of the Maha cultivation season and prevailing monsoon rains.

Nearly 7,000 cases have been reported so far this year, while around 300 people die from the disease annually, medical professionals have warned.

The Epidemiology Unit and the Health Promotion Bureau have jointly issued a special advisory urging the public to take precautionary measures to prevent a further rise in infections due to the expected rainy weather.

Ratnapura, Kegalle, Galle and Matara have reported the highest numbers of cases so far this year.

Farmers engaged in paddy cultivation and people involved in outdoor activities are particularly vulnerable, especially during periods of heavy rain and flooding.

Leptospirosis is a bacterial infection transmitted from animals to humans. The bacteria enter water and soil through the urine of infected rats and other domestic and wild animals.

People can become infected when contaminated water or mud enters the body through cuts, wounds or breaks in the skin.

Health authorities have urged farmers and others working in muddy or waterlogged areas to take preventive measures, including obtaining recommended preventive medication after consulting a medical professional.

They have also advised people to wear protective footwear, such as rubber boots, and gloves when working in contaminated environments. Any cuts or wounds should be cleaned and covered with waterproof dressings before entering water or muddy areas.

Medical professionals have warned that symptoms of rat fever can resemble those of dengue and influenza.

People experiencing fever, severe headaches, muscle pain or redness of the eyes have been advised to seek immediate medical attention rather than resort to self-medication.

Health authorities highlighted that early diagnosis and timely treatment are essential to prevent serious complications and deaths.
