Sri Lanka sets 5% inflation target for next 3 years

Agreement sets an accountability margin of 2 percentage points above and below target

Central Bank said its recommendation followed a comprehensive technical assessment covering Sri Lanka’s economic structure, historical and empirical evidence, monetary policy considerations and credibility of framework

Assessment also considered stakeholder views and international experience and practices

Sri Lanka has set a 5 percent inflation target for the next three years, under a new agreement between the government and Central Bank, following a scheduled review of its monetary policy framework.

The agreement sets an accountability margin of two percentage points above and below the target, establishing thresholds of 3 percent and 7 percent for quarterly average headline inflation.

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, who also serves as Finance Minister and Central Bank Governor Dr. Nandalal Weerasinghe signed the Monetary Policy Framework Agreement on October 1. It took effect the same day and was published in an extraordinary gazette.

“The Central Bank of Sri Lanka shall aim to maintain a quarterly headline inflation rate at the target of 5 percent,” the agreement stated.

The review was conducted under the Central Bank of Sri Lanka Act, which requires the inflation target and related parameters to be reassessed every three years or sooner if exceptional circumstances warrant. The initial agreement was signed in October 2023.

The Central Bank said its recommendation followed a comprehensive technical assessment covering Sri Lanka’s economic structure, historical and empirical evidence, monetary policy considerations and the credibility of the framework.

The assessment also considered stakeholder views and international experience and practices.

The Finance Ministry accepted the recommendation after reviewing the proposal, the Central Bank said in a statement on October 2.

Inflation will be measured using the Colombo Consumer Price Index published by the Census and Statistics Department. The quarterly rate will be calculated as the simple average of the year-on-year inflation readings for the three months of each calendar quarter.

The accountability margin therefore applies to the quarterly average rather than an individual month’s inflation reading.

“The Central Bank remains committed to achieving the inflation target through forward-looking and data-driven policy decisions, in pursuit of its primary objective of achieving and maintaining domestic price stability,” the statement said.
