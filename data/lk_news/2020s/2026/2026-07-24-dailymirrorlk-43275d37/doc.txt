Early optimism gives way to weaker close at the CSE

The Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) closed lower on Friday as early buying interest gave way to profit-taking in several index-heavy counters, pushing both benchmark indices into negative territory by the end of the session. While the market opened on a positive note with broad-based buying across counters, selling pressure intensified during the latter half of trading, erasing the morning’s gains.

The All Share Price Index (ASPI) declined by 26.63 points (-0.13%) to close at 21,172.74, while the S&P SL20 Index fell 3.31 points (-0.06%) to 5,942.56. Total market turnover amounted to LKR 2.45 billion, with 93.56 million shares changing hands.

Despite the weaker close, investor sentiment remained mixed, with healthy participation observed in selected retail counters. Market activity was largely driven by negotiated transactions, as crossings accounted for LKR 1.67 billion, representing 68% of the day's total turnover.

The Retailing sector emerged as the highest contributor to market turnover, recording LKR 1.04 billion with 38.25 million shares traded. UML.N dominated the sector, generating LKR 1.03 billion in turnover while accounting for 38.15 million shares traded. The largest crossing of the day was also recorded in UML.N, amounting to LKR 1.03 billion involving 38.00 million shares.

Market breadth remained marginally negative, with 118 gainers against 129 decliners, reflecting mild selling pressure across the broader market.

On the positive side, LOLC.N contributed 10.62 points to the ASPI, followed by gains from LOFC.N, MELS.N, CARS.N, and DFCC.N. Meanwhile, HNB.N was the biggest drag on the index, shedding 8.47 points, while CINS.N, GRAN.N, COMB.N, and NDB.N also weighed on overall market performance.

Among notable trades, UML.N attracted significant investor interest, recording the day's highest turnover of LKR 1.03 billion with 38.15 million shares traded, while closing with a 4.82% price gain.

Overall, the market reflected a cautious trading environment, with profit-taking limiting early gains despite healthy retail participation. The high proportion of crossings suggests continued institutional activity, while investors remain selective amid the current market conditions.
