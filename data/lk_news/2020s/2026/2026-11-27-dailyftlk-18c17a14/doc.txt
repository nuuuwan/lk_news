Sri Lanka secures first national GI certificate for Ceylon Cinnamon

EDB receives landmark certification following EU recognition; industry targets $ 1 b exports by 2030

Authorities pursuing national GI branding for local products with export potential including Ceylon Tea, pepper, kithul, pineapple, king coconut, blue sapphire and Dumbara mats

Sri Lanka’s Ceylon Cinnamon industry has secured another milestone with the National Intellectual Property Office (NIPO) issuing the country’s first Geographical Indication (GI) certificate, formally recognising the product’s national identity and strengthening efforts to position it as a premium global export.

The certificate was presented to the Sri Lanka Export Development Board (EDB) yesterday, in the presence of Trade Minister Wasantha Samarasinghe and representatives of the public and private sectors, following nearly a decade of collaboration among stakeholders to secure international recognition for the country’s signature spice.

The development builds on the European Union’s (EU) recognition of Ceylon Cinnamon as a GI on 2 February 2022, making it the first Sri Lankan product to secure GI registration in the EU. The national certification is expected to reinforce protection of the product’s geographical identity, distinguish authentic Sri Lankan cinnamon from substitutes and support its positioning in premium international markets.

Sri Lanka earned around $ 250 million from cinnamon exports in 2025, with the industry now targeting expansion into a $ 1 billion export sector by around 2030, supported by GI recognition, improved quality, value addition and wider market access.

The GI designation is intended to strengthen Sri Lanka’s competitive advantage by ensuring that products marketed as Ceylon Cinnamon meet specified quality and geographical origin requirements. It is also expected to support higher prices, export diversification, increased production and employment across the value chain.

A key component of the certification framework is the Association for Ceylon Cinnamon Protected Geographical Indication (ACCPGI), established to represent stakeholders across the industry. The association is responsible for an internal control mechanism to monitor compliance with the GI specification. Growers, processors, traders, exporters and other supply-chain participants must register with the association to export Ceylon Cinnamon to the EU under the protected name.

The achievement follows sustained efforts by the EDB, with technical assistance from the EU Delegation to Sri Lanka, the United Nations Industrial Development Organisation (UNIDO) and the World Trade Organization (WTO). Financial support was provided by the Netherlands-based Common Fund for Commodities, while the International Finance Corporation (IFC) supported promotional activities and industry training.

The EDB also worked with the Trade and Foreign Affairs Ministries, Department of Commerce, Department of Export Agriculture, NIPO, the Spice Council and the Spices and Allied Products Producers' and Traders' Association (SAPPTA) to secure the recognition, despite objections raised by competing countries before the European Commission.

Established with effect from 1 September 2023, the Cinnamon Development Department is also tasked with elevating cinnamon from a relatively minor export crop into a major commercial plantation industry by improving production, quality and export performance.

Meanwhile, authorities are pursuing national GI branding for other Sri Lankan products with export potential, including Ceylon Tea, pepper, kithul, pineapple, king coconut, blue sapphire and Dumbara mats, as part of broader efforts to strengthen the country’s product identity and expand value-added exports.

Those present at the certificate presentation included Ministry of Trade Secretary K.A. Vimalenthirarajah, NIPO Acting Director General Himali Hettihewage, EDB Chairman and CEO Mangala Wijesinghe and EDB Director General Erandika Dissanayake.
