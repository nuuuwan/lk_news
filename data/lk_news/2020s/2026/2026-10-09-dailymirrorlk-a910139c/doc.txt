Govt. moves to standardize building rules nationwide

Colombo, Oct. 9 (Daily Mirror) - The Government has launched a national initiative to formulate and establish Sri Lanka’s first-ever National Building Code of Sri Lanka (NBCSL), in a move aimed at strengthening the safety, quality, sustainability and disaster resilience of buildings across the country.

Defence Secretary Air Vice Marshal Sampath Thuyacontha (Retd.) chaired a meeting on the initiative at the Ministry of Defence on October 8, with the participation of senior officials from the National Building Research Institute (NBRI), National Planning Department, Urban Development Authority (UDA) and Construction Industry Development Authority (CIDA).

The project proposal submitted by the NBRI to the National Planning Department has been approved for implementation during 2026–2028. Cabinet approval for the project was granted on October 21, 2025, based on a memorandum submitted by the President and Minister of Defence.

The initiative is being jointly managed by the NBRI, CIDA and UDA, in consultation with relevant government institutions, professional bodies, academia, the construction industry and other stakeholders.

Sri Lanka currently does not have a single, legally established national building code. Building design and construction practices rely on a combination of international standards, including the International Building Code, Eurocodes, British Standards and Indian Standards, together with local regulations and sector-specific requirements.

Authorities say the use of multiple standards can sometimes result in inconsistencies in building design verification, approvals and enforcement. The proposed national code is therefore intended to establish a unified framework that is both aligned with international best practices and adapted to Sri Lanka’s local conditions.

The need for such a framework is particularly important given Sri Lanka’s exposure to natural hazards including floods, landslides and high winds, as well as the country’s diverse climatic, geological and environmental conditions.

The proposed National Building Code will cover 10 key technical areas, including structural safety, fire safety, sustainability and universal accessibility. It will also establish practical mechanisms for building permits, inspections, compliance and enforcement.

The initiative has two main objectives: to consolidate Sri Lanka’s fragmented building regulations into a unified national framework and to establish the institutional mechanisms required for consistent implementation across the country.

The formulation process will include extensive consultations with government agencies, professional organizations, academic institutions, construction industry representatives and the public. Authorities say this will help ensure that the final code is technically sound, relevant to Sri Lanka and practical to implement.

A qualified consultancy firm will be selected through a competitive procurement process in accordance with National Procurement Agency guidelines. The assignment is expected to take 24 months and culminate in the preparation and gazetting of the National Building Code of Sri Lanka.

The project will be financed through the Consolidated Fund of the Government of Sri Lanka.

The Ministry of Defence and NBRI have also called for Expressions of Interest from qualified consultancy firms to undertake the nationally significant assignment.
