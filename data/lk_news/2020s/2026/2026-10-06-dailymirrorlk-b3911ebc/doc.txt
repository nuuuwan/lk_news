IMF urges fuel price adjustments, targeted relief as war risks persist

Sri Lanka should allow the domestic fuel prices to reflect the international price movements if the Middle East war persists, while protecting the vulnerable households through the targeted cash assistance, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) said.

The recommendation places the household relief alongside the need to preserve the cost-recovery energy pricing, as the country faces the risk of further external shocks.

In its statement announcing a staff-level agreement on the seventh review of Sri Lanka’s bailout programme, the IMF said the government support should be delivered within the budget, carefully costed and limited in duration.

“Support should be well-targeted, on budget, carefully costed and time-bound,” IMF Mission Chief Evan Papageorgiou said.

The IMF warned that poorly designed support could undermine fiscal and debt sustainability, erode confidence and reverse the recovery. Its prescription is to reflect the international fuel price movements in the domestic prices while using the poverty-targeted cash transfers to cushion the impact on those least able to absorb it.

For the households and businesses, the implications extend beyond the fuel pump. The higher fuel costs can raise the transport and distribution expenses, adding pressure to the living costs and business margins.

The IMF also called for stronger social safety nets, saying successive shocks had exposed the need to improve their coverage, targeting and responsiveness.

That would require assistance to reach the vulnerable households more effectively and respond more quickly when the economic conditions deteriorate.
