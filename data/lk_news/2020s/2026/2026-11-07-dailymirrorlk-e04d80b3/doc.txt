Fairway sets sights beyond Colombo as Kandy, Habarana and Yala join 500-key push

Kandy, Habarana and Yala resorts to add 80 keys to portfolio already past 250

Robotics and self-check-in anchor lean operating model at Fairway Colombo

Fairway is turning its gaze beyond the capital, with resorts in Kandy, Habarana and Yala in the pipeline that would add 80 keys to a portfolio already lifted past 250 by the Fairway Trinco deal, carrying the group to two-thirds of its 500-key ambition, according to Fairway Leisure’s top management.

Over a luncheon with reporters in Colombo recently, Leisure Sector Chief Executive Officer Abdul Basheer Cader and Chief Operating Officer Gemunu Cooray outlined a model where the properties acquired by the group are handed to its leisure arm for operation.

The established operations account for over 210 keys, of which 181 sit at Fairway Colombo, the Fort hotel that has anchored the business since 2016. Across the wider group, Fairway Holdings employs around 600 staff.

Fairway Trinco brought a further 46 keys when the Trincomalee beachfront resort came under Fairway’s management in September through a long-term agreement, under which it is being lifted to a four-star standard. The addition took the portfolio past 250 keys, consistent with the group’s own reckoning that it stands roughly halfway to its goal.

The next leg, mapped out at the luncheon, takes in 33 keys at Mount Randholee Resorts & Spa in Kandy, 24 at Sorowwa Resort and Spa in Habarana, in the heart of the Cultural Triangle and 23 at Tranquil Sanctuary near Yala. The management described these as “all in the making”, noting that growth would come largely through management contracts, with some properties to be acquired outright. Dambulla and Anuradhapura also feature on the group’s radar.

Should all three materialise, the portfolio would climb to around 335 keys, leaving more than 160 still to be secured. Colombo’s 181 keys, which account for about 70 percent of the portfolio today, would see their share ease to around 55 percent, while the keys outside the capital would roughly double to over 150, spread across the hill country, the Cultural Triangle, the east coast and down south.

Beyond the city, the group’s existing stays are more intimate in scale, led by The Planter’s Legacy, three cottages overlooking Gregory Lake in Nuwara Eliya, where Fairway also runs a park of eight static caravans sleeping two, four or six guests, each fitted with a compact kitchen, having retired its mobile motor homes.

In Galle, Fairway Sunset offers eight two and three-bedroom serviced apartments, while Galkanda Manor in Matale is managed rather than owned.

However, Fairway Colombo was not spared a dip in March, reliant as it is on India and other Asian markets for volume. A deep bench of corporate clients helped steady occupancy, which the officials pointed out “didn’t drop below 85 percent”.

The arrangement cuts both ways, they explained, with corporates filling rooms in the off-season on the understanding that peak-season rates cannot be extended to them, a balance that has reshaped the hotel’s guest mix.

That resilience, the management argued, rests on a lean operating model in which most services are outsourced and only the essentials are kept in-house. Fairway Colombo’s delivery robot, introduced in 2024, is an extension of that thinking, handling in-room orders that the officials conceded staff did not relish.

It moves within the hotel’s floor-level access control, which confines guests to their own floors and politely asks anyone in its path to step aside. The management noted that the hotel was recognised in 2025 as Sri Lanka’s most technologically advanced.

Positioning Fairway Colombo firmly as a city hotel, they cited high taxes and investors’ expectations of healthy returns as reasons to steer clear of such labour-intensive service.

Automation is set to go further still, with self-check-in to be rolled out across all 181 keys at Fairway Colombo within two years.

Meanwhile, the group launched its own travel desk earlier this year to streamline the handling of incoming guests and is pushing its travel arm, Fairway Travels, as a lever to lift the yields quickly.

“All hotels have a travel desk but we want to do it better,” an official observed.

Beyond its rooms, Fairway Colombo has carved out a place on the city’s cultural calendar, staging a showcase of traditional dance every Thursday from 6:30 p.m. to 8:30 p.m. whatever the season and hosting Art & Jazz on the Street along Hospital Street on the last Saturday and Sunday of each month.
