CM Vijay’s ally visits LTTE ex-chief Prabhakaran’s home in Sri Lanka. BJP slams, Congress stays mum

INDIA TODAY - A visit to the former residence of slain chief of Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE), Velupillai Prabhakaran, by Viduthalai Chiruthaigal Katchi (VCK) president Thol Thirumavalavan has triggered a political controversy in Tamil Nadu, with the BJP accusing him of insulting former Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi's memory. The Congress, which shares power with the VCK in Chief Minister C Joseph Vijay's government, has so far remained silent. Rajiv was the Congress President when he was killed by the LTTE on May 21, 1991.

For a rainbow coalition like the one in place in Tamil Nadu, there are certain no-go areas for its constituents. The former home of Prabhakaran in Sri Lanka is one such politically sensitive site. Thirumavalavan, a key ally of Vijay's government, visited the property associated with the former LTTE chief, whose organisation assassinated Rajiv Gandhi in 1991.

On Tuesday (November 17), VCK chief Thol Thirumavalavan visited the site of Prabhakaran's former residence in Valvettithurai, in Sri Lanka's Jaffna district. Videos shared by the Dalit leader showed him walking around the property where Prabhakaran spent his childhood.

Prabhakaran's house, however, no longer exists. The Sri Lankan Army demolished Prabhakaran's ancestral home in 2010, a year after the end of the civil war. The property was subsequently levelled, and what remains today is largely an empty plot of land, with a board marking the site where the LTTE chief once lived.

Thirumavalavan was in Sri Lanka to attend the funeral of veteran Tamil politician MK Sivajilingam, held on November 16. He also visited the Chemmani memorial site in Jaffna, associated with the discovery of mass graves, to express solidarity with victims and their families.

His visit to Prabhakaran's former residence has now drawn a sharp political reaction from the BJP, which has sought to turn the issue into a question for the Congress.

Narayanan Thirupathy, chief spokesperson of the BJP in Tamil Nadu, strongly condemned Thirumavalavan's visit to Prabhakaran's Jaffna home in a post on X on Tuesday.

He called it a "shameful and unpardonable act", Thirupathy said Prabhakaran was not a freedom fighter but the head of a banned terrorist organisation responsible for the assassination of former Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi.

"Any Indian political leader who chooses to visit his house is not showing solidarity with Tamils, he is insulting the memory of Rajiv Gandhi," he said.

The BJP spokesperson also questioned the Congress's continued association with the VCK in the Tamil Nadu government.

Pointing to the fact that the Congress lost its former Prime Minister to an LTTE suicide bombing, Thirupathy accused the party of sharing power with a political ally whose leader had visited the residence of the man responsible for the assassination.

He went a step further, demanding that the Congress either break its alliance with the VCK or carry the political burden of sharing power with a party whose leader, according to him, was glorifying Rajiv Gandhi's assassin.

The BJP's attack has put the Congress in a political catch 22 position. The party has so far not issued a public response to Thirumavalavan's visit or the allegations made by the BJP.

Velupillai Prabhakaran founded and led the LTTE, an armed separatist organisation that fought for an independent Tamil state in Sri Lanka. The organisation was responsible for several attacks during Sri Lanka's civil war and was banned in India.

Its most consequential association with Indian politics remains the assassination of former Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi.

On May 21, 1991, Rajiv Gandhi was assassinated by an LTTE suicide bomber at Sriperumbudur in Tamil Nadu while campaigning for the Lok Sabha elections. The assassination remains one of the most defining and painful events in the Congress party's history.

For the Congress, therefore, any public gesture involving Prabhakaran carries political sensitivities.

A visit to the former residence does not establish that Thirumavalavan endorses the LTTE's armed campaign or its assassination of Rajiv Gandhi. His engagement with Sri Lankan Tamil issues has long involved questions of displacement, political representation, human rights and accountability for alleged wartime atrocities.

His attendance at Sivajilingam's funeral and visit to the Chemmani memorial also need to be viewed in that broader context.

However, political symbolism cannot be separated from historical memory. A public visit to the former residence of the man who led the organisation responsible for Rajiv Gandhi's assassination inevitably invites political questions, particularly when the visitor belongs to an alliance that includes the Congress.

The controversy is also significant because of the political arithmetic underpinning Vijay's government.

The Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam (TVK) emerged as the single-biggest party in the 2026 Tamil Nadu Assembly election, winning 108 seats. However, it fell short of the 118 seats required for a majority in the 234-member Assembly.

Vijay needed the support of other parties to form the government. The Congress, Left parties, Indian Union Muslim League and VCK extended support, taking the alliance's numbers beyond the majority mark.

The VCK had only two MLAs, but its political importance was not limited to its legislative strength. Its support was part of the coalition arithmetic that helped Vijay cross the majority threshold.

Thirumavalavan is also an influential Dalit political voice in Tamil Nadu, and his party brings questions of social justice, Dalit rights and Tamil identity into the ruling alliance.

His visit, therefore, presents Vijay's government with a politically sensitive situation. While the chief minister might not be directly involved in the controversy, the actions of an important ally have given the opposition an opportunity to question the ideological and historical contradictions within the ruling coalition.

Members of the Nehru-Gandhi family have said they have forgiven Rajiv Gandhi's killers and bear no ill-will against anyone.

In 2018, Rahul Gandhi, then the Congress President, said at an event in Malaysia that he and his sister, Priyanka Gandhi, had "completely forgiven" their father Rajiv Gandhi's killers.

He reiterated that in 2021, saying, "I don't have anger or hatred towards anybody. Of course, I lost my father and for me, it was a very difficult time."

Despite the stance, the Congress finds itself in a delicate position.

Rajiv Gandhi's assassination remains a defining historical issue for the party. At the same time, its participation in Vijay's government requires it to maintain working relations with the VCK, which is an important constituent of the ruling arrangement.

A public confrontation with Thirumavalavan could create friction within the coalition. However, remaining silent could give the BJP an opportunity to question the Congress's position on the LTTE and Prabhakaran.

The BJP has already sought to make the issue of the Congress's political consistency, rather than just Thirumavalavan's visit.

In Tamil Nadu, where the Sri Lankan Tamil question carries a long and complicated political history, the response of the Congress and Vijay's government could prove as significant as Thirumavalavan's visit itself.
