Programme planned to certify chemical-free fruits

Colombo, August 21 (Daily Mirror) - The Government is planning to introduce a certification programme to ensure that fruits sold in the local market are free from harmful chemical residues, while measures are also being considered to promote safe fruit-ripening practices.

Trade, Commerce, Food Security and Cooperative Development Minister Wasantha Samarasinghe instructed officials to identify fruit-ripening facilities operating under hygienic conditions and develop a mechanism to certify them at a special discussion held at the Ministry yesterday (20).

The programme is also expected to promote the establishment of technical facilities where fruits can be ripened safely without the use of toxic chemicals.

The Minister stressed that all stakeholders in the fruit supply chain, including farmers, middlemen, fruit traders and supermarket chains, should be made aware of the importance of producing, distributing and selling fruits free from harmful substances.

He instructed officials to launch awareness programmes on the benefits of certification and promote Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) among those involved in fruit production and distribution.

As an initial step, officials were also directed to identify several fruit producers and establish model technical stalls for the safe ripening of fruits. The Department of Agriculture, Sri Lanka Standards Institution (SLSI), Industrial Technology Institute (ITI) and National Post-Harvest Management Institute are expected to provide technical assistance and expertise for the initiative.

Another key measure discussed was the introduction of rapid test kits capable of detecting chemical residues in fruits within a short period. Officials were instructed to identify suitable testing kits and take steps to procure them to facilitate the testing of fruits entering the market.

Representatives of the Consumer Affairs Authority, Department of Agriculture, Fruit Research and Development Institute, SLSI, ITI, National Post-Harvest Management Institute, Hector Kobbekaduwa Agrarian Research and Training Institute and Sri Lanka Customs attended the discussion.

Several measures were identified during the meeting, including the introduction of a special certification for traders who comply with SLS 1767 and Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) standards and do not use toxic chemicals. Consumers would then be encouraged to purchase fruits from certified outlets.

Plans were also discussed to establish standards for safe fruit-ripening chambers using ethylene gas and provide technical assistance to small and medium-scale traders.

Training programmes will be conducted in collaboration with institutions such as HARTI, ITI and SLSI to educate traders on safe fruit-ripening methods and the proper identification of ripe fruits.
