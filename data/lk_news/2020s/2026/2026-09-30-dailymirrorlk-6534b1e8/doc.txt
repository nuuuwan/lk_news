Gotabaya Rajapaksa: Claims of disappearances were made to damage Sri Lanka’s name

Colombo, Sep. 30 (Daily Mirror) - Former President Gotabaya Rajapaksa yesterday told the Colombo Magistrate’s Court that allegations of abductions and disappearances of individuals allegedly committed during his tenure as Defence Secretary were completely false and that they had been made entirely with the intention of gaining political advantage and damaging the reputation of the country.

Rajapaksa made these remarks while giving evidence via Zoom from the Colombo Magistrate’s Court in connection with a case being heard before the Jaffna Magistrate’s Court over the disappearance of social activists Lalith Weeraraj and Kugan Muruganandhan on December 10, 2011.

The complaint was filed before the Jaffna Magistrate’s Court by relatives of the missing persons, with Gotabaya Rajapaksa named as a witness in the case.

The former President had previously informed court that he was unable to travel to the Jaffna Magistrate’s Court to give evidence due to threats to his life, and that he was prepared to give evidence before any court other than the Jaffna Magistrate’s Court.

Subsequently, when the case was taken up before the Jaffna Magistrate’s Court on February 6 this year, counsels representing Gotabaya Rajapaksa requested court to permit him to give evidence online.

Accordingly, the court granted permission for the request on June 2.

On that occasion, the court ordered Gotabaya Rajapaksa to appear before the Colombo Chief Magistrate’s Court today to give evidence.

The evidence was recorded from the Jaffna Magistrate’s Court on behalf of the prosecution under the direction of Counsel Nuwan Bopage.

While giving evidence, Gotabaya Rajapaksa, responding to several questions put to him, said that he could not remember the relevant incidents as a considerable period of time had passed.

He also stated that claims by certain persons that the military and intelligence agencies were involved in incidents of abductions and disappearances had been made for political advantage and to damage the reputation of the country.

Q: Do you accept that if civilians disappear in a country, it poses a threat to national security?

A: Gotabaya Rajapaksa: “People disappear for various reasons. The police take specific action regarding such incidents. Not every disappearance is brought before the National Security Council. There are instances where information is received. If it is a special situation, information has been received.”

Thereafter, while giving evidence regarding the alleged abduction of former Western Provincial Council member Sagala Senaratne, Gotabaya Rajapaksa said that he did not remember the incident.

Q: When former Western Provincial Council member Sagala Senaratne was abducted, you received information regarding it, didn’t you?

A: Gotabaya Rajapaksa: “I cannot answer that, Your Honour. That happened 15 years ago.”

Q: Sagala Senaratne stated at a media briefing that his life was saved because of the then President and you. Do you remember that?

Q: I suggest to you that you became aware of the abduction of Sagala Senaratne after it occurred. Do you accept that?

Thereafter, questions were put to him regarding the abduction of journalist Keith Noyahr. Responding to those questions, Gotabaya Rajapaksa said that he remembered Keith Noyahr but did not remember the details of the incident concerning him.

A: Gotabaya Rajapaksa: “I remember him. I remember the incident to some extent. I do not remember the details.”

Q: What did you mean when you said you remember it to some extent?

A: Gotabaya Rajapaksa: “I remember that an incident took place. I did not come prepared for this today. That is why I said I remember it to some extent.”

A: Gotabaya Rajapaksa: “Your Honour, to my knowledge, Keith Noyahr had not disappeared.”

Q: Do you know that he was abducted by a group of persons and subjected to torture?

A: Gotabaya Rajapaksa: “I do not know that he was subjected to an abduction. But I know that he disappeared and was found.”

Q: Do you remember receiving a telephone call informing you that he had disappeared?

A: Gotabaya Rajapaksa: “It was something that happened 15 years ago. I do not remember now who spoke to me over the telephone.”

Q: Do you remember informing the then Inspector General of Police Jayampathi Wickramaratne about it?

A: Gotabaya Rajapaksa: “If I had learned about such an incident, I would have informed the Inspector General of Police about it.”

Q: Do you remember informing Defence Secretary Major General Hendawitharana about it?

A: Gotabaya Rajapaksa: “This happened a long time ago. Therefore, I do not remember it now.”

Q: Do you know that Keith Noyahr had sustained serious injuries and was later left at a certain location?

Q: I suggest to you that Keith Noyahr was released without being killed after being abducted, on your instructions.

A: Gotabaya Rajapaksa: “I reject that, Your Honour. I initially said that such an incident had occurred. I do not know how it happened or who did it. Also, when I am asked about it after 15 years, I do not remember.”

Q: I suggest to you that in a report submitted to the Criminal Investigation Department in January 2017, you had reported the matter to the Deputy Inspector General of Police.

Q: Do you remember that five officers of the Army Tripoli Platoon were named as suspects in connection with the incident?

Q: I suggest to you that, as Defence Secretary at the time, you directly intervened and gave instructions regarding abductions and disappearances.

A: Gotabaya Rajapaksa: “I reject that. I was not involved in any operation on the ground. My duty as Defence Secretary was to convey the policies adopted by the Government to those institutions. I was not directly involved in any operation on the ground.”

Q: Is the witness saying that there were operations by persons in the security forces involving abductions and disappearances?

A: Gotabaya Rajapaksa: “What I am saying, Your Honour, is that I was not involved in any operation. I functioned as a civilian. I did not give instructions in any way regarding other matters. I did not have the authority to do so.”

Q: In addition to serving as Defence Secretary, you served as President of the country from 2019 to 2022, correct?

Q: As Defence Secretary and subsequently as President, you were aware of abductions and disappearances in this country, weren’t you?

A: Gotabaya Rajapaksa: “Your Honour, those were matters presented with the intention of gaining political advantage. There is no truth in them. Those things were said with the intention of damaging the reputation of the country and gaining political advantage.”

Further evidence is scheduled to be recorded on November 27.
