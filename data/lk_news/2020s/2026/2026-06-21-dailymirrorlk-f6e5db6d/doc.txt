New regulations tighten monitoring of import payments

The government has introduced stringent regulations to tighten oversight of outward remittances linked to import transactions, responding promptly to recent disclosures of an $85 million bogus import payment fraud.

Aimed at systematically tracking payments and halting further foreign exchange leakages, the new measures require strict coordination between commercial banks and Sri Lanka Customs to verify all international transactions.

The framework was implemented through the Imports and Exports (Control) Regulations No. 06 of 2026, published in Gazette Extraordinary No. 2493/39 on June 18 under the Imports and Exports (Control) Act No. 1 of 1969. Signed by President Anura Kumara Dissanayake in his capacity as the Minister of Finance, Planning, and Economic Development, the directives officially took effect on June 19.

The gazette specifically amends the Special Import License and Payment Regulations No. 1 of 2011. Under the revised Regulation No. 4, all commercial banks are now mandated to assign a unique transaction number to every import-related foreign currency payment. Banks must immediately transmit detailed information to the Customs Department in real-time, including the importer’s Taxpayer Identification Number, the addresses of both the remitter and beneficiary, banking codes, and proforma invoice numbers.

A central feature of the regulatory update, introduced under the new Regulation No. 8(4) is the mandatory registration of importers. Any entity seeking to initiate an advance payment for goods must first be formally registered as an eligible importer with Sri Lanka Customs. Commercial banks are strictly prohibited from processing advance payments unless this registration is complete, ensuring that all outward remittances are backed by legitimate commercial activities.

Addressing concerns over potential trade disruptions, Deputy Finance and Planning Minister Dr. Anil Jayantha Fernando clarified that the gazette is strictly designed to monitor foreign exchange outflows and secure the national reserves. He emphasised that the regulations target preventing unscrupulous entities from using the import-export process as a cover to siphon funds overseas, and will not impose new restrictions or bureaucratic bottlenecks on legitimate trade operations.
