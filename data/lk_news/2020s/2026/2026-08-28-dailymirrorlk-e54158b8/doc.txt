Development focus should not be lost

Sri Lanka stands at a crucial point in its history. After years of economic difficulties, political uncertainty and declining public confidence, the country needs the  government to  provide stability, restore investor confidence and deliver meaningful improvements to the lives of its people. While accountability for past wrongdoing is important, the Government must ensure that its pursuit of those responsible does not overshadow the equally urgent task of national development.

There is no question that corruption, abuse of public funds and misuse of power must be addressed. Sri Lanka has suffered enormously from poor governance and decisions that placed political interests above the national interest. If credible evidence exists against individuals who have misused public resources, investigations should be carried out and justice should take its course. A functioning democracy cannot turn a blind eye to wrongdoing.

However, accountability must not become the central measure of government performance. Citizens did not elect a government merely to investigate the past. They also expect it to create a better future.

The danger arises when political attention becomes excessively concentrated on previous governments and their alleged failures. Endless investigations, accusations and political arguments may satisfy short-term political expectations, but they do little to put food on people’s tables, create employment or improve public services. Sri Lanka’s economic recovery remains fragile. The country needs sustained investment, stronger exports, improved productivity and new employment opportunities. Small and medium-sized businesses need an environment in which they can grow. Farmers need reliable markets and modern agricultural support.

These challenges cannot wait while politicians remain occupied with yesterday’s battles.

One of the most serious problems facing Sri Lanka is the continuing departure of skilled and educated young people seeking better opportunities abroad. Their decision is understandable when salaries, career prospects and living conditions elsewhere appear more attractive. But every professional who leaves represents not merely an individual loss, but a potential loss to the country’s future development. The Government must therefore create conditions in which young Sri Lankans can believe that their ambitions can be fulfilled at home.

Infrastructure development must also remain a priority. Roads, public transport, energy, digital connectivity, water supply and urban facilities are essential to economic growth. Development should not be limited to Colombo or major cities. Rural communities must also benefit from investment and modernisation.

At the same time, education and healthcare require sustained investment. A country’s greatest resource is its people, and no long-term development strategy can succeed without a healthy, educated and skilled population. Government policies should therefore focus not only on immediate economic indicators but also on building human capital for the next generation.

None of this means that the Government should abandon investigations into past wrongdoing. Quite the opposite. Those found guilty through due process should face appropriate legal consequences. But the process must be transparent and free from the perception that investigations are being used as political weapons. Justice also loses credibility when it appears selective. If accountability is the objective, the same standards must apply regardless of political affiliation, position or influence.

The Government must also resist the temptation to present every national problem as the result of its predecessors. Governments inherit problems, but they are elected to solve them. At some point, explanations about the past must give way to measurable results in the present.

Sri Lanka’s people need to see progress in their everyday lives. They want reliable public services, affordable living costs, decent employment, better education, efficient transport and an economy capable of providing hope for their children. The country cannot afford a choice between accountability and development. It needs both. The past should be investigated where necessary, but the future must remain the Government’s principal concern.

Sri Lanka has already lost valuable time to political conflict, economic mismanagement and instability. Another generation should not be asked to pay the price because the country remains trapped in arguments about the past.

The Government must therefore look backwards only long enough to learn the lessons and establish accountability, while looking forward with determination. Sri Lanka needs a government that can deliver justice without losing sight of progress. The past must be accounted for, but the future must be built.
