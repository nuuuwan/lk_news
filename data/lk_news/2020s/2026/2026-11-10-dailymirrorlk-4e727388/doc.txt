Parliament is Debating the Wrong Emergency

Parliament is debating the Protection of the State from Terrorism Bill and the Prevention of Organised Crimes Bill. Both deserve public consultation and broad consensus. The President has rightly assured that they will not be rushed to a vote without it.

Yet while Parliament debates terrorism, a more immediate crisis is unfolding inside our own homes. It needs no weapon. It begins with a phone we hand to our child.

Every parent knows the moment. The child is crying or restless. So we hand over our phone and say, watch YouTube, do not disturb. We believe we are buying harmony. In truth, we are creating dependence. Today, a five-year-old can unlock a phone and open YouTube or TikTok. A seven-year-old learns to hide what he watched. A nine-year-old screams when the phone is taken away. An eleven-year-old can access pornography, violence and drug content on a parent’s phone. This is not harmless entertainment.

Australia has shown that a country can act. It became the first nation to ban children under 16 from social media on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, YouTube, X, Reddit and Twitch. There is no parental consent loophole. The responsibility is on the platforms, with fines up to AUD 49.5 million. New Zealand, the UK, France and Turkey are considering similar measures. Sri Lanka must follow with a Children’s Online Safety Bill now. First, no social media account under 16. Platforms must verify age. Second, any device given to a child must operate in Kids Mode by law, with no autoplay and no algorithm feeding violence or sexual content to a six-year-old. Third, punish the platforms, not exhausted parents.

A terrorist can destroy life once. Phone addiction damages a child’s brain, eyes, sleep, studies, manners and respect every single day. Which is the greater threat? A terrorist in hiding, or a generation that cannot sit for ten minutes without a screen?

Parliament must debate terrorism properly. But first, it must protect our children. A country that cannot protect its children from a phone cannot protect itself from anything.
