Construction growth slows in September amid bitumen shortage

Sri Lanka’s construction activity expanded at a slower pace for the second consecutive month in September, with the Construction Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) easing to 57.8 from 59.4 in August, while a persistent shortage of bitumen delayed several road projects, Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) data showed.

The September reading marked the slowest pace of expansion since March, when the index stood at 57.1, and was 3.6 points below July’s 61.4. When compared to the same month last year, the index was 9.8 points lower than the 67.6 recorded in September 2025, which at the time marked the strongest rise in activity since late 2021. A reading above 50 denotes expansion on a month-on-month basis.

According to CBSL, growth was supported by a sustained flow of new construction projects. However, the shortage of bitumen remained a major constraint, a bottleneck that survey respondents have repeatedly flagged since mid-year.

In contrast, the New Orders Index rose to 65.6 from 62.5 in August, driven primarily by government-funded projects, marking its third successive monthly gain. The gap between new orders and overall activity widened to 7.8 points from 3.1 points a month earlier, suggesting that fresh work was coming in faster than contractors were able to execute it. Hiring also moderated, with the Employment Index easing to 58.1 from 59.7 in August and 61.8 in July. Recruitment was supported by steady project availability, although many firms continued to cite shortages of skilled workers, the Central Bank noted.

The Quantity of Purchases Index increased to 62.5 from 59.4 in August, reflecting procurement requirements for ongoing and newly awarded projects.

Meanwhile, supply-side pressures intensified, with the Suppliers’ Delivery Time Index climbing 6.2 points to 65.6, its highest reading since May’s 71.2, signalling that delivery times lengthened at a faster rate than in August.

Expectations for construction activity over the next three months remained positive, underpinned by newly awarded projects, despite ongoing challenges in securing materials and skilled labour.

Sri Lanka’s expansion continued to contrast with much of Europe, where construction activity contracted in the Eurozone, the UK, France, Germany and Italy, although the pace of decline eased in all but Germany. Ireland was the only market in the comparison to record growth, albeit at a slower rate, as per S&P Global data cited by the Central Bank.
