Controversial 22nd Amendment to Constitution to be presented in Parliament today

Colombo, Aug. 18 (Daily Mirror) - The Twenty-Second Amendment to the Constitution Bill, which is at the centre of controversy, will be presented to Parliament today, opening the door to legal battles.

If enacted, it provides for the extension of the retirement age for Supreme Court judges to 67 and for Appeal Court judges to 65.

The date of retirement of the Chief Justice is also extended by the legislation. “The date of retirement of Chief Justice shall be either the date on which he or she reaches the age of sixty-seven years or the date on which a period of six years is completed from the date of appointment as the Chief Justice, whichever occurs earlier," as per the Bill

In addition, the Judicature Amendment Bill extends the retirement age of Magistrates and District judges up to 62 years, while it will also increase the cadre of High Court judges to 120 from the current number of 110.

The presentation of the 22nd amendment will pave the way for legal battles against the legislation. A period of seven days will be provided for anyone to challenge the legislation after the presentation as per the law.

Free Lawyers CEO Keerthi Tennekoon said they will file a petition against the Bill, calling on the Supreme Court to declare that the legislation will have to be approved by a special majority and by the people through a referendum. Groups of President’s Counsel are also expected to file petitions against the legislation, as it has been revealed by President’s Counsel Tisath Wijegunawardene. Convener of the Joint Opposition Professor G. L. Peiris has also said that they will file legal action against the amendment this week.

The two legislations were gazetted on August 7, while many, including the Mahanayake Theras, have voiced their concern.
