Sri Lanka prepares rubber sector for EU deforestation rules

ECONOMYNEXT – A technical workshop was held in Colombo to strengthen Sri Lanka’s understanding of and preparedness for the European Union Deforestation Regulation (EUDR).

The workshop, organised by the European Union-funded Green Recovery Facility (GRF), implemented by Expertise France, brought together representatives of government institutions, the rubber industry and other stakeholders to engage directly with European experts on the regulation’s requirements and implications for Sri Lankan exporters.

The EUDR aims to minimise the EU’s contribution to global deforestation and forest degradation by setting requirements for certain commodities – cattle, cocoa, coffee, oil palm, rubber, soya and wood – placed on or exported to the EU market.

Natural rubber and range of rubber-derived products, including tyres, rubber hoses and conveyor belts, fall within the regulation’s scope, making traceability, reliable production data and supply chain coordination important considerations for Sri Lanka’s rubber industry.

“Rubber is an important export sector for Sri Lanka, supporting livelihoods and contributing to the national economy. Maintaining access to the European Union market requires the sector to adapt to evolving requirements, including the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR),” Gunadasa Samarasinghe, Secretary to the Ministry of Plantation and Community Infrastructure said.

“It is therefore essential that producers, processors and exporters are equipped with the knowledge and systems needed to meet these requirements and maintain their competitiveness.”

The workshop provided an opportunity to engage directly with the European Commission, clarify the EUDR requirements and identify practical steps to strengthen Sri Lanka’s preparedness, safeguard livelihoods and sustain rubber exports to the EU.

“The European Union is committed to working in partnership with Sri Lanka to support practical preparedness for the EUDR, especially through technical dialogue, stronger traceability and support for producers and smallholders,” Virginie Lafleur-Tighe, Head of Partnerships, Delegation of the European Union to Sri Lanka said.

“Our shared objective is to promote sustainable production, protect forests and help maintain responsible trade links with the EU.”

Alessio Cartosio, Policy Officer at the European Commission’s Directorate-General for Environment, outlined the objectives of the EUDR, the obligations of EU operators and traders, the implications for producers in countries exporting to the EU, and the arrangements for implementing the regulation.

Peter Aldinger, Forest Governance Expert at the European Forest Institute (EFI), presented findings from the Institute’s 2025 assessment of Sri Lanka’s preparedness for the EUDR, focusing on the rubber sector and areas requiring further attention.

The workshop also brought together government and private-sector perspectives through contributions from Rohana Hapugaswatte, Director General of the Rubber Development Department; Manoj Udugampola, representing the Planters’ Association of Ceylon; and Buddhi Gunasekara, Vice Chairman of the Colombo Rubber Traders’ Association.

“Understanding the requirements of the EUDR is an important first step. Equally important is translating this understanding into practical action, particularly in areas such as traceability, access to reliable information, institutional coordination and support for producers and businesses,” said Mahmoud Gaballah, Expertise France Country Representative and Portfolio Manager in Sri Lanka.

The workshop forms part of broader European Union-supported efforts to help Sri Lanka respond to evolving environmental and sustainability requirements affecting international trade. (Colombo/Oct22/2026)
