ENCARE SECURES NATIONAL BRONZE AT CNCI ACHIEVER AWARDS 2026

Building on its foundation in clinical waste management, EnCare has expanded into environmental engineering, bringing specialist waste services, recycling and resource recovery together through one trusted partner.

Sisili Hanaro EnCare (Pvt) Ltd received the National Bronze Award in the Large Category, Service Sector, at the CNCI Achiever of Industrial Excellence Awards 2026, organised by the Ceylon National Chamber of Industries (CNCI). The achievement marks an important milestone for a Sri Lankan company working in one of the country’s most demanding service sectors: the safe management of hazardous and specialised waste.

The award also draws attention to work that is often out of sight, yet essential to hospitals, industries and communities. Over more than a decade, EnCare has developed its services around the technical and regulatory demands of the waste it handles.

EnCare was established as a joint venture between Sisili Projects Consortium and South Korea’s Hanaro Eng. Co. Ltd. Its clinical waste management operations were developed under a tripartite agreement involving the Ministry of Health, the Central Environmental Authority (CEA) and the Sisili Hanaro joint venture. The arrangement combined local knowledge with international engineering experience within a regulated operating framework.

EnCare commenced clinical waste management operations in early 2014, when major hospitals were dealing with substantial backlogs. With its pilot facility still under development, the company handled approximately 30,000 kg of accumulated clinical waste alongside daily service requirements. During the COVID-19 pandemic, its in-house engineering team designed and built an interim treatment line within two weeks to help keep essential operations running. These experiences shaped the operational discipline and engineering expertise that remain central to EnCare’s continued progress.

Today, EnCare provides clinical waste collection, treatment and disposal for hospitals, laboratories and clinics. Its door-to-door service extends sanitary and clinical waste collection to households, bringing a structured solution to waste that can be difficult to manage through conventional domestic disposal channels.

For industrial and commercial clients, the portfolio includes pharmaceutical and cosmetic waste, hazardous chemical waste and marine waste management, alongside certified secure destruction of confidential documents and data. EnCare also operates an established business-to-business e-waste service covering segregation, recycling and secure handling of discarded electronics. These services allow clients to address different waste requirements through a single provider without overlooking the controls each stream demands.

Through its established Material Recovery Facility (MRF), EnCare collects, sorts and recycles plastic waste from business clients. Alongside its e-waste management services, these operations contribute to a circular economy by recovering valuable materials and returning them to productive use, reducing the volume of waste sent for disposal. This approach builds on EnCare’s core expertise in managing specialised waste streams.

Not all waste streams, however, are suitable for recycling. Clinical, pharmaceutical and hazardous chemical waste require controlled handling and where necessary, specialised treatment and disposal. EnCare’s integrated approach addresses both priorities, recovering resources wherever safe and practicable while ensuring that waste requiring specialised treatment is managed responsibly.

Compliance, traceability and service reliability remain central to EnCare’s operations. The company’s progress also rests from the experience of its engineering and operational teams and on sustained cooperation with regulatory authorities, public sector institutions, business partners and clients.

The National Bronze Award acknowledges how far that collective effort has brought EnCare. Its next chapter will be defined by the same practical challenge that shaped its beginnings: finding the right solution for each waste stream, whether that means safe treatment, secure destruction or recovering materials that still have value.
