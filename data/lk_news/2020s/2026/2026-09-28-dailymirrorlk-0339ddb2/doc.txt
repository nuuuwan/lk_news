Sri Lanka among world’s ten fastest improvers in WEF tourism index

Score up 4.3% to 3.85, more than double the global average gain

Climbs four places to 69th, second in South Asia after India

Sri Lanka ranked among the 10 fastest-improving economies globally in the latest benchmark for tourism development conditions. It lifted its score by 4.3 percent to climb four places to 69th among 110 economies, narrowly ahead of India on the pace of improvement, according to World Economic Forum (WEF) data.

Sri Lanka was one of seven developing South and South-East Asian economies among the WEF’s 10 fastest improvers between 2024 and 2026, and one of only two from South Asia alongside Nepal. Albania topped the list with a 7 percent gain, followed by Vietnam at 6.3 percent, Laos at 6.1 percent, Qatar at 6 percent and Malaysia at 5.8 percent. Thailand improved by 5.6 percent, the Philippines and Morocco by 5.5 percent each, and Nepal by 5.1 percent. India, which also posted a 4.3 percent gain on a rounded basis, narrowly missed the list.

Sri Lanka scored 3.85 out of 7 in the Travel & Tourism Development Index 2026. Its gain was more than double the 2.1 percent average improvement across all ranked economies, which the WEF described as the fastest pace since 2019. Even so, the country sits 0.33 points short of the top 50, where Israel holds 50th place with 4.18.

The WEF recalculated earlier editions under a revised framework covering 110 economies, which placed Sri Lanka 73rd in the comparable 2024 results. The 2024 edition, which assessed 119 economies, had originally ranked the country 76th, as reported by Mirror Business at the time.

However, the recent momentum is yet to restore lost ground. Sri Lanka still sits four places below the 65th position it held in the recalculated 2019 results, and its score has grown by just 2.7 percent over the seven years. That is marginally behind the 2.9 percent average improvement across the index over the same period.

Within South Asia, Sri Lanka ranked second of the five economies assessed. India leads the subregion after jumping nine places to 31st with a score of 4.42. Because India improved at a similar rate from a considerably higher base, the gap between the two neighbours widened marginally from around 0.55 points to 0.57 points. Nepal climbed eight places to 86th and Bangladesh held at 98th, while Pakistan fell seven places to 95th and was the only South Asian economy to record a lower score. The Maldives is not covered by the index.

Meanwhile, South-East Asian competitors turned faster improvement into bigger jumps in rank. Malaysia surged 13 places to 26th, Thailand rose 10 places to 38th and Vietnam advanced seven places to 52nd, while Laos moved up 10 places to 75th. Sri Lanka’s score is above the South Asian average of 3.71 but short of the Asia-Pacific average of 4.29, leaving it 13th among the 18 Asia-Pacific economies ranked. Among the 29 lower-middle-income economies in the index, based on the World Bank’s classification, Sri Lanka ranked fifth, behind India, Vietnam, the Philippines and Egypt.

While the report does not publish pillar scores for individual economies, its South Asia profile shows where the subregion’s weaknesses lie. South Asia scored just 1.43 out of 7 on tourist services and infrastructure and 3.24 on business environment, the lowest of any subregion globally on both counts.

By contrast, it was the top-ranked subregion for price competitiveness, at 5.73, and for tourism demand sustainability, at 4.52. Its natural resources score rose by 5.1 percent, the third-largest subregional increase.

That pricing advantage stands out against a global squeeze. Price competitiveness declined by 3.1 percent on average between 2024 and 2026, and travel became less affordable in three out of four economies as higher labour, food and energy costs fed into prices, the WEF noted.

Looking at the large emerging tourism economies, the report argued that “the next stage of convergence will depend less on attracting visitors and more on supporting them and travel and tourism businesses.” It went on to observe that “what separates them from the leaders is the depth of the infrastructure, services and enabling conditions needed to support tourism.”

Japan topped the 2026 rankings, displacing the United States, followed by Spain, Australia and France. The Institute of Policy Studies of Sri Lanka (IPS) served as the WEF’s partner institute for the country. (NF)
