Independent foreign team sought for post-mortem on tusker Kawanthissa’s death

A wildlife photographer has requested the Galgamuwa Magistrate’s Court to order an independent foreign team to conduct a post-mortem examination into the death of the tusker Kawanthissa.

The request was made by wildlife photographer Pathum Bandara, who submitted an affidavit and made several requests to court when the magisterial inquiry into the death of the tusker came up before the Galgamuwa Magistrate’s Court on November 4, 2026, by way of a motion.

President’s Counsel Upul Jayasuriya, appearing for Bandara, also requested the court to issue orders for an assessment of the monetary value of the tusker and to ensure the preservation of its remains and body parts.

He further sought an order bringing the investigation into the death of the tusker under the direct supervision of the Magistrate.

The Magistrate is scheduled to deliver the court’s decision on these requests on November 18.

Kawanthissa was an iconic, 11-foot-tall wild Asian elephant and one of Sri Lanka’s tallest and largest tuskers. It roamed the Galgamuwa forest range before dying on October 16, 2026. A gunshot injury to its right front leg was discovered, and the resulting impact is suspected to have contributed to its death.
