Is it time to do away with autocratic rule?

Brazilian president Lula Da Silva won a fourth term in office over his right-wing challenger Flávio Bolsonaro

Four decisive elections happening across four continents in a span of ten days could profoundly shape global politics. Already, two have concluded with ‘surprising’ results for both the contenders and the public.

Brazilian president Lula Da Silva won a fourth term in office over his right-wing challenger Flávio Bolsonaro. Despite his victory, analysts note that Lula faces the difficult task of funding his promised social and welfare programmes while reining in rising public debt levels and slowing economic growth.

In Serbia, its long-standing leader Aleksandar Vučić admitted defeat to a student-led protest movement in a snap parliamentary election. The student movement was formed less than two years ago in response to a railway station disaster blamed on corruption and negligence, but fought the election with candidates from a cross-section of Serbian society.

The attention has now diverted towards the Israeli election where the results are expected on October 29. While there has been no clear winner from early polls, analysts opine that Arab parties could make ‘kingmaker’ in the Israeli election with ‘existential dimensions.’

According to media reports, Israel’s Arab parties are projected to win 19 seats in the Israeli parliament, after voting among Palestinian citizens of Israel surged to their highest levels in decades. Both Netanyahu and Gadi Eisenkot, contenders at the ongoing election have said that they will not form a coalition with Arab parties. At the outset, Netanyahu said that right-wing parties have won a ‘decisive majority’ and called for the formation of a national ‘right-wing’ government. In the US, the upcoming mid-term polls on November 3 would determine who would control the US Congress for the rest of Trump’s presidency. The Democratic Party needs a net gain of just three seats to take control of the 435-member House of Representatives. To achieve a 51-49 Senate majority, Democrats need a net gain of four seats. That requires flipping at least two states Trump won by double digits in 2024, such as Ohio, Iowa, Alaska, Texas or Kansas.

According to experts, the upcoming mid-term poll is not a typical mid-term. Trump faces historic approval-rating lows due to an unpopular war in Iran, aggressive immigration enforcement, tariffs, high gas prices, and his co-opting of the Justice Department.

Statistics issued by the Council on Foreign Relations indicates the following; 46% of self-identified Democrats have said they would vote on Election Day mainly to oppose Trump. In comparison, 37 percent of self-identified Democrats have said they would be voting mainly to support a candidate in their area. 51% Americans do not have confidence in the honesty of US elections. 62.9% of adult voters think the country is on the ‘wrong track.’

According to prediction markets, prior to early September, Democrats had less than a 50% chance to sweep both chambers of Congress. But their odds have risen from around 30% earlier in the year, before the start of the U.S.-Iran war and higher gas prices that have hit consumers.

Irrespective of the country, factors that affect people including high cost of living could topple governments in no time. The multi-layered crisis in Europe marked by public unrest, soaring living costs, a severe housing crunch and mounting fiscal pressures is a classic example. Sri Lanka is no stranger to this phenomenon and so are several other Asian countries such as Bangladesh and Nepal.

Even though autocratic rule favours certain groups and people in authority, global data point to a prolonged trend of democratic backsliding and the expansion of authoritarianism. Think tanks on political advocacy indicate that global freedom has faced consecutive years of decline, with dozens of countries experiencing worsening political rights and civil liberties due to military coups, executive overreach, and crackdowns on dissent.

Despite facing distinct contemporary pressures, democracy remains an important form of government and a vital framework for human development, peace and long-term societal progress.
