Police issue safety alert for elderly living alone

Colombo, Nov. 17 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka Police have urged the public to take additional precautions to protect elderly people living alone, warning that isolated homes are increasingly being targeted by criminals seeking cash and gold jewellery.

Police said elderly people were particularly vulnerable when their children left for work, migrated overseas or moved to other provinces.

Police noted that some robberies targeting elderly people living alone had escalated into murders as perpetrators attempted to achieve their criminal objectives.

To minimise such risks, police have advised the public to take several precautionary measures, including conducting background checks on paid caregivers, domestic helpers and care centre employees, and obtaining police clearance reports before hiring them.

They have also recommended displaying emergency contact numbers, including those of the nearest police station, the 119 police emergency hotline, the 1990 Suwa Seriya ambulance service, a close neighbour and the family doctor, in a clearly visible place inside the house.

The public has been urged to inform the relevant Grama Niladhari and Community Police Committee about elderly people living alone in their areas to facilitate closer monitoring of their safety.

Police have further advised families to provide elderly parents with easy-to-use mobile phones containing pre-saved contact numbers of family members and police, maintain good relationships with neighbours and avoid allowing unknown persons into their homes for various services or odd jobs.

Ensuring adequate lighting around houses at night has also been highlighted as an important safety measure.

Police appealed to the public to pay greater attention to elderly parents living alone, regularly check on their well-being and take timely precautions to identify and prevent potential criminal threats.
