Sri Lankan marine biologist Asha de Vos named Fellow of UK Marine Biological Association

Colombo, Nov. 23 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lankan marine biologist Dr. Asha de Vos has been appointed a Fellow of the Marine Biological Association (MBA) in the United Kingdom in recognition of her contributions to marine biology, conservation and public understanding of the ocean.

The appointment, announced by the MBA on November 18, recognises her decades of research on marine mammals, particularly blue whales in the Northern Indian Ocean, as well as her work in marine conservation, education and science communication.

Under the Royal Charter of the MBA, Fellowship is awarded to individuals who have made outstanding and impactful contributions to marine biology.

The association described de Vos as an internationally renowned marine biologist, ocean educator, author and pioneer in long-term blue whale research in the Northern Indian Ocean. She is also recognised as Sri Lanka’s first deep-sea explorer and aquanaut.

Her research has contributed to scientific understanding of marine mammals in the waters around Sri Lanka, while her efforts to promote ocean conservation have helped make marine science accessible to wider audiences through public lectures, books, documentaries and social media.

De Vos has authored three books on the ocean aimed at readers of different age groups.

Educated at the University of St Andrews, the University of Oxford and the University of Western Australia, she later left academia to establish Oceanswell, recognised as Sri Lanka’s first marine conservation research and education organisation.

Through Oceanswell, she has worked to strengthen marine science capacity in Sri Lanka by training and mentoring a new generation of marine scientists.

The MBA said her Fellowship recognised not only her individual achievements but also the growing contribution of Sri Lanka’s marine science community.

De Vos has received several international honours for her work, including being named among the BBC's 100 Women in 2018. She was also named Sri Lankan of the Year by Lanka Monthly Digest in the same year.

In 2020, she received the inaugural Maxwell-Hanrahan Award in Field Biology and was named Sea Hero of the Year by Scuba Diving magazine.

Her other accolades include the Vanithabhimani Lifetime Achievement Award and the Tällberg-SNF-Eliasson Global Leadership Prize in 2021, and the British Ecological Society’s Equity and Diversity Champion title in 2023.

In 2026, she was selected as a Thinker-in-Residence at the Forrest Research Foundation.

She is also an Adjunct Research Fellow at the Oceans Institute of the University of Western Australia.
