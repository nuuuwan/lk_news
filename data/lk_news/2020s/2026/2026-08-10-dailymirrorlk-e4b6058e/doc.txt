Firms exiting tax holidays, exhausting losses face quarterly tax payments

KPMG urges taxpayers to assess revised circular’s implications ahead of first quarterly instalment deadline on August 15

Taxpayers expecting no assessable income in current year will not be required to make an instalment payment; however, they must submit a declaration to IRD in prescribed format

The companies emerging from tax holidays or exhausting carried-forward losses could face quarterly income tax payments in 2026/27, despite having no taxable income in the previous year, under the revised guidelines issued by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD).

The rules are contained in Revised Circular No. SEC/2026/E/06, dated August 6, which sets out the calculation of quarterly income tax instalments for the 2026/27 year of assessment and

A tax update issued by KPMG Sri Lanka flagged the revised circular and urged the taxpayers to assess its implications ahead of the first quarterly instalment deadline on August 15.

Under the revised guidance, a taxpayer with no taxable income in the previous year may still be required to estimate and pay tax, if carried-forward losses have expired or are no longer sufficient to offset income in the current year.

The circular illustrates this through a company that had no taxable income in 2025/26, after setting off the brought-forward business losses. With only a portion of those losses remaining for 2026/27, the company is required to estimate its taxable income and make quarterly payments based on the resulting liability. A similar approach applies to the companies whose tax exemptions, including those granted under the Board of Investment agreements, expired at the end of 2025/26. Such businesses must estimate their current tax liability using the previous year’s income and applying the tax rules and rates relevant to 2026/27, without taking the expired exemption into account.

The move could have cash-flow implications for the companies transitioning out of tax holidays or nearing the end of accumulated tax-loss relief, as the quarterly payments may arise before their final annual liability is determined.

The taxpayers expecting no assessable income in the current year will not be required to make an instalment payment. However, they must submit a declaration to the IRD in the prescribed format.

The businesses anticipating a significant decline in income may also seek to calculate lower instalments by excluding income from operations or investments that are not expected to continue. They must support the adjustment with calculations and evidence, demonstrating the loss of business or investment income.

The companies initially paying instalments under the standard method can request a revision later in the year, if their projected taxable income falls below the previous year’s level. Applications must be submitted ahead of the relevant instalment deadline together with the supporting information.

The standard calculation uses the previous year’s gross income tax liability, less instalments and withholding or advance income tax credits paid or expected to be paid during the current year. The remaining amount is divided among the instalments yet to fall due.
