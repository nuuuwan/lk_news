Debt-to-GDP ratio falls to 88.8% by June: Deputy FinMin

Sri Lanka’s debt-to-GDP ratio fell to 88.8 percent by June 2026, from 95 percent at end-2025, Finance Deputy Minister Dr. Anil Jayantha Fernando said, rejecting the opposition claims that the country’s debt burden has increased.

Dr. Fernando said the 95 percent debt-to-GDP level reached by December 2025 was a target that had originally been envisaged for 2032, with the ratio declining further during the first half of this year.

“The debt burden as a percentage of GDP was 95 percent by December 2025, a target to be achieved by 2032. By June 2026, it had further declined to 88.8 percent,” Dr. Fernando said, according to a statement issued by the Finance Ministry.

His comments come in response to the opposition allegations that the government has sharply increased borrowing, raising concerns over the trajectory of public debt, following Sri Lanka’s sovereign default and subsequent debt restructuring.

Dr. Fernando argued that an increase in the nominal amount of borrowing should not automatically be interpreted as an increase in the country’s debt burden, saying debt sustainability should instead be assessed against the government’s capacity to repay and manage its obligations.

He said the amount of debt and the capacity of a country to borrow were two separate economic considerations and that the claims over the debt position should be supported by statistical evidence. According to Dr. Fernando, Sri Lanka’s debt burden has declined both in numerical terms and relative to the size of the economy.

The government has maintained that fresh borrowing must be viewed within the broader context of debt sustainability, economic growth and repayment capacity, as Sri Lanka continues its post-default fiscal and debt restructuring programme.
