Amana Bank’s quarterly earnings slide 24% on impairment swing

Amana Bank’s third-quarter earnings per share fell 24 percent to 97 cents, from Rs.1.28 a year earlier, as an impairment charge and higher operating costs squeezed profit, despite a modest increase in the net financing income.

Profit after tax fell to Rs.535.6 million in the three months ended September 30, from Rs.705.9 million a year earlier, according to the bank’s interim financial statements filed with the Colombo Stock Exchange.

Total comprehensive income, which includes the changes in the value of certain investments, fell 38 percent to Rs.582.3 million.

The biggest drag on the quarterly earnings was a Rs.375.1 million swing in impairments. The bank booked a Rs.119 million charge, compared with a Rs.256.1 million write-back a year earlier.

The operating expenses rose 8 percent to Rs.1.45 billion, with the personnel costs and depreciation accounting for most of the increase.

The net financing income rose 2 percent to Rs.2.30 billion, as the financing expenses grew faster than the financing income.

The lower fee and trading income pulled the total operating income down one percent to Rs.2.71 billion. Pre-tax profit fell 27 percent to Rs.877.7 million, while a 32 percent decline in income tax expense cushioned the fall in earnings.

For the first nine months, the earnings per share rose to Rs.3.01, from Rs.2.92, with the net profit up 3 percent to Rs.1.66 billion. Pre-tax profit was broadly flat at Rs.2.75 billion and the lower income tax expense accounted for most of the increase in net profit.

The net asset value per share rose to Rs.48.36 at end-September, from Rs.45.52 at end-December. The shares closed September at Rs.26.80, about 45 percent below the book value.
