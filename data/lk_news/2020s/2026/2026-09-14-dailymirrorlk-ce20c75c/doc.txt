SLPA to launch FDI-backed logistics hub at Colombo Port in three months

SLPA to establish foreign direct investment-backed logistics hub within a free zone at the Port of Colombo in two to three months

Cabinet approval for a new committee to address maritime industry concerns expected this week, with active input from NVOCCs

The Sri Lanka Ports Authority (SLPA) is preparing to launch a comprehensive logistics hub within a free zone at the Port of Colombo over the next two to three months, backed by foreign direct investments (FDI).

The announcement was made during the 9th Annual General Meeting of the Sri Lanka Association of NVOCC Agents (SLANA), held in Colombo last week.

Addressing the gathering, SLPA Chairman Dr. Parakrama Dissanayake revealed that the upcoming project represents a significant policy shift aimed at transitioning the nation into a true logistics center.

“Over the last 40 years, while handling millions of TEUs, we never fully became a logistics hub because a logistics hub requires establishments outside the Port of Colombo. We never had that,” Dr. Dissanayake stated. “On the advice of the minister, within the next two to three months, we will be truly launching a logistics hub, ideally in a free zone in the Port of Colombo. So from a policy end, changing altogether, SLPA will be launching this with FDI.”

Speaking as the Chief Guest, Minister of Ports and Civil Aviation Anura Karunathilaka assured the shipping community that the government is actively responding to industry feedback regarding regulatory frameworks. Acknowledging the concerns raised by the local shipping community, he announced the imminent formation of a new industry committee to evaluate these issues, with cabinet approval expected this week.

“We have already prepared a cabinet paper; maybe next week or within next week you will get the cabinet approval to appoint a committee,” the Minister stated. He offered his firm assurance that the input of NVOCCs will be actively taken into consideration during the committee’s deliberations to ensure a fair and sustainable regulatory environment for local enterprises.

Both Minister Karunathilaka and Dr. Dissanayake took the opportunity to praise the critical role of NVOCCs in driving the country’s maritime success. Minister Karunathilaka highlighted that SLANA members are crucial to the national logistics framework and commended their ongoing contribution to the broader Sri Lankan economy.

Dr. Dissanayake echoed these sentiments, noting that NVOCCs operate at the vital intersection between ship operators and cargo owners, making them indispensable to the operational resilience of the Port of Colombo.

However, Dr. Dissanayake cautioned that the global shipping landscape is rapidly consolidating, placing terminal operators and NVOCCs increasingly at the mercy of major global players.

“Globally, all container ships put together, what is the total shipboard capacity? It’s 34.6 million TEUs. Of that 34.6 million TEUs, the top 10 shipping lines control 84.5 percent. The top five shipping lines control 65.2 percent,” he pointed out.

The top five carriers by capacity are MSC, Maersk, CMA CGM, COSCO and Hapag-Lloyd.

“The bottom line is that ports, terminal operators, NVOCCs, are at the mercy of these five shipping lines,” he added.  Noting that these leading carriers have transformed from simple ship operators into comprehensive supply chain service providers equipped with massive hardware advantages, he urged SLANA members to shift their operational roles.

“SLANA’s role should change from representing NVOCC agents, to supply chain orchestrators enabling Sri Lanka’s next-generation logistics,” he advised.

To remain competitive amidst these structural changes, Dr. Dissanayake elaborated on three essential pillars for the industry’s future agenda.

“Pillar number one is resilience. Always have another route,” he advised, referencing how the Sri Lankan tea industry successfully survived past crises by rerouting shipments. He identified digitalisation and sustainability as the second pillar, warning that future cargo shipments to Europe will demand strict accountability for carbon footprints.

Finally, he emphasised human capital and proactive agility as the third pillar. Reminding the industry that people ultimately make or break organizations, he urged proactive policy advocacy and concluded that the future will not belong to the biggest or the cheapest, but to the agile. (NF)
