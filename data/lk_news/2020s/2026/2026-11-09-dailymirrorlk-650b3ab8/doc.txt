Strong 2Q lifts Hemas 1H revenue to Rs.66.6 bn as borrowings triple on Twiga acquisition

Hemas Holdings PLC grew its second-quarter revenue by 17 percent year-on-year (YoY) to Rs.37.8 billion, but earnings attributable to shareholders slipped 3.0 percent to Rs.2.0 billion as higher finance costs and a heavier tax charge absorbed the rebound in operating profit, a stock filing showed.

However, the September quarter marked a sharp recovery from the June quarter, with EBITDA nearly doubling from Rs.2.3 billion to Rs.4.5 billion, up 10.5 percent YoY, and earnings more than doubling from Rs.937 million.

For the six months ended September, revenue grew 9.5 percent to Rs.66.6 billion and EBITDA edged up 0.9 percent to Rs.6.8 billion, reversing a 14.1 percent decline at the end of the first quarter. First-half earnings still fell 9.7 percent to Rs.2.9 billion, dragged by a weaker first quarter.

The group absorbed much of the increase in raw material, energy and freight costs triggered by the Middle East conflict to protect affordability and volumes, narrowing its gross margin by 3.1 percentage points to 29.7 percent. Nearly half of its revenue comes from businesses subject to regulated pricing.

“Experience has shown that volume erosion costs a business far more over the long term than a temporary adjustment to margins during a period of high inflation,” Group CEO Ashish Chandra noted in the interim report.

Net finance costs climbed to Rs.249 million in the quarter from around Rs.56 million a year earlier, as total borrowings, including bank overdrafts, more than tripled to Rs.25.2 billion by end-September from Rs.7.5 billion at end-March. The group, which still held net cash of around Rs.3.5 billion at end-June, closed the half with net debt of around Rs.11.9 billion and net gearing of 16.6 percent.

The operating cash outflow for the six months widened to Rs.8.4 billion from Rs.171 million a year earlier, driven by a Rs.12.3 billion increase in net working capital as the group built seasonal inventory at Atlas and Twiga and offered distributors longer payment terms.

All four sectors grew their quarterly revenue. Hospitals led with 19.3 percent growth and a 26.9 percent rise in earnings to Rs.265 million. Consumer Brands revenue rose 18.3 percent to Rs.15.6 billion, including Twiga from 17 August, but earnings fell 30.6 percent to Rs.1.1 billion.

Life Sciences earnings dropped 26.8 percent to Rs.623 million despite 16.2 percent revenue growth, as the business awaited approval of regulated price revisions.

Meanwhile, Hemas paid Rs.5.46 billion through Atlas Axillia for its first overseas acquisition, a 75 percent stake in Kenya’s Twiga Stationers & Printers Limited, recognising provisional goodwill of Rs.1.4 billion pending the valuation of Twiga’s intangible assets. Atlas also holds a put and call option over the remaining 25 percent, allowing either party to trigger its sale to Atlas.

The Board declared an interim dividend of Rs.0.25 per share, amounting to around Rs.749 million, payable on or before 4 December. The Employees’ Provident Fund (EPF) raised its stake to 5.39 percent from 4.18 percent at end-June, adding 36.3 million shares during the quarter to become the fifth largest shareholder.
