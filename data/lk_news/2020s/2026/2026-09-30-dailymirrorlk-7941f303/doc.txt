Govt to drop or amend Clause 17 of Anti- Corruption Bill requiring referendum

Colombo, Sep. 30 (Daily Mirror) - The Government has no intention of holding a referendum on Clause 17 of the Anti-Corruption (Amendment) Bill, which the Supreme Court has determined requires approval by the people, Cabinet Spokesman Minister Nalinda Jayatissa said yesterday.

He said the Government would seek to pass the remaining provisions of the Bill with the required two-thirds majority in Parliament, in accordance with the Supreme Court determination.

Speaking at the weekly Cabinet decision briefing, Jayatissa said the debate on the Bill would be held in Parliament on October 8.

Asked whether Clause 17, which deals with bail applications, would be removed or amended at the committee stage, the Minister said the Government would either remove or amend the provision.

The Supreme Court determined that Clause 17 is inconsistent with Article 4(c) of the Constitution and therefore requires the special majority stipulated under Article 84(2), as well as approval by the people at a referendum under Article 83.

The Court determined that Clauses 3, 4, 6(4) and 6(5) are not inconsistent with the Constitution and may be passed by a simple majority in Parliament.

However, Clause 6(7) was found to be inconsistent with Articles 12(1) and 14(1)(a) and may be passed only with the special majority required under Article 84(2).

The Court further determined that Clause 7 is inconsistent with Article 12(1) and that the proposed repeal of Section 81(e) should be withdrawn. If Clause 7 is retained in its present form, it too would require the special majority under Article 84(2), read with Article 82(5).

Meanwhile, Clause 11(1), which proposes the introduction of Section 88(1)(f), was found not to be inconsistent with Articles 12(1) or 14A(1) and may be passed by a simple majority.

Clause 11(2), as proposed to be amended at the committee stage, was also determined not to be inconsistent with Articles 10, 12(1), 14(1)(a) or 14A and may likewise be passed by a simple majority.
