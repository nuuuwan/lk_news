Avoid sun exposure from 11 am to 3 pm - Health officials

Colombo, Aug. 20 (Daily Mirror) - The Ministry of Health has warned the public that prolonged exposure to direct sunlight during the prevailing hot weather could cause serious heat-related illnesses and, in severe cases, lead to death.

According to Community Health Specialist Dr. Inoka Suraweera of the Environmental and Occupational Health Unit, people should take extra precautions as temperatures remain high across several parts of the country.

Speaking at a media briefing on El Niño weather conditions at the Health Promotion Bureau yesterday (19), she said excessive heat exposure could cause a range of health problems, including skin rashes and muscle cramps.

She said the risk is particularly high among young children, elderly people and those living with non-communicable or chronic diseases.

People who work outdoors are also at increased risk, with agricultural workers among those who may be exposed to direct sunlight for long periods, she added.

Dr. Suraweera advised the public to avoid unnecessary exposure to direct sunlight, particularly between 11 a.m. and 3 p.m., when heat and solar radiation can be particularly intense.

She urged those who have to work outdoors to take regular breaks and avoid remaining under direct sunlight for prolonged periods.

Maintaining adequate fluid intake is also essential to prevent dehydration, she said, advising the public to drink plenty of water throughout the day.

She also recommended drinking boiled and cooled water, stressing that “cooled” water does not necessarily mean ice-cold or refrigerated water.

Dr. Suraweera further advised people to use standardised skin-care products to minimise skin problems caused by prolonged exposure to heat and sunlight.

She warned that heat-related symptoms should not be ignored, particularly among vulnerable groups, as prolonged exposure to extreme heat can develop into serious health conditions
