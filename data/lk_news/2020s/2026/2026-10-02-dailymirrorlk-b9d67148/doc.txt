Sri Lanka’s trade gap hits US$7.2bn

Sri Lanka’s import bill grew more than seven times as fast as merchandise export earnings in the first eight months of 2026, widening the trade deficit to US$ 7.18 billion as higher fuel costs increased pressure on the country’s external finances.

Imports rose 24.1 percent to US$ 16.56 billion during January-August, while merchandise exports increased 3.3 percent to US$ 9.38 billion, according to the Central Bank’s latest external sector report.

The mismatch added US$ 2.92 billion to the trade deficit compared with the corresponding period of 2025, when the gap stood at US$ 4.26 billion. Stronger workers’ remittances helped cushion the deterioration, although weaker tourism earnings and a smaller services surplus reduced support from other foreign exchange sources.

“The current account recorded a cumulative deficit of US$ 291 million during January-August 2026, reflecting pressures on the external sector stemming from the escalation of the Middle East conflict,” the Central Bank said.

The eight-month current account deficit compared with a surplus of US$ 2.05 billion a year earlier, marking a substantial reversal in the balance of the country’s trade, services, income and transfer flows.

Fuel accounted for a sizeable part of the increase in import expenditure. The January-August fuel bill rose 61.6 percent year-on-year to approximately US$ 4 billion, despite monthly expenditure declining for the fourth consecutive month in August.

Vehicle imports totalled a further US$1.68 billion over the eight months. Spending on personal and commercial vehicle imports fell 24.2 percent year-on-year to US$189 million in August.

The Central Bank said the terms of trade deteriorated in both August and the first eight months, as import prices increased faster than export prices.

August brought some relief, with the current account recording a US$ 133.4 million surplus after four consecutive months of deficits. The Central Bank attributed the improvement to a lower trade deficit than in recent months, reflecting reduced import expenditure

However, the monthly trade gap remained wider than a year earlier. Merchandise imports rose 12.8 percent to US$ 1.91 billion in August, while exports fell 3.3 percent to US$ 1.24 billion, leaving a deficit of US$ 673.5 million against US$ 414.2 million in August 2025.

Growth in combined goods and services exports was also modest. Total receipts increased 1.5 percent to US$ 14.08 billion during January-August, with services earnings declining 1.8 percent to US$ 4.70 billion.

The services account surplus contracted 21.4 percent to US$ 2.10 billion as receipts weakened and payments overseas rose 22.9 percent to US$ 2.60 billion.

Tourism earnings fell 10 percent to US$2.06 billion during the eight months, while arrivals declined 2 percent to 1.54 million. The earnings estimates reflect the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority’s revised methodology, introduced in May and applied retrospectively from January 2026.

Computer and IT/BPO services receipts declined 16.5 percent to US$ 459.9 million over the same period, including a 56.3 percent year-on-year fall in August to US$ 31.4 million. The report did not explain the decline.

Sea and air transport receipts increased 4.6 percent to US$ 1.33 billion during January-August, providing some support to services earnings.

On the expenditure side, spending on travel abroad rose 62.1 percent to US$ 815.2 million, while payments for sea and air transport services increased 20.2 percent to US$ 800.1 million.

Gross official reserves rose to US$ 6.9 billion at end-August, supported by the Central Bank’s foreign exchange purchases. The reserves, which include the swap facility with the People’s Bank of China, provided 3.4 months of import coverage.

The rupee depreciated 6.3 percent against the US dollar during the year to end-September, closing the month at Rs.330.83.
