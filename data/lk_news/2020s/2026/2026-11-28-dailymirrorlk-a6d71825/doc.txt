Sri Lanka completes national GI registration for Ceylon Cinnamon

Sri Lanka has completed the national Geographical Indication (GI) registration of Ceylon Cinnamon, with the National Intellectual Property Office (NIPO) officially presenting the country’s first national GI certificate to the Sri Lanka Export Development Board (EDB).

The achievement follows a nearly decade-long effort involving the EDB, public and private sector institutions and stakeholders in the cinnamon industry.

The development comes after Ceylon Cinnamon became the first Sri Lankan product to receive Protected Geographical Indication (PGI) status from the European Union in February 2022.

A Geographical Indication is a mark that identifies a product or service as originating from a particular geographical area and reaching the market with a high standard of quality. This is the first GI registered in the European Union for a Sri Lankan product. Securing it for cinnamon, a crop unique to Sri Lanka, is a special distinction. Obtaining the “Ceylon Cinnamon GI” to represent the whole of Sri Lanka is a major achievement.

The purpose of registering Ceylon Cinnamon as a GI is to distinguish products with a Sri Lankan identity in the international market from substitutes. This gives Sri Lanka a competitive advantage and allows greater market diversity, higher production volumes and better prices from consumers.

It will also strengthen the competitiveness of Sri Lankan products in distinctive markets such as the European Union, increase exports, and generate higher incomes and employment through value chain

To elevate cinnamon from a minor export crop to a major commercial plantation crop, and to improve production, quality and exports, the Cinnamon Development Department of Sri Lanka was established with effect from 01 September 2023. In 2025, Sri Lanka earned US$ 250 million from cinnamon exports. With the GI status, the industry is expected to grow into a US$ 1 billion export industry by around 2030. With the aim of improving Sri Lanka’s value-added products, steps are also being taken to grant national Geographical Indication branding to export products in demand, such as Sri Lankan tea, pepper, kithul, pineapple, king coconut (thambili), blue sapphire and Dumbara mats.

Those who joined the occasion included Wasantha Samarasinghe, Minister of Trade, Commerce, Food Security and Cooperative Development; K. A. Vimalenthirarajah, Secretary to the Ministry of Trade, Commerce, Food Security and Cooperative Development; Himali Hettihewage, Acting Director General of the National Intellectual Property Office of Sri Lanka (NIPO); Mangala Wijesinghe, Chairman/Chief Executive Officer of the EDB; and Erandika Dissanayake, Director General of the EDB.
