CEJ demands AG implement Supreme Court directives on X-Press Pearl disaster

Colombo, Nov. 10 (Daily Mirror) - The Centre for Environmental Justice (CEJ) has formally demanded that the Attorney General take immediate steps to implement the directions issued by the Supreme Court in its landmark judgment on the MV X-Press Pearl disaster, warning that it may seek further legal remedies over alleged delays in giving effect to the ruling.

In a letter of demand dated November 2026, addressed to Attorney General Parinda Ranasinghe (Jnr.), the CEJ has called for a written explanation of the measures taken to implement the Supreme Court's directives, including investigations into offences arising from the disaster and the consideration of criminal proceedings against those responsible.

The demand follows the Supreme Court judgment delivered on July 24, 2025, in four fundamental rights applications, including SC/FR/168/2021 filed by the CEJ and its Executive Director, Hemantha Ranjith Sisira Kumara.

In paragraphs 895 and 896 of the judgment, the Supreme Court held that the failure of the Attorney General to perform his statutory duty of indicting the owners and operators of the MV X-Press Pearl under Section 26(a) of the Marine Pollution Prevention Act, and the decision to institute civil proceedings against the ship-owning group in Singapore rather than before the Sri Lankan High Court exercising Admiralty jurisdiction, infringed the fundamental rights guaranteed under Article 12(1) of the Constitution.

The Court also issued a series of directions in paragraph 897, requiring further investigations into offences arising from the disaster and consideration of criminal proceedings against all offenders.

Among other directions, the Court required the Attorney General to review investigative material collected by the Criminal Investigation Department and other law enforcement authorities, including the Marine Environment Protection Authority, and consider instituting criminal proceedings against those identified as responsible.

The Criminal Investigation Department was also directed to conduct further investigations into matters identified by the Attorney General, ensuring that such investigations were completed within three months. The Attorney General was required to report to the Supreme Court every three months on the investigative and prosecutorial action taken to implement the judgment.

The Court further directed the Attorney General to undertake a review of existing maritime and environmental legislation, in consultation with relevant authorities and independent experts, and advise the Government on necessary legislative reforms to bring domestic laws in line with international standards.

Meanwhile, the CEJ stated that it had submitted a Bill of Costs to the Attorney General on January 16, 2026, seeking reimbursement of expenses incurred in pursuing the fundamental rights litigation. However, it claimed that it had received neither a response nor reimbursement.

The CEJ has requested the Attorney General to respond within 14 days, identifying the steps taken to implement the judgment and explaining any outstanding matters or delays.
