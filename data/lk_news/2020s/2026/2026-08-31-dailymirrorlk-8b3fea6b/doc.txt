Crime bosses hit where it hurts: Rs. 6B assets frozen

Colombo, Aug. 31 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka Police have frozen assets worth more than Rs. 6 billion linked to organized crime networks, drug trafficking and financial fraud, following 113 investigations, according to the police.

Of the total assets identified, around Rs. 5.423 billion was linked to 102 major drug traffickers and organized crime figures. Property worth approximately Rs. 209 million was seized from three key underworld figures as part of the investigations.

Police also confiscated assets valued at around Rs. 367 million from eight suspects allegedly involved in financial pyramid schemes, child exploitation, illegal explosives and large-scale fraud.

The assets identified by authorities include 55 houses valued at Rs. 1.437 billion, while 138 acres of land have been valued at approximately Rs. 1.322 billion. Police have also seized 142 motor vehicles worth around Rs. 1.171 billion.

In addition, authorities have recovered or frozen Rs. 461 million in cash and 27 bank accounts containing approximately Rs. 428 million. The investigations also resulted in the seizure of 12 multi-day fishing trawlers valued at Rs. 305 million.

Other assets include eight commercial buildings valued at Rs. 250 million, 21 shop units worth approximately Rs. 214 million, and gold and jewellery valued at Rs. 168 million. Miscellaneous assets account for a further Rs. 239 million.

Meanwhile, Sri Lanka Police have stepped up international efforts to track down suspects who fled the country. A total of 66 fugitives have been repatriated to Sri Lanka from overseas, including 38 individuals wanted in connection with organized crime and other serious offences.

A further 28 organized crime suspects have been brought back to Sri Lanka under INTERPOL Red Notices, according to police.

Authorities have obtained 110 INTERPOL Red Notices, including 55 targeting organized crime leaders and major narcotics traffickers. Since 2023, 42 suspects have been extradited to Sri Lanka under INTERPOL Red Notices.

Police currently maintain 236 active INTERPOL Red Notices internationally. Of these, 96 target alleged organized crime syndicate leaders, while 93 additional notices are currently being processed as authorities work to secure the return of more fugitives to Sri Lanka.
