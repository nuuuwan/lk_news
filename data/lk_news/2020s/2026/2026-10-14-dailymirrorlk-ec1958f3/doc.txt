The primary goal of the initial four-year Extended Fund Facility (EFF) program held with the IMF was achieving economic stability. That stability has been successfully achieved, and the next step is achieving economic growth while protecting that stability. If we can enter into a new agreement for that growth, we are ready to go.

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake attended the economic summit organised by the Ceylon Chamber of Commerce at the Shangri-La Hotel in Colombo on Monday evening. It was held under the theme “Positioning Sri Lanka in a Changing Global Economy: Resilience, Reforms, and the Future of Economic Policy,”. The event brought together representatives from the public and private sectors, policymakers, business leaders and international investors to explore opportunities to attract foreign direct investment (FDI), expand exports and promote sustainable economic growth. The summit aimed to showcase Sri Lanka’s investment potential to the international community and strengthen public-private sector collaboration to support the country’s long-term economic development. The following are the questions raised at the summit and the answers provided by President Dissanayake:

Q A special program is currently being implemented to strengthen the rural economy and eradicate poverty. Also, nearly 5 million people receive the Aswesuma benefit. How are we going to integrate this group into the economic mainstream?

Our main challenge is eradicating rural poverty. A World Bank report presented recently states that rural poverty is at a level of 16.9%. This cannot be completed in a year or two like building a building or constructing a road. A program with a targeted, continuous plan is required for that.

At the same time, there is a vulnerable social group in any country that must be looked after by the government. That cannot be dismissed. Not everyone becomes participants in the economic mainstream. In any country, there is a social group that needs to be looked after through some social safety net. The problem we have now is that this social group is large. There are several reasons for that. The failure to create new economic opportunities for rural people has contributed to it. Rural people have a very limited number of economic opportunities. Those economic opportunities are either agriculture, marine or freshwater fishing, or running a small grocery store. Therefore, if we are eradicating rural poverty, the first challenge is how we are going to create new economic opportunities for them.

For a long time, our agriculture existed at a subsistence level rather than as a commercial economy. Therefore, while utilizing it for food security, new economic opportunities must be created for rural people. When these people create economic opportunities on their own, the lack of capital capabilities, new technology, and market capabilities has been an obstacle. Therefore, our responsibility is to fulfill these tasks. We have already initiated a collateral-free loan scheme.

Through the collateral-free loan scheme started for rural entrepreneurs who lack capital and assets, nearly 18 billion rupees have been provided so far, building new entrepreneurs. Also, new technology must be integrated into it. Furthermore, the wealth generated by the government should be pushed towards the rural people. In that regard, we follow two strategies. Mainly, a rural development path is required. For that, from this year’s budget, we have allocated 35 billion rupees for the development of small tanks and canals. Also, 25 billion rupees has allocated for the Prajasakthi rural program. Including 24 billion rupees for rural roads through the Road Development Authority, nearly 100 billion rupees has been directly directed to the rural people.

Moreover, our economic strategy is to go for a development strategy while keeping the village people in the village itself. Accordingly, the 2027 budget has paid more attention to creating a fast communication system and a developed road system between the village and the city. It is through such programs that solutions must be found for rural poverty. In that case, answers are definitely not received tomorrow from a solution sought today. It is a long-term program with a continuous plan. We have already initiated it.

One of the most important milestones of our economic strategy is the port. Currently, the Ports Authority owns the East Terminal. This terminal transfers containers approximately 0.5 million, meaning about 500,000. However, the East Terminal has the capability of handling 3.2 million containers. Looking at it that way, day by day we are not making maximum use of this resource. Therefore, I do not believe that the task of handling the East Terminal can be done solely by the government alone.

Q How do we reduce the time taken for project approvals in investments?

In the upcoming budget, we have planned a capital expenditure of 2 trillion rupees. It is a huge increase compared to past eras. Out of the capital expenditures allocated by the government in 2023, 64% has been spent. In 2024, 54% has been spent. However, in 2025, which had elections and natural disasters, we were able to raise it up to 76%.

There was a mindset in our country that the State machinery is always engaged in corruption. Due to certain laws formulated step by step in the past saying this is to prevent it, today it has reached a level where no task can be done swiftly. The old system was prepared only for purchasing goods. However, are we only purchasing goods anymore? We purchase solutions through a software system. However, such a system is not in our procurement procedure. Therefore, our expectation is to identify those special fields and formulate a new procurement procedure. Therefore, we have identified a number of special and new fields where a new procurement system should be introduced. We have paid attention to this. Also, previously, the procurement procedure starts only after the budget is passed and provisions are allocated. Mostly, these procurement activities conclude around August and September. Now we have resolved that. Now the Secretary to the Treasury certifies that funds are allocated. Accordingly, calling for tenders and selecting suppliers can begin. As an example, the Kurunegala-Galewela expressway. Accordingly, we mostly expect to award the tender by January. Therefore, we have planned such a large number of projects. Also, we must move to the e-procurement process. Additionally, we are taking steps to retain people with specialist skills within the public service by placing them on a special salary scale. It is through that we can bring capital expenditures to a value close to 100%.

Q We recently talked about customs laws, labour laws, public-private partnership laws, and state-owned enterprise management laws, didn’t we?

The law regarding State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs) will be submitted to the Cabinet in about another two weeks. Then it can be gazetted quickly. The Public-Private Partnership law has now been prepared by the Legal Draftsman’s Department and referred to the Attorney General. We can carry out further activities based on the recommendations of the Attorney General. Also, our labor law is contained in about 12 acts. These labor laws are in several acts of various forms.

There is an act already prepared. Even if it takes some time, it will be reviewed again very quickly. Also, I saw suggestions presented by the Chamber of Commerce regarding the Customs Ordinance. There were also views from the Customs Department. After a long time, we have been able to reach an agreement. The most suitable proposal is to amend the Customs Ordinance. I think the proposals for it have now been sent to the Legal Draftsman’s Department. They will quickly prepare and present that act. Most likely, all these acts can be passed in Parliament in January and February.

Q We talk about implementing promotional programs in regional countries regarding the tourism industry sector. Are you saying that our procurement process affects this? Can’t these funds be allocated for that?

Yes. How do we purchase a promotional plan? According to our procurement methodology, a desk can be purchased. However, what is a promotional publicity program? It is an idea. An idea. How do we buy an idea? Our procurement methodology should change according to that. One can say it was done that way in history. How has it been done like that? Who says it was done like that? Selecting one organization as good is not selecting another organization. Then who measures this? Where is this measured? Attempts have been made for about a year and a half now for this promotion. The Tourism Authority says that tenders will be called in January. In addition to that, the Authority has started a small-scale promotional project in five countries. In addition to that, there is a proposal presented by business organizations engaged in the tourism sector. We are looking into that as well. If they bring a certain percentage of tourists more than the tourists they bring this year, we are allocating funds from the coming budget for the Sri Lanka Tourism Authority to make a payment for the tourists brought exceeding that target.

Q The government has paid special attention to renewable energy. We saw work being done to increase battery storage capacity. We believe that the private sector as well as rooftop solar power should be involved. Can we expect amendments to the payment methodology in the future? Also, what is the thought on the improvement of the transmission network? Views are expressed that four to five billion will be spent for that?

The Sri Lankan government expects to carry out a high-tension transmission system from Kilinochchi to Habarana with the assistance of the Asian Development Bank. In addition to that, there are several other identified transmission systems such as Puttalam-Wariyapola, Habarana-Veyangoda. We plan to construct them through the private sector. All transmission systems existing so far in Sri Lanka have been built by the Electricity Board. There is an expectation to invite private investments for those transmission systems. We have already selected four grid lines for that. Let us tender those four very quickly. Then the private sector can connect to the transmission system.

Also, we pay attention to the energy sector. One, the stability and continuity of energy. Second, the cost incurred for energy. Our energy policy is built on these two.

If we take the feed-in tariff methodology, it is not a tender methodology. We purchase electricity at a value introduced by the PUCSL. That is what was used over a period of time. However, if we take the second strategy of reducing the cost incurred to purchase energy, it is wrong. Rather than purchasing by giving such a specific value, when purchased through an open competitive tender procedure, we can get it cheaper.

As an example, when we assumed office, there was an electricity project in Mannar. We had agreed to take a unit of it for 8.22 US cents. We discussed with that company and notified them that we are ready if it comes below 5 dollars. However, since they did not come to that amount, that agreement broke down. Then we tendered two 50 megawatts. The first 50 megawatts came to us at 3.96 US cents. For the 50 megawatts in the second tender, we got 3.74 US cents. It is this competitiveness that brings us more profit.

Therefore, rather than the feed-in tariff, we focus on completing the generation system required by us through competitiveness. Especially, there was a view about our government that it is a government against renewable energy. When we assumed office, the total solar power was entirely 1400 megawatts. However, within these two years, 1500 new megawatts have been generated. We have entered into agreements for another 1100 megawatts. It will be completed by next year. Within our government, 2600 new megawatts will be generated from solar power.

Also, the other side of renewable energy is that a large space is spent for it. If we take solar power, generally, building one solar megawatt takes about three and a half acres. In a small country like ours, there is a question of whether that much land area is directed to build a solar megawatt. That is why, according to the idea of reservoir-based floating solar systems, a 5-megawatt project was started at the Ibbankaduwa tank. The total reservoir of Ibbankaduwa is 1000 acres. It took us 10 acres to produce 5 megawatts. That means 1%. That means we allocated 1% from a reservoir and built 5 megawatts in Ibbankaduwa. Then our land is not subjugated. Therefore, our higher attention is paid to floating solar systems. Now we have started Randenigala. We expect to tender both the EIA and the building of the generation system to the private sector. Also, we expect to go for floating solar systems in several selected reservoirs such as Moragahakanda, Samanalawewa, Chandrikawewa. Because we need to move towards the renewable energy direction. The special thing in our energy was that, more than any other commodity, it must be produced at that time and used at that time. This is the problem. Every other commodity can be stored by us. However, this is the problem we face in electricity. In solar, we had a higher capability to produce during daytime. Then we faced a problem. Our capacity increases during daytime. Then we had to cut some purchases. There is a huge criticism about that as well. However, system management experts say that our capacity is high during daytime.

If so, we should store daytime solar and use it at nighttime. For that, we have currently called a tender for 160 megawatts. I think by the month of November, 13 will be completed. The remaining 3 will also be completed. We also tendered 250 megawatts of batteries. It is currently being evaluated. I am happy to say that according to the information of the ones being evaluated now, there is a 10% price reduction from these 250 compared to when we tendered 160 megawatts of batteries.

Next, we expect to tender another 150 megawatts to the owners of the existing solar power.

Also, there is a problem regarding solar panels on household roofs. We give tax concessions to batteries greater than one megawatt. There is a tax for batteries lower than that. Therefore, they get discouraged. We are thinking whether a tax concession can be given to those less than a megawatt. However, we have a technical problem: how do we technically identify the battery used to store solar and the other battery?

If that problem is resolved, we do not have any other problem. Now 69% of the total generation capacity is renewable capacity.

Some days, if we take yesterday, 70%-75% of our energy becomes renewable energy. It changes daily according to the situation. For the past four days continuously, we have not used a single liter of diesel to generate electricity. Therefore, this journey towards the renewable energy sector is very successful. It will be a huge victory. Our expectation is to supply uninterrupted energy and supply energy at a fair price. We are operating on those two principles.

Also, infrastructure is growing and new economic opportunities are being created in a number of fields including the port, renewable energy sector, Port City, and the tourism industry. All of you, invest without fear. We provide the protection and confidence required for that. Finally, that is the request to be made of you. Thank you very much.

Q In a country like Singapore, port revenue contributes about 7%-8% to the Gross National Product. However, in Sri Lanka, it still gets a contribution of only 2%-3%?

One of the most important milestones of our economic strategy is the port. Currently, the Ports Authority owns the East Terminal. This terminal transfers containers approximately 0.5 million, meaning about 500,000. However, the East Terminal has the capability of handling 3.2 million containers. Looking at it that way, day by day we are not making maximum use of this resource. Therefore, I do not believe that the task of handling the East Terminal can be done solely by the government alone. Therefore, the Cabinet has decided to keep the ownership of the terminal with the government, establish a company for management, and hand over its shares to private investors.

Several globally-powerful shipping companies are negotiating for the management of the East Terminal. Next is the second phase of the West Terminal. Its breakwater has to be built. The estimate for that was previously 1.1 billion dollars. Now it costs 1.4 billion dollars. If we incur that cost and build the breakwater of the West Terminal, we can carry out container operations of 3.2 million there as well. Provisions will be allocated for initial study activities of this from the upcoming budget. The feasibility study report is already being prepared by the Asian Development Bank. It is planned to provide an interim report by this December and a complete report by next April. That is how we expect to develop the terminal system. Next, is container transfer only what is done from the port? Shouldn’t we do something beyond that? We must think. There we can develop the port as a logistics center. For a long time, calling for tenders for that had created a lot of problems. It ended up going to court. Now all those problems have ended, and an agreement has been reached to pay the compensation amounts related to the companies and end it. To minimize container congestion inside the port, work is underway to build a 150-base yard in Kerawalapitiya. There is also a problem regarding taxes in bunkering. We are considering that as well and expect to provide a relief for it from the upcoming budget. Since this is international trade, tax levies must be done subject to international standards.

Q The problem that existed five or six years ago in the field including big data, AI, quantum computing, blockchain, which fall under new industries, was that there was not enough labor contribution. The problem now is that 17,000 join this sector annually from State and private universities. However, there are not enough jobs. This is not a problem that exists only in Sri Lanka. How do we take the tech sector forward?

The real problem is what level this skilled workforce we create is at? They are at the middle level. This job crisis has emerged right there. If we go to the field higher than that, job opportunities expand further. Job opportunities expand massively from the public sector. If we take the business field too, the domestic industry has a need for skilled people. If we completely change this system, I think that change can be brought about. We have this challenge.

We expect to establish an AI center in Wijerama in collaboration with Singapore. The relevant Cabinet paper will be able to be passed next week. We believe that we have to take this skill higher from here and find solutions to this crisis. Also, increasing the online working pattern further with organizations like BPOs, creating international opportunities are a number of fields we have paid attention to. Answers can be obtained through that.

Even degree courses must go even higher. The IT engineer we require in the beginning is this. However, relative to the IT sector developing massively by now, we have failed to create graduates with the skill required by us. Therefore, our IT sector must go higher in two or three layers. That is the only solution available.

Q Mr. President, with the problems that came to us along with the Middle East crisis, a subsidy for diesel is currently being provided. If the Middle East war drags on further, will we give this subsidy even for two years? Or do we move to a targeted economic stimulus?

After the Middle East war began, our main challenge was to continuously maintain energy supply including fuel, electricity, and gas. While social unrest was created in many countries of the world due to the breakdown of this continuity, we successfully ensured that continuity. No indicator can predict how long this war will drag on.

It is because our economy and social life, rising after a massive economic crisis and business collapse, cannot bear another big shock that we provided a structural subsidy package. As a result, we were able to achieve a 5.2% economic growth rate in the first quarter.

During the first five months, 60 billion rupees was spent to provide a subsidy of 100 rupees per liter of diesel and 20 rupees per liter of petrol. Although the subsidy was not given in the month of September, since the situation escalated again, a subsidy of 70 rupees per liter of diesel is provided up to October, November, and December, and 41 billion rupees is spent for that.

The subsidy should go to the targeted public, such as the tractor used for agriculture or the merchant distributing bread. However, due to the weakness in a direct data system, we had to give an overall subsidy. In the future, we will discuss with Dialog and Mobitel companies and take steps to formulate a methodology where the subsidy is provided only to the targeted public. Although the private sector was invited to competitiveness, private companies have not acted to provide fuel at the price provided by the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC). While the CPC is making a profit of 28 billion rupees and is ready to provide a share of that profit as relief to the people, private companies have kept oil in stores and restricted distribution. Passing a price difference of 132 rupees per liter of diesel onto the people is not fair.

Therefore, we decided to start 75 new petrol sheds through the CPC for the people in the areas where private companies are reluctant to go. No company has the right to subject the energy sector, which affects the social and economic survival of the country, to uncertainty or to stop fuel distribution. Private entrepreneurs should have a sense of social justice as well as profit.

Q The government has arrived at the final six months of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) program. After restructuring debt, going into this program has brought many advantages, but what is the government’s view on going into a new IMF program in the future?

Even though we have come higher in ratings, foreign reserves still need to be expanded to the expected level. If these factors can be perfected, we can go. However, amidst the uncertainty existing in the world, the fuel import cost has increased significantly. Last year’s fuel import cost was 4042 million dollars, and within the first eight months of this year alone, it has increased to 4082 million dollars. According to this trend, about 6 billion dollars may be spent for fuel at the end of the year, which is a major challenge to be borne by the economy.

The primary goal of the initial four-year Extended Fund Facility (EFF) program held with the IMF was achieving economic stability. That stability has been successfully achieved, and the next step is achieving economic growth while protecting that stability. If we can enter into a new agreement for that growth, we are ready to go.

Since there was no major contradiction between the government’s economic vision and IMF agreements, we were able to successfully conclude the 4 years of the program without a conflict. A group of scholars in the economic field including the Ministry of Finance and the Central Bank Governor are discussing what the future program should be like. During the remaining six months, taking external economic shocks also into consideration, a suitable decision is scheduled to be reached.

Q There are a large number of foreign investors, local and foreign ambassadors, our business community, chamber members, and government officials here. What is your final message to us?

We have built the transparency required inside the country; that is the first step. As the second step, enacting laws and regulations necessary for investment and business security and protection is at a considerable concluding stage. Also, we are developing the infrastructure required for investments.

The tasks vested in the government are creating transparency, enacting laws and regulations, and developing infrastructure. Therefore, let us do our part; you do your part. During the past period, there was some doubt and questioning regarding the removal of S-VAT (Simplified Value Added Tax). However, since S-VAT, a leakage of 10 billion rupees per month was taking place, and it has been possible to control it successfully.

We want to create a strong domestic business group and entrepreneurs who can successfully interconnect with foreign investments and large international companies within Sri Lanka. Our budget has also paid attention to that. Especially if going to rural regions, we are ready to provide a concessional system for land and some services.

Also, infrastructure is growing and new economic opportunities are being created in a number of fields including the port, renewable energy sector, Port City, and the tourism industry. All of you, invest without fear. We provide the protection and confidence required for that. Finally, that is the request to be made of you. Thank you very much.
