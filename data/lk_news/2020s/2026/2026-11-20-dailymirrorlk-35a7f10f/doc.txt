SriLankan could break even by end 2026: Deputy Minister

SriLankan Airlines could break even or turn a small profit by end 2026, Deputy Aviation Minister Ruwan Kodithuwakku told parliament yesterday, although Finance Ministry figures showed the national carrier incurring a loss of Rs. 8.9 billion in the first five months of its financial year.

The April–August net loss would have reached Rs. 20.3 billion without a one-off reversal of Rs. 11.4 billion in withheld Rolls-Royce discounts and suspended credits, the ministry’s figures showed.

Revenue rose 35 percent to Rs. 154.6 billion from Rs. 114.6 billion a year earlier. Expenditure climbed 35.2 percent to Rs. 160.4 billion as aircraft fuel costs surged 78.2 percent, leaving the airline with a Rs. 5.8 billion operating loss. Foreign exchange losses of Rs. 11.9 billion and financing costs of Rs. 7.1 billion added to the pressure on the bottom line.

The ministry said tensions in the Middle East disrupted flights, restricted access to airspace and forced costly diversions. In the financial year ended March 2026, SriLankan’s pre-tax loss had already tripled to Rs. 23.2 billion from Rs. 7.6 billion a year earlier, as financing and currency costs wiped out earnings before interest and tax of Rs. 26.4 billion.

The government has extended US$ 30 million, or about Rs. 10 billion, in short-term assistance to meet the airline’s immediate operating and liquidity needs. It is also restructuring a US$ 175 million sovereign-guaranteed bond that matured in 2024 and pursuing a settlement of legacy debt. The Treasury guarantees a further Rs. 3.8 billion in obligations to the International Air Transport Association.

Kodithuwakku put the airline’s outstanding loans at US$ 118 million to Bank of Ceylon and US$ 138 million to People’s Bank. He also cited a US$ 70 million overdraft facility from Bank of Ceylon.

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake told parliament in his 2027 Budget speech that SriLankan had accumulated Rs. 528 billion in losses by 2025 since the government took control in 2008.

“SriLankan Airlines has become a burden on the General Treasury,” he stressed.

Kodithuwakku said the government would decide how to restructure the carrier once it received the report of an expert committee. He said maintaining international air links was a consideration in that decision.

“Sri Lanka is an island and essentially needs connectivity with the world. The national carrier plays an important role when it comes to building connectivity with the world,” he said.

The Cabinet appointed the committee in June under Presidential Senior Adviser on Digital Economy Dr. Hans Wijayasuriya, with International Finance Corporation as transaction adviser. The review covers the airline’s corporate structure, operating model and potential partnerships, as well as options for raising capital, restructuring debt and allocating risk.
