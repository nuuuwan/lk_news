Over 200,000 disputes referred to Mediation Boards annually

Colombo, August 14 (Daily Mirror) - More than 200,000 disputes are referred to mediation boards across Sri Lanka each year, the Ministry of Justice and National Integration said.

Addressing the 36th National Mediation Day celebration at the Sri Lanka Foundation Institute in Colombo, Minister of Justice and National Integration Harshana Nanayakkara, said the increasing number of cases and resulting delays have become a major challenge for the judicial system.

He said that mediation has helped resolve around 200,000 disputes, preventing further pressure on the courts and reducing the potential backlog of cases.

The Minister said 2026 marks a significant year for the country’s mediation process, with more than Rs. 800 million allocated through the Budget for the Mediation Boards Commission. The funding will support delayed training programmes, timely appointments of mediators and the strengthening of community mediation boards at the divisional secretariat level.

Special land mediation boards have also been established in all 25 districts, while special financial dispute mediation boards have been introduced in six districts — Colombo, Gampaha, Anuradhapura, Polonnaruwa, Kandy and Monaragala — to help reduce court-referred financial disputes.

Meanwhile, a Dispute Data Management System is being implemented alongside the ongoing digitisation of the judicial system. Initial data from the special land mediation boards has already been integrated into the system.

Nanayakkara also expressed appreciation for more than 9,000 voluntary mediators across the country, who contribute their time and expertise without financial expectations to help resolve disputes and promote peaceful settlements.

Ministry Secretary and President’s Counsel Ayesha Jinasena said community mediation boards handle more than 200,000 disputes annually, resolving a significant proportion of them and helping transform potential conflicts into peaceful settlements and greater social cohesion.

She said the Ministry plans to expand child-friendly mediation programmes, special land mediation boards and financial dispute resolution boards nationwide to further promote a culture of mediation.

Jinasena also said that the Civil and Commercial Disputes Act has been passed by Parliament to establish a modern legal framework for commercial and business disputes in line with international standards, while ensuring equal access to justice across Sri Lanka’s multicultural communities.
