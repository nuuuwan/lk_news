Sri Lanka delays key socio-economic report over data accuracy, compilation

ECONOMYNEXT – The release of Sri Lanka’s Household Income and Expenditure Survey (HIES) report for 2025 will be delayed by two months due to more time needed for the accurate compilation, the Parliament said in a statement.

The data for HIES is collected and compiled by the Department of Census and Statistics (DCS), operating under the Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development.

The Parliament Committee on Public Finance (CoPF) early this week discussed the current situation regarding poverty, income distribution, and household welfare.

Attention focused on the collection of social and economic data, the scientific methodologies used to calculate the poverty line, and how that data is utilized for policymaking.

The relevant data are collected by the Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development, the Department of Census and Statistics, the Central Bank of Sri Lanka, and the Welfare Benefits Board,

“Officials informed the committee that although the report was originally scheduled for release in October 2026, the release date has been deferred to December 2026 due to complexities in data cleaning, delays in computerization, cadre vacancies, and work related to the Economic Survey,” the Parliament said in a statement on Friday (16).

“It was revealed during the discussion that data was gathered from 25,000 households island-wide over a 12-month period for this survey, and data cleaning, verification, and analysis work are currently underway.”

“The necessity of publishing the results of such surveys in a timely manner to identify changes in economic and social conditions more accurately was emphasized.”

It said the necessity of updating the consumer basket used to calculate the poverty line to reflect contemporary social and economic changes was highlighted.

“Discussions particularly focused on whether calculations based on outdated data and commodity baskets accurately reflect the current cost of living and basic public needs.”

“Attention was also drawn to the requirement that data used in identifying beneficiaries for social welfare programs such as Aswesuma accurately reflect current household income and expenditure levels.”

Despite consecutive rounds of economic stabilization and fiscal consolidation, Sri Lanka continues to suffer significant social spending leakages and fiscal inefficiencies due to the absence of a unified, accurate, and real-time household data architecture.

Legacy welfare mechanisms have historically relied on outdated administrative registries, localized self-reporting, and fragmented institutional databases, creating severe inclusion and exclusion errors where non-poor households capture state relief while vulnerable agrarian populations remain omitted.

Attempts to rationalize relief mechanisms such as universal energy and fuel price cushions deployed during external market shocks further highlight these structural vulnerabilities, as the state lacks the integrated digital infrastructure required to deliver targeted, end-user cash transfers to specific groups like smallholder farmers, informal transport operators, and micro-entrepreneurs.

Consequently, policy implementations like the Aswesuma welfare scheme continue to face operational friction, leaving fiscal allocations exposed to administrative leakages and diminishing the overall efficacy of state safety nets during broader macroeconomic shocks. (Colombo/October 16/2026)
