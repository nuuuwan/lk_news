New Colombo Port Health office opens

A newly constructed Colombo Port Health Office, aimed at strengthening Sri Lanka’s public health infrastructure and emergency preparedness, was inaugurated on July 28 at the Port of Colombo.

The facility, designed by Design Consortium International (DCIL), was constructed through a grant from the Government of Japan and implemented by the International Organization for Migration (IOM), a United Nations agency.

Officials said the new facility marks an important step in enhancing border health security by improving the capacity of health authorities to respond to public health emergencies at one of the country’s busiest entry points.

Designed as a compact yet highly functional building, the Colombo Port Health Office combines operational efficiency with modern architectural features while addressing the challenges of construction within a restricted port environment.

DCIL Managing Director and Architect Migara Alwis said the project had to be completed within a short timeframe while meeting strict security requirements and withstanding the challenging marine conditions at the Port of Colombo.

The facility was constructed using a pre-engineered hybrid building system to accelerate construction and ensure long-term durability. Its façade incorporates advanced materials with self-cleaning properties and heat-reducing surfaces designed to withstand the corrosive marine environment.

The inauguration was attended by Minister of Ports and Civil Aviation Anura Karunathilaka, Minister of Health and Mass Media Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa, Japanese Ambassador to Sri Lanka Akio Isomata, senior government officials, health authorities and representatives of the Embassy of Japan.
