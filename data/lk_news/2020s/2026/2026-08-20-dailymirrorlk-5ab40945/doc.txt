Sri Lanka Is the Only South Asian Country Its Neighbors All View Favorably, Pew Survey Finds

Colombo, Aug. 20 (Daily Mirror) - A new Pew Research Center survey of public opinion in South Asia has turned up an unusual result: Sri Lanka is the only country in the region that India, Pakistan and Bangladesh all view more favourably than unfavourably. Those three countries rarely agree on much when it comes to each other. On Sri Lanka, they do.

Pew researchers Jacob Poushter, Sneha Gubbala and William Miner surveyed 3,566 people in India, 1,039 in Pakistan, 1,044 in Bangladesh and 2,513 in Sri Lanka between February 8 and May 7, 2026. The goal was to measure how the region's largest countries see each other. What came back was a map of old rivalries, with Sri Lanka sitting apart from most of them.

Roughly half of adults in India, Pakistan and Bangladesh view Sri Lanka favourably, and no more than a third in any of the three see it unfavourably. Pakistan (57%) and Bangladesh (54%) rate it warmest. India's view has also improved, up six points since Pew last ran this survey in 2024. No other country in the poll gets the same consistent goodwill from all three of its neighbors.

India's numbers split hard along political lines. Seventy-nine percent of Sri Lankans view India favourably, and 70% say they trust Prime Minister Narendra Modi to do the right thing in world affairs. Bangladeshi's are far more divided: only 42% view India favourably, down 15 points since 2024. In Pakistan, that figure drops to 7%.

Pakistanis and Bangladeshi's were also the two groups most likely to name India as their country's greatest threat. Indians return the favour: they most often name Pakistan.

Pakistan and Bangladesh have warmed to each other since 2024. Fifty-four percent of Bangladeshi's now view Pakistan favourably, up from 40% two years ago, while 66% of Pakistanis view Bangladesh favourably (Pew doesn't report a comparable 2024 figure for that pairing). Sri Lankan views of Pakistan have climbed too, from 44% in 2024 to 57% now, and Sri Lankan views of Bangladesh have risen 9 points over the same period, to 56%.

India is the outlier here as well. Just 8% of Indians view Pakistan favourably, and only a quarter view Bangladesh favourably, a 10-point drop since 2024.

Younger adults, those under 35, tend to view their neighbors more warmly than adults 50 and older. Younger Indians rate Sri Lanka more favourably than older Indians do (54% versus 43%), and are somewhat less negative toward Pakistan (9% favorable versus 5%), though most Indians of every age still view Pakistan unfavourably.

Religion matters too, at least in India. Indian Muslims hold warmer views of Pakistan and Bangladesh than Indian Hindus do: 21% favorable toward Pakistan among Muslims versus 6% among Hindus, and 41% versus 23% on Bangladesh. Even so, two out of three Indian Muslims still view Pakistan negatively. In Sri Lanka, Hindus expressed more confidence in Modi than Sri Lankan Muslims or Buddhists did.

Sri Lanka's position matters because of where it sits: a small state that India and China have both tried to pull closer in recent years, through ports, loans and infrastructure deals. Being liked by India, Pakistan and Bangladesh at once suggests Colombo has avoided getting locked into any one country's camp, at least in how ordinary people in those countries see it.

Whether that goodwill counts for anything in trade talks or debt negotiations is a different question. But it's a rare asset in a region where most cross-border views run negative.
