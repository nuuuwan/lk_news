LOLC 1Q earnings rise as portfolio drag eases

LOLC Holdings PLC reported a 34 percent increase in first-quarter (1Q) profit attributable to shareholders, as higher income and a sharp reduction in losses across several non-financial businesses outweighed rising impairment charges and weaker profitability in its core financial services segment.

Profit attributable to equity holders rose to Rs. 6.18 billion in the three months ended June 30, from Rs. 4.63 billion a year earlier, according to the diversified group’s unaudited interim financial statements. Earnings per share increased to Rs. 13 from Rs. 9.74.

Overall Group profit for the quarter more than doubled to Rs. 4.76 billion from Rs. 2.37 billion, with the year-earlier period including a Rs. 483 million loss from discontinued operations. Profit from continuing operations increased 67 percent to Rs. 4.76 billion.

Non-controlling interests recorded a Rs. 1.41 billion loss, compared with a Rs. 2.02 billion loss from continuing operations a year earlier.

Gross income climbed 34 percent to Rs. 123.44 billion, while net interest income rose 26 percent to Rs. 34.75 billion. Revenue from the Group’s non-financial operations increased 33 percent to Rs. 43.13 billion, helping gross profit from those activities rise 38 percent to Rs. 18.34 billion.

The improvement at the top line did not translate proportionately into operating earnings as costs accelerated. Personnel expenses rose 24 percent to Rs. 20 billion, while other operating expenses jumped 49 percent to Rs. 16.91 billion. Impairment losses on financial assets increased 61 percent to Rs. 8.65 billion from Rs. 5.36 billion.

As a result, operating profit grew at a slower 22 percent to Rs. 19.88 billion. Net finance costs eased 3 percent to Rs. 12.58 billion, helping profit before tax from continuing operations surge 78 percent to Rs. 7.58 billion. The tax charge more than doubled to Rs. 2.82 billion.

The segment numbers show that the earnings improvement was driven less by stronger profitability in LOLC’s dominant financial services operation and more by reduced losses elsewhere in the portfolio.

Financial services generated Rs. 64.18 billion in gross income, up from Rs. 48.57 billion a year earlier, but operating profit fell to Rs. 11.44 billion from Rs. 12.35 billion. The segment’s profit before tax consequently declined to Rs. 11.44 billion from Rs. 12.35 billion.

The offset came from a substantial improvement in several other businesses. Manufacturing and trading narrowed its pre-tax loss to Rs. 1.27 billion from Rs. 2.09 billion, while leisure and real estate reduced its loss to Rs. 2.59 billion from Rs. 3.60 billion. Plantation and agriculture swung to a Rs. 316 million pre-tax loss from a Rs. 6 million profit a year earlier.

The Group’s “others and eliminations” category also improved sharply, recording a pre-tax loss of Rs. 191 million compared with a Rs. 3.56 billion loss in the corresponding quarter. Profit from equity-accounted investees fell to Rs. 277 million from Rs. 691 million.

LOLC’s balance sheet continued to expand. Total assets reached Rs. 2.46 trillion at end-June, up from Rs. 2.32 trillion at end-March and Rs. 2.10 trillion a year earlier. Advances and other loans increased to Rs. 1.05 trillion from Rs. 981.81 billion three months earlier, while deposit liabilities rose to Rs. 739.56 billion from Rs. 694.27 billion.

The expansion came with heavier funding and credit costs. Interest-bearing borrowings increased to Rs. 714.81 billion from Rs. 673.25 billion at end-March, while financial-services impairment charges rose to Rs. 8.51 billion from Rs. 5.22 billion in the year-earlier quarter.

Cash flow remained under pressure despite the stronger reported earnings. The Group used Rs. 10.18 billion of cash in operating activities during the quarter, though that was an improvement from the Rs. 23.57 billion outflow a year earlier. Investment activities absorbed a further Rs. 38.38 billion, while financing activities generated Rs. 32.07 billion.

LOLC also recorded Rs. 29 billion in other comprehensive income, largely reflecting foreign currency translation gains from its overseas operations. That lifted total comprehensive income to Rs. 33.76 billion for the quarter, compared with Rs. 8.65 billion a year earlier.
