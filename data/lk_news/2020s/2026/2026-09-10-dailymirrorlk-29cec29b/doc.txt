Are you using condoms correctly?

Colombo, Sep. 10 (Daily Mirror) - Health officials are urging people to use condoms correctly and consistently to reduce the risk of HIV and other sexually transmitted infections (STIs), while warning against the use of substandard condoms.

The call comes as the National STD/AIDS Control Programme (NSACP) steps up efforts to expand HIV testing and improve access to HIV prevention services.

As part of these efforts, the NSACP is encouraging the public to use the KNOW4SURE.LK online platform to access HIV prevention and testing services, including HIV self-testing and discreetly ordering quality condoms and lubricants.

Consultant Venereologist Dr. Vino Dharmakulasinghe of the NSACP told the Daily Mirror that the platform provides a convenient and confidential way for people to access HIV-related services, particularly those who may be reluctant to visit a clinic.

She said users can assess their risk of HIV and other STIs, access HIV self-testing services, locate nearby testing centres and make appointments for HIV screening.

“People can use the platform confidentially to find out their HIV risk and access testing services. We encourage more people to use this service so that we can increase HIV testing and identify infections at an early stage,” Dr. Dharmakulasinghe said.

The platform also allows users to order condoms and lubricants, as well as HIV test kits, providing a discreet way to access essential HIV prevention and testing products.

Health officials stressed that condoms should be used correctly and consistently during sexual activity to provide effective protection against HIV and other STIs. They also highlighted the importance of obtaining condoms from reliable sources and avoiding substandard products that may compromise their effectiveness.

Users can reserve HIV screening services at government clinics or selected private practitioners. HIV testing at government clinics is provided free of charge, while a nominal fee may apply at private facilities.

Dr. Dharmakulasinghe said the platform is particularly important as many people from high-risk and vulnerable populations now meet partners through social media platforms and dating applications but may not be connected to conventional HIV prevention, testing and treatment services.
