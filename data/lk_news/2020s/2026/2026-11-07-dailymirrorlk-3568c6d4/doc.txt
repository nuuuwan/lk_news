129mm rainfall in one hour caused Colombo flash floods: CMC

The exceptionally heavy rainfall of around 129mm within one hour was the main cause of flash floods in Colombo yesterday (6), Colombo Municipal Council (CMC) officials said.

They informed a meeting at the Presidential Secretariat today that Colombo’s existing drainage system was designed to withstand rainfall of only around 30mm per hour.

Following the discussions, Secretary to the President Dr Nandika Sanath Kumanayake instructed officials to establish a special task force led by the CMC, with the participation of the Tri-Forces, Police and relevant institutions, to ensure a swift and effective response to similar emergencies.

The meeting, chaired by Dr Kumanayake, focused on identifying the causes of the sudden flooding and implementing immediate measures to prevent a recurrence.

According to the President’s Media Division, discussions also centred on preparing short-, medium- and long-term plans to upgrade Colombo’s drainage system.

Officials revealed that the indiscriminate disposal of waste had blocked several drains and canals, worsening the flooding situation. The need to urgently clear obstructed waterways and take legal action against those responsible for improper waste disposal was also highlighted.

Unauthorised constructions within the CMC limits were identified as another factor obstructing drainage systems and canals. Officials discussed removing such structures and relocating affected residents to suitable alternative locations.

The meeting also highlighted the importance of raising public awareness about proper waste disposal and keeping waterways clean.

Colombo Mayor Vraie Cally Balthazaar, Deputy Mayor Hemantha Kumara, senior officials of the CMC, the Road Development Authority, the Sri Lanka Land Development Corporation, the Urban Development Authority, the Tri-Forces, the Police and members of the Clean Sri Lanka Task Force attended the meeting.
