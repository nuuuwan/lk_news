DFCC Bank 2Q earnings fall as costs offset stronger fee income

DFCC Bank profit fell 17 percent year-on-year to Rs. 2.33 billion in the second quarter of 2026, as a sharp increase in operating expenses outweighed stronger fee income and lower impairment charges.

Group profit before tax declined 21 percent to Rs. 3.23 billion from Rs. 4.07 billion a year earlier, while earnings per share fell to Rs. 5.12 from Rs. 6.40.

The earnings decline came despite an improvement in net operating income, highlighting the impact of a rapidly expanding cost base during the quarter.

Group total operating income rose just 2 percent to Rs. 11.57 billion, while impairment charges fell 31 percent to Rs. 1.47 billion. That helped net operating income increase 10 percent to Rs. 10.10 billion. Operating expenses, however, surged 42 percent to Rs. 5.74 billion from Rs. 4.05 billion a year earlier. Personnel expenses increased 84 percent to Rs. 2.60 billion, while other expenses rose 18 percent to Rs. 2.67 billion. As a result, operating profit before taxes on financial services fell 15 percent to Rs. 4.36 billion.

At the Bank level, which accounts for the bulk of Group operations, second-quarter profit after tax fell 20 percent to Rs. 2.19 billion, while profit before tax declined 23 percent to Rs. 3.04 billion.

Net interest income at the Bank rose just 1 percent to Rs. 7.81 billion as a 17 percent increase in interest expenses largely absorbed an 11 percent rise in interest income. Net fee and commission income provided stronger support, increasing 25 percent to Rs. 2.27 billion.

DFCC attributed the higher cost base partly to annual salary revisions and performance-based incentive payments, alongside continued investment in technology and digital infrastructure, marketing and business development.

The second-quarter reduction in impairments provided some relief to earnings, with charges falling to Rs. 1.47 billion from Rs. 2.13 billion a year earlier. That contrasted with the first-half trend, where increased provisioning was a significant drag on profitability.

For the six months ended June, Group profit after tax from continuing operations fell 28 percent to Rs. 4.14 billion from Rs. 5.75 billion, while Group profit before tax declined 29 percent to Rs. 5.80 billion from Rs. 8.17 billion. Profit attributable to equity holders fell 30 percent to Rs. 4.05 billion.

At the Bank level, first-half profit after tax from core operations declined 30 percent to Rs. 3.90 billion from Rs. 5.56 billion, while profit before tax fell 31 percent to Rs. 5.48 billion.

Chief Executive Officer Thimal Perera acknowledged the weaker earnings, saying the performance reflected decisions taken to strengthen the lender’s risk buffers.

“Reported profitability was lower than in the corresponding period,” Perera said, adding that the bank had “strengthened impairment provisioning through model refinements and management overlays” while maintaining a selective approach to lending.

First-half impairment charges increased 33 percent to Rs. 4.63 billion from Rs. 3.48 billion. DFCC said it strengthened provisioning in response to global and domestic economic risks, including additional management overlays for exposures to higher-risk sectors and customer segments and provisions for specific large-group exposures.

The higher provisioning came alongside stronger underlying income generation. At Group level, net interest income increased 6 percent to Rs. 16.17 billion, while net fee and commission income rose 29 percent to Rs. 4.19 billion. Group total operating income increased 6 percent to Rs. 23.66 billion.

Those gains were outweighed by the combination of higher impairments and a 31 percent increase in Group operating expenses to Rs. 11.11 billion, leaving operating profit before taxes on financial services 24 percent lower at Rs. 7.92 billion.

The balance sheet continued to expand despite the weaker earnings. Group assets increased 7 percent from December to Rs. 920.89 billion, while deposits grew 12 percent to Rs. 630.91 billion. At the Bank level, net loans and advances increased 9 percent to Rs. 558.53 billion.

The Bank’s CASA portfolio increased 14 percent from end-2025, lifting the CASA ratio to 24.99 percent, while its net interest margin stood at 3.66 percent.

Asset quality also improved. The Bank’s net Stage 3 impaired loan ratio declined to 3.61 percent at end-June from 4.55 percent at end-December, even as the loan portfolio expanded.

Perera said the improvement was achieved “even as we strengthened our prudential buffers”, with the bank maintaining a selective lending approach and tighter risk management.

Capital ratios eased as the balance sheet expanded. The Bank’s Tier 1 capital ratio declined to 11.947 percent from 13.550 percent at end-December, while the total capital ratio edged down to 15.707 percent from 15.933 percent. DFCC is also completing regulatory and administrative formalities for a Basel III-compliant Tier II debenture issue of up to Rs. 15 billion.

DFCC’s balance sheet is set to expand further in the second half following the completion of its acquisition of Standard Chartered Bank’s Wealth and Retail Banking business in Sri Lanka, effective August 1.

The transaction added about 50,000 customer accounts and around 260 employees and expanded DFCC’s network to 139 locations.
