Over 11,900 youths taken into protective custody in drug crackdown

Colombo, Nov. 28 (Daily Mirror) - More than 11,900 youths, including 233 females, have been taken into protective custody by police after being identified as victims of drug trafficking networks since the launch of the 'Ratama Ekata' national operation in October 2025.

Police Media Spokesman ASP F.U. Wootler said 11,737 male youths and 233 female youths, aged between 18 and 21, had been taken into protective custody during the operation.

He said the aim was not simply to penalise these young people but to refer them for court-directed rehabilitation, counselling and probation to help them overcome drug-related problems and return to society.

Meanwhile, police have arrested 6,305 suspects involved in serious crimes, including robbery, assault and murder, while checking or questioning more than nine million people across the country under the ongoing national operation.

Wootler said police had also arrested 143,612 suspects wanted under open and day warrants during special operations conducted so far this year.

Addressing the media, he said 7,586 successful raids had been carried out following information received from the public through the dedicated 1818 hotline.

He assured the public, both in Sri Lanka and overseas, that information provided through the hotline would be treated confidentially and urged people to continue supporting police efforts to combat drug trafficking.

Providing an update on anti-narcotics operations, Wootler said police had seized 2,982 kilogrammes of heroin, 3,758 kilogrammes of crystal methamphetamine (Ice), 11,328 kilogrammes of cannabis, and 303 kilogrammes of cocaine so far this year.

In addition, more than 4.5 million narcotic pills and capsules had been seized. He said these drugs were being circulated in society, with schoolchildren among the intended targets.

Authorities were taking steps to destroy the seized narcotics in accordance with legal procedures, he added.

The Police Spokesman urged parents to pay attention to changes in their children's behaviour and social circles, saying police were ready to provide guidance and assistance to protect young people from drug abuse and trafficking.
