Sri Lanka’s parliament panel discusses new Procurement Act amid slow public fund utilization

ECONOMYNEXT – Sri Lanka Parliament’s Committee on Public Finance (COPF) this week discussed the progress regarding the drafting of a new Procurement Act, the legislature said in a statement, amid slow utilization of public investments despite record capital expenditure allocation this year.

Sri Lanka has only used around Rs. 385 billion of the allocated Rs. 1,380 billion capital expenditure this year. Capital spending is vital for public investment like infrastructure to boost economic growth.

The CoPF discussed measures taken to transform the public procurement process into a more efficient, transparent, and systematic mechanism and progress regarding the drafting of a new Procurement Act.

“During the discussion on the public procurement process, existing standards, and key areas requiring improvement, attention was focused on delays arising in procurement operations, issues in the registration process, legal framework deficiencies, and operational challenges when utilizing digital systems, the Parliament said in a statement.

“In particular, the necessity of minimizing delays when purchasing pharmaceuticals and other essential goods was thoroughly discussed.”

“To expedite the procurement process, proposals were presented regarding enhancing financial thresholds for procurement committees, introducing a long-term framework agreement system for repetitive purchases, and formulating sector-specific procurement guidelines.”

The CoPF also evaluated the implementation of the Procurement Management Information System (PROMIS), a digital platform designed to digitize public procurement operations.

“It was highlighted that this system is currently being used to monitor procurement procedures across 394 active procurement units, covering 19 Ministries. Plans are underway to expand this network to cover all provincial councils and government departments by 2027.”

Acknowledging the delays in capital spending, President Anura Kumara Dissanayake last week at an investor forum said the country’s existing procurement system was fundamentally built on the mindset that the state apparatus is susceptible to corruption.

He admitted that successive governments created layer upon layer of regulations, and now those rules have become so restrictive that nothing can get done.

He promised to formulate modern procurement systems and emphasized the need of flexible, modern procurement mechanisms.

Sri Lanka’s chronic failure to utilize its full budgetary allocations for capital investment reflects deep-rooted structural inefficiencies in public financial management and project execution, analysts and economists say.

They warn of lower economic growth in the future if the government fails to implement its planned capital investment projects.

The government has repeatedly said most of the spending occurs in the last quarter of the year.

Year after year, bureaucratic delays, protracted procurement disputes, politicized project selection, and a lack of technical expertise within line ministries leave a significant portion of capital expenditure unspent on the Treasury books.

This capital under-utilization carries severe long-term repercussions for the island’s economic trajectory.

By failing to deploy allocated funds into critical infrastructure such as transport networks, modernized power grids, and digital public systems, Sri Lanka suffers from lower productivity growth and worsening structural bottlenecks as well as a decline in overall gross domestic product (GDP) potential.

In a fragile post-default macroeconomic climate, the persistent shortfall in capital spending has sent a negative signal to international development partners and private investors.

It demonstrates an institutional inability to absorb capital effectively, thereby stalling the very structural transformation required to safely out-grow a historic debt burden. (Colombo/October 22/2026)
