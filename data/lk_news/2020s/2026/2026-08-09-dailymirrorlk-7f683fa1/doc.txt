Foreign investment in  govt. securities surpasses three-year high

Foreign investment in Sri Lanka’s rupee-denominated government securities has officially surpassed its recent three-year peak of Rs. 192 billion, driven by a fresh wave of inflows during the first week of August.

Market indicators reveal that foreign investors injected an additional Rs. 4 billion in net purchases over the past week, pushing total holdings toward the Rs. 193 billion milestone. This resurgence highlights a growing appetite among international buyers for domestic debt instruments following prolonged periods of economic restructuring.

Official figures from the Central Bank of Sri Lanka for the week ending August 6, 2026, corroborate this upward trajectory, showing that the rupee value of Treasury Bills and Treasury Bonds held by foreign investors increased by approximately 2.1 percent compared to the preceding week.

Specifically, the outstanding stock of government securities held by foreigners rose from Rs. 188.82 billion to Rs. 192.85 billion during this period. While the total outstanding stock of government securities stands at a massive Rs. 18.63 trillion, the steady foreign accumulation is a positive signal for the external sector.

The renewed confidence in Sri Lankan government paper is unfolding against a backdrop of stable market conditions and robust overall demand. During the reporting week, yield rates for Treasury Bills remained broadly stable across both the primary and secondary markets, and Treasury Bond yields demonstrated similar stability in secondary market trading. Furthermore, the primary auction for Treasury Bills experienced strong demand, recording an oversubscription rate of approximately 2.4 times.

Analysing the broader macroeconomic canvas, the influx of foreign capital is intricately tied to recent exchange rate movements and external sector performance. As of August 7, 2026, the year-to-date depreciation of the Sri Lanka rupee against the US dollar stood at 7.6 percent, with the average exchange rate for the US dollar recording at Rs. 335.59. This currency adjustment, alongside a buying rate of Rs. 331.02 and a selling rate of Rs. 340.16, appears to have created an attractive entry point for foreign investors seeking yield in local currency assets.

Furthermore, the Central Bank’s proactive role in the domestic foreign exchange market has bolstered investor sentiment. In July 2026, the Central Bank recorded a net absorption of US dollars 349 million, effectively reinforcing the country’s external buffers. Consequently, gross official reserves were provisionally estimated at a healthy US dollars 6.59 billion by the end of July.

This comfortable liquidity position, coupled with a total outstanding domestic market liquidity surplus of Rs. 246.81 billion by August 7, 2026, reflects a definitive stabilization of Sri Lanka’s macroeconomic environment.
