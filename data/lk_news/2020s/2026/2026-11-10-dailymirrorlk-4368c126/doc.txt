Prison measles outbreak raises fears of wider spread despite elimination status

Colombo, Nov. 10 (Daily Mirror) - Amidst a minor measles outbreak reported at three prison facilities, concerns have been raised over the possibility of a wider spread of the disease, particularly given Sri Lanka’s efforts to maintain its measles elimination status.

In light of the outbreak detected at the Colombo Remand Prison, Magazine Remand Prison and Welikada Prison, authorities have suspended producing inmates before courts as a precautionary measure.

Courts had been informed of the decision in writing, and no inmates from the three facilities were produced before courts yesterday.

However, Government Medical Officers’ Association (GMOA) Secretary Dr Prabath Sugathadasa said occasional outbreaks could occur, but Sri Lanka was in a relatively safe position as the national vaccination programme had continued without interruption.

He stressed that maintaining high vaccination coverage was essential to preventing the disease from spreading beyond isolated clusters.

Meanwhile, Health Ministry sources said the situation within the affected prisons is under control, with strict measures being implemented to prevent further transmission.

A vaccination programme has also been launched among inmates as part of efforts to contain the outbreak.

Sri Lanka was declared free of indigenous measles transmission by the World Health Organisation (WHO) in July 2019. However, the country experienced a resurgence of the disease in May 2023, with more than 700 cases reported following gaps in vaccination coverage.

Following supplementary immunisation campaigns conducted with the support of international health agencies, Sri Lanka regained its measles elimination status in January 2025.

Children in Sri Lanka receive two doses of the measles and rubella (MR) vaccine through the national immunisation programme.
