Statutory rape: Young father gets ten-year rigorous imprisonment reduced to two years

In a rare move that prioritises an offender’s reformation and rehabilitation over a lengthy custodial sentence, the Court of Appeal has reduced a 10-year rigorous imprisonment sentence imposed on a young man convicted of raping his girlfriend when she was under 15 years of age to two years, taking into consideration that he was only 19 at the time of the offence and is now a married father of a young child.

A two-judge Bench comprising Justices P. Kumararatnam and Pradeep Hettiarachchi held that the circumstances of the case warranted an approach focusing on the offender’s reformation and rehabilitation rather than a lengthy custodial sentence.

The Court observed that the accused, who had just attained adulthood at the time of the incident, would need guidance, supervision and rehabilitation to become a good parent rather than being subjected to punishment that would brand him as a criminal.

The judgement was delivered in an appeal filed by the accused, who challenged the sentence imposed by the Ampara High Court while withdrawing his appeal against the conviction.

The High Court had sentenced him to one year of rigorous imprisonment for kidnapping a girl under 16 years of age and 10 years of rigorous imprisonment for raping her. It had also imposed fines of Rs.20,000 and Rs.50,000 respectively, together with compensation of Rs.300,000.

The incident occurred in October 2020, when the victim was 15 years and nine months old. According to the judgement, the victim had eloped with the accused following a love affair between them. She had admitted that she voluntarily left home with him and had consensual sexual intercourse only once with the accused. The accused was 19 years old at the time of the incident. By the time he gave evidence before the High Court, he was 23, married and had a child aged two and a half years.

The Court relied on the Supreme Court determination (Supreme Court Reference No. 03 of 2008) which recognised that a mandatory minimum sentence under Section 364(2)(e) of the Penal Code was in conflict with constitutional provisions and that courts retained judicial discretion to impose an appropriate sentence.

The Court acknowledged the seriousness of the offence but observed that the accused had only recently attained adulthood when the incident occurred and that the circumstances included a love affair between the parties.

However, the fines and compensation imposed by the High Court were left unchanged. The appeal was dismissed subject to the variation of the sentence.
