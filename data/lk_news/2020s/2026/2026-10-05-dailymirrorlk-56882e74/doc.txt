Sri Lanka rejects claims of US pressure over Iranian vessels

Colombo, Oct. 5 (Daily Mirror) - Foreign Minister Vijitha Herath today rejected reports that the United States had pressured Sri Lanka not to provide food or water to Iranian vessels anchored in international waters, saying the Government had taken decisions independently while protecting the country’s sovereignty, independence and neutrality.

Speaking at an event, Herath said claims that US pressure had influenced Sri Lanka’s position were completely false, adding that the United States had neither issued instructions nor intervened in the matter.

He said the Government would not hesitate to intervene on humanitarian grounds when necessary, while continuing to act in accordance with international maritime law.

Herath said around 47 foreign vessels were currently anchored in international waters beyond Sri Lanka’s 12-nautical-mile territorial limit. Of these, around 19 were Iranian vessels that had remained in the region for some time.

He said the situation had become more complex due to developments in the Strait of Hormuz, with vessels also remaining in international waters near countries such as Malaysia and Indonesia.

The Minister said the Government had never supplied or transported goods to vessels subjected to US sanctions.

“If private-sector entities provide goods or services to such vessels, they must understand the circumstances and take responsibility for their actions,” he said.

Herath said that Sri Lanka would continue to make decisions based on its national interests and sovereignty while complying with international maritime law.

He also dismissed reports that Sri Lanka had been instructed by the US not to provide food or water to the Iranian vessels, reiterating that no such directive, intervention or pressure had been communicated to the Government.
