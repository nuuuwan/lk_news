Riyadh’s tallest skyscraper hit in Houthi strike

RIYADH, Nov 19 (Reuters) - Riyadh's tallest skyscraper, headquarters of the Saudi sovereign wealth fund, was struck on ​Thursday by a suspected Houthi missile in the latest blow in the Iran-backed Yemeni group's war with the kingdom.

The strike blew out ‌windows across seven to eight floors of PIF Tower, bending parts of its metal facade. It was the second time in less than two weeks that a Houthi attack damaged a landmark in Riyadh's financial district, with state oil giant Aramco's building hit in a November 8 attack.

Entrances to the King Abdullah Financial District (KAFD) were closed and people were being evacuated, ​while ambulances and fire trucks were on the scene. There were no immediate reports of casualties.

Three sources familiar with the matter said the ​building had been struck by an incoming Houthi missile. The Saudi-led coalition fighting the Houthis said it had "dealt with a ⁠missile threat" launched by the Houthis towards Riyadh, and that its initial assessment showed debris had fallen and material damage had been reported.

PIF, which stands ​for the Saudi Public Investment Fund, acknowledged receiving a Reuters request for comment. The Houthis issued no immediate statement on the strike.

The Houthis have been firing ​at Saudi airports and oil infrastructure since Yemen's frozen civil war was reignited in July amid the US-Iran war. On the ground in Yemen, the group has seized swathes of territory from the Saudi-backed government, forcing it to stage a major retreat at the weekend.

Residents of the Saudi capital were awoken by a loud boom in the early hours of Thursday.

The Houthi-run ​Saba news agency reported that Saudi aircraft had carried out airstrikes on the Houthi-controlled Yemeni capital Sanaa early on Thursday, without providing further details.

The conflict has dealt a big blow to Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman's plans to develop and diversify the economy of Saudi Arabia, the world's ‌biggest oil ⁠exporter and a US ally.

The prince met US Central Command chief Admiral Brad Cooper in Riyadh on Wednesday, Cooper's third publicly reported visit to Saudi Arabia since the Yemen conflict resumed.

Reuters reported on Wednesday that Saudi officials now foresee the campaign stretching on for up to six months, according to two Western officials briefed by the Saudis on the kingdom's plans. A third source familiar with the Saudi plans confirmed the accounts.

The sources said the Saudis, who replaced the commander ​of their coalition fighting in Yemen ​this week, want to see their ⁠allies make gains on the ground before they enter any negotiations with the Houthis.

But meanwhile the situation in Yemen has appeared to favour the Houthis, who have seized Yemen's entire Red Sea coast including the strategically vital Bab ​el-Mandeb Strait at its mouth, and have surrounded the city of Taiz in highlands overlooking the government's temporary capital ​Aden.

In the wider war, ⁠which began in February when the United States and Israel attacked Iran, there has been no sign of a breakthrough in negotiations mediated by Qatar.
