US Ambassador leads pledge of service for 34 new Peace Corps volunteers in Sri Lanka

Thirty-four Americans took the Peace Corps Pledge of Service this week, beginning a two-year commitment to work alongside Sri Lankan teachers and communities in the Northern and North Central provinces to strengthen English-language education.

Through classroom co-teaching, teacher workshops, and collaboration with local educators, Peace Corps Volunteers will help students build American English-language skills. Beyond the classroom, they will live with Sri Lankan host families, share American traditions, discover Sri Lankan culture, and build lasting friendships that strengthen US-Sri Lanka ties.

US Ambassador to Sri Lanka Eric Meyer administered the Peace Corps Pledge of Service to the 34 Americans at a ceremony in Kotapanwila, Bulathsinhala, joined by Foreign Affairs Deputy Minister Arun Hemachandra and Education Ministry Director of the English and Foreign Languages Branch Nimali Baduraliya.  The group represents the fourth cohort since the Peace Corps program was re-established in Sri Lanka in 2018 and the largest single cohort sent to the country. The ceremony recognised the Volunteers’ commitment to service and the vital role of Sri Lankan teachers, host families, and communities in welcoming and supporting them.

“The strength of America has always been found in its people—in their skills, their determination, and their willingness to step forward and serve,” said Ambassador Meyer. “These 34 Americans are taking those strengths halfway around the world, bringing their talents into Sri Lankan classrooms and becoming part of the communities they serve. Over the next two years, they will do more than teach. They will become neighbours, friends, and members of families who welcome them into their homes. And when they return to America, they will leave behind something that cannot be measured in lessons taught: a deeper understanding of who Americans are, what we value, and the friendships that make our country stronger.”

Education Ministry Deputy Director of Education & Liaison to Peace Corps Sri Lanka Ananda Arasu said:  “Peace Corps volunteers play a valuable role in strengthening English language education in Sri Lanka. Through their dedication, commitment and collaboration with local teachers, they create meaningful opportunities for students to develop their English communication skills. Their contribution goes beyond language teaching, fostering cultural exchange, mutual understanding and global citizenship. This partnership reflects the power of international cooperation in building a brighter future for Sri Lankan learners.”

Before taking the pledge, the Volunteers completed nine weeks of intensive training in Sinhala and Tamil, Sri Lankan culture, and teaching methodologies.  English-language education has long been a bridge between the United States and Sri Lanka, opening doors to new ideas, higher education, and opportunities in the global economy. Peace Corps Volunteers bring American teaching experience, creativity, and a spirit of service into Sri Lankan classrooms, working alongside local educators to help students build the skills and confidence to pursue their ambitions. Beyond the classroom, Sri Lankan host families open their homes and communities to these Americans, sharing their language, traditions, and everyday lives. It is through these personal connections that Peace Corps leaves one of its most enduring legacies: friendships, mutual understanding, and bonds between our countries that last long after the Volunteers return home.
