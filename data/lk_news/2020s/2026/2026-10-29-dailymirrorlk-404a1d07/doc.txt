Indirect taxes ’super high’, no decision on property - Dy. Finance Minister

Colombo, Oct. 29 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka's indirect taxes are "super high" and the tax structure needs to change, but the government has not taken a decision to implement a property tax and will not make any surprise tax moves, Labour Minister and Deputy Minister of Finance and Planning Dr. Anil Jayantha Fernando said on Tuesday.

Responding to a question at the Institutional Investor Forum, jointly organised by the Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka (SEC) and the Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE), ahead of the presentation of the 2027 Budget on November 12, Dr. Fernando explained that the property tax had initially been proposed under the economic recovery programme. The government, however, had faced obstacles in implementing it and concerns over its impact.

"There are pros and cons on having taxes. We are not going to do something unless it is clear, especially in tax policies, and that is aligned with these basic principles," he remarked, adding, "We have not taken a decision to implement."

At the same time, he cautioned that any reset of the tax structure cannot come at the cost of fiscal consolidation and revenue mobilisation.

"If that is gone, again the economy would be in real trouble," he warned, assuring that tax decisions would be discussed adequately with stakeholders and made public.

In his address, Dr. Fernando revealed that government revenue reached 16.7 percent of GDP at the end of 2025, already 1.3 percentage points above the original target of 15.4 percent set for 2030. He expects revenue to rise to around 18 percent of GDP by 2030, creating fiscal space for much-needed public investment.

Once revenue reaches that level, indirect taxes could be brought down, he indicated. While describing the 18 percent VAT rate as relatively high, he noted that it cannot be cut at this stage without damaging revenue mobilisation, and said future tax changes would be guided by the principles of fairness, simplicity and convenience.

His remarks on the property tax echo President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, who said last month that the 2027 Budget would not include a property tax, arguing that property is built from earnings that have already been taxed.

A nationwide property tax was among the revenue measures agreed under the International Monetary Fund (IMF) programme in 2023. It was later replaced by a proposed imputed rental income tax targeted for April 2025, which was dropped after the November 2024 general election. The IMF has since reportedly pushed for a tax on secondary residential properties in the 2027 Budget, while the government maintains that its planned revenue measures are sufficient to meet the targets.
