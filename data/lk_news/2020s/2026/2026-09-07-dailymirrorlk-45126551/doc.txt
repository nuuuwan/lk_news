Govt. to clear unauthorized coastal builds, develop coast as tourist attraction

Colombo, Sep. 7 (Daily Mirror) - The Government has decided to remove unauthorized constructions along Sri Lanka’s coastal belt as part of plans to develop the coastal areas as a tourist attraction.

Deputy Environment Minister Anton Jayakody told the Daily Mirror that all unauthorized structures erected along the coast would be removed under the relevant regulations.

The Department of Coast Conservation and Coastal Resource Management has identified around 2,500 unauthorized constructions along the island’s coastline. Director General Dr. P. B. Terney Pradeep Kumara said action to remove such structures had already begun.

According to the Department, the unauthorized constructions violate coastal regulations and could pose environmental risks.

Last month, authorities demolished at least 22 unauthorized structures, including beach-side restaurants and cafés, along the Ahangama coastline.

Meanwhile, the Department has launched another phase of action targeting unauthorized constructions between Bambalapitiya and Dehiwela. Demolition orders have reportedly been issued against 19 unauthorized structures belonging to eight individuals, including properties along Bambalapitiya Marine Drive.

The owners have been instructed to remove the structures and their contents before September 10. The Department has warned that if the structures are not removed by the deadline, they will be demolished by the authorities without further notice.

The notice also states that the Government will not be responsible for any damage caused during the removal of the structures.

Under the Coast Conservation and Coastal Resource Management Act No. 57 of 1981, a valid permit is required for construction within the coastal zone. The Act also empowers the Department to remove unauthorized constructions without obtaining a court order.
