Budgetary shortfalls, strike actions leave governance at a crossroads

Observations made regarding the 2027 Budget have not been satisfactory. It cannot simply be described as ‘promising’ when looking at the real situation. Already, the prices of vegetables have been increased. Minister of Trade, Commerce, Food Security and Cooperative Development Wasantha Samarasinghe cited El Niño conditions affecting India as possible reasons for the increase in vegetable prices. But the weather cannot be blamed for every shortcoming.

Apart from farmers and even the public, medical trade unions have expressed their disappointment towards the government in the form of an island-wide strike action on Monday (23).

The Government Medical Officers Association (GMOA) claims that the government has ignored their demands and haven’t included allowances that were promised to them in the recent budget.

One of their main concerns is with regards to the payment of the Disturbance, Availability and Transport (DAT) Allowance. Medical officers currently receive Rs. 70,000 per month. But in his budget speech President Dissanayake proposed to increase it by Rs. 25,000 raising the monthly DAT allowance to Rs. 95,000.

But according to the GMOA a DAT allowance of Rs. 120,000 has been approved since 2018. They want the remaining Rs. 50,000 from the Rs. 120,000 allowance to be paid in full to consultants, administrators and grade medical officers.

Another demand is to include medical practitioners under Public Administration Circular No. 2/99 issued in 1999. This makes medical officers in Grade 1 positions eligible for a transport allowance. However, for the past 26 years medical practitioners have been excluded from this circular.

According to the GMOA, these are not new demands but the government has failed to respond to them. They claim that these benefits are being given to executive grade public officials and that medical officers too deserve these benefits.

Other demands include payment of outstanding public holiday and extra-duty allowances, changes to the tax regime affecting doctors and salary adjustments.

They have also proposed the establishment of a dedicated Sri Lanka Medical Service, improved career development opportunities, stronger primary healthcare and measures to address staffing shortages and workforce management problems in the public health sector.

These kinds of strike action hampers the regular functioning at government hospitals. In the end it is the common man who suffers due to unavailability of medical care for their health conditions.

Patients in hospitals in the outskirts of Colombo who do not have access to or cannot afford private medical facilities are mostly affected by these decisions. They sometimes have to travel long distances for their health checkups. In low income households, medical expenses are a burden and they rely on the government healthcare system as much as possible.  Due to unaffordability, even a patient with a fractured leg or spinal problem is compelled to travel by bus to reach the hospital. If they are supposed to hire a vehicle to travel to hospital they have to do so but it mostly happens after they have obtained money by mortgaging jewellery or other valuables.

Without a doubt, doctors, consultants, medical practitioners and officers working at all levels deserve to be provided with allowances and other benefits. They work under stressful conditions, sometimes without proper sleep, meals and having to stay away from their families for long periods of time. They don’t have proper boarding facilities and as a result many doctors are compelled to do private practice.

However, due to administrative issues of this nature, the quality and efficiency of Sri Lanka’s healthcare system has been largely impacted. In order to restore quality and efficiency, staff working in administrative and non-executive levels should be treated equally.

Sri Lanka’s state sector needs a complete overhaul to rectify this issue. It is an issue that not only affects medical practitioners or doctors but the entire state sector.

The public service is the backbone of state administration, delivering essential daily services and implementing government policy across the country. Therefore, it is the duty of the government to ensure that at least some of the key demands of these state sector public officials are being met.
