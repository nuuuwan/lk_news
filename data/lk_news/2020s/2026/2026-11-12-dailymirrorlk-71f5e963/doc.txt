Sri Lanka considers mandatory standards for imported electric vehicles and batteries

Colombo, Nov. 12 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka is considering introducing mandatory regulations and standards for electric vehicles, batteries, controllers and charging stations imported into the country, following concerns over the absence of proper testing and certification requirements.

The issue was discussed at a recent meeting of the Sectoral Oversight Committee on Science, Technology and Digital Transformation held in Parliament under the chairmanship of MP Dr. Janaka Senarathna.

Officials informed the Committee that the National Engineering Research and Development Centre (NERDC) had facilities to test electric vehicle batteries, including their capacity, durability and lifespan.

However, concerns were raised over the lack of mandatory standards governing electric vehicles and related equipment imported into Sri Lanka.

The Committee stressed the need to introduce appropriate standards and make testing and certification compulsory to ensure the safety and quality of electric vehicles.

Discussions also focused on the technology used to convert fuel-powered vehicles into electric vehicles, the need for specialised training programmes and the introduction of safety standards and regulatory mechanisms for such conversions.

The Committee further discussed the need to strengthen local technical capacity and ensure that electric vehicle technology meets appropriate safety requirements.

The meeting also considered the Annual Report of the NERDC for 2022, the National Science and Technology Commission for 2023, the Sri Lanka Inventors Commission for 2024 and the Ministry of Science and Technology's Annual Performance Report for 2025.
