SEC warns allegations risk denting confidence in capital market regulation

Concerns relate to statements made publicly in Colombo last month and subsequently circulated through social media and other media channels

Capital market regulator maintained that statements contained claims that were false, baseless, misleading, derogatory and defamatory and devoid of factual foundation

Asserts statements undermine public confidence in integrity, independence and proper functioning of securities regulatory framework established under Sri Lankan law

SEC has expressly reserved its right to seek appropriate relief, including injunctions, damages, compensation and costs

The capital market regulator, the Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka (SEC), has warned that the allegedly false and unsubstantiated claims against the regulator and its officials risk undermining public confidence in the integrity and independence of the country’s capital market regulatory framework. The SEC’s concerns relate to the statements made publicly in Colombo last month and subsequently circulated through social media and other media channels.

Taking a serious view of the matter, the capital market regulator maintained that the statements contained claims that were false, baseless, misleading, derogatory and defamatory and devoid of factual foundation.

The SEC said such statements were likely to cause serious reputational damage to the SEC, its chairman, members and officers, while more importantly undermining the public confidence in the integrity, independence and proper functioning of the securities regulatory framework established under the Sri Lankan law.

The regulator has also taken issue with claims that it said sought to impugn the integrity, impartiality, competence and conduct of the SEC and its officials in discharging their statutory duties and functions under Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka Act No. 19 of 2021.

It maintained that the statements were wholly unsubstantiated and made recklessly and/or maliciously, with the knowledge of their falsity or with reckless disregard for their truth or accuracy.

The SEC has further flagged the amplification of the claims via social media, noting that their wider dissemination had increased their reach and visibility among the public.

It warned that such statements had the tendency to mislead the investors and public regarding the lawful exercise of the SEC’s regulatory, supervisory, investigative and enforcement powers.

The development assumes significance, given the critical role of regulatory credibility and investor confidence in the continued development of Sri Lanka’s capital market.

In a firm response, the SEC has sought the withdrawal of the disputed statements and a public correction expressly acknowledging that the claims were inaccurate, misleading and made without factual basis.

It has also called for a written and public apology to the SEC, its chairman, members and officers, with the apology to be disseminated through the same media platforms or public forums capable of reaching the original audience.

The regulator has cautioned that failure to comply within the stipulated period could see it pursue available legal remedies, including the proceedings under the SEC Act and other applicable laws.

The SEC has expressly reserved its right to seek appropriate relief, including injunctions, damages, compensation and costs.
