Government plans major overhaul of international airports

Colombo, Nov. 12 (Daily Mirror) - President Anura Kumara Dissanayake today announced a series of development projects for Sri Lanka's international airports, including expanding passenger facilities at the Bandaranaike International Airport (BIA), upgrading Ratmalana Airport and attracting new investors to Mattala Airport.

Presenting the 2027 Budget in Parliament, the President said increasing BIA's passenger handling capacity was a priority as the airport was already handling more passengers than the first terminal's designed capacity of around six million.

Work has already commenced to increase the first terminal's capacity from six million to nine million passengers annually, with an investment of Rs. 15 billion by Airport and Aviation Services (Sri Lanka) Limited (AASL).

Construction of the long-delayed second terminal is also scheduled to commence shortly with assistance from the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA).

A domestic air terminal will be constructed within the airport premises next year at a cost of Rs. 800 million to facilitate international passengers travelling to domestic destinations.

The Government will also allocate Rs. 200 million for a feasibility study on widening the road in front of the airport and constructing a flyover to ease traffic congestion. A new car park will be built by AASL at a cost of Rs. 400 million.

The President further announced plans to establish a re-export hub within BIA on a Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT) basis, with private sector participation, to develop the airport as an air cargo handling hub.

Meanwhile, the Ratmalana International Airport will be developed to facilitate pilot training, domestic flights, business aviation and regional aviation.

A Rs. 6 billion project to rehabilitate and extend its existing runway from 1,400 metres to 1,771 metres will commence in 2027 using AASL funds. The project is intended to improve aviation safety and support the airport's expanded operations.

Referring to Mattala International Airport, the President said it had accumulated losses exceeding Rs. 70 billion since commencing flight operations in 2013. The airport was constructed with a loan of US$190 million, and its losses continued to increase annually.

He said Expressions of Interest (EOIs) had already been called and concluded to develop several areas of the airport in partnership with international companies.

The proposed investment areas include airport operations, data centres, food and catering services, and aircraft services and maintenance.

Steps are now being taken to call for Requests for Proposals (RFPs) from the selected companies, with the Government expecting to select new investors within the next year.
