Jerome Fernando is a very well-known name in the country. While many still criticise him as he calls himself the son of God, his followers have strengthened locally and internationally. In an exclusive interview with Daily Mirror, Jerome says that while each one is free to speak and criticise, the hate does not get to him, as he is on the path of spirituality where God has chosen him to deliver His message. Following are the excerpts of his interview

Q. It’s been one year since I last interviewed you, and ever since then, the name Jerome Fernando has grown in terms of popularity and unpopularity. While your followers have grown, criticism has also grown quite a bit. What do you have to say about the things you have achieved up to date?

What I’ve managed to accomplish is based on a vision God gave me many, many years ago. I have not diverted my attention from that vision. The vision is very simple: to reach as many people internationally, plant a church globally in every country there are 194-plus countries, and bring international visitors to Sri Lanka.

That is why the Miracle Dome is located not even a kilometre from the airport, thereby bringing economic development to Sri Lanka and being one of the most unconventional first-time churches in this country to do such a feat. That is the primary focus of the vision.

While I’m reaching the globe through different television stations, etc., the locals get blessed in the process. So the healing of the sick, international prophecies, prophecies about local artists, this and that—a lot is happening. It is all very spiritual.

The tangible is what we measure when we see people and how their lives are being transformed. It’s not just the healing, it’s not just the prophetic; it’s their well-being, their deliverance, them coming out of darkness into God’s marvellous light. As you are aware, in this region there’s a lot of witchcraft and a lot of the occult that is being practised.

So when people come to me and they get delivered, they’re introduced into a new world. That’s the tangible result. Their families change, their generations change, their children change.

And of course, moving on from there, we are building a 1,500-bed facility for the poor because 60,000 sick people, for example, came to the Miracle Dome last year. But that number is going to double in less than a year. So I’m going to have a facility where the sick can also be housed.

These are a few accomplishments that I can mention amongst many others, the spiritual plus the tangible.

Right now, 40 global branches that I can say are mine, that are directly under my umbrella and influence.

However, saying that, because we’ve had over 10,000 unique international visitors in the last two years from different countries. When we calculate the number of countries, our influence is in over 100 countries. Given us calculating the physical attendance of an international visitor, we can give an account for 100 countries. But whereas the physical church goes, they are planted in maybe 40 countries.

Q.So you said you have churches in 40 countries. How have those governments accepted your presence or the presence of your church in those countries? Have you faced any issues from these people?

We have had the most favourable response from every country where we’ve had to plant a church. I think the reason for that is that there are no preconceived notions. They’re not looking at a church with a preconceived notion. The facts are on the table. The directors of the organisation have to be from that country, and there are no strings attached to anything.

They do a background check on the ministry, the financial transparency, etc., and they clear us. For example, just a few months ago, we were registered with the UK’s Charity Commission.

Now, that is a very rare feat for a Sri Lankan church because you cannot get the UK’s Charity Commission to register you if you are playing around. You have to play with a straight bat, know what you’re doing, for the UK’s Charity Commission to approve you. That’s just one example.

Q.So compared to how you have been well received in those countries, to what you are facing here, because just a few years ago we saw that you had to face remand over some comments you had made. And in your last interview, you said unfortunately successive Sri Lankan governments were not too open in accepting you. When you compare how the international governments have accepted you and given you the respect that you deserve to what you are facing in this country, what can you say about that?

Nevertheless, I love my country, and I honour the king because that’s what the scripture says. Honour the king, pray for those in leadership that you may live a peaceful life. So whoever is in power, I honour those powers.

That is the word of God, and I submit to those powers. I have not violated any laws. I’m a taxpayer of this country. I abide by national law, and that’s why I keep standing.

Now, I appreciate foreign governments seeing my influence. For example, this might be a bit premature for me to say, but in a month or two I’m going to be given a diplomatic position from the government of Eswatini. My wife and I will have to go and meet His Majesty the King.

Then, right now, there are talks with the government of Zimbabwe to appoint me as the Deputy High Commissioner for Zimbabwe in Sri Lanka. It’s still at the level of discussion, and we will see the results in a couple of months.

So these are examples that foreign nations know who I am, but unfortunately the Sri Lankan government has yet to recognise what I can do for them. I’m not going to hold a placard and say I can help you, but if they are willing to open a door, they will know the wisdom I carry and how I can help them move this country forward, upwards and even fast forward.

Q.For your followers, you are a prophet, but many still criticise you that you are self-proclaimed and somewhat in the name of religion, you are misleading the people. What can you say about this? Because this is a question which comes up quite a lot when the name Jerome Fernando comes up.

Who said I am self-proclaimed? See, who endorses a mango tree when it bears mangoes? If the mango tree is bearing mangoes, it’s obviously a mango tree.

So when a prophet produces fruit that matches its office, he’s no longer self-proclaimed. People who say I am self-proclaimed don’t know what they’re saying. It’s just that they have what is called mob culture, whoever is saying what, let me also go and say it.

I’m not a self-proclaimed person or a self-proclaimed prophet. The Bible says that in the mouth of two or three witnesses a thing is established.

I have my spiritual father who has pointed his finger and said, You are a prophet. Him saying that means God has said it. That’s two witnesses right there.

And then I have my people, that’s over a hundred thousand, maybe close to a million local followers, who will say I am a prophet of God. I’ve got more witnesses to say that I am a prophet. So it’s no longer self-proclaimed.

But what was the other thing that they were saying? Misleading in my sermons or misleading the people? It’s like a student correcting a teacher, saying, ‘teacher, I don’t agree with your teaching.’ You have to be at a higher position than a teacher to correct a teacher.

I have three PhDs. I’m a Doctor of Philosophy, I have a professorship in leadership, and I’m a Doctor of Divinity. I have a master’s in business administration and a bachelor’s in business administration.

For someone to say I am misleading in my messages, then you have to be of a higher standing or rank to be able to correct me. A child never corrects a parent. A student never corrects a teacher. You have to be either on the same level or higher.

If someone is of that stature, higher than me or at the same level, and they’re saying I’m misleading, I respect that. Although I will not worship at the shrine of other people’s opinions, they have the right to perceive a thing and have their own narrative. I have no problems with that.

It does not, because I’ve come to a place of what I call being impregnable, where nothing anyone does affects me anymore. I’m made of elephant skin.

Do I have emotion? Yeah. This is where prison helped me. Being remanded for 33 days for preaching helped me in that way because for 33 days I was with terrorists and the so-called convicted drug lords. I had to sleep with the cockroaches and the mice and eat prison food. So that gave me a ruggedness to my soul, where right now I just don’t care too much about what people say.

Q.Since you hold the title of prophet, and that means the messenger of God, the son of God, and in your weekly sermons we see you healing people. Many people from other countries have come to you just to get healing done by you. If you have that power, why can’t you help what is happening in the world today? There’s so much suffering happening. There’s so much of killing happening. For example, you know, children in Gaza are dying.There are wars happening everywhere else. Why can’t you be a little bit more vocal and help to make the world a better place?

Yeah, so that’s exactly what I’m doing. I think our readers will know this statement: charity starts at home. How do I reach the world if I’m not first impactful in my own country?

So my focus is, I know my capacity, I know my influence, I know my impact, and I know my authority.

When Jesus sent his disciples, he said, ‘Don’t go beyond a certain border.’ He said Reach the Israelites first. But later, after his resurrection, he tells them, ‘Now you can go to the outermost parts of the earth.’

That means that the authority was delegated. You, as the Editor, don’t go to India and start leading other people’s newspapers. You have a delegated authority.

In the same manner, I can’t go to Gaza yet. But there’ll come a day when I’ll be able to use that influence progressively and help more people. For the moment, I am doing more than what is required, given the authority, influence and freedom that I have.

Q.A lot of people on social media see the miracles which happen every week. So some people ask, if the Prophet has so much healing power, why doesn’t he go to the cancer hospital and heal the sick children? Why doesn’t he go to the general hospital and heal the sick there? What can you say about that?

A simple word is – ‘environment’. I would ask the same people, do you like the cemetery? Will you have a birthday party at the cemetery? Would you even build your house in a cemetery? The answer is no.

Why? Because we don’t like that environment. There are environments that healings don’t take place, and there are environments where healing takes place.

That is my first answer. The second answer is that the Miracle Dome in itself is called the medical dome. People have mispronounced it.

Why? Because this has become a hospital. With 1,500 sick people, which doctor in this country, you tell me, sees 1,500 patients a week? I don’t think any doctor has the capacity or the time to see 1,500 sick people a week. Yet, that’s what I do here.

And people are wanting me to go to hospital and heal the sick. No, the hospital is coming here.

Absolutely. No one has been rejected, refused or even rebuked. Everybody from spiritual leaders to diplomatic people, to the poor, to the rich, they all come here. The thing is, healing is not the only thing I’m preaching.

Q.You say you do so many healings a week, and there are a lot of miracles which happen in your Miracle Dome as well. What is that one miracle which would have happened in your lifetime, which happened by you, which is still very much alive in your mind?

Thank you so much for that question. I can’t forget any because every miracle is special, every healing is special, every sign, every wonder is special, every deliverance is special because, at the end, I know I’m only a vessel.

That’s the reality of it. Power belongs to God. When that power flows through me, I know I’m not the source. Because of what Jesus has finished, I have an easy job to do.

It’s called the finished work of Christ. Jesus paid a price for humanity, rose again from the dead, gave us his Holy Spirit, and every time the power flows through me, I think, man, I’m so honoured. God is so great. So every miracle is special. But when a child gets healed, I can never forget it.

Either they were dumb, they started speaking; they were deaf, they started hearing; they were crippled, they started walking; or they had cancer, they got healed. When it is a child, it is unforgettable.

One of the recent miracles that took place here was children that are demon-possessed being brought in chains, physically chained. Because they’re demon-possessed, they eat their own flesh, and the parents can’t control them.

I just laid hands and moved on. One week later, the parent comes and says, “Prophet after you laid your hand on my child, my child became free, now we do not have to keep our child chained and the child talks to us with so much love.” You can never forget those things, because they’re children.

Q.In your earlier answer, you told me witchcraft has really increased. Why do you say that?

Because witchcraft is a business. Witchcraft, by definition, is invoking ungodly, evil spirits to control people’s minds using curious arts. It’s so prevalent nowadays because it brings in money for the witch doctor. Our people are gullible and desperate; they want to feel something tangible, so they get hooked. What is meant to help them doesn’t, so they spend money, try everything, and eventually come here. Then they hear the gospel of Jesus Christ: you don’t need all of this, a man died on the cross to pay your penalty, and believing that brings freedom. So yes, witchcraft is a business today.

Q.What are the biggest misconceptions Sri Lankans have about Prophet Jerome Fernando?

That’s a good question. The biggest misconception is that I’m in it for the money. They don’t understand how much I gave up to prove to God that I am in this for the calling. If they don’t know my seed, they shouldn’t judge my house; if they don’t know my sacrifice, they shouldn’t judge my prosperity. I’m not in it for luxury, I’m in it for legacy. That is the main thing they misunderstand.

Q.So regarding your family: recently your daughter got engaged to Uebert Angel’s son. In a previous interview, you mentioned he was the divine father who pointed at you as the chosen one. People say your daughter chose this path to keep the religion or message within the family. How do you address those allegations?

A - It’s not an allegation; it’s an observation. The observation is right, but the interpretation is wrong. As fathers, we are responsible for our generation and our legacy. According to Proverbs 22:6–7, you must train up a child in the way they should go. You can’t just let your child marry anyone and risk ruining a legacy. Prophet Angel and I, as fathers, recognised what God wanted. It wasn’t just our choice either; both children wanted it as well. To an outsider, it looks like keeping it within the family, but it is actually responsible fatherhood to protect the generation.

I would want it to follow for my second daughter as well. In fact, I wish I had four daughters, because Prophet Angel’s family has four sons.

Q.What are your views about religious leaders commenting on politics? Is it their mandate, or should they stick to preaching?

For me, Christ is the example. We live where four worlds collide: the natural, spiritual, financial, and political. These four worlds impact everyone’s life whether you like it or not, and at some point, you have to address them according to your sphere of influence. I see nothing wrong with a spiritual leader addressing political issues because Jesus did it. He confronted Pilate and Herod, even calling Herod a fox. Jesus was tough and addressed political, spiritual, and financial leaders alike.

QRecently, 15 suspects were sentenced to rigorous imprisonment over the Easter Sunday attacks. Do you think sentences of 200 to 260 years bring justice for the victims?

I would use the word consolation. It brings encouragement, but justice cannot heal deep pain. How can legal justice heal a parent who lost a child, or someone left blind or paralysed? From a legal standpoint, sending people to prison brings natural justice, but the blood of the innocent continues to speak from the soil until things are dealt with spiritually.

Q.Would you like to comment on how governments have handled the Easter Sunday attack investigations?

The present government broke a shackle and crossed a threshold that others weren’t capable of handling, so their stance on natural justice is commendable. However, a government can only bring natural solutions; they cannot bring spiritual solutions. That is where we come in.

Q.Have any of the victims’ families approached you for spiritual healing?

They may have attended sessions in the healing room, though I don’t see them face-to-face in close proximity. God directed me to build the Miracle Dome in Negombo in 2016, three years before the attacks happened, to wipe away people’s tears. I am not omniscient, but God moved through my actions before the problem occurred so a solution would be ready. Every time I stand to heal the sick or help someone, I am acting like a justice minister bringing God’s spiritual justice.

Q.You are famous for your predictions. Sri Lanka went through a struggle a few years ago. We were on the brink of collapse. What economic or political predictions do you have for Sri Lanka?

Seventy per cent of this nation is poor, and until the government empowers, educates, and changes the mindset of that 70%, economic instability will remain a continuous cycle. Most spiritualists here just predict by studying historical patterns, but as a prophet, I speak what God tells me. Right now, we must thank God for this government for stabilising the economy, but they must also be grateful to Ranil Wickremesinghe, who stepped in for two years to give them that chance. Without gratitude, fortitude, and developing the soul of the people, lasting prosperity will not happen.

QWith opposition members facing jail time for fraud during their tenure, have any politicians asked you for prayers to avoid facing the law?

No. Nobody has approached me because they don’t have my number. I am a scarce resource and prefer to keep to myself. Even when word reaches me through my inner circle that someone wants to meet, I decline.

Q.You mentioned building 1,500 beds for the sick. How many people have you healed over the past two years?

It’s impossible to record every healing. In the Bible, Jesus performed more miracles than could be documented, and many people who get healed here pass the word on to others without testifying directly. But as a baseline, my right hand is laid on no less than 200,000 people a year. Now, I am teaching people to receive healing simply through the power of God’s word without hands being laid. Recently, I stood for 14 hours straight laying hands on nearly 8,000 people who queued up just to give an offering.

This is normal to me. Every person has a different normal. I was born a prophet, though I didn’t realise it while playing professional U-19 cricket for Sri Lanka or working at MAS Holdings. At age 29, I heard the audible voice of God telling me to leave everything and follow Him. I sacrificed my career to obey, but this life feels normal to me now.

Q.You are still a young man. Sometimes, do you think, do you feel, what if life were different for you? Do you ever think, what if I were just a man with a normal family?

This is normal. Right now, the life that I am living is normal to me. Every person has a different normal. See, I was born a prophet. That’s the thing readers should know. But I didn’t know I was a prophet. Because you are born with a calling, and you are vacant to that calling. I was a professional cricketer.

Until I got injured, I played for my country. Under-19 Sri Lanka. Go to the internet; it’s there.

I captained my school. I was a professional in the apparel sector. I worked for MAS Holdings. I had my own buying office. I worked in the marketplace. Even to this day. I have my influence in the market. But it was when I was 29 that I heard the voice of God. Like the audible voice of God.

God told me, ‘I have called you for something great. Leave everything that you are doing and follow me.’

My oldest daughter was 4 years old. I was carrying her, and I heard that voice. I left everything. And the church was only 100 or 150 people.

And it was that single decision that brought me to where I am. So there is a sacrifice. So my life is normal.

Responsibility keeps you awake. Not black coffee. Responsibility is more powerful than black coffee. It keeps you awake. So I sleep at 5 in the morning. Every single day. Because I hear God better when humans are asleep. That also, and we tend to pray through the night.

We tend to engage in solving spiritual matters when people are asleep. So when humans are asleep, we do the work so that when they are awake, the job is already done. So I pray for my people.

I pray for my financial partners. I rarely pray for my family. I rarely pray for myself. But I pray for others. I pray for the nation. So responsibility keeps me awake.
