Easter massacre – after seven years, the mystery continues

The country was in disarray and no one appeared to be in control

Easter Sunday in April 2019 will long be remembered by Lankan Catholics. On that day, quite out of the blue, as Christians were celebrating the holiest day in their calendar, suicide bombers blew themselves up in churches and three luxury hotels. Two-hundred and seventy people died and over 500 were injured.

The courts have found 15 persons guilty of the dastardly crime, while  nine were acquitted. Yet Lankans are yet to understand why this country was picked to perpetrate the bloody atrocity. Christian and  Muslim communities in this our common homeland have lived peacefully through the centuries. There had been no ‘bad blood’ so to say between the two communities.

The country had just come out of a bloody 30-year war. All communities were yearning for peace. Now there was every danger the mass killings would trigger another communal war - with Christians attacking their Islamic brethren. Thanks to the timely intervention of Catholic leader Malcolm Cardinal Ranjith,  tempers were soothed and a calamity averted.

The Lankan government was unstable at the time. The president who also doubled as defence minister, was in open conflict with his prime minister. It led to a number of security lapses. Making matters even worse, the president was out of the country and the prime minister was out of the capital when the attacks occurred.

The rumour mills had a field day churning out fake news stories by the dozen. The rumour had it that Catholic ministers in the Cabinet had information regarding a possible attack and stayed away from churches on the day. When a particular minister announced that his father had advised him to stay away from church that day, it seemed to confirm the rumour.

The country was in disarray and no one appeared to be in control. The president who was out of the country, for reasons best known to himself,  delayed his return. The then Prime Minister claimed his hands were tied as the president had kept him out of security briefings.

Presidential Commissions of Inquiry, a Parliamentary Select Committee, and a number of administrative/ministerial review bodies inquired into the bombings, but nothing was made public. Speculation grew and suspicions increased. It was in this atmosphere of political instability that former Secretary Defence Gotabaya Rajapaksa announced his candidature for the presidential election of November 2019.  After his election victory at the polls,  however, little progress was made in bringing justice to the victims of the bombings. Allegations of a cover up by the president himself began to surface, after he began meddling with the work of officers inquiring into the Easter Sunday attacks.

Following President Gotabaya’s ouster from the presidency, Mr. Ranil Wickremesinghe was voted president by parliament. During his presidency,  the Supreme Court found former president Maithripala Sirisena guilty of “actively undermining” the government, resulting in the “serious lapses” that allowed the terrorist attacks to happen.

The Attorney General also indicted the former Defence Secretary and former IGP before the Colombo Trial-at-Bar. But, the then Trial-at-Bar bench acquitted both accused, without calling for their defence after the prosecution concluded leading its evidence.

However,  after the retrial process, the Colombo Trial-at-Bar  sentenced former Inspector General of Police (IGP) to death after finding him guilty on several charges, including criminal dereliction of duty for failing to take action to prevent the Easter Sunday terrorist attacks, despite receiving prior intelligence warnings.

Nearly seven years have passed since the terrible terrorist attack. A former President has been found guilty of negligence and was ordered to pay millions in reparation to the victims.  The then IGP was sentenced to death for the same crime as was the defence secretary. A former president was ousted and recently as mentioned earlier, 15 persons have been found guilty of involvement in the crime. The present government is presently carrying out a separate inquiry into the same Easter Sunday attack. But, the country is still unaware why it was targeted, or the reason behind the attack.

We the people can only hope the ongoing investigation will help answer this question.
