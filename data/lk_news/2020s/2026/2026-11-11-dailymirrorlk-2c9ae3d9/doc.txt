COPF questions banks’ lending priorities

Colombo, Nov. 11 (Daily Mirror) - Credit extended to Sri Lanka's private sector reached Rs. 2.1 trillion during the first nine months of 2026, up from Rs. 1.9 trillion during the corresponding period last year, Central Bank officials told the Committee on Public Finance (COPF).

The increase in private sector lending, inflation targeting and the stability of the banking system were among the key issues discussed at a recent COPF meeting held in Parliament under the chairmanship of MP Dr. Harsha de Silva.

Central Bank Governor Dr. P. Nandalal Weerasinghe and other senior Central Bank officials attended the meeting, along with Deputy Minister Chathuranga Abeysinghe and several MPs.

During the meeting, officials explained the Central Bank's decision to maintain the headline inflation target, based on the Colombo Consumer Price Index (CCPI), at 5% over the next three years.

They also revealed that seven special reports had been submitted to Parliament on occasions when inflation remained below the lower limit of the target range of 3%.

Of the Rs. 2.1 trillion in private sector credit extended during the first nine months of this year, approximately 74% had been provided by licensed commercial banks.

However, the Committee raised concerns over changes in the composition of lending, noting that the share of short-term loans had increased from 42% to 47%, while the share of long-term loans had declined from 35% to 29%.

The Committee also questioned whether bank lending was being sufficiently directed towards capital formation, new investments and the expansion of production capacity. The growth of different loan categories, including mortgage and vehicle loans, was also discussed.

The country's long-term economic growth potential was another key area of discussion, with emphasis placed on the need for reforms in the trade, labour and land sectors alongside appropriate monetary policies.

Central Bank officials and Committee members also highlighted the importance of human capital development, improving productivity and attracting foreign direct investment to support sustainable economic growth.

Providing an update on financial system stability, Central Bank officials said the banking sector's Stage 3 non-performing loan ratio had declined from 9.7% to 8.8%, while capital and liquidity levels remained above regulatory requirements.

Regarding NDB Bank, officials informed the Committee that the forensic audit conducted in relation to the bank, together with the report of the Sri Lanka Accounting and Auditing Standards Monitoring Board, had been referred to the Attorney General on October 15, 2026.

They said regulatory measures had also been imposed on the bank, including restrictions on dividend distributions, branch expansion and directors' remuneration.

The Committee also discussed institutions illegally accepting public deposits and financial scams involving the use of artificial intelligence to create fake images and voices of the Central Bank Governor, the Prime Minister and MPs.

Central Bank officials said measures were being taken in coordination with the Telecommunications Regulatory Commission, Sri Lanka Computer Emergency Readiness Team (CERT), Police and Meta to prevent such scams.
