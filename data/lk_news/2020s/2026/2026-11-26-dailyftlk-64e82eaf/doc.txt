India-backed housing project to provide 37 homes for Kalutara disaster-hit families

The foundation stone was laid for the construction of 37 houses for families displaced by a natural disaster in 2017 in Bulathsinhala, Kalutara on Tuesday (24), ending nearly nine years of uncertainty over permanent housing.

The houses will be built at Kiriwanakatiya and Kobowela estates under the Indian Housing Project, with assistance from the Government of India and funding from the Government of Sri Lanka. The initiative is being implemented under the guidance of the Ministry of Plantations and Community Infrastructure.

Each house will cost Rs. 3.2 million and be built on a 10-perch plot, with essential infrastructure including roads, drinking water and electricity. The total investment for the 37 houses is estimated at Rs. 118.4 million.

The foundation-laying ceremony was attended by Plantations and Community Infrastructure Minister Samantha Vidyarathna and the Indian High Commission Development Cooperation Division Head Roshni Thomson.

Addressing the event, Vidyarathna said the Government recognised the hardships endured by the affected families and their children since they were asked to leave high-risk areas in 2017.

He said the Government’s responsibility extended beyond providing housing, with plans to improve the community’s social and economic conditions, healthcare and education through the ‘Vibration’ programme, with the aim of establishing a fully developed and cohesive village.

Kalutara District MPs Dhanushka Ranganath and Nandana Pathmakumara, local government representatives, Ministry officials and residents also attended the event.
