PSTA places media in jeopardy: UN raises concern

Colombo, October 30 (Daily Mirror)- The Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) has raised concerns that the proposed anti-terrorism bill poses heightened risks to journalists, civil society actors, and for monitoring and documentation of militarized and other activities with broad human rights implications.

In its report the Office said that such threat originates from overbroad definitions of confidential information outlined in speech-related offences under Sections 2, 8–11, 15, 74 of the Prevention of State from Terrorism Bill (PSTA).

According to the report, the government has reproduced the same definition in the original draft presented in December 2025 despite UN concerns at that time.

The definition of “confidential information” included in the PSTA is similar to the one included in the December 2025 Bill. OHCHR’s concerns outlined in in its earlier legal comment continues to stand. In particular, section 74 (Interpretation) defines “confidential information” to include information relating to the “functions, movements, or whereabouts” of a specified person, as well as “any secret code, word, password or encryption detail relating to national security”. The definition further extends to information “likely to have an adverse impact on national security and defence,” rendering it excessively broad and imprecise and risking the criminalization of a wide range of legitimate activities, the report says.

The report says this could include monitoring and documentation of, for example, public-facing checkpoints, or other deployments and operations of security personnel, as well as routine reporting or social media posts relating to raids or arrests, thereby posing heightened risks to journalists, civil society actors, and for monitoring and documentation of militarized and other activities with broad human rights implications.
