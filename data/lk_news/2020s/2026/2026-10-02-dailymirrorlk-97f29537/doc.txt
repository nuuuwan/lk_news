A call to serve justice to children

It is not enough to remember children only on children’s day. There have been many incidents of neglect, abuse and harassment experienced by children. Recently, a child from Wattegama was severely beaten by an individual who claimed to be the lover of her mother and was abandoned on the road. One part of the child’s face was swollen as a result of the wounds. Many other incidents of violence and harassment towards children from outskirts go unreported.

Sri Lanka is celebrating Children’s Day on October 1 with various programmes highlighting the importance of children. But it is not enough to keep celebrating children on a particular day when many children from rural areas experience such horrible situations. Childhood trauma cannot be cured and it would only make matters worse. It would result in violence among youth. Some may even be compelled to take their own lives. At least 4000 cases pertaining to child abuse, sexual assault and violence are still dragging in courts.

It is the duty of this government to establish a special investigation unit to expedite these cases and ensure that justice is being served to victims and perpetrators are being brought before the law. In most cases of sexual assault towards children and youth,  it is a known person who becomes a perpetrator. Without justice being expedited we cannot expect a brighter future for children. It is up to the Minister and authorities to decide on priorities.
