Janashakthi Finance profit plunges 84% as auditors warn Rs.270mn hole may run deeper

Audited FY26 profit at Rs.64.7mn, against Rs.402.7mn in interim accounts

Further adjustments to profit, assets and EPS cannot yet be quantified

Janashakthi Finance PLC’s audited profit for the year ended March 2026 came in 84 percent below the figure it had earlier disclosed to the market, after its auditors qualified the accounts over an unreconciled Rs.270.5 million balance, which the company is yet to account for, a filing with the Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) showed.

The company further warned that the hit to its financials may deepen. Since the auditors were unable to determine whether further adjustments would be necessary, “any additional cumulative impact on profit or loss, net assets, total assets, total income, earnings per share or any other financial statement item cannot presently be reliably quantified”, according to the filing.

The finance company, formerly Orient Finance PLC, reported an audited profit of Rs.64.7 million, against the Rs.402.7 million shown in its interim accounts, a revision which cuts earnings per share to about Rs.0.31, from an implied Rs.1.91.

The qualification centres on a debit balance of Rs.270,542,779 carried under other assets, against which the company made a full provision, noting that “the reconciliation process was not completed prior to the approval of the financial statements”.

KPMG, the company’s external auditors, “were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the nature, accuracy, classification and recoverability” of the balance, nor could they verify whether the provision itself was adequate.

The provision alone is equivalent to 48 percent of the pre-tax profit reported in the interim accounts and 7 percent of equity. However, it accounts for only part of the Rs.338 million decline in profit.

Since the provision was treated as a disallowable expense and brought no tax relief, the income tax charge rose 62.4 percent to Rs.261.3 million, absorbing 80 percent of the pre-tax profit of Rs.326 million, compared with an effective rate of 28.6 percent implied in the interim numbers.

The fallout also extends into the current year’s accounts, as the audited equity of Rs.3.61 billion sits Rs.429.5 million below the unaudited March figure, on which the June quarter interim statements, released on August 11, were built. On the audited base, the net assets per share fell to around Rs.17.09, from Rs.19.12.

Meanwhile, the June quarter accounts point to a business still expanding at pace, with the profit rising 51 percent year-on-year to Rs.90.4 million, already exceeding the audited profit for the whole of the previous financial year, on the back of a 35 percent jump in the interest income to Rs.1.83 billion.

Gold loans generated Rs.70.7 million or 78 percent of the quarter’s profit, while the leasing segment’s contribution fell 42 percent to Rs.24.4 million. Bank overdrafts climbed 68 percent to Rs.1.42 billion over the same three months.

Janashakthi Limited controls close to 93 percent of the company through direct and custodial holdings. The company has been listed on the CSE’s Second Board since March 2021, as its 6.14 percent public float falls short of the minimum public holding requirement.

The company stated that it intends to complete remedial action “at the earliest possible time and within the timeframe stipulated under the Listing Rules, subject to independent verification by the external auditors”. (NF)
