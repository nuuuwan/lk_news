Colombo logistics centre targets US $ 400mn investment

Sri Lanka is planning a logistics centre within the Port of Colombo, targeting an investment of US $ 350 million to US $ 400 million, as the authorities seek to expand the port’s role in integrated supply chain services.

Sri Lanka Ports Authority Chairman Dr. Mahinda Parakrama Dissanayake said the proposed facility would be developed on a 14-acre site within the port, with a request for proposals expected to be issued within two months.

Addressing Sri Lanka Economic and Investment Summit 2026, organised by the Ceylon Chamber of Commerce, in Colombo, he said the project was expected to strengthen the port’s ability to provide shipping lines with end-to-end logistics solutions.

The proposed centre is intended to expand the port-related services beyond container handling by integrating the logistics activities and improving the supply chain support for shipping operators.

The investment forms part of a broader programme to expand the capacity and competitiveness of the Port of Colombo, which is expected to handle up to 14 million twenty-foot equivalent units (TEUs) by early 2028.

The projected demand is estimated at around 10 million TEUs, while additional projects are being planned to accommodate future growth.

A separate breakwater project is expected to add 3.2 million TEUs in capacity, with the associated port development programme estimated to require US $ 1.6 billion to US $ 1.7 billion in investment.

The studies for the proposed Colombo North Port are also around 90 percent complete, with the project expected to add a further 11 million TEUs in capacity. The financial feasibility studies are scheduled for completion by April 2027.

The proposed logistics centre is expected to support Sri Lanka’s ambition to strengthen its position as a regional maritime and logistics hub by attracting investment into higher-value services.

The project’s progress will depend on the proposals received, investment structure selected and ability to develop commercially sustainable operations.

Galle is also being considered for investments in floating docks, shipbuilding and ship-repair facilities, with potential project values estimated at US $ 300 million to US $1 billion.

The proposed developments could help Sri Lanka expand its maritime service offerings and create opportunities in ship maintenance, repair, industrial services and related sectors.

Meanwhile, discussions are underway with the Indian government on development plans for the Kankesanthurai Port. India has offered a grant of up to US $ 61.5 million for the port’s development.

Dr. Dissanayake also said marine services outside the Port of Colombo, including the operations around Galle and other coastal areas, were already servicing around 500 ships a month.

However, limited space within the Port of Colombo remains a constraint on expanding these activities, highlighting the potential role of regional ports in accommodating additional maritime services.

Developing specialised facilities outside Colombo could help distribute port-related investment across the country while creating new opportunities for the local businesses and employment.

The realisation of these projects will depend on detailed planning, feasibility assessments, investment commitments and the ability to attract private-sector participation. (CPS)
