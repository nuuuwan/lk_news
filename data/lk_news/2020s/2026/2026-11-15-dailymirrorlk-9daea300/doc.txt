New ten-storey Professorial Ward Complex opens at Karapitiya

Colombo, Nov. 15 (Daily Mirror) - The newly constructed ten-storey Professorial Ward Complex (PWC) of the Faculty of Medicine, University of Ruhuna, in Karapitiya was officially handed over to the public today, at a ceremony held under the patronage of Minister of Health and Mass Media Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa.

Addressing the gathering, Minister Jayatissa highlighted the unprecedented financial allocations made to the education and healthcare sectors in the current national budget. He stated that Rs. 740 billion has been allocated for education, while Rs. 710 billion has been set aside for healthcare.

The Minister also recalled that construction of the Professorial Unit at Galle National Hospital began in 2019 with an initial estimated cost of Rs. 1.3 billion. However, the project was eventually completed at a total cost of Rs. 2.808 billion.

He further announced that Rs. 8.5 billion will be invested in several other development projects at Galle National Hospital. In addition, new professorial units are planned for the medical faculties in Sabaragamuwa, Badulla and Kalutara.

Highlighting the government's commitment to modernizing healthcare facilities, the Minister revealed that Rs. 21.5 billion has been allocated for purchasing advanced medical equipment, including MRI and Linear Accelerator (LINAC) machines. He also stated that the government aims to complete all hospital construction projects that were suspended midway by 2028.

The newly opened Professorial Ward Complex is equipped with modern healthcare facilities and has a total capacity of 300 beds. These beds are allocated for medical, surgical, psychiatric and specialized gynaecological wards. The complex also features an eight-bed Intensive Care Unit (ICU), three fully equipped operating theatres, eight outpatient clinic rooms and a dedicated clinical examination hall.

The project was undertaken through a Memorandum of Understanding between the Ministries of Higher Education and Health. Funding for construction was provided by the Ministry of Higher Education, while the Ministry of Health contributed the land. The construction work was carried out by the Central Engineering Consultancy Bureau of Sri Lanka (CECB).

In addition to government funding through annual budget allocations, the project received valuable support from the Ruhuna University Medical Faculty Alumni Association (RUMSAA) and several philanthropists.

With the sponsorship of the Mahabodhi TempleTemple in Guangzhou, China, and coordination facilitated by the Consular Office, essential hospital beds were donated for the wards, operating theatres and ICU. Meanwhile, groups of medical faculty alumni contributed furniture for the entrance lobby, outpatient clinics, examination hall and medical wards, with the total value of these donations exceeding Rs. 25 million.

Furthermore, an agreement has been reached through Lions Clubs International to provide a donation of USD 150,000 to fully equip one of the operating theatres.

During his address, Minister Jayatissa also provided an update on developments in healthcare sector recruitment. He stated that the cadre review of the health sector, which had not been carried out since 2017, has now been completed. Approval is expected to be obtained to recruit approximately 7,000 doctors over the next three years.

The Minister further explained that appointments had initially been planned for 1,472 doctors who had completed their internship training. However, once the ongoing legal proceedings are concluded, the government intends to expedite appointments for nearly 2,000 individuals, including these doctors.

Additionally, arrangements have been made to recruit 3,000 nursing officers and 500 graduates as part of efforts to strengthen the country's healthcare workforce.
