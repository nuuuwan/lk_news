India amends visa rules for Pakistan, Sri Lanka

India has introduced new passport and visa rules for foreigners. The changes cover select Pakistani citizens, minority refugees from Sri Lankan Tamils, Tibetans, Afghanistan, Bangladesh and Pakistan.

Separate travel relaxations have been issued for Nepalese and Bhutanese citizens. The new amendments provide relief to those who cannot renew their travel documents or were allowed to enter India under certain circumstances.

The renewed provisions are part of the Immigration and Foreigners (Exemption) (Amendment) Order, 2026, published by the Union Home Ministry on November 5.

According to a PTI report, the exemptions apply to specific conditions, and several categories of people will still need to obtain a relevant visa if they want to continue their stay in India.

Registered Sri Lankan Tamil citizens who sought refuge in India on or before January 9, 2015, have also been granted relief from the standard passport and visa requirements under the amended order.

These include Tibetans who entered India with permits issued by the Indian embassy in Kathmandu between 1959 and May 30, 2003.

The relief also covers those who arrived after that date using special entry permits through designated immigration checkpoints along the India-Nepal border.
