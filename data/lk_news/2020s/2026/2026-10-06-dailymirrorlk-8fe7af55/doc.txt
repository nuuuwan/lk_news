Nearly 3,000 face legal action over seat belt violations on expressways

Colombo, Oct. 6 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka Police have intensified enforcement of mandatory seat belt regulations on expressways, with nearly 3,000 people already facing legal action for failing to comply with the rules.

The regulations require all passengers travelling in vehicles on expressways, including those seated in the rear, to wear seat belts.

Deputy Inspector General of Police (DIG) in charge of Traffic Control and Road Safety W. P. J. Senadheera said the enforcement was aimed primarily at protecting lives rather than penalising motorists.

He warned that at high speeds, a sudden braking incident or collision can result in serious or fatal injuries to passengers who are not properly restrained. Vehicles travelling on expressways can reach speeds of around 100 km/h or more, making seat belts particularly important in preventing occupants from being thrown from their seats or suffering severe impact injuries.

Referring to a recent multi-vehicle collision involving several cars and a bus, DIG Senadheera said passengers escaped serious injuries because they had been wearing seat belts.

Police have also clarified that the number of passengers permitted in a vehicle on an expressway is limited by the number of functioning seat belts available.

For example, if a rear seat is fitted with only two seat belts, only two passengers are permitted to occupy those seats. An additional passenger travelling without a seat belt could result in legal action.

However, Gazette notifications provide certain exemptions from the mandatory seat belt requirement. These include children below 12 years of age, passengers shorter than 135 centimetres (approximately 4 feet 5 inches), and individuals who have been medically advised not to wear seat belts.

DIG Senadheera urged motorists and passengers covered by the exemptions to use appropriate alternative restraints wherever possible.

He said that the regulations were introduced as a road safety measure aimed at preventing serious injuries and saving lives, particularly on high-speed expressways.
