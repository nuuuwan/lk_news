No more VAT on the entire cinema ticket price: NFC

Colombo, Nov. 18 (Daily Mirror) - The 2027 National Budget proposals have addressed a long-standing Value Added Tax (VAT) issue affecting Sri Lanka's cinema industry, paving the way for the release of ticket revenue shares previously held up by tax-related complications, the National Film Corporation (NFC) said.

According to the NFC, complications had arisen following the introduction of VAT on the cinema sector in 2024, particularly concerning VAT registration thresholds, tax collection and remittances to the Inland Revenue Department.

Under the revised arrangements proposed in the 2027 Budget, VAT will no longer be calculated on the total gross ticket price paid by audiences. Instead, each stakeholder, including film producers, cinema halls, film distribution boards and the NFC, will be liable for VAT based on their respective share of ticket revenue.

National Film Corporation Chairman Sudath Mahadivulwewa said that the new arrangement will also allow each stakeholder to register separately for VAT according to the income received from their allocated percentage of ticket sales and independently collect and remit the relevant tax to the Inland Revenue Department.

Meanwhile, the portion of ticket revenue allocated to local government authorities as entertainment tax will remain exempt from VAT.

Under the existing ticket revenue-sharing structure, excluding entertainment tax, film producers are entitled to 40% of ticket revenue at air-conditioned cinemas and 45% at non-air-conditioned cinemas. Cinema halls receive 50% and 45%, respectively, while distribution boards receive 10% at both types of cinemas.

The corporation levy shared by the NFC and cinema halls is Rs. 6.50 per ticket at air-conditioned cinemas and Rs. 6 at non-air-conditioned cinemas.
