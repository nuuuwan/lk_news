Homeopathic medical sector raises concerns over regulatory issues

Colombo, Aug. 17 (Daily Mirror) - Concerns have been raised within Sri Lanka’s homeopathic medical sector over alleged irregularities in registrations, professional qualifications and the regulation of homeopathic medicines.

The concerns were formally raised in a letter addressed to Health Minister Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa by Dr. S. Ramesh, Vice President of the Government Registered Homeopathic Doctors Association.

In the letter, Dr. Ramesh called for the cancellation of 49 registrations which he alleged had been issued by an improperly appointed interim committee without the required examinations.

He also called for the verification of certain professional qualifications and raised concerns over alleged regulatory gaps in the importation of homeopathic medicines.

The letter further proposed several measures, including the appointment of a senior homeopathic doctor to oversee homeopathic drug imports at the National Medicines Regulatory Authority (NMRA), and the designation of the Government Homeopathic Hospital as a teaching hospital.

It also called for compulsory clinical training for practitioners, a review of provisional registrations and the categorisation of registrations based on age and professional experience.

Dr. Ramesh also urged the Ministry to ensure that funds collected by the relevant college are used directly for training and professional development.

The association has requested an urgent meeting with the Health Ministry to discuss the concerns raised in the letter and address what it described as ongoing regulatory and anti-corruption issues within the sector.

Responding to the concerns, an official from the Health Ministry told Daily Mirror that the attention has been drawn to the matter to iron out the concerns.
