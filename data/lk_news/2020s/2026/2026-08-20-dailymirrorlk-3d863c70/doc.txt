Judicial Retirement Age: Let the Judges’ Wishes Guide the Way

Opposition and divergent views have emerged among stakeholders over the Government’s proposal to amend the Constitution and increase the retirement age of judges. While  President A K Dissanayake appears determined to proceed, a practical and less contentious solution deserves consideration.

First and foremost, why not fill the existing vacancies in the Supreme Court and Court of Appeal without delay? This would immediately strengthen the judiciary and address concerns arising from the present shortage of judges.

Secondly, the Government should ascertain whether the serving judges themselves wish to continue in office beyond the existing retirement age. If a significant number are willing to serve longer, the case for increasing the retirement age could be objectively assessed. If they are not, there would be little justification for pursuing such an amendment.

Rather than prolonging the controversy through repeated meetings, protests and counter-protests, the Government should adopt a simple, consultative and pragmatic approach.

Let the needs of the judiciary—and the wishes of its serving judges—determine the way forward, rather than allowing the issue to become a prolonged constitutional confrontation.
