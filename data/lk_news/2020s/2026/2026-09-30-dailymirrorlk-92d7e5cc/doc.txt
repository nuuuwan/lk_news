Control failures emerge from NDB’s  Rs. 13.64bn fraud probe

Final assessment by Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu India LLP puts the fraud at Rs. 13.6397bn

NDB says Deloitte had made recommendations relating to “control failures, technology lapses and governance improvements” relevant to the incident

The Bank discloses it has enlisted an expert foreign agency specialising in tracing stolen funds and digital assets

National Development Bank PLC (NDB) yesterday disclosed it has received the final Deloitte forensic report into its multi-billion-rupee fraud, confirming losses of Rs. 13.64 billion and identifying control failures, technology lapses and governance weaknesses linked to the incident.

The final assessment by Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu India LLP puts the fraud at Rs. 13.6397 billion, Rs. 60 million higher than the Rs. 13.5797 billion estimated in its interim report in June. NDB had initially estimated the fraud at Rs. 13.2 billion in April. The additional Rs. 60 million will be recognised in accordance with applicable accounting standards.

The final report also placed the governance and control environment surrounding the fraud into sharper focus. NDB stated that Deloitte had made recommendations relating to “control failures, technology lapses and governance improvements” relevant to the incident.

In a disclosure to the Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE), the bank said the recommendations were being acted upon and that the Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) would continue to be updated on the measures taken.

Meanwhile, in a significant escalation of efforts to recover the money, NDB shared it has enlisted an expert foreign agency specialising in tracing stolen funds and digital assets.

“The Bank is also pursuing all avenues available to it to recover the misappropriated funds including availing the services of an expert foreign agency specializing in tracing stolen money and digital assets,” NDB said.

The bank said the findings of the final forensic report generally accorded with those identified internally. The matters have already been reported to law enforcement authorities, with action against the alleged perpetrators pending before criminal courts.

NDB has also initiated and is pursuing internal action against individuals identified as responsible for collusion, lack of supervision and control lapses that facilitated the fraudulent activities.

Deloitte’s breakdown highlighted that the bulk of the fraud was concentrated in 2025. Of the final Rs. 13.64 billion, Rs. 9.617 billion, or about 70.5 percent, relatesld to the financial year ended 31 December 2025.

A further Rs. 1.463 billion related to the period before 1 January 2025, while Rs. 2.560 billion was attributed to the quarter ended 31 March 2026.

Deloitte was commissioned by NDB in consultation with and in line with recommendations from the CBSL’s Director of Bank Supervision following detection of the fraud.

Under the agreed terms of reference, Deloitte was required to submit its report directly to the CBSL, with NDB kept on copy, a structure intended to ensure the independence of the forensic process.

NDB stressed that no customer had suffered losses as a result of the fraud, with customer balances remaining intact. It also reiterated the bank remained well capitalised with sound levels of liquidity.

While noting the final forensic report would not mark the end of the process, NDB said it viewed the findings as a step towards rebuilding credibility and strengthening its overall governance and control framework.

The bank said the measures arising from the investigation are expected to strengthen its ability to deal with existing and emerging threats facing financial institutions, including those stemming from technological developments.
