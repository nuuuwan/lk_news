Consumers deserve truth

Aloe vera is a medicinal plant long researched as a solution for gastritis and digestion. So, when brands market “Aloe Vera Yoghurt” as a health drink, the public naturally assumes it is good for adults, especially diabetics and gastric patients.

I checked the label of Aloe Vera Yoghurt, produced by one of the country’s oldest conglomerates. It contains *10.8g of sugar per 100ml*. Another popular brand starting with the letter ‘S’ has similar sugar levels. Taste it and you will know why: it is very, very sweet. Clearly designed to attract children, not to heal adults.

Here is the bigger deception:  The most important ingredient — how much actual Aloe Vera is in 100ml — is NOT mentioned anywhere on the label and the sugar content is printed in unreadable, tiny letters, deliberately hiding the truth from consumers.

If this is truly a “health drink”, then where is the “Diabetic Aloe Vera Drink” version with less sugar for adults who need it? Why are the makers of these products misleading the public by hiding behind the “aloe vera” name while selling sugary yoghurt?

1. Make it mandatory to declare Aloe Vera content per 100ml.

3. Stop brands from using “health” claims to deceive the gullible public with acidity health issues.
