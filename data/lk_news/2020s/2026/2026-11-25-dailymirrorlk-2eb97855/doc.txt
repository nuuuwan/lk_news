Hambantota Port provides refuge to disabled cargo vessel

Colombo, Nov. 25 (Daily Mirror) - Hambantota International Port (HIP) has provided refuge to a disabled cargo vessel after flooding in its engine room left it unable to operate, with the ship now safely berthed at the port for repairs.

The Cyprus-flagged general cargo vessel MV AAL Gibraltar was towed to Sri Lanka by salvage tug SPM Arjun after being declared a dead ship. Its departure date remains unconfirmed.

The 159.99-metre vessel, which is reportedly carrying approximately 23,000 tonnes of cargo, is currently at a lay-up berth, where its local agent, Ceyline Maritime Services (Pvt) Ltd, will coordinate the necessary repairs.

Following a no-objection from the Director General of Merchant Shipping and recommendations from the Harbour Master and the Marine Environment Protection Authority (MEPA), HIP deployed four tugs – Waruna, Vasabha, Ravana and Pandukabhaya – to assist SPM Arjun in manoeuvring the disabled vessel from its anchorage to the berth.

Before the vessel was granted clearance to enter the port, HIP also coordinated three offshore assistance operations while it remained at sea.

On September 23, tug Ravana transported surveyors, technicians and provisions to the vessel, approximately 12 nautical miles offshore.

On October 13, tugs Pandukabhaya and Waruna provided assistance for around 14 hours while the towing tug refuelled. Later, on October 23, tug Waruna delivered spare parts to the vessel approximately 12 nautical miles from the port.

The berthing operation has provided the vessel with shelter and access to shore-based support following several weeks offshore.

HIP said its marine services include afloat repairs, underwater services, surveys, provisions, bunker supplies and lay-up facilities, subject to availability.

The port also maintains firefighting and rescue tugs on round-the-clock standby, supported by a dedicated Emergency Response Unit and equipment to handle oil and chemical spills.

HIP has previously participated in several major maritime emergency operations.

During the MT New Diamond tanker fire off Sri Lanka in September 2020, two tugs from Hambantota joined the wider firefighting operation, while the port received 20 crew members from the vessel.

In February 2022, HIP's Emergency Response Unit, together with its Quality, Health, Safety and Environment Department and Port Control, responded to a fire in the emergency generator room of the drillship West Polaris, which was berthed at the port. The fire was brought under control within 33 minutes of the teams' arrival, preventing serious damage to the vessel and surrounding area.

HIP said its location, approximately 10 nautical miles from the main East-West shipping route, further strengthens its ability to assist vessels offshore and respond to maritime emergencies.

Meanwhile, Ceyline Maritime Services provides ship agency and engineering support at ports across Sri Lanka, including vessel repairs, lay-up assistance, underwater inspections and the clearance and delivery of ship spare parts.
