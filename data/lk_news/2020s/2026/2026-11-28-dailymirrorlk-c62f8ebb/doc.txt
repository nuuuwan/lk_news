An Idle Radiation Machine Needs a Patient-Facing Answer

Daily Mirror’s November 18 report about costly radiotherapy equipment remaining unused at Badulla and Hambantota raises a question more urgent than the price of the machines: what happens to a patient whose treatment must proceed while the equipment waits?

An installation certificate is not a completed health service. Hospital administrators and health officials should publish a clear, dated explanation of the remaining barriers, the person responsible for each step and the next verified date for a patient-ready service. Until then, patients need a referral route that is explained in ordinary language, including where to seek help when travel or private treatment costs make the alternative impossible.

Public scrutiny should seek solutions, not blame without evidence. The report presents concerns that deserve examination; officials should be able to respond with verifiable timelines and patient-protection measures.

When Jesus stopped for Bartimaeus in Mark 10, he attended to a person rather than treating a crowd as the entire story. Patients awaiting treatment deserve that same seriousness. The measure of progress is not how much technology has arrived. It is whether a patient can obtain the care it was purchased to provide.
