Sajith at Polduwa: Undeniable success, but could be more inclusive

An opinion poll in the Sunday Times (27 Sep.) on the 2nd anniversary of AKD-NPP rule shows 58% dissatisfaction and only 42% satisfaction. This means that President Dissanayake has retained the vote—below an absolute majority—that brought him into office, but that the JVP-NPP’s high popularity at the Parliamentary election has been reduced to the same level as his, while he hasn’t increased his popularity either.

The 58% negative vote should gladden the Opposition, but it really can’t because the Opposition contains a bipolar split between Sajith/SJB and Namal/SLPP. Namal may have run the gruelling legal gauntlet by Presidential election 2029.

There are only two scenarios/templates in the playbook which can break the Opposition stalemate in 2029:

I. Sajith emulates his father’s 1988 breakthrough victory beating the formidable Sirimavo Bandaranaike by swinging the poor among SLFP voters and JVP supporters through the inspiring vision of Janasaviya, the radical poverty-reduction program.

II. Namal combines and emulates CBK’s 1994 repositioning of her party and 1999 defeat of Ranil, and his father’s patriotic-populist 2005 victory over Ranil.

The UNP fails to recognise that its decades-long inability to elect a President to lead the country was because it de-Premadasaised its economic ideology post-1993, and failed to transition over a decade ago to Sajith as leader.

There is no bigger indictment of the UNP and the pro-Ranil caucus in the SJB than the electorate’s verdict on ‘unmandated’ President Wickremesinghe who was consigned to third place in 2024 having ‘saved the country’.

Sajith Premadasa probably hasn’t forgotten that Ranil dragged his feet and defeated him in 2019, ran as spoiler and defeated him in 2024, and only succeeded in getting Anura Dissanayake and the JVP-NPP elected as our rulers.

Namal is rightly allergic – for ideological ‘brand’ reasons – to any alliance with Ranil’s UNP (hence the latter’s Anuradhapura absence).

The UNP has no successor to Ranil. The party can avoid electoral-political extinction only if it recognises Sajith as leader/candidate  and enters an equation with the SJB as main force. That’s the last move with which Ranil can redeem himself and be assured of respectful recognition and status.

The rest of the Joint Opposition including the SLFP should recognise Namal as leader/candidate, abandoning delusions of a non-Sajith/non-Namal candidate with a mass base, national resonance and double-digit viability.

Sajith Premadasa and the SJB scored a significant tactical and symbolic victory with their impressive ‘swarm’ demonstration, over 5000-strong, at Polduwa on 24 September, proving the SJB’s dominant status within the Opposition.

That victory was more limited than it might have been, and had an avoidable downside, because of an arrogant, disruptive streak on the part of some overtly right-wing SJB organisers/MPs—notorious loose cannon—exhibiting sectarianism, isolationism and unilateralism.

To succeed and prevail, Sajith and the SJB should treat five strategic political realities as fundamentals:

1. A ‘One-Party State’ project cannot be defeated by a mirroring ‘One-Party Opposition’ project and posture. It can only be defeated by a broad, politically pluralist democratic project with social democracy at the core.

2. The SJB which since its inception failed twice (2020, 2024) to break out of its parent/predecessor UNP’s 20% electoral bunker, cannot afford to risk going it alone, not least in a three-way race. It must win over the middle-ground, the swing voters and new voters, to constitute a majority.

3. The interests of the whole must, in general, supersede the interests of the part, and the leading part can succeed only if it incorporates and represents the interests of the whole.

4. Sajith Premadasa is SJB leader and Opposition Leader, and as such must lead, include and represent the interests of the whole Opposition—not just one (albeit biggest) component, his own party, though axiomatically that would be primary.

5. The SJB is the main and leading Opposition party and must be recognised as such, but it does not hold a monopoly of the Oppositional space. SJB primacy must not translate into exclusivity or self-quarantine. To lead, the SJB must draw in others who follow; form alliances, blocs, partnerships; act in unison or coordination with diverse parties on common issues against a common authoritarian adversary.

The conviction by the court of those most closely involved in the Easter Sunday massacre is welcome but is only half the story and does not—cannot—complete the search for justice.

The verdict deals with ‘sins of commission’—those who assisted or played some role in the Easter massacre by their active involvement. But there were also ‘sins of omission’. Evil was committed not only by those who blew up the innocent and those who assisted them along the way, but also by those who had been informed that the evil deed would happen and chose to do nothing about it, or dragged their feet with the result that a preventable atrocity was not prevented.

In a superb piece of investigative reportage, the Jaffna Monitor reconstructs the story of the 17-day delay.

‘…India’s warning to the State Intelligence Service arrived on April 4, 2019, followed by further information on April 18 and 19; on the evening of the 20th and again at first light on the 21st, the head of the SIS passed his own warnings to the inspector general of police and the defence secretary…

… On April 4, the warning arrived in Colombo through official channels, as a WhatsApp message to Nilantha Jayawardena, the director of the State Intelligence Service. Zahran Hashim and his associates were preparing a suicide attack on important churches…Six men were named, Zahran and his brother Rilwan among them. The message was confirmed in writing at nine the following morning, and a second Indian source sent the same substance three hours later.

…On April 7 a top-secret letter reached Sisira Mendis, the Chief of National Intelligence. It set out the same warning and added the methods the group might use: a suicide attack, an armed attack, a knife attack, an attack with a truck.

On April 8 the defence secretary was told, and advised that the police be informed.

On April 9 the security chiefs met. That same day Jayawardena sent the inspector general of police a report identifying four of the six men India had named.

…Then, on April 20, the Indian counterpart messaged Jayawardena again. He wanted feedback on what he had sent at the start of the month. He added that the attack was expected “at any time on or before 21.04.2019,” and that a dry run had already been carried out at Kattankudy.

That was the same evening a former Sea Tiger in Puthukudiyiruppu took a call from an Indian police officer and relayed the warning to his handler in Vavuniya.

The last message came at 8.27 the next morning. It said the attackers would probably move within hours. The first bomb went off eighteen minutes later…’

As respected author of several books on the LTTE war, MR Narayan Swamy has underscored:

‘…It is by now public knowledge that an Indian agency passed on highly credible intelligence which, had it been acted upon, could not only have failed the murder conspiracy but also led to the arrests of the bombers. Sadly, the inputs received were not followed up. No amount of planning by the terrorists and no amount of overt or covert help they allegedly received from anyone within Sri Lanka or outside could have succeeded in killing people en masse if the warnings had been heeded…’
