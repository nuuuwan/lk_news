Station Masters flag up-country rail safety without NBRO approval

Colombo, Oct. 5 (Daily Mirror) - The Sri Lanka Railway Station Masters’ Association (SLRSMU) has raised serious safety concerns over the resumption of train services on the Up-country railway line, alleging that operations resumed without approval from the National Building Research Organisation (NBRO).

SLRSMU General Secretary Kasun Chamara Jayasekara said the Railway Department should conduct a fresh assessment of the line before continuing services, particularly as warnings had reportedly been issued about possible earthslips and rockfalls at two other locations.

“We request the department to reinvestigate and look into the matters properly prior to starting train services, without endangering the lives of passengers,” Jayasekara said.

His remarks followed the derailment of the first office train from Peradeniya to Colombo Fort after services on the line resumed.

The train, which left Peradeniya at around 4.35 a.m. today (5), derailed between Ihalakotte, Kadigamuwa and Gangoda after earth and rocks had fallen onto the track.

The engine and one carriage derailed after the driver was reportedly unable to clearly see the obstruction on the track.

Train services between Colombo Fort, Peradeniya and Gampola resumed on October 2, following a nearly 10-month closure caused by extensive damage to the railway line during Cyclone Ditwah.

Following today's derailment, affected passengers travelled through alternative routes to the Kadigamuwa bus route and then by bus to Rambukkana.

The Railway Department later arranged an additional train from Rambukkana to Colombo to transport the stranded passengers.
