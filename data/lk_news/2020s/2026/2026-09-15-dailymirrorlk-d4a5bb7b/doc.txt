EY urges IRD to expedite cases ahead of Dec. 2

EY has called on the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) to expedite the pending tax audits, reviews and penalty-related decisions to ensure the taxpayers are not prevented by the administrative delays from accessing a time-bound interest waiver that expires on December 2.

The professional services firm said faster processing would be critical as the taxpayers seeking relief must settle both the principal tax liability and any applicable penalties within the stipulated six-month period to qualify for the waiver.

Under Section 42 of Inland Revenue (Amendment) Act No. 11 of 2026, the commissioner-general is required to write off the outstanding interest on the underpayments and late payments relating to the Income Tax, Surcharge Tax and Debt Repayment Levy, subject to the prescribed conditions.

The amendment was certified on June 3, giving the taxpayers until December 2 to make all qualifying payments.

EY cautioned that practical difficulties could arise where the taxpayers have sought reductions in penalties and are still awaiting decisions from the IRD. Any penalty ultimately determined must also be paid before the deadline for the taxpayer to qualify for the interest waiver.

It said the IRD should therefore deal expeditiously with the penalty reduction requests and other outstanding matters that could affect the taxpayers’ ability to complete the required payments within the relief period.

“The purpose of the measure is to facilitate the collection of the outstanding revenue,” EY said, adding that this would be best achieved through an efficient administrative process that allows the taxpayers to regularise their affairs before the concession expires.

The relief could be particularly significant, given that the interest on the underpaid or late taxes is generally charged at 1.5 percent per month or part thereof from the original due date until payment. For the businesses caught in long-running disputes, the accumulated interest could consequently represent a substantial portion of the overall liability.

The concession applies to the assessments relating to the years of assessment up to and including 2024/25, that are subject to pending tax audits, requests for administrative review or appeals.

EY noted that the IRD has already instructed the tax officials to expedite the pending audits to allow the taxpayers to qualify for the relief. It said similar urgency was needed for pending administrative reviews, penalty remission applications, ongoing appeals and confirmation of relief granted.

It suggested that a dedicated administrative framework, standard application procedures, published turnaround times and expedited decision-making could improve compliance and revenue collection.

“The objective should be to facilitate settlement rather than create procedural roadblocks that prevent the taxpayers from accessing the concession Parliament intended to provide,” EY said.

Significantly, the taxpayers with assessments under dispute may also be able to secure the interest waiver without abandoning their appeal rights.

According to EY, the IRD guidance indicates that the taxpayers may pay the principal tax and applicable penalty within the amnesty period to qualify for the interest waiver while continuing to challenge the assessment through the available review and appeal mechanisms.

This could allow the taxpayers to halt further accumulation of interest while an appeal proceeds. If the taxpayer ultimately succeeds, the IRD guidelines provide a mechanism to seek a refund of the tax paid, although EY noted that the legislation itself does not specifically address this situation.
