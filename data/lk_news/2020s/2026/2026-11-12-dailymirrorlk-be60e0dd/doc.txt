Budget 2027: Key proposals on growth, welfare and development

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake presented the 2027 Budget in Parliament yesterday, outlining a wide range of proposals aimed at strengthening the economy, improving public services, creating employment opportunities and raising living standards.

The Budget places emphasis on increasing local production, attracting domestic and foreign investment, expanding exports, developing infrastructure, improving education and healthcare, and strengthening social protection programmes.

It also includes major investments in roads, railways, electricity, agriculture, irrigation, housing and digital technology, while providing financial assistance to low-income families and vulnerable groups.

The Government has allocated Rs. 2 trillion for capital expenditure in 2027, representing public investment of 5.12 per cent of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP), including provisions for reconstruction following Cyclone Ditwah.

The Budget aims to maintain economic stability while moving towards a more productive and export-oriented economy. The Government has set a medium-term target of achieving sustainable economic growth of 7 per cent or higher.

The Government expects to maintain public revenue at 15.8 per cent of GDP in 2027, while total expenditure is projected to reach 20.3 per cent of GDP, including the supplementary provisions for disaster reconstruction.

The budget deficit is projected at 4.5 per cent of GDP, while the primary surplus is expected to remain at 2.3 per cent.

The Government also expects to maintain public investment above 4 per cent of GDP from 2027 onwards.

The President said the Government would continue to strengthen public finances, manage debt and improve the efficiency of public institutions.

The current International Monetary Fund (IMF) programme is expected to conclude in March 2027. A committee has also been appointed to study the possibility of entering into another programme after the completion of the existing arrangement.

A considerable allocation has been made for improving school education, higher education and vocational training.

The monthly allowance paid to trainee teachers at Colleges of Education will be increased from Rs. 10,500 to Rs. 20,000 with effect from April 2027.

The Government has allocated Rs. 3,000 million for the digital transformation of provincial schools. The five-year programme aims to provide computers, smart boards, internet facilities and other digital learning equipment to schools.

The Government expects to provide reliable internet connections to the remaining 750 secondary schools that have not yet received them.

A further Rs. 35,000 million has been allocated for digitalisation, teacher training and infrastructure development, including improvements to teachers' colleges, National Colleges of Education, school buildings and laboratories.

The academic allowance paid to university lecturers will be increased by Rs. 25,000, with 50 per cent of the increase scheduled to be paid from July 2027 and the remaining amount from July 2028.

Payments to visiting lecturers will also be increased threefold, from Rs. 1,500 to Rs. 4,500 per hour.

The daily allowance for student monks at Pirivenas will increase from Rs. 15 to Rs. 50, while the allowance for lay students will rise from Rs. 5 to Rs. 25.

The annual allowance for Dhamma School teachers will increase from Rs. 7,500 to Rs. 12,500.

The Government has also allocated Rs. 11 billion for the development of vocational education and training facilities, including the modernisation of 25 selected Technical Colleges.

A total capital allocation of Rs. 131 billion has been made to maintain and improve public healthcare services.

The Government plans to purchase medical equipment, including MRI scanners, CT scanners and other specialised equipment, to improve diagnostic and treatment facilities in hospitals.

An additional Rs. 5,000 million has been allocated for medical equipment, while Rs. 3,000 million has been allocated for purchasing ambulances.

The Government also plans to establish 1,000 Arogya Wellness Centres between 2027 and 2029, with an investment of Rs. 17,440 million. An allocation of Rs. 3,600 million has been made for 2027.

These centres are expected to provide healthcare services closer to communities, particularly in areas where access to hospitals is limited.

The Thambuttegama Base Hospital will receive Rs. 300 million to develop a modern Accident and Emergency Treatment Unit.

The Government also plans to introduce robotic technology for surgical procedures.

Allowances paid to medical officers will increase by Rs. 25,000, from Rs. 70,000 to Rs. 95,000. Special duty allowances for nursing officers and paramedical staff will also be increased from Rs. 6,000 to Rs. 12,000.

The increases will be implemented in two stages, with 50 per cent being paid from July 2027 and the balance in 2028.

The Government has allocated Rs. 230 billion for social protection programmes, including the continuation of Aswesuma benefits for poor and extremely poor families.

The monthly allowance for kidney patients and persons with disabilities will be increased from Rs. 10,000 to Rs. 12,500.

A further Rs. 25,000 million has been allocated for an island-wide rural development programme under the Praja Shakthi National Movement, with priority given to poverty reduction.

Another Rs. 4,500 million has been allocated to empower selected families through programmes aimed at helping them improve their economic conditions.

The Budget also includes Rs. 182 million for the development and protection of vulnerable children through programmes focusing on health, education and overall well-being.

The Government has proposed a monthly interim allowance of Rs. 2,500 for public sector pensioners who retired between 2020 and 2024.

The payment will begin on July 1, 2027, and continue until the planned pension revision under the new 2025 basic salary structure is implemented.

The final phase of the pension revision programme for public servants who retired before 2020 will also be implemented in 2027.

Several allowances paid to public sector employees will be revised, including allowances for health workers, field officers, Grama Niladharis, members of the armed forces, police personnel and judicial staff.

The monthly transport allowance for eligible public officers who use their private vehicles for official duties will increase from Rs. 50,000 to Rs. 100,000.

The Government has allocated Rs. 251 billion for highway, expressway and railway development through the Budget. Including additional proposals and reconstruction funding, total spending on these sectors is expected to reach Rs. 487 billion.

An allocation of Rs. 30,000 million has been made for the Kurunegala-Galewela section of the Central Expressway, while Rs. 27,000 million has been allocated for the first phase of the Ruwanpura Expressway from Kahathuduwa to Ingiriya.

Another Rs. 53,450 million has been allocated to complete the Kadawatha-Mirigama section of the Central Expressway by September 2028.

The Government has also allocated Rs. 4,500 million to complete the Pothuhera-Rambukkana section by May 2027.

A further Rs. 23,050 million has been allocated for the Rambukkana-Galagedara section.

An allocation of Rs. 22,000 million has been made to widen and develop selected roads to reduce traffic congestion and improve road safety.

The Government also plans to invest Rs. 80 billion in railway electrification.

The first phase will cover the Kelani Valley railway line from Maradana to Homagama, the Main Line from Maradana to Ragama and the Coastal Line from Maradana to Panadura.

The Government also plans to purchase five new trains and allocate Rs. 2,000 million to renovate railway stations.

An allocation of Rs. 95 billion has been made to modernise railway and passenger bus services, improve connections between transport networks and promote sustainable public transport.

The Metro Bus service, currently operating around Colombo, is expected to be extended to Kandy, Galle and Jaffna.

The Government plans to introduce 100 electric buses received as a donation from China. An allocation of Rs. 4,500 million has been made for the construction of depots, charging facilities and training.

Another Rs. 5,000 million has been allocated to purchase 250 small buses for rural and upcountry areas.

The Government also plans to introduce improved digital systems for vehicle registration, ownership transfers and road safety.

The Budget places emphasis on increasing agricultural production, improving rural livelihoods and strengthening food security.

A total of Rs. 77 billion has been allocated for irrigation development, including the rehabilitation of damaged irrigation systems and the implementation of new projects.

The Government has allocated Rs. 21 billion for the rehabilitation of tank systems, minor irrigation infrastructure and agricultural roads damaged by Cyclone Ditwah.

Another Rs. 19,500 million has been allocated for the Mahaweli Water Security and Investment Programme, including the North Central Province Greater Canal Project.

A further Rs. 15 billion has been allocated for the maintenance and development of minor irrigation systems.

The Government has also allocated Rs. 5,000 million for the development of the Wayamba Canal Project and other irrigation improvements.

In the fisheries sector, Rs. 5,000 million has been allocated for the construction of fisheries anchorages and the upgrading of the Pesalai Fisheries Harbour.

Another Rs. 1,000 million has been allocated to improve the Peliyagoda Fish Market, while Rs. 500 million has been allocated for the renovation of the North Sea Fishing Net Factory.

The Government has also proposed financial assistance for goat farming, aquaculture and fisheries-related businesses.

A sum of Rs. 5,000 million has been allocated for fertiliser for tea cultivation and Rs. 1,000 million for fertiliser for rubber cultivation.

The Government plans to strengthen local industries and attract more investment to create employment and increase exports.

An allocation of Rs. 4,000 million has been made for the development of the Valachchenai Industrial Estate, feasibility studies for the proposed Puliyankulam Industrial Estate and further development of industrial estates, including Ingiriya, Dambulla, Raigama-Millaniya and Katunayake.

The Government has also allocated Rs. 67,687 million for interest subsidies and refinancing facilities for micro, small and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs).

Through these schemes, the Government expects to provide approximately Rs. 174 billion in concessionary loans to entrepreneurs through State and private banks.

The Government also plans to strengthen trade agreements and improve access to international markets.

A Sri Lanka-European Union Sustainable Trade and Economic Integration Project will be implemented with a grant of Rs. 3,430 million between 2027 and 2030 to improve export competitiveness and diversify exports.

The Budget proposes several measures to expand the digital economy and improve the use of technology in public services.

An allocation of Rs. 10,000 million has been proposed for digital economy development programmes.

These include establishing a National Artificial Intelligence Centre of Excellence, improving digital government services, expanding the Lanka Government Cloud and strengthening digital payment systems.

The Government also plans to provide broadband vouchers to 750,000 selected school students.

A further Rs. 9,000 million has been allocated through the Telecommunications Regulatory Commission to expand broadband coverage under the Gamata Sannivedanaya programme.

The programme aims to complete 573 new telecommunications towers by 2028.

The Government has also allocated Rs. 23,800 million for research and development activities in 2027.

The Government plans to increase the contribution of renewable energy to the national electricity supply and strengthen the electricity transmission network.

The share of renewable energy in installed electricity generation capacity is expected to increase from around 62 per cent to 69 per cent by 2029.

A subsidy of Rs. 25,000 per kilowatt of battery storage capacity will be provided to domestic consumers installing new rooftop solar systems, subject to a maximum payment of Rs. 250,000.

An allocation of Rs. 3,500 million has been made for this programme.

The Government also plans to develop a 400 kV electricity transmission line from Kilinochchi to New Habarana, with Rs. 500 million allocated for preliminary work.

The programme to expand wind power generation is expected to add 370 MW by 2029, including 50 MW in 2027.

Floating solar panels are also planned for 10 reservoirs, with the potential to generate around 500 MW of electricity.

The Government plans to strengthen Sri Lanka's position as a regional shipping and logistics hub by expanding port capacity and related services.

The Colombo Port is expected to increase its shipping capacity to more than 30 million twenty-foot equivalent units by 2040.

Plans include developing the West Terminal II, studying the development of the Colombo North Port and establishing a logistics centre through a public-private partnership.

The Government also plans to increase bunkering capacity at the Port of Colombo by 50,000 metric tons with private sector participation.

The development of the Trincomalee Port as a fuel storage and energy hub will also receive attention.

An allocation of Rs. 100 million has been made to improve passenger facilities at the Sigiriya Domestic Airport, while Rs. 200 million has been allocated to improve access roads to the Katunayake Airport and conduct a feasibility study on additional passenger facilities.

Several housing programmes have been included in the Budget to support low-income families, displaced people and those affected by disasters.

Rs. 5,000 million has been allocated to complete houses that were partially constructed under previous housing programmes.

Another Rs. 4,000 million has been allocated for houses for displaced people in the Northern and Eastern provinces.

A further Rs. 2,000 million has been allocated to accelerate housing projects for low-income families, while Rs. 1,000 million has been set aside to construct houses damaged by disasters but not covered under the main reconstruction programme.

The Budget also includes allocations for water supply projects in Greater Kilinochchi, Kundasale-Haragama, Eheliyagoda and the Jaffna and Vanni districts.

The Government has allocated Rs. 2,900 million for 13 new disaster management projects.

These include improving safe centres, purchasing emergency response equipment, establishing an island-wide digital radio communication system and improving early warning facilities.

A modern Doppler Radar System is also expected to be installed in 2027 with Japanese assistance.

Another Rs. 5,000 million has been allocated as advance preparedness to meet possible El Niño-related risks.

The Government has also proposed a supplementary allocation of Rs. 260 billion for 2027 to complete reconstruction activities following Cyclone Ditwah.

Environmental protection measures include coastal conservation, air quality monitoring and the protection of the Central Highlands.

Several tax changes have been proposed to reduce costs for businesses, encourage investment and improve the competitiveness of the economy.

The Special Commodity Levy on imported sprats will be reduced from Rs. 100 to Rs. 10 per kilogram, while the levy on Maldive fish will be reduced from Rs. 302 to Rs. 10 per kilogram.

The levy on powdered and unpowdered dried chillies will also be reduced by Rs. 50 per kilogram with effect from November 13, 2026.

The Government has proposed a 25 per cent reduction in existing CESS rates on 800 selected import categories from April 2027.

The Ports and Airports Development Levy on imported newsprint under specified categories will be removed from January 1, 2027.

However, the Special Commodity Levy on imported sugar will be increased by Rs. 10 per kilogram to protect the local sugar industry.

The Tourism Development Levy imposed on the turnover of tourism establishments will be abolished from April 1, 2027, to reduce the tax burden on the tourism sector.

The Government expects these tax changes to result in a revenue reduction of Rs. 21,150 million during 2027.

President Dissanayake said the Budget proposals would be implemented through clearly identified institutions, with measurable targets and greater transparency.

The Government expects the proposed investments and reforms to move the country beyond economic stabilisation towards a period of higher production, stronger exports, better employment opportunities and improved living standards.
