Bangladesh raises minimum wage for garment workers after weeks of protests

abc NEWS - Bangladesh raised the minimum monthly wage for entry-level garment workers Wednesday by 36% to 17,000 takas ($136) from 12,500 takas after weeks of violent protests that closed factories and disrupted production as workers demanded the minimum wage be doubled.

The workers took to the streets, fought with police and attacked factories for weeks as they demanded better wages and said higher inflation had made their lives difficult.

The garment industry is Bangladesh’s main sector to earn foreign currency through exports, mostly to the United States and Europe. It represents about 80% of the country’s total exports and directly employs about 4 million workers, mostly women from rural areas.

A government-appointed wage board approved the new wage structure early Wednesday after nearly 12 hours of meetings between workers’ and employers’ representatives.

The six-member board, including two representatives each for workers and employers, approved the new wage structure in a 4-2 vote supporting 17,000 takas as the minimum wage. The other two board members sided with workers’ revised demand for 18,000 takas per month.

The wage board also revised monthly wages for other workers.

It was not immediately clear if the workers would accept the new wage structure.
